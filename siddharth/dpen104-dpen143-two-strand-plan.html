DPEN104 · DPEN143 · Two-strand programme

Pre-read, keep warm, then HD in class

Three phases, one pair of subjects. First a complete pre-reading pass (21 Nov – 30 Jan), then a light March–June keep-warm cycle, then the 13-week in-session class aimed at HD. Pre-reading builds the map; class converts it into marks.

This is lesson work, not summary reading. Every day you open the linked DPEN104 and DPEN143 pages, watch their short videos, and solve the worked examples and practice cards on paper before revealing the solutions. Tutorials and tests appear only as samples — the full banks are saved for revision mode.
Day 23 is Sunday 13 December. That is the freeze point: finish the shared circuits-and-fields package and write both sheets from memory. Nothing new starts on 14–15 December.

Phase 2 — March to June keep-warm

Forty-five to sixty minutes, twice a week. About 8–10 hours a month. The job is to keep the January pattern sheets usable while other subjects take the heavy hours. The 13-week class will reteach everything; warmth means you still know which law to reach for.

Session shape. Three minutes rereading that week’s sheet. Twenty to twenty-five minutes redoing one problem you already solved in Nov–Jan, on paper, no notes. Ten minutes on the matching weekly lesson only if you stalled. Write one line: date, topic, clean / units / sign / method. Never open two week pages in one sitting. Never start a topic you skipped in January.

Phase 3 — 13-week in-session class, aim HD

HD is converting the first pass into exam marks, not rereading the courses. At UOW College that is roughly 85+. Treat class as second pass plus assessment. If you sit in lectures trying to learn \(R_{Th}\) from scratch, the pre-reading was wasted.

You lose HD on method, signs, units, and mixed setups — not on “I never saw Snell.” From week 1 of class keep an error log: units / sign / killed-source / frame / from-the-normal / \(\tau\) before numbers. HD is shrinking that list to almost empty.

Weekly rhythm (both subjects)

About 8–10 focused hours outside class, not four hours a day.

Night before lecture, 20–30 minReread that week’s sheet. Skim the matching weekly lesson. Do not re-watch all the videos. You are priming so the lecture is a check against official emphasis.
Same day, 15 minAdd anything the lecturer stressed that is not on your sheet. That gap is what they will examine.
Tutorial = the HD workClosed-book, on paper, before solutions. Redo every miss within 48 hours. 104 uses TUT 1–6. 143 uses that week’s practice cards, then Weeks 5 and 9 reviews when those modules close.
Weekend, 2–3 h mixedTwo 104 and two 143 problems from older weeks. If every weekend is only this week, a Thévenin-plus-transient still freezes you.

Tests and the final

In-class testsSit the copies on the tests page timed, no notes, about ten days before the real ones. Anything below clean method is a weekend repair, not a whole-topic restart.
FinalThe 100-mark mock plus 143 Week 5, Week 9, and Week 13, each once timed in week 12 and once in week 13. First sit finds holes. Second sit proves they closed. Do not collect a third mock for comfort.
Mixing ruleShared ideas (KVL, capacitors, Faraday) can still share a day. After 143 leaves circuits, do not mix 104 phasors with 143 photoelectric in one sitting.
What to stopRereading Guides 1–4 cover to cover. Re-watching every video. Untimed “I’ll just look at the solution.” Starting optional topics the lecturer did not mention.

Bar for a class week

TutorialThis week’s set done closed-book, misses redone within 48 hours.
Mixed weekendOne older 104 and one older 143 problem clean.
Sheet + logThe pattern sheet still fits on one page. The error log did not grow, or one old tag was retired.
If those four are true, you are on an HD path even if a lecture felt foggy. Fog in week 8 is normal; a growing error log is not. Pre-reading and March–June keep you from drowning. The 13 weeks are where marks are made.
