Parametric + Special Functions Set 1 — Worked Answers

Check the setup and restrictions, not only the final expression.
  1. Since $x=2t-1$, $t=(x+1)/2$. Therefore $$y=\left(\frac{x+1}{2}\right)^2+3=\frac{(x+1)^2}{4}+3.$$ The parameter interval gives $-3\le x\le3$. The traced part is the parabola segment $y=(x+1)^2/4+3$ for $-3\le x\le3$.
  2. Eliminating $t$ gives $$\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}=\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1.$$ For $0\le t\le\pi$, $y\ge0$. The motion starts at $(3,0)$, passes through $(0,2)$, and ends at $(-3,0)$. It traces the upper half of the ellipse counterclockwise.
  3. Use the sum/difference structure: $$x^2-y^2=\left(t+\frac1t\right)^2-\left(t-\frac1t\right)^2=4.$$ For $t>0$, AM-GM gives $x=t+1/t\ge2$. Thus $x^2-y^2=4$, and only the right branch $x\ge2$ is traced.
  4. At the origin, $x=0$ gives $t^2=1$, so $t=\pm1$; both also make $y=t(t^2-1)=0$. Away from $dx/dt=0$, $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3t^2-1}{2t}.$$ At $t=1$, the gradient is $1$; at $t=-1$, it is $-1$. The curve crosses itself at the origin with tangents $y=x$ and $y=-x$.
  5. The sign changes at $x=3$: $$f(x)=\begin{cases}6-3x,&x<3,\\x-6,&x\ge3.\end{cases}$$ For $x<3$, $6-3x=3$ gives $x=1$. For $x\ge3$, $x-6=3$ gives $x=9$. Solutions: $x=1,9$; the corner is $(3,-3)$.
  6. First create the two jumps: $$G(x)=-2+\bigl(1+\operatorname{sgn}(x+1)\bigr) +\frac32\bigl(1+\operatorname{sgn}(x-2)\bigr).$$ This has the right values off the breakpoints, but $G(-1)=-1$ and $G(2)=3/2$. Point corrections are supplied by $1-\operatorname{sgn}|x-a|$, which equals $1$ only at $x=a$. Hence $$\boxed{g(x)=G(x)+1-\operatorname{sgn}|x+1| -\frac32\bigl(1-\operatorname{sgn}|x-2|\bigr).}$$
  7. Continuity at $2$ requires $$2k+1=4-k\quad\Rightarrow\quad k=1.$$ The left derivative is $k=1$, while the right derivative of $x^2-k$ at $2$ is $2(2)=4$. The function is continuous but not differentiable at $x=2$.
  8. Break at $x=-2,1$. For $x<-2$, the left side is $(1-x)+(-x-2)=-2x-1$, giving $x=-3$. For $-2\le x<1$, it is $(1-x)+(x+2)=3$, so there is no solution to $3=5$. For $x\ge1$, it is $(x-1)+(x+2)=2x+1$, giving $x=2$. $x=-3$ or $x=2$.
  9. $$\begin{aligned} f'(x)&=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{(x+h)^2-3(x+h)-(x^2-3x)}h\\ &=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{2xh+h^2-3h}{h} =\lim_{h\to0}(2x+h-3)=2x-3. \end{aligned}$$ $f'(x)=2x-3$.
  10. At $x=4$, $$\frac{f(4+h)-f(4)}h=\frac{|h|}{h} =\begin{cases}1,&h>0,\\-1,&h<0.\end{cases}$$ The one-sided limits differ. Therefore $f'(4)$ does not exist.
  11. $$\begin{aligned} f'(x)&=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\frac1{x+h}-\frac1x}{h} =\lim_{h\to0}\frac{x-(x+h)}{hx(x+h)}\\ &=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{-1}{x(x+h)}=-\frac1{x^2}. \end{aligned}$$ This calculation requires $x\ne0$. $\dfrac{d}{dx}(1/x)=-1/x^2$.
  12. Here $dx/dt=2(t-1)$ and $dy/dt=3(t-1)(t+1)$. A horizontal tangent needs $dy/dt=0$ and $dx/dt\ne0$, so $t=-1$, giving $(x,y)=(3,2)$. At $t=1$ both derivatives vanish, so it is not automatically vertical. For $t\ne1$, $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3(t+1)}2\longrightarrow3\quad(t\to1),$$ so the tangent there has finite gradient $3$. Horizontal at $(3,2)$; there are no vertical tangents.
