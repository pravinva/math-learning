MATH141 Revision — Differential Equations Set 1 — Worked Answers

Worked solutions — check separation and the constant, not only the final line.
  1. Direct integration: $y=2x^3-x^2+x+C$. With $y(0)=3$: $C=3$. $y=2x^3-x^2+x+3$
  2. $y=-e^{-x}+C$. $y(0)=0\Rightarrow C=1$. $y=1-e^{-x}$
  3. Separate: $\dfrac{dy}{y}=5\,dx$ (for $y\neq0$). $\ln|y|=5x+K$, so $y=Ae^{5x}$. $y(0)=2\Rightarrow A=2$. $y=2e^{5x}$
  4. Same method: $y=Ae^{-2x}$. $y(0)=10\Rightarrow A=10$. $y=10e^{-2x}$
  5. $\dfrac{dy}{y}=x\,dx\Rightarrow\ln|y|=\dfrac{x^2}{2}+K\Rightarrow y=Ae^{x^2/2}$. $y(0)=4\Rightarrow A=4$. $y=4e^{x^2/2}$
  6. $y\,dy=x\,dx\Rightarrow\dfrac{y^2}{2}=\dfrac{x^2}{2}+C$. $y(0)=2\Rightarrow C=2$. So $y^2=x^2+4$, and $y>0$ gives $y=\sqrt{x^2+4}$
  7. $\dfrac{dy}{1+y^2}=x\,dx\Rightarrow\arctan y=\dfrac{x^2}{2}+C$. $y(0)=0\Rightarrow C=0$. $y=\tan(x^2/2)$ (on an interval about $0$ where this stays defined)
  8. The RHS is a sum $x+y$, not a product $f(x)g(y)$. It cannot be separated into $x$-only and $y$-only factors for all $x,y$. (Needs an integrating factor — beyond MATH141 scope.) Not separable
  9. Doubling every $3$ h: $N=200\cdot2^{t/3}$. After $9$ h: $200\cdot2^3=1600$. After $4$ h: $200\cdot2^{4/3}$. $1600$; $200\cdot2^{4/3}$
  10. $A=8000e^{0.06t}$. Double when $e^{0.06t}=2\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{\ln2}{0.06}=\dfrac{50\ln2}{3}$. $\dfrac{\ln2}{0.06}$ years
  11. $\dfrac{dS}{10-S}=\dfrac{dt}{50}$. Integrate: $-\ln|10-S|=\dfrac{t}{50}+C$. $S(0)=5\Rightarrow-\ln5=C$. So $\ln\dfrac{10-S}{5}=-\dfrac{t}{50}$, hence $S=10-5e^{-t/50}$. At $t=50$: $S=10-5e^{-1}=10-\dfrac{5}{e}$. $S=10-5e^{-t/50}$; $S(50)=10-5/e$
  12. Differentiate: $y'=-\sin\theta+i\cos\theta=i(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)=iy$. Also $y(0)=\cos0+i\sin0=1$. Solve $\dfrac{dy}{d\theta}=iy$, $y(0)=1$: separate $\dfrac{dy}{y}=i\,d\theta$ (or quote $y=Ae^{i\theta}$), so $y=Ae^{i\theta}$ with $A=1$. Thus $y=e^{i\theta}$. Both $\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ and $e^{i\theta}$ solve the same IVP, so they are equal. $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$
