4.6ASine and cosine powers

For $\int\sin^m x\cos^n x\,dx$, first inspect the parity of the exponents. An odd exponent lets you save one factor to become $du$ and convert all remaining factors using $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$. If both powers are even, no lone factor remains, so use the half-angle identities $\sin^2x=(1-\cos2x)/2$ and $\cos^2x=(1+\cos2x)/2$.

Recognition rule

Odd sine power: save $\sin x\,dx$, rewrite the rest in $\cos x$, then use $u=\cos x$. Odd cosine power: save $\cos x\,dx$, rewrite the rest in $\sin x$, then use $u=\sin x$. Both even: repeatedly lower powers with half-angle identities.

Worked example — an odd power

Evaluate $\int\sin^3x\cos^2x\,dx$. Save $\sin x\,dx$ and write $\sin^2x=1-\cos^2x$:

$$\int(1-\cos^2x)\cos^2x\sin x\,dx=-\int(u^2-u^4)\,du=-\frac{u^3}{3}+\frac{u^5}{5}+C.$$

Thus the answer is $-\cos^3x/3+\cos^5x/5+C$. The minus sign is from $du=-\sin x\,dx$.

Pitfall check

Do not use $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$ by itself when both powers are even: it does not leave the differential needed for a substitution. Half-angle identities are the power-lowering tool for that case.

4.6BTangent and secant powers

The useful pair is $\tan^2x=\sec^2x-1$ and $\sec^2x=1+\tan^2x$. An even power of secant is valuable because $\sec^2x\,dx=d(\tan x)$. An odd power of tangent is valuable because $\sec x\tan x\,dx=d(\sec x)$. Choose the identity that leaves one of these derivative pairs behind.

Worked example — reserve $\sec^2x$

$$\int\tan^2x\sec^2x\,dx=\int(\sec^2x-1)\sec^2x\,dx.$$

It is cleaner to notice immediately that $u=\tan x$ gives $\int u^2\,du=\tan^3x/3+C$. Rewriting first is useful only when it makes the surviving derivative pair visible.

Why this differs from sine/cosine

$\tan x$ and $\sec x$ do not satisfy a “sum equals one” identity. Instead their identity deliberately contains $\sec^2x$, the derivative of $\tan x$. That is why parity is checked differently: even secant or odd tangent is the helpful feature.

4.6CProduct-to-sum and $\int\sec x\,dx$

When angles are different, a product may be harder than a sum. Product-to-sum identities turn it into integrable sines or cosines:

Product-to-sum identities

$$\sin A\cos B=\tfrac12[\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)],\quad \cos A\cos B=\tfrac12[\cos(A+B)+\cos(A-B)]$$ $$\sin A\sin B=\tfrac12[\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)].$$

Worked example — the secant result

Multiply by a carefully chosen $1$:

$$\int\sec x\,dx=\int\frac{\sec x(\sec x+\tan x)}{\sec x+\tan x}\,dx=\int\frac{\sec^2x+\sec x\tan x}{\sec x+\tan x}\,dx.$$

The numerator is the derivative of the denominator, hence $\boxed{\int\sec x\,dx=\ln|\sec x+\tan x|+C}$. This is not a random trick: build an $f'/f$ form.

PracticeTry before revealing

1
Find $\int\sin^3x\,dx$.
SolutionWrite $\sin^3x=(1-\cos^2x)\sin x$. With $u=\cos x$, $du=-\sin x\,dx$: $-\int(1-u^2)du=-\cos x+\cos^3x/3+C$.
2
Find $\int\sin^2x\,dx$.
Solution$\sin^2x=(1-\cos2x)/2$, so $\int\sin^2x\,dx=x/2-\sin2x/4+C$.
3
Find $\int\sin^2x\cos^3x\,dx$.
SolutionSave $\cos x$, then $\cos^2x=1-\sin^2x$. $u=\sin x$ gives $\int u^2(1-u^2)du=\sin^3x/3-\sin^5x/5+C$.
4
Find $\int\cos^4x\,dx$.
Solution$\cos^4x=[(1+\cos2x)/2]^2=(3+4\cos2x+\cos4x)/8$. Integrating gives $3x/8+\sin2x/4+\sin4x/32+C$.
5
Find $\int\tan^3x\sec^2x\,dx$.
Solution$u=\tan x$, $du=\sec^2x\,dx$, hence $\int u^3du=\tan^4x/4+C$.
6
Find $\int\tan^2x\,dx$.
SolutionUse $\tan^2x=\sec^2x-1$: $\tan x-x+C$.
7
Find $\int\sec^3x\tan x\,dx$.
SolutionSave $\sec x\tan x\,dx$, and let $u=\sec x$. Then $\int u^2du=\sec^3x/3+C$.
8
Find $\int\sin3x\cos2x\,dx$.
Solution$\sin3x\cos2x=\frac{1}{2}(\sin5x+\sin x)$. Thus $-\cos5x/10-\cos x/2+C$.
9
Find $\int\cos4x\cos x\,dx$.
Solution$\cos4x\cos x=\frac{1}{2}(\cos5x+\cos3x)$, so $\sin5x/10+\sin3x/6+C$.
10
Prove by differentiation that $\ln|\sec x+\tan x|$ is an antiderivative of $\sec x$.
SolutionDifferentiate: $\frac{\sec x\tan x+\sec^2x}{\sec x+\tan x}=\frac{\sec x(\tan x+\sec x)}{\sec x+\tan x}=\sec x$, on intervals where the expression is defined.
Practice 11

Find $\displaystyle\int\sin^4 x\cos^3 x\,dx$.

Worked solution

Save one $\cos$: $\int\sin^4 x(1-\sin^2 x)\cos x\,dx$. $u=\sin x$: $\dfrac{u^5}{5}-\dfrac{u^7}{7}+C$.

Practice 12

Find $\displaystyle\int\cos^2 x\sin^2 x\,dx$ using a power-reducing identity.

Worked solution

$\sin^2\cos^2=\dfrac14\sin^2 2x=\dfrac18(1-\cos 4x)$: $\dfrac{x}{8}-\dfrac{\sin 4x}{32}+C$.

Practice 13

Find $\displaystyle\int\tan^4 x\,dx$.

Worked solution

$\tan^4=\tan^2(\sec^2-1)=\tan^2\sec^2-\tan^2$, then again: $\dfrac13\tan^3 x-\tan x+x+C$.

Practice 14

Find $\displaystyle\int\sec^4 x\,dx$.

Worked solution

$\sec^4=\sec^2(1+\tan^2)$: $\tan x+\dfrac13\tan^3 x+C$.

Practice 15

Find $\displaystyle\int\sin 5x\sin 2x\,dx$ via product-to-sum.

Worked solution

$\dfrac12\int(\cos 3x-\cos 7x)\,dx=\dfrac16\sin 3x-\dfrac{1}{14}\sin 7x+C$.

Practice 16

Find $\displaystyle\int\sin x\cos 4x\,dx$.

Worked solution

$\dfrac12\int(\sin 5x-\sin 3x)\,dx=-\dfrac{1}{10}\cos 5x+\dfrac{1}{6}\cos 3x+C$.

Practice 17

Show that $\displaystyle\int\tan x\,dx=-\ln|\cos x|+C$ (or $\ln|\sec x|+C$).

Worked solution

$\tan=\sin/\cos$; $u=\cos x$: $-\ln|\cos x|+C$.

Practice 18

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{\sin^3 x}{\cos^2 x}\,dx$.

Worked solution

Rewrite as $\sin x(\sec^2 x-1)$. With $u=\cos x$, $du=-\sin x\,dx$: $$-\int(u^{-2}-1)\,du=\dfrac{1}{u}+u+C=\sec x+\cos x+C.$$

Practice 19

Find $\displaystyle\int\cos^3 x\,dx$.

Worked solution

$\int(1-\sin^2 x)\cos x\,dx=\sin x-\dfrac13\sin^3 x+C$.

Practice 20

Decide strategy first: for $\displaystyle\int\sin^2 x\cos^2 x\,dx$, should you save a factor or use identities? Carry out the better plan.

Worked solution

Even powers of both — use identities / double angle, not saving a factor. As above: $\dfrac{x}{8}-\dfrac{\sin 4x}{32}+C$.
