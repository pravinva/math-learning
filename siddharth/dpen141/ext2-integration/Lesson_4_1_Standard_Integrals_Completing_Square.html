1 Integration reverses differentiation

Core principle

If $F'(x)=f(x)$, then $\int f(x)\,dx=F(x)+C$. The constant is unavoidable: differentiation erases constants. Integration is not a hunt for a single answer but for a whole family whose derivative is the integrand.

Before choosing a technique, ask: what derivative could have produced this? This recognition question prevents formula collecting from replacing reasoning.

Worked example

$\int(6x^2-4\sin x+3e^x)dx=2x^3+4\cos x+3e^x+C$. Differentiate each term to check it returns the integrand.

2 The standard pattern library

Essential forms

$$\int x^n dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C\ (n\neq -1),\quad \int e^{ax}dx=\frac{e^{ax}}a+C,\quad \int\frac{dx}{x}=\ln|x|+C.$$

$$\int\sin(ax)dx=-\frac{\cos(ax)}a+C,\quad\int\cos(ax)dx=\frac{\sin(ax)}a+C.$$

$$\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}=\arcsin\frac{x}{a}+C,\qquad\int\frac{dx}{x^2+a^2}=\frac{1}{a}\arctan\frac{x}{a}+C.$$

Recognition warning

The power rule does not apply to $x^{-1}$. That exponent would force division by zero; $\int x^{-1}dx=\ln|x|+C$ is its own special case.

3 Completing the square

Why reshape a quadratic?

A quadratic $x^2+bx+c$ hides its centre. Completing the square writes it as $(x+\frac b2)^2+k$, making the $u^2+a^2$ or $a^2-u^2$ pattern visible. Always make the coefficient of $x^2$ equal to $1$ first.

Worked example

$$x^2-6x+13=(x-3)^2+4.$$ Therefore $\int\frac{dx}{x^2-6x+13}=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\frac{x-3}{2}+C$. The shift $x-3$ has derivative $1$, so no extra factor is needed.

4 Splitting a linear numerator

Derivative matching

For $\frac{mx+n}{x^2+bx+c}$, rewrite $mx+n=A(2x+b)+B$. The first part integrates to $A\ln|x^2+bx+c|$; complete the square in the remaining constant-over-quadratic part.

Worked example

$$\frac{3x+5}{x^2+2x+5}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{2x+2}{x^2+2x+5}+\frac2{(x+1)^2+4}.$$

So the integral is $\frac{3}{2}\ln(x^2+2x+5)+\arctan\frac{x+1}{2}+C$.

5 A decision checklist

Choose deliberately

1. Simplify and split sums. 2. Is it a standard form or a constant-factor version? 3. Is there $f'/f$? 4. Is there a quadratic that needs completing the square? 5. Does a linear numerator need splitting into a derivative part plus a constant? Only then move to later techniques.

Do not complete the square mechanically when factoring, substitution, or a direct standard integral is already clearer.

P Practice with full solutions

Attempt each question before revealing the solution. A correct method matters as much as the final antiderivative: keep constants, show substitutions, and differentiate your answer when unsure.

Practice 1

$\int(4x^3-2/x)dx.$

Worked solution

$\int4x^3dx=x^4$ and $\int-2/x\,dx=-2\ln|x|$. Answer: $x^4-2\ln|x|+C$.

Practice 2

$\int e^{5x}dx.$

Worked solution

The inner derivative is $5$, so compensate: $\frac{1}{5}e^{5x}+C$.

Practice 3

$\int\cos(3x)dx.$

Worked solution

$\frac{1}{3}\sin(3x)+C$; differentiating gives $\cos(3x)$.

Practice 4

$\int\frac{dx}{x^2+9}.$

Worked solution

Here $a=3$: $\frac{1}{3}\arctan(x/3)+C$.

Practice 5

$\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{25-x^2}}.$

Worked solution

Match $a=5$: $\arcsin(x/5)+C$.

Practice 6

$\int\frac{dx}{x^2+4x+8}.$

Worked solution

$x^2+4x+8=(x+2)^2+4$. Hence $\frac{1}{2}\arctan((x+2)/2)+C$.

Practice 7

$\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{4+4x-x^2}}.$

Worked solution

$4+4x-x^2=8-(x-2)^2$. Thus $\arcsin((x-2)/(2\sqrt2))+C$.

Practice 8

$\int\frac{2x+7}{x^2+2x+5}dx.$

Worked solution

$2x+7=(2x+2)+5$. The result is $\ln(x^2+2x+5)+\frac{5}{2}\arctan((x+1)/2)+C$.

Practice 9

$\int\frac{x+1}{x^2+2x+10}dx.$

Worked solution

Numerator is half the denominator derivative. Answer $\frac{1}{2}\ln(x^2+2x+10)+C$.

Practice 10

$\int\frac{4x-1}{x^2-2x+5}dx.$

Worked solution

$4x-1=2(2x-2)+3$ and denominator $(x-1)^2+4$. Result $2\ln(x^2-2x+5)+\frac{3}{2}\arctan((x-1)/2)+C$.

Practice 11

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{2x^2+8}$. Rewrite carefully to an arctan form.

Worked solution

Factor out 2: $\dfrac12\int\dfrac{dx}{x^2+4}$. With $a=2$: $$\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac12\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)+C=\dfrac14\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)+C.$$

Practice 12

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{7-6x-x^2}}$ by completing the square.

Worked solution

$7-6x-x^2=16-(x+3)^2$. Hence $$\sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{x+3}{4}\right)+C.$$

Practice 13

For $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{5x+1}{x^2+4x+13}\,dx$, split the numerator into $A(2x+4)+B$ and finish.

Worked solution

$A=\dfrac52$, $B=-9$. Then $$\dfrac52\ln(x^2+4x+13)-3\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{x+2}{3}\right)+C.$$

Practice 14

Without integrating, decide whether $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{x^2-6x+8}$ is arctan or log. Justify.

Worked solution

$x^2-6x+8=(x-3)^2-1$, a difference of squares, so log / partial fractions — not arctan.

Practice 15

Now evaluate $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{x^2-6x+8}$.

Worked solution

$\dfrac{1}{(x-2)(x-4)}=\dfrac{1/2}{x-4}-\dfrac{1/2}{x-2}$, so $$\dfrac12\ln\left|\dfrac{x-4}{x-2}\right|+C.$$

Practice 16

Find $\displaystyle\int\sec^2(3x-1)\,dx$ and verify by differentiation.

Worked solution

$\dfrac13\tan(3x-1)+C$. Differentiating recovers $\sec^2(3x-1)$.

Practice 17

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{9-4x^2}}\,dx$. Watch the coefficient of $x^2$.

Worked solution

Let $u=2x$: $2\int\dfrac{du}{\sqrt{9-u^2}}=2\sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{2x}{3}\right)+C$.

Practice 18

Explain the error in claiming $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{1}{x^2+6x+10}\,dx=\tan^{-1}(x^2+6x+10)+C$.

Worked solution

Arctan needs the completed-square variable as the input. Correct: $(x+3)^2+1$ gives $\tan^{-1}(x+3)+C$.

Practice 19

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\left(e^{-2x}+\dfrac{1}{2x+1}\right)dx$.

Worked solution

$-\dfrac12 e^{-2x}+\dfrac12\ln|2x+1|+C$.

Practice 20

Is $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{2x+6}{x^2+6x+10}\,dx=\ln|x^2+6x+10|+C$ fully correct? Justify.

Worked solution

Yes — the numerator is exactly the derivative of the denominator.
