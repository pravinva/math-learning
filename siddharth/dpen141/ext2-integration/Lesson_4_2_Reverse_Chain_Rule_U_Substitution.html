1 The reverse chain rule

Pattern

If $\frac d{dx}F(g(x))=F'(g(x))g'(x)$, then $\int F'(g(x))g'(x)dx=F(g(x))+C$. The outer function tells you the antiderivative; the inner derivative tells you the required multiplier.

Worked example

$\int 6x(3x^2+1)^5dx$. Since $d(3x^2+1)=6x\,dx$, reverse the power rule: $\frac{(3x^2+1)^6}{6}+C$.

2 Formal u-substitution

A controlled rewrite

Set $u=g(x)$, calculate $du=g'(x)dx$, and rewrite every $x$-dependent part in terms of $u$. The aim is an integral in one variable, not an expression containing both $u$ and $x$.

Worked example

For $\int x\sqrt{x^2+4}\,dx$, let $u=x^2+4$, so $du=2x\,dx$. Then $\frac{1}{2}\int u^{1/2}du=\frac{1}{3}u^{3/2}+C=\frac{1}{3}(x^2+4)^{3/2}+C$.

3 Compositions to notice

Frequent signals

Algebraic: $(ax+b)^n$, $\sqrt{q(x)}$. Exponential: $e^{q(x)}$. Logarithmic: $q'(x)/q(x)$. Trigonometric: $\sin(q(x))q'(x)$, $\sec^2(q(x))q'(x)$. The inside may be a polynomial, trig function, exponential, or logarithm.

Constant factors

If $du=8x\,dx$ but you have $2x\,dx$, write $2x\,dx=\frac14du$. Adjust constants before integrating; do not add them afterward by guesswork.

4 Avoiding dx mistakes

The differential is part of the substitution

$du$ is not decoration. In $u=\sin x$, $du=\cos x\,dx$ means the whole product $\cos x\,dx$ is replaced. Writing $du=\cos x$ or leaving $dx$ after substitution breaks the change of variable.

Worked example

$\int \frac{\cos x}{1+\sin x}dx$: $u=1+\sin x$, $du=\cos xdx$, so $\int du/u=\ln|1+\sin x|+C$.

5 When substitution is not the tool

Sanity check

A promising $u$ must simplify the whole integrand. In $\int xe^x dx$, $u=x$ leaves $e^x$ and $du=dx$ does not solve the product; integration by parts is better. Test the rewrite before committing.

P Practice with full solutions

Attempt each question before revealing the solution. A correct method matters as much as the final antiderivative: keep constants, show substitutions, and differentiate your answer when unsure.

Practice 1

$\int 8x(x^2+1)^4dx.$

Worked solution

$u=x^2+1$, $du=2x dx$, so $8x dx=4du$. Answer $\frac45(x^2+1)^5+C$.

Practice 2

$\int \sin(7x)dx.$

Worked solution

$u=7x$, $dx=du/7$. Answer $-\cos(7x)/7+C$.

Practice 3

$\int e^{2x-3}dx.$

Worked solution

$u=2x-3$, $du=2dx$. Answer $\frac{1}{2}e^{2x-3}+C$.

Practice 4

$\int\frac{3x^2}{x^3+5}dx.$

Worked solution

$u=x^3+5$, $du=3x^2dx$. Answer $\ln|x^3+5|+C$.

Practice 5

$\int\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}dx.$

Worked solution

$u=x^2+9$, $x dx=du/2$. This is $\frac{1}{2}\int u^{-1/2}du=\sqrt{x^2+9}+C$.

Practice 6

$\int\cos x\,e^{\sin x}dx.$

Worked solution

$u=\sin x$, $du=\cos xdx$. Answer $e^{\sin x}+C$.

Practice 7

$\int\sec^2(4x)dx.$

Worked solution

$u=4x$, $dx=du/4$. Answer $\frac14\tan(4x)+C$.

Practice 8

$\int\frac{dx}{x(\ln x)^2}$.

Worked solution

$u=\ln x$, $du=dx/x$. Then $\int u^{-2}du=-u^{-1}+C=-1/\ln x+C$.

Practice 9

$\int\sin x\cos^3x\,dx.$

Worked solution

Set $u=\cos x$, $du=-\sin xdx$. Then $-\int u^3du=-\cos^4x/4+C$.

Practice 10

$\int\frac{6x}{(3x^2-1)^2}dx.$

Worked solution

$u=3x^2-1$, $du=6x dx$. Answer $\int u^{-2}du=-1/(3x^2-1)+C$.

Practice 11

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{x^3}{\sqrt{x^4+1}}\,dx$.

Worked solution

$u=x^4+1$, $du=4x^3\,dx$: $\dfrac12\sqrt{x^4+1}+C$.

Practice 12

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{\cos(\ln x)}{x}\,dx$ for $x>0$.

Worked solution

$u=\ln x$: $\sin(\ln x)+C$.

Practice 13

Find $\displaystyle\int x\,e^{x^2+3}\,dx$.

Worked solution

$u=x^2+3$: $\dfrac12 e^{x^2+3}+C$.

Practice 14

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{x\sqrt{\ln x}}$ for $x>e$.

Worked solution

$u=\ln x$: $2\sqrt{\ln x}+C$.

Practice 15

Why is $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{x+1}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\,dx$ not a pure reverse-chain? Set up the split.

Worked solution

Need $\dfrac12\int\dfrac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\,dx+\int\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}=\sqrt{x^2+1}+\ln|x+\sqrt{x^2+1}|+C$.

Practice 16

Find $\displaystyle\int\sin^5 x\cos x\,dx$.

Worked solution

$u=\sin x$: $\dfrac16\sin^6 x+C$.

Practice 17

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{3}{(2x-5)^4}\,dx$.

Worked solution

$u=2x-5$: $-\dfrac{1}{2(2x-5)^3}+C$.

Practice 18

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{e^{2x}}{1+e^{2x}}\,dx$.

Worked solution

$u=1+e^{2x}$: $\dfrac12\ln|1+e^{2x}|+C$.

Practice 19

Correct a student who sets $u=\sin x$ in $\displaystyle\int\sin x\cos^2 x\,dx$ but keeps $\cos^2 x$ in the $u$-integral. Finish the integral properly.

Worked solution

Better: $u=\cos x$, $du=-\sin x\,dx$: $-\int u^2\,du=-\dfrac13\cos^3 x+C$.

Practice 20

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{x}{(x^2+4)^3}\,dx$.

Worked solution

$u=x^2+4$: $-\dfrac{1}{4(x^2+4)^2}+C$.
