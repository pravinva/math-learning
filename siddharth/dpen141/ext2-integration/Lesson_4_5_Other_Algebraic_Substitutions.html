1 Why introduce an algebraic substitution?

Aim: one simpler variable

Some radicals contain a repeated expression but not its derivative. A substitution such as $u^2=x+a$ can turn both the radical and $dx$ into powers of $u$, leaving a polynomial or rational integral. The point is simplification, not merely changing symbols.

Worked example

$\int\sqrt{x+1}dx$ is already a power integral, but let $u^2=x+1$ to see the mechanism: $x=u^2-1$, $dx=2u du$, and the integral becomes $2\int u^2du=2u^3/3+C=\frac23(x+1)^{3/2}+C$.

2 Root substitutions

Typical choices

For $\sqrt{x+a}$, use $u^2=x+a$. For $\sqrt[3]{x+a}$, use $u^3=x+a$. For $\sqrt{x^2+a}$, a different substitution may be needed later; do not assume every square root wants $u^2=$ its inside.

Track dx explicitly

From $u^2=x+a$, solve for $x=u^2-a$ and differentiate: $dx=2u\,du$. Both replacements are needed. The extra $u$ from $dx$ often removes the remaining radical.

3 Rationalising fractional powers

Worked example

$$\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}+1}.\quad u=\sqrt{x},\ x=u^2,\ dx=2u\,du.$$

Then $\int\frac{2u}{u+1}du=\int(2-\frac2{u+1})du=2u-2\ln|u+1|+C$. Return to $x$: $2\sqrt{x}-2\ln(\sqrt{x}+1)+C$.

A useful test

If the integrand contains several powers whose exponents share a denominator, set $u=x^{1/n}$ for that denominator. This often converts every fractional power to an integer power.

4 When to try it

Beyond reverse chain rule

Try an algebraic substitution when radicals prevent ordinary algebra from working, when a denominator looks like $\sqrt{x}\pm1$, or when terms such as $x^{1/2},x^{3/2}$ suggest a common root variable. First look for simple simplification or a reverse-chain pattern; substitution is a tool, not a ritual.

Worked example

For $\int\frac{x}{\sqrt{x+2}}dx$, set $u^2=x+2$. Then $x=u^2-2$, $dx=2u du$, and the integrand becomes $2(u^2-2)du$. This is $\frac23u^3-4u+C=\frac23(x+2)^{3/2}-4\sqrt{x+2}+C$.

5 Check the domain and answer

Good finishing habits

Radicals may impose $x\ge0$ or another interval. Keep logarithm absolute values unless positivity is guaranteed. After back-substituting, differentiate the answer; this is especially valuable because errors in $dx$ are common.

P Practice with full solutions

Attempt each question before revealing the solution. A correct method matters as much as the final antiderivative: keep constants, show substitutions, and differentiate your answer when unsure.

Practice 1

$\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}}$.

Worked solution

This is $\int x^{-1/2}dx=2\sqrt{x}+C$. With $u=\sqrt{x}$, it also becomes $\int2du=2u+C$.

Practice 2

$\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}+2}$.

Worked solution

$u=\sqrt{x}$, $dx=2u du$. $2u/(u+2)=2-4/(u+2)$. Answer $2\sqrt{x}-4\ln(\sqrt{x}+2)+C$.

Practice 3

$\int\sqrt{x+3}\,dx$.

Worked solution

$u^2=x+3$, $dx=2u du$. $2\int u^2du=\frac23(x+3)^{3/2}+C$.

Practice 4

$\int\frac{x}{\sqrt{x+1}}dx.$

Worked solution

$u^2=x+1$, $x=u^2-1$, $dx=2u du$. Integral $2\int(u^2-1)du=\frac23u^3-2u+C=\frac23(x+1)^{3/2}-2\sqrt{x+1}+C$.

Practice 5

$\int\frac{dx}{1+\sqrt[3]{x}}$.

Worked solution

$u=\sqrt[3]{x}$, $x=u^3$, $dx=3u^2du$. Divide $3u^2/(1+u)=3u-3+3/(u+1)$. Answer $\frac{3}{2}u^2-3u+3\ln|u+1|+C$, then replace $u$.

Practice 6

$\int\frac{\sqrt{x}}{1+x}dx.$

Worked solution

$u=\sqrt{x}$ gives $dx=2u du$ and $\frac{\sqrt{x}}{1+x}dx=\frac{2u^2}{1+u^2}du=2-\frac2{1+u^2}$. Answer $2\sqrt{x}-2\arctan\sqrt{x}+C$.

Practice 7

$\int\frac{dx}{x+\sqrt{x}}$.

Worked solution

$u=\sqrt{x}$: denominator $u^2+u=u(u+1)$, $dx=2u du$. Integral $\int2/(u+1)du=2\ln(\sqrt{x}+1)+C$.

Practice 8

$\int\frac{x+1}{\sqrt{x+1}}dx.$

Worked solution

Simplify first: $(x+1)^{1/2}$. Answer $\frac23(x+1)^{3/2}+C$; a substitution would be unnecessary extra work.

Practice 9

$\int\frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x+1}}dx.$

Worked solution

$u^2=x+1$, $x=u^2-1$, $dx=2u du$. Integral $2\int(u^2-1)^2du=2(u^5/5-2u^3/3+u)+C$. Replace $u=\sqrt{x+1}$.

Practice 10

$\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}(1+\sqrt{x})}$.

Worked solution

$u=\sqrt{x}$ gives $dx=2u du$ and integral $\int2/(1+u)du=2\ln(1+\sqrt{x})+C$.

Practice 11

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{1+\sqrt{x+1}}$ with $u=\sqrt{x+1}$.

Worked solution

$2\sqrt{x+1}-2\ln|1+\sqrt{x+1}|+C$.

Practice 12

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{x^2}{\sqrt{4-x}}\,dx$.

Worked solution

With $u=\sqrt{4-x}$: $-32u+\dfrac{16}{3}u^3-\dfrac{2}{5}u^5+C$.

Practice 13

Outline a rationalising substitution for $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$, then explain why the standard arcsin form is preferable.

Worked solution

Exotic $t$-substitutions work but are long; the integrand is already $\sin^{-1}x+C$. Prefer recognition.

Practice 14

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{x^{1/2}+x^{1/3}}$ with $u=x^{1/6}$.

Worked solution

$6\left(\dfrac{u^3}{3}-\dfrac{u^2}{2}+u-\ln|u+1|\right)+C$ with $u=x^{1/6}$.

Practice 15

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{1+x}\,dx$ with $u=\sqrt{x}$.

Worked solution

$2\sqrt{x}-2\tan^{-1}\sqrt{x}+C$.

Practice 16

Find $\displaystyle\int x\sqrt{2x+3}\,dx$.

Worked solution

With $u=\sqrt{2x+3}$: $\dfrac{u^5}{10}-\dfrac{u^3}{2}+C$.

Practice 17

When is $u^2=ax+b$ a better first move than reverse chain? Give a criterion.

Worked solution

When $x$ itself must be rewritten in terms of the root (polynomial times $\sqrt{ax+b}$), not when you already see $f'/ \sqrt{f}$.

Practice 18

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{x}(x+1)}$.

Worked solution

$u=\sqrt{x}$: $2\tan^{-1}\sqrt{x}+C$.

Practice 19

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{1+x}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx$ two ways: directly, and with $u=\sqrt{x}$.

Worked solution

Both give $2\sqrt{x}+\dfrac23 x^{3/2}+C$.

Practice 20

Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{(1+\sqrt[3]{x})\sqrt[3]{x}}$.

Worked solution

$u=x^{1/3}$: $3u-3\ln|1+u|+C$.
