MATH141 Revision — Differentiation Set 3 — Worked Answers

Worked solutions — check method and restrictions, not only the final line.
  1. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{3t^2-1}{2t}$ for $t\neq0$. $(3t^2-1)/(2t)$
  2. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{3}{2}\cot t$. At $t=\pi/4$: $-\dfrac{3}{2}$. gradient $-3/2$
  3. $y'=-x/y$; at $(3,4)$: $-3/4$. Tangent $3x+4y=25$. $3x+4y=25$
  4. $1+8=12$, so the point lies on the curve. Differentiating: $3x^2+3y^2y'=6y+6xy'$. At $(1,2)$: $3+12y'=12+6y'\Rightarrow y'=3/2$. $3/2$
  5. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{\sin t}{1-\cos t}$ (equivalently $\cot(t/2)$ for $t\neq0$). $\sin t/(1-\cos t)$
  6. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{3t}{2}$; $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{3}{4t}$. At $t=1$: $3/4$. $3/4$
  7. $e^y y'=1+y'\Rightarrow y'=1/(e^y-1)$ for $e^y\neq1$. $1/(e^y-1)$
  8. $y'=-y/x=-1$ at $(2,2)$; normal has slope $1$, so $y=x$. $y=x$
  9. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2t^2$; $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=4t^2$. $2t^2$ and $4t^2$
  10. Implicit: $y'=-x/y$. A radius to $(x_0,y_0)$ has slope $y_0/x_0$ (if $x_0\neq0$). Product: $(-x_0/y_0)(y_0/x_0)=-1$, so the radius is orthogonal to the tangent. Vertical/horizontal radii are handled separately and also work. Shown
  11. $y'=\dfrac{\cos y}{\cos y+x\sin y}$. $\cos y/(\cos y+x\sin y)$
  12. $dy/dt=3(t-1)(t+1)$, $dx/dt=2(t-1)$. Horizontal when $dy/dt=0$ and $dx/dt\neq0$: $t=-1$, point $(3,2)$. $(3,2)$ only
