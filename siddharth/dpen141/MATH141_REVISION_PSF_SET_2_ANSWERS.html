Parametric + Special Functions Set 2 — Worked Answers

Each solution includes the restriction or limit argument that earns the method marks.
  1. $$\frac{(x-1)^2}{4}+\frac{(y+2)^2}{9}=\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1.$$ On the stated interval, $\cos t\ge0$, so $x\ge1$. At $t=-\pi/2$ the point is $(1,-5)$; it moves through $(3,-2)$ to $(1,1)$. The right half of the ellipse is traced from bottom to top, counterclockwise.
  2. We have $y^2=t^6=(t^2)^3=x^3$. Since $x=t^2$, $0\le x\le4$, while $-8\le y\le8$ and the sign of $y$ matches the sign of $t$. The path is $y^2=x^3$, $0\le x\le4$: from $(4,-8)$ into the cusp $(0,0)$ and out to $(4,8)$.
  3. $$x^2=\left(t+\frac1t\right)^2=t^2+2+\frac1{t^2}=y+2,$$ so $y=x^2-2$. If $t>0$, $x\ge2$; if $t<0$, $x\le-2$. $y=x^2-2$ with $x\in(-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)$.
  4. Equal $x$-coordinates arise for $t$ and $-t$. Their $y$-coordinates are opposites, so they agree when $t^3-4t=0$. The nonzero solutions are $t=\pm2$, both giving $(2,0)$. Also $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3t^2-4}{2t},$$ which equals $2$ at $t=2$ and $-2$ at $t=-2$. The curve crosses itself at $(2,0)$ with two distinct tangents.
  5. Continuity requires $a+b=1$. Differentiability requires the left gradient $a$ to equal the right derivative $2x$ at $x=1$, so $a=2$. Therefore $b=-1$. $a=2,\ b=-1$.
  6. $$f(x)=\begin{cases}x^2-4,&x\le-2\text{ or }x\ge2,\\4-x^2,&-2<x<2.\end{cases}$$ At $x=-2$, the outer derivative is $2x=-4$ and the inner derivative is $-2x=4$. At $x=2$, the inner derivative is $-4$ and the outer derivative is $4$. $f$ is not differentiable at $x=\pm2$.
  7. For $x<-2$, the sum is $-2$. At $x=-2$, it is $-1+0=-1$. For $-2<x<1$, it is $-1+1=0$. At $x=1$, it is $0+1=1$; for $x>1$, it is $2$. The solution set is $(-2,1)$.
  8. If $x>0$, $|x|\operatorname{sgn}(x)=x(1)=x$. If $x<0$, it is $(-x)(-1)=x$. At $x=0$, it is $0\cdot0=0$. Replacing $x$ by $x-3$ gives $|x-3|\operatorname{sgn}(x-3)=x-3$. The expression simplifies to $x-1$.
  9. $$\begin{aligned} f'(x)&=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sqrt{x+h}-\sqrt x}{h} \frac{\sqrt{x+h}+\sqrt x}{\sqrt{x+h}+\sqrt x}\\ &=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+h}+\sqrt x} =\frac1{2\sqrt x}. \end{aligned}$$ $f'(x)=1/(2\sqrt{x})$ for $x>0$.
  10. $$f'(0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(h)-f(0)}h =\lim_{h\to0}h\sin(1/h).$$ Because $|h\sin(1/h)|\le|h|\to0$, the squeeze theorem gives the limit $0$. $f$ is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0)=0$.
  11. Continuity: $$|g(x)|=|x\sin(1/x)|\le|x|\to0=g(0),$$ so $g$ is continuous at $0$. The difference quotient is $$\frac{g(h)-g(0)}h=\sin(1/h),$$ which has no limit as $h\to0$. $g$ is continuous but not differentiable at $0$.
  12. At $t=2$, the point is $(5,6)$ and $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3t^2-1}{2t},\qquad \left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{t=2}=\frac{11}{4}.$$ Thus the tangent is $y-6=\frac{11}{4}(x-5)$. For the second derivative, $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\frac{\frac d{dt}\left(\frac32t-\frac1{2t}\right)}{2t} =\frac{\frac32+\frac1{2t^2}}{2t},$$ so at $t=2$ it is $(13/8)/4=13/32$. $y-6=\frac{11}{4}(x-5)$ and $d^2y/dx^2=13/32$.
