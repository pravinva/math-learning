Substitution with trigonometric composites

From Week 6A: integrals like \(\int \sin^n x\cos x\,dx\), \(\int f^{\prime}(x)\sin(f(x))\,dx\), etc.

1. Short lesson

For \(\displaystyle\int \sin(f(x))f^{\prime}(x)\,dx=-\cos(f(x))+C\). Similarly \(\displaystyle\int \cos(f(x))f^{\prime}(x)\,dx=\sin(f(x))+C\).

Odd powers of \(\sin\) or \(\cos\) pair well with the other trig factor's derivative.

Use identities to simplify when direct substitution is unclear.

Worked example. \(\displaystyle\int \sin^2 x\cos x\,dx\). Let \(u=\sin x\Rightarrow \dfrac{u^3}{3}+C\).

2. Key points summary

  • \(\int \sin(f)f^{\prime}=-\cos(f)+C\)
  • \(\int \cos(f)f^{\prime}=\sin(f)+C\)
  • Odd-power trig: substitute \(u=\sin\) or \(u=\cos\).
  • Pythagorean identities help.
  • Radians.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\displaystyle\int \sin^n x\cos x\,dx=\dfrac{\sin^{n+1}x}{n+1}+C\)
  • \(\displaystyle\int \cos^n x\sin x\,dx=-\dfrac{\cos^{n+1}x}{n+1}+C\)
