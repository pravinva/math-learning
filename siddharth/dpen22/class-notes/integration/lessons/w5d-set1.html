Simple substitution (indefinite)

From Week 5D: let \(u=g(x)\), \(du=g^{\prime}(x)\,dx\), rewrite integral in \(u\).

1. Short lesson

Substitution reverses the chain rule. Choose \(u\) so that \(du\) appears (up to a constant) in the integrand.

Steps: (1) choose \(u\), (2) find \(du\), (3) substitute, (4) integrate in \(u\), (5) back-substitute.

Good choices: inside a power, inside \(\ln(\cdot)\), inside \(e^{(\cdot)}\), or denominator of a fraction.

Worked example. \(\displaystyle\int 2x(x^2+1)^3\,dx\). Let \(u=x^2+1\), \(du=2x\,dx\Rightarrow \dfrac{u^4}{4}+C=\dfrac{(x^2+1)^4}{4}+C\).

2. Key points summary

  • Pick \(u\) so \(du\) is in integrand.
  • Include constant factors in \(du\).
  • Integrate in \(u\).
  • Back-substitute \(u=g(x)\).
  • Check by differentiating.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(u=g(x)\), \(du=g^{\prime}(x)\,dx\)
  • \(\displaystyle\int f(g(x))g^{\prime}(x)\,dx=\int f(u)\,du\)
