Fundamental Theorem of Calculus & definite integrals

From Week 5A: evaluate \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b)-F(a)\) where \(F^{\prime}=f\).

1. Short lesson

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC): if \(F^{\prime}=f\), then \(\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b)-F(a)\).

Notation: \(\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \big[F(x)\big]_a^b = F(b)-F(a)\).

No \(+C\) needed for definite integrals — constants cancel.

Worked example. \(\displaystyle\int_0^2 3x\,dx = \big[\tfrac{3x^2}{2}\big]_0^2 = 6\).

2. Key points summary

  • Find antiderivative \(F\).
  • Evaluate \(F(b)-F(a)\).
  • No constant \(C\) in definite integrals.
  • FTC links integration and differentiation.
  • Units: area, displacement, etc.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b)-F(a)\)
  • \(F^{\prime}=f\)
  • \(\displaystyle\int_a^b k\,dx = k(b-a)\)
