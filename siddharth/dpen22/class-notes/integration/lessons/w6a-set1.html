Substitution with exponential composites

From Week 6A: integrals involving \(e^{f(x)}\) and related forms.

1. Short lesson

For \(\displaystyle\int f^{\prime}(x)e^{f(x)}\,dx\), let \(u=f(x)\) gives \(e^u+C\).

Exponential composites often pair with the derivative of the exponent.

Adjust constants when the derivative differs by a factor.

Worked example. \(\displaystyle\int 3e^{3x}\,dx=e^{3x}+C\). Or \(\displaystyle\int x e^{x^2}\,dx=\dfrac{e^{x^2}}{2}+C\).

2. Key points summary

  • \(\int f^{\prime}e^f=e^f+C\)
  • Match derivative of exponent.
  • Scale constants as needed.
  • Change limits for definite.
  • Check by differentiating.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\displaystyle\int f^{\prime}(x)e^{f(x)}\,dx=e^{f(x)}+C\)
  • \(\displaystyle\int e^{ax}\,dx=\dfrac{e^{ax}}{a}+C\)
