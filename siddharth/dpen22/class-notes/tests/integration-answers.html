Test 1 — Answers

Q1. Expand the square: \[ \left(x-\frac3x\right)^2=x^2-6+\frac9{x^2}. \] The integral is then a sum of powers of \(x\), so the correct first step is \(\boxed{\text{(B) Expand}}\).
Q2. Write \(\sqrt{x}=x^{1/2}\). The power rule applies to this term, while \(1/x\) has a logarithmic primitive: \[ \int\left(x^{1/2}+\frac1x\right)dx =\frac{x^{3/2}}{3/2}+\ln|x|+C =\frac23x^{3/2}+\ln|x|+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q3. An antiderivative is \[ \int(2x^3+2)\,dx=\frac{x^4}{2}+2x. \] Therefore \[ \left[\frac{x^4}{2}+2x\right]_{-1}^{2} =\left(8+4\right)-\left(\frac12-2\right) =\frac{27}{2}. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(E)}}\).
Q4. Factor the function to locate its zeros: \[ x^3-4x^2-x+4=(x-4)(x-1)(x+1). \] The graph crosses the axis at \(x=-1,1,4\), so its sign changes across the bounded regions. Geometric area requires \(\int |f(x)|\,dx\), which must be split at the sign changes. Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q5. Since \[ \frac{d}{dx}\sin\left(\frac{x}{3}\right) =\frac13\cos\left(\frac{x}{3}\right), \] the required coefficient is \(9\): \[ \int3\cos\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)dx =9\sin\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q6. Let \(u=3x^2-2x\). Then \(du=(6x-2)\,dx\), so \[ \int(6x-2)\sqrt{3x^2-2x}\,dx =\int u^{1/2}\,du =\frac23u^{3/2}+C. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{\frac23(3x^2-2x)^{3/2}+C}, \] which is answer \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q7. Integrate the gradient: \[ y=\int(1+3x)\,dx=x+\frac32x^2+C. \] Use the point \((4,10)\): \[ 10=4+\frac32(4)^2+C=28+C, \qquad C=-18. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{y=x+\frac32x^2-18}. \]
Q8. Apply the power rule term by term: \[ \int(9x^5+3x^2)\,dx =9\frac{x^6}{6}+3\frac{x^3}{3}+C =\boxed{\frac32x^6+x^3+C}. \]
Q9. Separate the two terms. The \(1/x\) term produces a logarithm: \[ \int\left(3x^3-\frac4x\right)dx =3\frac{x^4}{4}-4\ln|x|+C. \] Hence \[ \boxed{\frac34x^4-4\ln|x|+C}. \]
Q10. On \(0\le x\le1\), \(x-x^2=x(1-x)\ge0\), so the area is \[ A=\int_0^1(x-x^2)\,dx =\left[\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1 =\frac12-\frac13. \] Thus \(\boxed{A=\frac16\text{ units}^2}\).
Q11. The upper curve is \(2-x^2\) and the lower curve is \(x^2\). Their intersections satisfy \[ x^2=2-x^2\;\Longrightarrow\;x^2=1, \] giving \((\pm1,1)\). Therefore \[ \begin{aligned} A&=\int_{-1}^{1}\big[(2-x^2)-x^2\big]\,dx\\ &=\left[2x-\frac23x^3\right]_{-1}^{1} =\frac83. \end{aligned} \] Hence \(\boxed{A=\frac83\text{ units}^2}\).
Q12. Integrate each term: \[ \int(e^{2x}+4x)\,dx =\frac12e^{2x}+2x^2+C. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{\frac12e^{2x}+2x^2+C}. \]
Q13. Let \(u=x+5\), so \(du=dx\). Then \[ \int\frac1{x+5}\,dx=\int\frac1u\,du =\ln|u|+C. \] Thus \[ \boxed{\ln|x+5|+C}. \]
Q14. An antiderivative is \[ \int(1-\sin2x)\,dx=x+\frac12\cos2x. \] Consequently \[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^{\pi/2}(1-\sin2x)\,dx &=\left[x+\frac12\cos2x\right]_0^{\pi/2}\\ &=\left(\frac\pi2-\frac12\right)-\frac12 =\frac\pi2-1. \end{aligned} \] Therefore \(\boxed{\frac\pi2-1}\).
Q15. Let \(u=x^2-3\). Then \(du=2x\,dx\), so \[ \int2x(x^2-3)^3\,dx =\int u^3\,du =\frac{u^4}{4}+C. \] Substituting back gives \[ \boxed{\frac14(x^2-3)^4+C}. \]
Q16. Let \(u=x^2+9\), so \(du=2x\,dx\). Then \[ \int\frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\,dx =\int u^{-1/2}\,du =2u^{1/2}+C. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{2\sqrt{x^2+9}+C}. \]

Test 2 — Answers

Q1. Expanding gives \[ (x+1)^2=x^2+2x+1, \] which can be integrated term by term. Thus the correct first step is \(\boxed{\text{(A) Expand}}\).
Q2. By the power rule, \[ \int x^{1/2}\,dx =\frac{x^{3/2}}{3/2}+C =\frac23x^{3/2}+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q3. Since \(\int\cos x\,dx=\sin x\), \[ \int_0^{\pi/2}\cos x\,dx =\left[\sin x\right]_0^{\pi/2} =1-0=1. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q4. The function is nonnegative on \([0,4]\), so \[ A=\int_0^4\sqrt{x}\,dx =\left[\frac23x^{3/2}\right]_0^4 =\frac23(8)=\frac{16}{3}\text{ units}^2. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q5. Because \(\frac{d}{dx}(3x)=3\), \[ \int e^{3x}\,dx=\frac13e^{3x}+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q6. Let \(u=x^2\), so \(du=2x\,dx\). Then \[ \int xe^{x^2}\,dx =\frac12\int e^u\,du =\frac12e^u+C. \] Thus \[ \boxed{\frac12e^{x^2}+C}, \] which is answer \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q7. Integrate the gradient: \[ y=\int(2x-5)\,dx=x^2-5x+C. \] Use \((3,1)\): \[ 1=9-15+C,\qquad C=7. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{y=x^2-5x+7}. \]
Q8. Rewrite \(1/x^2=x^{-2}\) and apply the power rule: \[ \int(x^4-x^{-2})\,dx =\frac{x^5}{5}+\frac1x+C. \] Thus \[ \boxed{\frac15x^5+\frac1x+C}. \]
Q9. Rewrite the integrand as \(x^{-1/2}\): \[ \int_1^4x^{-1/2}\,dx =\left[2x^{1/2}\right]_1^4 =2(2)-2(1)=2. \] Therefore \(\boxed{2}\).
Q10. The positive region extends from the roots \(x=-2\) to \(x=2\): \[ \begin{aligned} A&=\int_{-2}^{2}(4-x^2)\,dx\\ &=\left[4x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-2}^{2} =\frac{32}{3}. \end{aligned} \] Hence \(\boxed{A=\frac{32}{3}\text{ units}^2}\).
Q11. On \([0,1]\), \(x\ge x^2\). Therefore \[ A=\int_0^1(x-x^2)\,dx =\left[\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1 =\frac16. \] Thus \(\boxed{A=\frac16\text{ units}^2}\).
Q12. Integrate term by term: \[ \int(3\cos2x-2\sin x)\,dx =\frac32\sin2x+2\cos x+C. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{\frac32\sin2x+2\cos x+C}. \]
Q13. Since \(\frac{d}{dx}(e^{-x})=-e^{-x}\), \[ \int5e^{-x}\,dx=-5e^{-x}+C. \] Hence \(\boxed{-5e^{-x}+C}\).
Q14. An antiderivative is \(e^x+x^2\). Thus \[ \int_0^1(e^x+2x)\,dx =\left[e^x+x^2\right]_0^1 =(e+1)-1=e. \] Therefore \(\boxed{e}\).
Q15. Let \(u=2\sin2x\). Then \(du=4\cos2x\,dx\), so \[ \begin{aligned} \int\cos2x(2\sin2x)^3\,dx &=\frac14\int u^3\,du\\ &=\frac{u^4}{16}+C =\sin^4(2x)+C. \end{aligned} \] Therefore \(\boxed{\sin^4(2x)+C}\).
Q16. Let \(u=\sin x\), so \(du=\cos x\,dx\). Then \[ \int\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}\,dx =\int\frac1u\,du =\ln|u|+C. \] Thus \(\boxed{\ln|\sin x|+C}\).

Test 3 — Answers

Q1. Split the fraction: \[ \frac{x^2+1}{x}=x+\frac1x. \] Both resulting terms have standard primitives, so the correct first step is \(\boxed{\text{(A) Expand/split}}\).
Q2. Let \(u=2x+1\), so \(du=2\,dx\). Then \[ \int(2x+1)^5dx =\frac12\int u^5du =\frac{u^6}{12}+C. \] Hence \(\boxed{\frac1{12}(2x+1)^6+C}\), answer \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q3. An antiderivative is \(x^3-x\). Therefore \[ \int_0^1(3x^2-1)\,dx =\left[x^3-x\right]_0^1=0. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q4. The function is positive, so its area is \[ \int_0^1e^x\,dx =\left[e^x\right]_0^1=(e-1)\text{ units}^2. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q5. Since \(\frac{d}{dx}\cos3x=-3\sin3x\), \[ \int\sin3x\,dx=-\frac13\cos3x+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q6. Let \(u=x^2+1\), so \(du=2x\,dx\). Then \[ \int\frac{4x}{x^2+1}\,dx =2\int\frac1u\,du =2\ln|u|+C. \] Since \(x^2+1>0\), \[ \boxed{2\ln(x^2+1)+C}, \] which is answer \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q7. Integrate the gradient: \[ y=\int\frac1x\,dx=\ln|x|+C. \] The point \((e,2)\) gives \[ 2=\ln e+C=1+C,\qquad C=1. \] Therefore \(\boxed{y=\ln|x|+1}\).
Q8. Apply the power rule: \[ \begin{aligned} \int(6x^{1/2}-x^{-1/2})\,dx &=6\left(\frac23x^{3/2}\right) -2x^{1/2}+C\\ &=\boxed{4x^{3/2}-2x^{1/2}+C}. \end{aligned} \]
Q9. An antiderivative of \(3x^2\) is \(x^3\). Hence \[ \int_{-2}^{1}3x^2\,dx =\left[x^3\right]_{-2}^{1} =1-(-8)=9. \] Therefore \(\boxed{9}\).
Q10. The sine curve is nonnegative on \([0,\pi]\): \[ A=\int_0^\pi\sin x\,dx =\left[-\cos x\right]_0^\pi =1-(-1)=2. \] Thus \(\boxed{A=2\text{ units}^2}\).
Q11. Find the intersections: \[ x^2=4x-x^2 \;\Longrightarrow\;2x(x-2)=0, \] so \(x=0,2\). The upper curve is \(4x-x^2\), giving \[ \begin{aligned} A&=\int_0^2\big[(4x-x^2)-x^2\big]\,dx\\ &=\left[2x^2-\frac23x^3\right]_0^2 =\frac83. \end{aligned} \] Therefore \(\boxed{A=\frac83\text{ units}^2}\).
Q12. Let \(u=2x+1\), so \(du=2\,dx\): \[ \int\frac4{2x+1}\,dx =2\int\frac1u\,du =2\ln|u|+C. \] Thus \(\boxed{2\ln|2x+1|+C}\).
Q13. Use the standard primitives of \(\sec^2x\) and \(\cos x\): \[ \int(2\sec^2x-3\cos x)\,dx =2\tan x-3\sin x+C. \] Therefore \(\boxed{2\tan x-3\sin x+C}\).
Q14. Since an antiderivative of \(\sec^2x\) is \(\tan x\), \[ \int_0^{\pi/4}\sec^2x\,dx =\left[\tan x\right]_0^{\pi/4} =1-0=1. \] Thus \(\boxed{1}\).
Q15. Let \(u=x^2+1\). Then \(du=2x\,dx\), so \[ \int\frac{x}{x^2+1}\,dx =\frac12\int\frac1u\,du =\frac12\ln|u|+C. \] Since \(x^2+1>0\), \[ \boxed{\frac12\ln(x^2+1)+C}. \]
Q16. Let \(u=3x^2-2x\). Since \(du=(6x-2)\,dx=2(3x-1)\,dx\), \[ \int(3x-1)(3x^2-2x)^4\,dx =\frac12\int u^4\,du =\frac{u^5}{10}+C. \] Therefore \(\boxed{\frac1{10}(3x^2-2x)^5+C}\).

Test 4 — Answers

Q1. Expand the square: \[ \left(2+\frac1x\right)^2=4+\frac4x+\frac1{x^2}. \] The three terms can then be integrated separately, so the correct first step is \(\boxed{\text{(A) Expand}}\).
Q2. Rewrite the integrand as \(x^{-2}\): \[ \int x^{-2}\,dx =\frac{x^{-1}}{-1}+C =-\frac1x+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q3. An antiderivative is \(x^3/3+x\). Therefore \[ \int_0^2(x^2+1)\,dx =\left[\frac{x^3}{3}+x\right]_0^2 =\frac83+2=\frac{14}{3}. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q4. The curves meet where \[ 2x=x^2\;\Longrightarrow\;x(x-2)=0, \] so the bounded interval is \([0,2]\). Since \(2x\) is above \(x^2\), \[ \int_0^2(2x-x^2)\,dx =\left[x^2-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2 =\frac43\text{ units}^2. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q5. Because \[ \frac{d}{dx}\sin\left(\frac x2\right) =\frac12\cos\left(\frac x2\right), \] \[ \int\cos\left(\frac x2\right)dx =2\sin\left(\frac x2\right)+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q6. Let \(u=\sin x\), so \(du=\cos x\,dx\). Then \[ \int\sin x\cos x\,dx =\int u\,du =\frac12u^2+C. \] Thus \[ \boxed{\frac12\sin^2x+C}, \] which is answer \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q7. Integrate the derivative: \[ y=\int(4x^3-2)\,dx=x^4-2x+C. \] Using \(y(1)=5\), \[ 5=1-2+C,\qquad C=6. \] Hence \(\boxed{y=x^4-2x+6}\).
Q8. Rewrite \(2/x^3=2x^{-3}\): \[ \int(x^3-2x^{-3})\,dx =\frac14x^4+x^{-2}+C. \] Therefore \(\boxed{\frac14x^4+\frac1{x^2}+C}\).
Q9. An antiderivative is \(x^4-x\), so \[ \int_0^1(4x^3-1)\,dx =\left[x^4-x\right]_0^1=0. \] Thus \(\boxed{0}\).
Q10. The \(x\)-intercepts satisfy \[ x^2-1=0\;\Longrightarrow\;x=-1,1. \] The parabola opens upwards, with vertex \((0,-1)\). Its sketch is:
The curve is below the axis on \([-1,1]\), so that section must have its sign reversed: \[ \begin{aligned} A={}&\int_{-2}^{-1}(x^2-1)\,dx\\ &-\int_{-1}^{1}(x^2-1)\,dx\\ &+\int_{1}^{2}(x^2-1)\,dx\\ ={}&2\int_1^2(x^2-1)\,dx\\ &+\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^2)\,dx\\ ={}&2\left[\frac{x^3}{3}-x\right]_1^2\\ &+\left[x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{1}\\ ={}&2\left(\frac43\right)+\frac43=4. \end{aligned} \] Therefore \(\boxed{A=4\text{ units}^2}\).
Q11. The intersections satisfy \(x^2=2x\), so \(x=0,2\). The line is above the parabola on this interval: \[ A=\int_0^2(2x-x^2)\,dx =\left[x^2-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2 =\frac43. \] Thus \(\boxed{A=\frac43\text{ units}^2}\).
Q12. Integrate each term: \[ \int(2e^x-3\sin x)\,dx =2e^x+3\cos x+C. \] Therefore \(\boxed{2e^x+3\cos x+C}\).
Q13. Use the logarithmic primitive: \[ \int\frac3x\,dx=3\ln|x|+C. \] Hence \(\boxed{3\ln|x|+C}\).
Q14. An antiderivative of \(\cos2x\) is \(\frac12\sin2x\): \[ \int_0^{\pi/2}\cos2x\,dx =\left[\frac12\sin2x\right]_0^{\pi/2} =0. \] Therefore \(\boxed{0}\).
Q15. Let \(u=x^2+4\), so \(du=2x\,dx\). Then \[ \int\frac{6x}{x^2+4}\,dx =3\int\frac1u\,du =3\ln|u|+C. \] Since \(x^2+4>0\), \[ \boxed{3\ln(x^2+4)+C}. \]
Q16. Let \(u=\sin x\), so \(du=\cos x\,dx\): \[ \int2\cos x\sin^5x\,dx =2\int u^5\,du =\frac13u^6+C. \] Thus \(\boxed{\frac13\sin^6x+C}\).

Test 5 — Answers

Q1. Expand the product: \[ x(x+2)=x^2+2x. \] This can be integrated term by term, so the correct first step is \(\boxed{\text{(A) Expand}}\).
Q2. Apply the power rule to both terms: \[ \int(x^{-1/2}+x^3)\,dx =2x^{1/2}+\frac14x^4+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q3. An antiderivative of \(3x^2\) is \(x^3\): \[ \int_{-1}^{1}3x^2\,dx =\left[x^3\right]_{-1}^{1} =1-(-1)=2. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(C)}}\).
Q4. On \([-1,1]\), \(x^2-1\le0\). A direct integral would give signed area, so geometric area requires \[ \int_{-1}^{1}|x^2-1|\,dx =\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^2)\,dx. \] Thus the correct choice is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q5. The power rule excludes the exponent \(-1\). Its standard primitive is \[ \int\frac1x\,dx=\ln|x|+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q6. Let \(u=x^2+5\), so \(du=2x\,dx\). Then \[ \int\frac{2x}{x^2+5}\,dx =\int\frac1u\,du =\ln|u|+C. \] Since \(x^2+5>0\), \[ \boxed{\ln(x^2+5)+C}, \] which is answer \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q7. Integrate the gradient: \[ y=\int(3x^2-4)\,dx=x^3-4x+C. \] Using the point \((1,2)\), \[ 2=1-4+C,\qquad C=5. \] Therefore \(\boxed{y=x^3-4x+5}\).
Q8. Rewrite \(3/x^2=3x^{-2}\): \[ \int(5x^4-3x^{-2})\,dx =x^5+3x^{-1}+C. \] Thus \(\boxed{x^5+\frac3x+C}\).
Q9. By the power rule, \[ \int x^{-2/3}\,dx=3x^{1/3}. \] Therefore \[ \int_1^8x^{-2/3}\,dx =\left[3x^{1/3}\right]_1^8 =3(2)-3(1)=3. \] Hence \(\boxed{3}\).
Q10. The \(x\)-intercepts satisfy \[ x^2-4=0\;\Longrightarrow\;x=-2,2. \] The parabola opens upwards, with vertex \((0,-4)\). Its sketch is:
The curve is below the axis on \([-2,2]\). Therefore \[ \begin{aligned} A={}&\int_{-3}^{-2}(x^2-4)\,dx\\ &-\int_{-2}^{2}(x^2-4)\,dx\\ &+\int_{2}^{3}(x^2-4)\,dx\\ ={}&2\int_2^3(x^2-4)\,dx\\ &+\int_{-2}^{2}(4-x^2)\,dx\\ ={}&2\left[\frac{x^3}{3}-4x\right]_2^3\\ &+\left[4x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-2}^{2}\\ ={}&2\left(\frac73\right)+\frac{32}{3} =\frac{46}{3}. \end{aligned} \] Therefore \(\boxed{A=\frac{46}{3}\text{ units}^2}\).
Q11. For \(x\ge0\), \[ x=x^3\;\Longrightarrow\;x(x-1)(x+1)=0, \] so the relevant intersections are \(x=0,1\). Since \(x\ge x^3\) on \([0,1]\), \[ A=\int_0^1(x-x^3)\,dx =\left[\frac12x^2-\frac14x^4\right]_0^1 =\frac14. \] Thus \(\boxed{A=\frac14\text{ units}^2}\).
Q12. Integrate term by term: \[ \int(4\cos x-e^x)\,dx =4\sin x-e^x+C. \] Therefore \(\boxed{4\sin x-e^x+C}\).
Q13. Let \(u=3x+1\), so \(du=3\,dx\). Then \[ \int\frac2{3x+1}\,dx =\frac23\int\frac1u\,du =\frac23\ln|u|+C. \] Thus \(\boxed{\frac23\ln|3x+1|+C}\).
Q14. An antiderivative is \(-\frac13\cos3x\). Hence \[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^{\pi/6}\sin3x\,dx &=\left[-\frac13\cos3x\right]_0^{\pi/6}\\ &=0-\left(-\frac13\right)=\frac13. \end{aligned} \] Therefore \(\boxed{\frac13}\).
Q15. Let \(u=x^2+1\), so \(du=2x\,dx\): \[ \int\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\,dx =\frac12\int u^{-1/2}\,du =u^{1/2}+C. \] Thus \(\boxed{\sqrt{x^2+1}+C}\).
Q16. Let \(u=\cos x\), so \(du=-\sin x\,dx\). Then \[ \int3\sin x\cos^2x\,dx =-3\int u^2\,du =-u^3+C. \] Therefore \(\boxed{-\cos^3x+C}\).

Test 6 — Answers

Q1. Expand the square: \[ \left(\sqrt{x}-\frac1{\sqrt{x}}\right)^2 =x-2+\frac1x. \] The resulting terms have standard primitives, so the correct first step is \(\boxed{\text{(A) Expand}}\).
Q2. Integrate term by term: \[ \int\left(2x^3-\frac1x\right)dx =\frac12x^4-\ln|x|+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q3. An antiderivative is \(x^2+x\). Therefore \[ \int_0^3(2x+1)\,dx =\left[x^2+x\right]_0^3 =9+3=12. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q4. The positive region lies between the roots \(-1\) and \(1\): \[ \begin{aligned} A&=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^2)\,dx\\ &=\left[x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{1} =\frac43\text{ units}^2. \end{aligned} \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q5. Since \(\int\sec^2x\,dx=\tan x\), \[ \int2\sec^2x\,dx=2\tan x+C. \] Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\text{(A)}}\).
Q6. Let \(u=2x^2-x\), so \(du=(4x-1)\,dx\). Then \[ \begin{aligned} I&=\int(4x-1)(2x^2-x)^5\,dx\\ &=\int u^5\,du\\ &=\frac16u^6+C. \end{aligned} \] Therefore \[ \boxed{\frac16(2x^2-x)^6+C}, \] which is answer \(\boxed{\text{(B)}}\).
Q7. Integrate the gradient: \[ y=\int(e^x+2)\,dx=e^x+2x+C. \] Using \((0,3)\), \[ 3=e^0+C=1+C,\qquad C=2. \] Therefore \(\boxed{y=e^x+2x+2}\).
Q8. Apply the power rule: \[ \int(x^{-3}+4x)\,dx =-\frac12x^{-2}+2x^2+C. \] Thus \(\boxed{-\frac1{2x^2}+2x^2+C}\).
Q9. An antiderivative is \(x^3/3-x^2/2\). Therefore \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{-1}^{2}(x^2-x)\,dx &=\left[\frac{x^3}{3}-\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{2}\\ &=\frac23-\left(-\frac56\right)=\frac32. \end{aligned} \] Hence \(\boxed{\frac32}\).
Q10. The \(x\)-intercepts satisfy \[ 3x-x^2=x(3-x)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;x=0,3. \] The parabola opens downwards, with vertex \(\left(\frac32,\frac94\right)\). Its sketch is:
The curve is below the axis on \([-1,0]\) and \([3,4]\), so those two signed integrals are negative. Geometrical area cannot be negative, so take the absolute value of each region: \[ \begin{aligned} A={}&\left|\int_{-1}^{0}(3x-x^2)\,dx\right|\\ &+\left|\int_{0}^{3}(3x-x^2)\,dx\right|\\ &+\left|\int_{3}^{4}(3x-x^2)\,dx\right|\\ ={}&-\int_{-1}^{0}(3x-x^2)\,dx\\ &+\int_{0}^{3}(3x-x^2)\,dx\\ &-\int_{3}^{4}(3x-x^2)\,dx\\ ={}&\int_{-1}^{0}(x^2-3x)\,dx\\ &+\int_{0}^{3}(3x-x^2)\,dx\\ &+\int_{3}^{4}(x^2-3x)\,dx\\ ={}&\frac{11}{6}+\frac92+\frac{11}{6} =\frac{49}{6}. \end{aligned} \] Therefore \(\boxed{A=\frac{49}{6}\text{ units}^2}\).
Q11. The intersections satisfy \[ x^2=3x\;\Longrightarrow\;x(x-3)=0, \] so \(x=0,3\). The line lies above the parabola between them: \[ \begin{aligned} A&=\int_0^3(3x-x^2)\,dx\\ &=\left[\frac32x^2-\frac13x^3\right]_0^3 =\frac92. \end{aligned} \] Therefore \(\boxed{A=\frac92\text{ units}^2}\).
