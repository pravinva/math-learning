The one idea behind all sixteen

Converting to sines and cosines never removes the need for the identity. Look at any orange block below and you will find \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\) being used anyway, three lines later, underneath a stack of nested fractions. The substitution route uses the same identity on line one, with nothing on top of it.

Set A — Worked answers

A1. \(\dfrac{1-\sin^2x}{\cos x}=\cos x\)

Block spotted: \(1-\sin^2x\). A 1 beside a lone squared sine is the Pythagorean identity rearranged, so the whole numerator is \(\cos^2x\).
\[ \frac{1-\sin^2x}{\cos x} =\frac{\cos^2x}{\cos x} =\cos x. \] RHS reached, two lines.
Nothing to convert here. There is no secant, cosecant, tangent or cotangent on the page, so sines and cosines are all you have. The only route forward is the block itself, which makes this the cleanest possible test of whether you see it. If this one took more than ten seconds, the recognition is the thing to drill.

A2. \(\dfrac{\csc^2x-1}{\cot x}=\cot x\)

Block spotted: \(\csc^2x-1\). From \(1+\cot^2x=\csc^2x\), subtracting 1 from both sides leaves \(\csc^2x-1=\cot^2x\).
\[ \frac{\csc^2x-1}{\cot x} =\frac{\cot^2x}{\cot x} =\cot x. \] RHS reached, two lines.
The grind, five steps: \[\frac{\csc^2x-1}{\cot x} =\frac{\dfrac{1}{\sin^2x}-1}{\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}} =\frac{\dfrac{1-\sin^2x}{\sin^2x}}{\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}} =\frac{\cos^2x}{\sin^2x}\cdot\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} =\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}=\cot x.\] Notice step three: \(1-\sin^2x\) becomes \(\cos^2x\). The identity you skipped on line one turns up anyway, now wrapped in a compound fraction.

A3. \(\dfrac{1+\tan^2x}{\sec x}=\sec x\)

Block spotted: \(1+\tan^2x\). This is \(\sec^2x\) outright, and a \(\sec x\) is already waiting underneath to cancel against it.
\[ \frac{1+\tan^2x}{\sec x} =\frac{\sec^2x}{\sec x} =\sec x. \] RHS reached, two lines.
The grind, five steps: \[\frac{1+\tan^2x}{\sec x} =\frac{1+\dfrac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}}{\dfrac{1}{\cos x}} =\frac{\dfrac{\cos^2x+\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}}{\dfrac{1}{\cos x}} =\frac{1}{\cos^2x}\cdot\cos x =\frac{1}{\cos x}=\sec x.\] Again \(\cos^2x+\sin^2x=1\) does the real work, three lines below where it could have.

A4. \(\dfrac{1-\cos^2x}{1-\sin^2x}=\tan^2x\)

Two blocks spotted: \(1-\cos^2x\) upstairs and \(1-\sin^2x\) downstairs. Substitute both at once, then read off the quotient identity.
\[ \frac{1-\cos^2x}{1-\sin^2x} =\frac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x} =\tan^2x. \] RHS reached, two lines.
Watch the trap. The two numerators look almost identical, and swapping them gives \(\cot^2x\), which is a very cheap way to lose three marks. Read which function is being squared, then write down the other one. \(1-\cos^2x\) leaves sine behind; \(1-\sin^2x\) leaves cosine behind.

A5. \(\dfrac{\sec^2x-1}{\csc^2x-1}=\tan^4x\)

Two blocks spotted: \(\sec^2x-1=\tan^2x\) and \(\csc^2x-1=\cot^2x\). Dividing by \(\cot^2x\) is multiplying by \(\tan^2x\), which is where the fourth power comes from.
\[ \frac{\sec^2x-1}{\csc^2x-1} =\frac{\tan^2x}{\cot^2x} =\tan^2x\cdot\tan^2x =\tan^4x. \] RHS reached, three lines.
The grind, five steps: \[\frac{\sec^2x-1}{\csc^2x-1} =\frac{\dfrac{1}{\cos^2x}-1}{\dfrac{1}{\sin^2x}-1} =\frac{\dfrac{1-\cos^2x}{\cos^2x}}{\dfrac{1-\sin^2x}{\sin^2x}} =\frac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}\cdot\frac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x} =\tan^4x.\] The fourth power appears at the very end of the long route, which is exactly when a tired reader writes \(\tan^2x\) and stops.

A6. \(\dfrac{1+\cos 2x}{1-\cos 2x}=\cot^2x\)

Two blocks spotted: \(1+\cos 2x=2\cos^2x\) and \(1-\cos 2x=2\sin^2x\). The twos cancel and the ratio of squares is \(\cot^2x\).
\[ \frac{1+\cos 2x}{1-\cos 2x} =\frac{2\cos^2x}{2\sin^2x} =\frac{\cos^2x}{\sin^2x} =\cot^2x. \] RHS reached, three lines.
The grind, five steps. Expanding \(\cos 2x=\cos^2x-\sin^2x\) instead: \[\frac{1+\cos^2x-\sin^2x}{1-\cos^2x+\sin^2x} =\frac{(\sin^2x+\cos^2x)+\cos^2x-\sin^2x}{(\sin^2x+\cos^2x)-\cos^2x+\sin^2x} =\frac{2\cos^2x}{2\sin^2x}=\cot^2x.\] This lands in the same place, and it needs you to spot that the loose 1 must be rewritten as \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x\) before anything cancels. Choosing the power-reduction form at the start skips that entirely.

A7. \(\dfrac{\sin 2x}{1-\cos 2x}=\cot x\)

Two blocks spotted: \(\sin 2x=2\sin x\cos x\) and \(1-\cos 2x=2\sin^2x\). Both carry a factor \(2\sin x\), so the fraction collapses to \(\cos x/\sin x\).
\[ \frac{\sin 2x}{1-\cos 2x} =\frac{2\sin x\cos x}{2\sin^2x} =\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} =\cot x. \] RHS reached, three lines.
Compare with Set 1 Q6 on the main proof test, \(\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{\sin 2x}=\tan x\). Same two blocks, flipped over, so the answer flips from \(\cot x\) to \(\tan x\). Getting one right and the other wrong points at the substitution rather than the algebra, since the algebra is identical.

A8. \(\dfrac{1+\cot^2x}{\cot^2x}=\sec^2x\)

Block spotted: \(1+\cot^2x=\csc^2x\). That turns the fraction into \(\csc^2x/\cot^2x\), and the sines cancel to leave \(1/\cos^2x\).
\[ \frac{1+\cot^2x}{\cot^2x} =\frac{\csc^2x}{\cot^2x} =\frac{1/\sin^2x}{\cos^2x/\sin^2x} =\frac{1}{\cos^2x} =\sec^2x. \] RHS reached, four lines.
Worth storing as a result. \(\dfrac{\csc^2x}{\cot^2x}=\sec^2x\) sits alongside the two ratios from the question sheet, \(\dfrac{\tan^2x}{\sec^2x}=\sin^2x\) and \(\dfrac{\cot^2x}{\csc^2x}=\cos^2x\). All three come from the same one-line cancellation, and having them ready removes the last two lines of this proof.

Set B — Worked answers

B1. \(\dfrac{1-\cos^2x}{\cos x}=\sin x\tan x\)

Block spotted: \(1-\cos^2x=\sin^2x\). Then split the \(\sin^2x\) as \(\sin x\cdot\sin x\) and let one of them pair with the \(\cos x\) underneath to build \(\tan x\).
\[ \frac{1-\cos^2x}{\cos x} =\frac{\sin^2x}{\cos x} =\sin x\cdot\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} =\sin x\tan x. \] RHS reached, three lines.
Read the RHS before choosing the split. The target contains a \(\tan x\), which tells you a \(\sin x/\cos x\) has to be assembled at the end. Letting the destination pick the split is a habit worth carrying into longer proofs.

B2. \((\sec^2x-1)\cot x=\tan x\)

Block spotted: \(\sec^2x-1=\tan^2x\). Then \(\cot x\) is \(1/\tan x\), so one power of tangent is removed.
\[ (\sec^2x-1)\cot x =\tan^2x\cdot\cot x =\tan^2x\cdot\frac{1}{\tan x} =\tan x. \] RHS reached, three lines.
The grind, five steps: \[\left(\frac{1}{\cos^2x}-1\right)\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} =\frac{1-\cos^2x}{\cos^2x}\cdot\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} =\frac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}\cdot\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} =\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}=\tan x.\] The reciprocal pairing \(\tan x\cdot\cot x=1\) is the whole question. Expanding hides it behind a common denominator.

B3. \(\dfrac{1+\cot^2x}{\csc x}=\csc x\)

Block spotted: \(1+\cot^2x=\csc^2x\). A \(\csc x\) is already underneath, waiting to cancel one power.
\[ \frac{1+\cot^2x}{\csc x} =\frac{\csc^2x}{\csc x} =\csc x. \] RHS reached, two lines.
The grind, five steps: \[\frac{1+\dfrac{\cos^2x}{\sin^2x}}{\dfrac{1}{\sin x}} =\frac{\dfrac{\sin^2x+\cos^2x}{\sin^2x}}{\dfrac{1}{\sin x}} =\frac{1}{\sin^2x}\cdot\sin x =\frac{1}{\sin x}=\csc x.\] This is A3 with sine and cosine exchanged. If A3 went smoothly and this one did not, the gap is in the cotangent family rather than in the method.

B4. \(\dfrac{1-\sin^2x}{1-\cos^2x}=\cot^2x\)

Two blocks spotted: \(1-\sin^2x=\cos^2x\) upstairs, \(1-\cos^2x=\sin^2x\) downstairs. Cosine over sine, squared.
\[ \frac{1-\sin^2x}{1-\cos^2x} =\frac{\cos^2x}{\sin^2x} =\cot^2x. \] RHS reached, two lines.
This is A4 upside down, and the answer inverts with it. Two lines of work, and the only place to slip is substituting a block with the function that was already written rather than the one it leaves behind.

B5. \(\dfrac{\csc^2x-1}{\sec^2x-1}=\cot^4x\)

Two blocks spotted: \(\csc^2x-1=\cot^2x\) and \(\sec^2x-1=\tan^2x\). Dividing by \(\tan^2x\) multiplies by \(\cot^2x\), giving the fourth power.
\[ \frac{\csc^2x-1}{\sec^2x-1} =\frac{\cot^2x}{\tan^2x} =\cot^2x\cdot\cot^2x =\cot^4x. \] RHS reached, three lines.
The step that gets skipped is the middle one. \(\dfrac{\cot^2x}{\tan^2x}\) tempts an immediate \(\cot^2x\), because the tangent looks like it has already been used up. Writing \(1/\tan^2x=\cot^2x\) as its own line makes the doubling visible.

B6. \(\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{1+\cos 2x}=\tan^2x\)

Two blocks spotted: \(1-\cos 2x=2\sin^2x\) and \(1+\cos 2x=2\cos^2x\). The twos cancel and what remains is \(\tan^2x\).
\[ \frac{1-\cos 2x}{1+\cos 2x} =\frac{2\sin^2x}{2\cos^2x} =\frac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x} =\tan^2x. \] RHS reached, three lines.
Sign discipline. The minus sign carries the sine and the plus sign carries the cosine, which reads backwards to most people the first few times. A way to keep it: at \(x=0\) the whole LHS is \(0/2=0\), and \(\tan^2 0=0\) agrees, while \(\cot^2 0\) is undefined. Ten seconds of substitution settles which way round it goes.

B7. \(\dfrac{1+\cos 2x}{\sin 2x}=\cot x\)

Two blocks spotted: \(1+\cos 2x=2\cos^2x\) and \(\sin 2x=2\sin x\cos x\). Both share a factor \(2\cos x\), leaving \(\cos x/\sin x\).
\[ \frac{1+\cos 2x}{\sin 2x} =\frac{2\cos^2x}{2\sin x\cos x} =\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} =\cot x. \] RHS reached, three lines.
The family of four. Together with A7, Set 1 Q5 and Set 1 Q6 this completes every arrangement of \(1\pm\cos 2x\) with \(\sin 2x\): \[\frac{\sin 2x}{1+\cos 2x}=\tan x,\quad \frac{1-\cos 2x}{\sin 2x}=\tan x,\quad \frac{\sin 2x}{1-\cos 2x}=\cot x,\quad \frac{1+\cos 2x}{\sin 2x}=\cot x.\] All four run on the same two substitutions. Learn to see the pair rather than four separate results.

B8. \(\dfrac{1+\tan^2x}{\tan^2x}=\csc^2x\)

Block spotted: \(1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x\). Then \(\sec^2x/\tan^2x\) cancels the cosines and leaves \(1/\sin^2x\).
\[ \frac{1+\tan^2x}{\tan^2x} =\frac{\sec^2x}{\tan^2x} =\frac{1/\cos^2x}{\sin^2x/\cos^2x} =\frac{1}{\sin^2x} =\csc^2x. \] RHS reached, four lines.
The mirror of A8, and the fourth member of the ratio family: \(\dfrac{\sec^2x}{\tan^2x}=\csc^2x\) beside \(\dfrac{\csc^2x}{\cot^2x}=\sec^2x\). Each one swaps the squared reciprocal for the other one. Once both are memorised, A8 and B8 are two lines each.

What to take from the sixteen

  • The scan costs five seconds and saves five lines. Before writing, check for \(1\pm\cos^2x\) or \(1\pm\sin^2x\), then the \(\sec^2x-1\) family, then \(1\pm\cos 2x\) and \(\sin 2x\). Convert to sines and cosines only when all three come up empty.
  • Converting early does not dodge the identity. Every orange route above uses \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\) anyway, buried under a compound fraction that has to be untangled first.
  • Six ratios are worth knowing cold: \(\dfrac{\tan^2x}{\sec^2x}=\sin^2x\), \(\dfrac{\cot^2x}{\csc^2x}=\cos^2x\), \(\dfrac{\sec^2x}{\tan^2x}=\csc^2x\), \(\dfrac{\csc^2x}{\cot^2x}=\sec^2x\), together with \(\tan x\cot x=1\) and \(\sin^2x/\cos^2x=\tan^2x\). Between them they close out ten of these sixteen questions on line two.
  • Re-sit Set 3 Q1, Q4, Q5 and Set 1 Q1, Q6 on the main proof test after this drill. Those five should now come out in two lines each. If they do, the gap has closed; if any still run long, the block table is the thing to revisit.
