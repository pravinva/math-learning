Trigonometric Identity Proof Tests — Worked Answers

Each solution begins with the pattern that selects the first identity. The proof then transforms the LHS continuously until it reaches the RHS.

How to revise: cover the proof, read only the question, and say the pattern aloud. Then compare your first move with the one shown. A different valid route is acceptable when every step is justified. Sets 1–3 match the usual exam proof pitch; Sets 4–5 are extra stretch.

Identity sheet — every form used on these tests

Keep this visible while proving. Most first moves come from recognising which block matches the algebra on the page.

Reciprocal

  • \(\csc x=\dfrac{1}{\sin x},\quad \sec x=\dfrac{1}{\cos x},\quad \cot x=\dfrac{1}{\tan x}\)
  • \(\sin x=\dfrac{1}{\csc x},\quad \cos x=\dfrac{1}{\sec x},\quad \tan x=\dfrac{1}{\cot x}\)

Convert \(\sec,\csc,\cot\) to sine and cosine when mixed functions stall.

Quotient

  • \(\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x},\quad \cot x=\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}\)
  • \(\tan x=\dfrac{\sec x}{\csc x},\quad \cot x=\dfrac{\csc x}{\sec x}\)
  • \(\sec x\csc x=\dfrac{1}{\sin x\cos x}\)

Pythagorean — sine and cosine

  • \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\)
  • \(\sin^2x=1-\cos^2x,\quad \cos^2x=1-\sin^2x\)
  • \(1-\sin^2x=(1-\sin x)(1+\sin x)\)
  • \(1-\cos^2x=(1-\cos x)(1+\cos x)\)

A square next to a \(1\) is almost always this identity, often after factoring.

Pythagorean — tan/sec and cot/csc

  • \(1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x\)
  • \(\tan^2x=\sec^2x-1,\quad \sec^2x-\tan^2x=1\)
  • \(1+\cot^2x=\csc^2x\)
  • \(\cot^2x=\csc^2x-1,\quad \csc^2x-\cot^2x=1\)

Difference of squares: \((\sec x-\tan x)(\sec x+\tan x)=1\), and the same for \(\csc,\cot\).

Double-angle

  • \(\sin 2x=2\sin x\cos x\)
  • \(\cos 2x=\cos^2x-\sin^2x\)
  • \(\cos 2x=2\cos^2x-1=1-2\sin^2x\)
  • \(\tan 2x=\dfrac{2\tan x}{1-\tan^2x}\)

Power-reduction / \(1\pm\cos 2x\)

  • \(1-\cos 2x=2\sin^2x\)
  • \(1+\cos 2x=2\cos^2x\)
  • \(\sin^2x=\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{2},\quad \cos^2x=\dfrac{1+\cos 2x}{2}\)
  • \(\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{1+\cos 2x}=\tan^2x\)

The mark \(1\pm\cos(\text{even multiple})\) points here, not to Pythagorean.

Squared binomials

  • \((\sin x+\cos x)^2=1+\sin 2x\)
  • \((\sin x-\cos x)^2=1-\sin 2x\)
  • \((\sec x+\tan x)^2=\dfrac{1+\sin x}{1-\sin x}\)
  • \((\sec x-\tan x)^2=\dfrac{1-\sin x}{1+\sin x}\)

Conjugate multiplications

  • \((1-\sin x)(1+\sin x)=\cos^2x\)
  • \((1-\cos x)(1+\cos x)=\sin^2x\)
  • \((\sec x-\tan x)(\sec x+\tan x)=1\)
  • \((\csc x-\cot x)(\csc x+\cot x)=1\)

If the denominator is \(1\pm\sin x\) or \(1\pm\cos x\), multiply by the conjugate.

Sum-to-product

  • \(\sin A+\sin B=2\sin\dfrac{A+B}{2}\cos\dfrac{A-B}{2}\)
  • \(\sin A-\sin B=2\cos\dfrac{A+B}{2}\sin\dfrac{A-B}{2}\)
  • \(\cos A+\cos B=2\cos\dfrac{A+B}{2}\cos\dfrac{A-B}{2}\)
  • \(\cos A-\cos B=-2\sin\dfrac{A+B}{2}\sin\dfrac{A-B}{2}\)

Use when two different angles appear as a sum or difference, e.g. \(x\) with \(3x\), or \(2x\) with \(4x\). The average is the surviving angle; the half-difference often cancels.

Set 1 — Core Identity Recognition

Q1. \(\dfrac{1-\cos^2x}{\sin x}=\sin x\)

Pattern chosen: \(1-\cos^2x\). It is the rearranged Pythagorean identity \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\), so replace the numerator with \(\sin^2x\).
\[ \frac{1-\cos^2x}{\sin x} =\frac{\sin^2x}{\sin x} =\sin x. \] RHS reached.

Q2. \(\dfrac{\sec^2x-1}{\tan x}=\tan x\)

Pattern chosen: \(\sec^2x-1\). The tangent Pythagorean identity \(1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x\) gives \(\sec^2x-1=\tan^2x\).
\[ \frac{\sec^2x-1}{\tan x} =\frac{\tan^2x}{\tan x} =\tan x. \] RHS reached.

Q3. \(\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{\sin x}=2\sin x\)

Pattern chosen: \(1-\cos 2x\). A doubled angle beside \(1-\) points to the power-reduction form \(1-\cos 2x=2\sin^2x\).
\[ \frac{1-\cos 2x}{\sin x} =\frac{2\sin^2x}{\sin x} =2\sin x. \] RHS reached.

Q4. \(\dfrac{1+\cos 2x}{\cos x}=2\cos x\)

Pattern chosen: \(1+\cos 2x\). A doubled angle beside \(1+\) points to \(1+\cos 2x=2\cos^2x\).
\[ \frac{1+\cos 2x}{\cos x} =\frac{2\cos^2x}{\cos x} =2\cos x. \] RHS reached.

Q5. \(\dfrac{\sin 2x}{1+\cos 2x}=\tan x\)

Pattern chosen: every angle is \(2x\), while the RHS uses \(x\). Expand the double angles so cancellation exposes \(\sin x/\cos x\).
\[ \frac{\sin 2x}{1+\cos 2x} =\frac{2\sin x\cos x}{2\cos^2x} =\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} =\tan x. \] RHS reached.

Q6. \(\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{\sin 2x}=\tan x\)

Pattern chosen: \(1-\cos 2x\) over \(\sin 2x\). Use the two double-angle forms \(2\sin^2x\) and \(2\sin x\cos x\), which share a factor \(2\sin x\).
\[ \frac{1-\cos 2x}{\sin 2x} =\frac{2\sin^2x}{2\sin x\cos x} =\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} =\tan x. \] RHS reached.

Q7. \((1+\tan^2x)\cos^2x=1\)

Pattern chosen: \(1+\tan^2x\). Replace it with \(\sec^2x\), whose reciprocal factor \(\cos^2x\) is already present.
\[ (1+\tan^2x)\cos^2x =\sec^2x\cos^2x =\frac{1}{\cos^2x}\cos^2x =1. \] RHS reached.

Q8. \((\csc x-\cot x)(\csc x+\cot x)=1\)

Pattern chosen: conjugate factors \((a-b)(a+b)\). Use difference of squares, then the identity \(\csc^2x-\cot^2x=1\).
\[ (\csc x-\cot x)(\csc x+\cot x) =\csc^2x-\cot^2x =1. \] RHS reached.

Q9. \(\dfrac{\sin x}{1+\cos x}=\dfrac{1-\cos x}{\sin x}\)

Pattern chosen: denominator \(1+\cos x\). Multiply by its conjugate \(1-\cos x\); the product becomes \(1-\cos^2x=\sin^2x\).
\[ \frac{\sin x}{1+\cos x} \cdot\frac{1-\cos x}{1-\cos x} =\frac{\sin x(1-\cos x)}{1-\cos^2x} =\frac{\sin x(1-\cos x)}{\sin^2x} =\frac{1-\cos x}{\sin x}. \] RHS reached.

Q10. \((\sin x+\cos x)^2=1+\sin 2x\)

Pattern chosen: a squared binomial containing \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\). Expansion creates \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x\) and \(2\sin x\cos x\), matching the Pythagorean and double-angle identities.
\[ \begin{aligned} (\sin x+\cos x)^2 &=\sin^2x+2\sin x\cos x+\cos^2x\\ &=1+\sin 2x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Set 2 — Core Identities Only

Q1. \(\dfrac{\tan x}{\sec x}=\sin x\)

Pattern chosen: a quotient over a reciprocal. Write \(\tan x=\sin x/\cos x\) and \(\sec x=1/\cos x\); the cosine factors cancel.
\[ \frac{\tan x}{\sec x} =\frac{\sin x/\cos x}{1/\cos x} =\sin x. \] RHS reached.

Q2. \(\dfrac{\csc x}{\sec x}=\cot x\)

Pattern chosen: a ratio of reciprocal functions. Convert both to sine and cosine; the result is the quotient identity for \(\cot x\).
\[ \frac{\csc x}{\sec x} =\frac{1/\sin x}{1/\cos x} =\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} =\cot x. \] RHS reached.

Q3. \(\dfrac{\sin x+\cos x}{\cos x}=1+\tan x\)

Pattern chosen: a sum over a single term from that sum. Split the fraction; one piece is \(1\) and the other is the quotient identity for \(\tan x\).
\[ \frac{\sin x+\cos x}{\cos x} =\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}+\frac{\cos x}{\cos x} =\tan x+1. \] RHS reached.

Q4. \(\dfrac{\sin x}{\csc x}+\dfrac{\cos x}{\sec x}=1\)

Pattern chosen: reciprocal functions in the denominators. Rewrite \(\csc x=1/\sin x\) and \(\sec x=1/\cos x\), which turns each fraction into a square.
\[ \frac{\sin x}{\csc x}+\frac{\cos x}{\sec x} =\sin x\cdot\sin x+\cos x\cdot\cos x =\sin^2x+\cos^2x =1. \] RHS reached.

Q5. \(\dfrac{\cos^2x}{\sin x}+\sin x=\csc x\)

Pattern chosen: terms that share the denominator \(\sin x\). Combine them; the numerator becomes the Pythagorean identity.
\[ \frac{\cos^2x}{\sin x}+\sin x =\frac{\cos^2x+\sin^2x}{\sin x} =\frac{1}{\sin x} =\csc x. \] RHS reached.

Q6. \(\dfrac{\sec^2x}{\tan^2x}=\csc^2x\)

Pattern chosen: a ratio of squares of reciprocal and quotient functions. Convert both to sine and cosine; the cosine squares cancel and leave \(1/\sin^2x\).
\[ \frac{\sec^2x}{\tan^2x} =\frac{1/\cos^2x}{\sin^2x/\cos^2x} =\frac{1}{\sin^2x} =\csc^2x. \] RHS reached.

Q7. \(\dfrac{1+\tan^2x}{1+\cot^2x}=\tan^2x\)

Pattern chosen: both Pythagorean companions appear together. Replace \(1+\tan^2x\) by \(\sec^2x\) and \(1+\cot^2x\) by \(\csc^2x\); the ratio of those reciprocals is \(\tan^2x\).
\[ \frac{1+\tan^2x}{1+\cot^2x} =\frac{\sec^2x}{\csc^2x} =\frac{1/\cos^2x}{1/\sin^2x} =\frac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x} =\tan^2x. \] RHS reached.

Q8. \(\sin^4x+2\sin^2x\cos^2x+\cos^4x=1\)

Pattern chosen: the three-term expansion of a square. The expression is \((\sin^2x+\cos^2x)^2\).
\[ \sin^4x+2\sin^2x\cos^2x+\cos^4x =(\sin^2x+\cos^2x)^2 =1^2 =1. \] RHS reached.

Q9. \(\dfrac{\tan^2x}{\sec x-1}=\sec x+1\)

Pattern chosen: \(\tan^2x\) over a factor \(\sec x-1\). Replace \(\tan^2x\) by \(\sec^2x-1\), which factors as \((\sec x-1)(\sec x+1)\).
\[ \frac{\tan^2x}{\sec x-1} =\frac{\sec^2x-1}{\sec x-1} =\frac{(\sec x-1)(\sec x+1)}{\sec x-1} =\sec x+1. \] RHS reached.

Q10. \(\dfrac{\sin^3x+\cos^3x}{\sin x+\cos x}=1-\sin x\cos x\)

Pattern chosen: a sum of cubes over the matching sum. Factor \(a^3+b^3=(a+b)(a^2-ab+b^2)\), cancel \(a+b\), then use \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\).
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\sin^3x+\cos^3x}{\sin x+\cos x} &=\frac{(\sin x+\cos x)(\sin^2x-\sin x\cos x+\cos^2x)}{\sin x+\cos x}\\ &=\sin^2x+\cos^2x-\sin x\cos x =1-\sin x\cos x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Set 3 — Core Practice (Exam Pitch)

Q1. \(\dfrac{1-\cos^2x}{\cos^2x}=\tan^2x\)

Pattern chosen: \(1-\cos^2x\) over \(\cos^2x\). Replace the numerator with \(\sin^2x\), which is the quotient identity for \(\tan^2x\).
\[ \frac{1-\cos^2x}{\cos^2x} =\frac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x} =\tan^2x. \] RHS reached.

Q2. \((\csc x-1)(\csc x+1)=\cot^2x\)

Pattern chosen: conjugate factors \((a-b)(a+b)\). Difference of squares gives \(\csc^2x-1\), which is \(\cot^2x\).
\[ (\csc x-1)(\csc x+1) =\csc^2x-1 =\cot^2x. \] RHS reached.

Q3. \(\cos x\tan x=\sin x\)

Pattern chosen: a product containing \(\tan x\). Write \(\tan x=\sin x/\cos x\); the cosine cancels.
\[ \cos x\tan x =\cos x\cdot\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} =\sin x. \] RHS reached.

Q4. \(\dfrac{\sec^2x-1}{\sec^2x}=\sin^2x\)

Pattern chosen: \(\sec^2x-1\) over \(\sec^2x\). The numerator is \(\tan^2x\); dividing by \(\sec^2x\) leaves \(\sin^2x\).
\[ \frac{\sec^2x-1}{\sec^2x} =\frac{\tan^2x}{\sec^2x} =\frac{\sin^2x/\cos^2x}{1/\cos^2x} =\sin^2x. \] RHS reached.

Q5. \(\dfrac{\csc^2x-1}{\csc^2x}=\cos^2x\)

Pattern chosen: \(\csc^2x-1\) over \(\csc^2x\). The numerator is \(\cot^2x\); dividing by \(\csc^2x\) leaves \(\cos^2x\).
\[ \frac{\csc^2x-1}{\csc^2x} =\frac{\cot^2x}{\csc^2x} =\frac{\cos^2x/\sin^2x}{1/\sin^2x} =\cos^2x. \] RHS reached.

Q6. \((\sin x+\cos x)^2+(\sin x-\cos x)^2=2\)

Pattern chosen: a pair of squared binomials. Expand both; the cross terms \(2\sin x\cos x\) cancel and two copies of \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x\) remain.
\[ \begin{aligned} (\sin x+\cos x)^2+(\sin x-\cos x)^2 &=\sin^2x+2\sin x\cos x+\cos^2x+\sin^2x-2\sin x\cos x+\cos^2x\\ &=2\sin^2x+2\cos^2x =2. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q7. \(\dfrac{\sin^4x-\cos^4x}{\sin^2x-\cos^2x}=1\)

Pattern chosen: a difference of fourth powers. Factor as a difference of squares; \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\) cancels with the remaining factor.
\[ \frac{\sin^4x-\cos^4x}{\sin^2x-\cos^2x} =\frac{(\sin^2x-\cos^2x)(\sin^2x+\cos^2x)}{\sin^2x-\cos^2x} =1. \] RHS reached.

Q8. \(\dfrac{1-\sin x}{\cos x}=\dfrac{\cos x}{1+\sin x}\)

Pattern chosen: denominator \(\cos x\) beside \(1-\sin x\). Multiply the LHS by the conjugate of the numerator; \(1-\sin^2x\) becomes \(\cos^2x\).
\[ \frac{1-\sin x}{\cos x}\cdot\frac{1+\sin x}{1+\sin x} =\frac{1-\sin^2x}{\cos x(1+\sin x)} =\frac{\cos^2x}{\cos x(1+\sin x)} =\frac{\cos x}{1+\sin x}. \] RHS reached.

Q9. \(\dfrac{\sin x}{\csc x}-\dfrac{\cos x}{\sec x}=\sin^2x-\cos^2x\)

Pattern chosen: reciprocal functions in the denominators. Each fraction becomes a square, and their difference is already the RHS.
\[ \frac{\sin x}{\csc x}-\frac{\cos x}{\sec x} =\sin^2x-\cos^2x. \] RHS reached.

Q10. \(\tan x+\cot x=\sec x\csc x\)

Pattern chosen: a sum of quotient functions. Convert both to sine and cosine over a common denominator; the numerator becomes the Pythagorean identity.
\[ \tan x+\cot x =\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}+\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} =\frac{\sin^2x+\cos^2x}{\sin x\cos x} =\frac{1}{\sin x\cos x} =\sec x\csc x. \] RHS reached.

Set 4 — Fractions, Conjugates and Angle Pairing

Q1. \(\sec x-\tan x=\dfrac{\cos x}{1+\sin x}\)

Pattern chosen: reciprocal and quotient functions appear together. Convert \(\sec x\) and \(\tan x\) to sine and cosine, combine them, then use a conjugate.
\[ \begin{aligned} \sec x-\tan x &=\frac1{\cos x}-\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} =\frac{1-\sin x}{\cos x}\\ &=\frac{1-\sin x}{\cos x}\cdot\frac{1+\sin x}{1+\sin x} =\frac{1-\sin^2x}{\cos x(1+\sin x)} =\frac{\cos x}{1+\sin x}. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q2. \(\dfrac1{1-\sin x}-\dfrac1{1+\sin x}=2\tan x\sec x\)

Pattern chosen: fractions with conjugate denominators. Use a common denominator; its product becomes \(1-\sin^2x=\cos^2x\).
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac1{1-\sin x}-\frac1{1+\sin x} &=\frac{(1+\sin x)-(1-\sin x)}{(1-\sin x)(1+\sin x)}\\ &=\frac{2\sin x}{1-\sin^2x} =\frac{2\sin x}{\cos^2x}\\ &=2\left(\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\right)\left(\frac1{\cos x}\right) =2\tan x\sec x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q3. \(\dfrac{\sin x}{1-\cos x}-\dfrac{\sin x}{1+\cos x}=2\cot x\)

Pattern chosen: matching numerators and conjugate denominators. Combine the fractions; the denominator becomes \(1-\cos^2x=\sin^2x\).
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\sin x}{1-\cos x}-\frac{\sin x}{1+\cos x} &=\frac{\sin x[(1+\cos x)-(1-\cos x)]}{1-\cos^2x}\\ &=\frac{2\sin x\cos x}{\sin^2x} =\frac{2\cos x}{\sin x} =2\cot x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q4. \((\tan x+\cot x)^2=\sec^2x\,\csc^2x\)

Pattern chosen: \(\tan x\) and \(\cot x\) are reciprocal quotient functions. Convert both to sine and cosine; their numerators combine using \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\).
\[ \begin{aligned} (\tan x+\cot x)^2 &=\left(\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}+\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}\right)^2\\ &=\left(\frac{\sin^2x+\cos^2x}{\sin x\cos x}\right)^2 =\frac1{\sin^2x\cos^2x}\\ &=\csc^2x\,\sec^2x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q5. \(\dfrac{\cos x-\cos 3x}{\sin 3x+\sin x}=\tan x\)

Pattern chosen: sums and differences of trig functions with angles \(x\) and \(3x\). Sum-to-product creates the average angle \(2x\) and half-difference \(x\), giving common factors.
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\cos x-\cos 3x}{\sin 3x+\sin x} &=\frac{2\sin 2x\sin x}{2\sin 2x\cos x}\\ &=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} =\tan x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q6. \(\dfrac{\sin 3x+\sin x}{\cos x-\cos 3x}=\cot x\)

Pattern chosen: the same angle pair appears in a sine sum and cosine difference. Sum-to-product creates a common \(2\sin 2x\) factor.
\[ \frac{\sin 3x+\sin x}{\cos x-\cos 3x} =\frac{2\sin 2x\cos x}{2\sin 2x\sin x} =\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} =\cot x. \] RHS reached.

Q7. \(\dfrac{1+\cos x}{\sin x}=\csc x+\cot x\)

Pattern chosen: a sum in the numerator can be split over the common denominator. The two resulting fractions are the reciprocal and quotient definitions on the RHS.
\[ \frac{1+\cos x}{\sin x} =\frac1{\sin x}+\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} =\csc x+\cot x. \] RHS reached.

Q8. \((\sec x+\tan x)^2=\dfrac{1+\sin x}{1-\sin x}\)

Pattern chosen: \(\sec x+\tan x\) converts to \((1+\sin x)/\cos x\). After squaring, \(\cos^2x=(1-\sin x)(1+\sin x)\) cancels one factor.
\[ \begin{aligned} (\sec x+\tan x)^2 &=\left(\frac1{\cos x}+\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\right)^2 =\frac{(1+\sin x)^2}{\cos^2x}\\ &=\frac{(1+\sin x)^2}{(1-\sin x)(1+\sin x)} =\frac{1+\sin x}{1-\sin x}. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q9. \(\dfrac{\sin A+\sin B}{\cos A+\cos B}=\tan\!\left(\dfrac{A+B}{2}\right)\)

Pattern chosen: sine and cosine sums using the same two angles. Sum-to-product gives the same factor \(\cos((A-B)/2)\) in numerator and denominator.
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\sin A+\sin B}{\cos A+\cos B} &=\frac{2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)} {2\cos\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)}\\ &=\tan\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right). \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q10. \(\dfrac{\cos A-\cos B}{\sin A+\sin B}=-\tan\!\left(\dfrac{A-B}{2}\right)\)

Pattern chosen: cosine difference over sine sum using the same angles. Sum-to-product produces a common \(\sin((A+B)/2)\) factor and preserves the minus sign from the cosine-difference formula.
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\cos A-\cos B}{\sin A+\sin B} &=\frac{-2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)} {2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)}\\ &=-\tan\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right). \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Set 5 — Mixed Multi-Step Pattern Selection

Q1. \(\dfrac{\cos 2x+\cos 4x}{\sin 4x+\sin 2x}=\cot 3x\)

Pattern chosen: both numerator and denominator pair the angles \(2x\) and \(4x\). Sum-to-product creates average angle \(3x\) and common half-difference factor \(\cos x\).
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\cos 2x+\cos 4x}{\sin 4x+\sin 2x} &=\frac{2\cos 3x\cos x}{2\sin 3x\cos x}\\ &=\frac{\cos 3x}{\sin 3x} =\cot 3x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q2. \(\dfrac{\sin 4x}{\sin x\cos x}=4\cos 2x\)

Pattern chosen: the denominator contains \(\sin x\cos x\), while the numerator has angle \(4x\). Expand \(\sin 4x\) in two double-angle stages until \(\sin x\cos x\) appears.
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\sin 4x}{\sin x\cos x} &=\frac{2\sin 2x\cos 2x}{\sin x\cos x}\\ &=\frac{2(2\sin x\cos x)\cos 2x}{\sin x\cos x} =4\cos 2x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q3. \(\dfrac{1-\cos 4x}{1-\cos 2x}=4\cos^2x\)

Pattern chosen: both numerator and denominator have \(1-\cos(\text{double angle})\). Apply \(1-\cos 2u=2\sin^2u\) first with \(u=2x\), then expand \(\sin 2x\).
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{1-\cos 4x}{1-\cos 2x} &=\frac{2\sin^22x}{2\sin^2x} =\frac{(2\sin x\cos x)^2}{\sin^2x}\\ &=4\cos^2x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q4. \(\dfrac{\cos 4x-\cos 2x}{\sin 4x+\sin 2x}=-\tan x\)

Pattern chosen: cosine difference and sine sum using \(4x\) and \(2x\). Sum-to-product gives average angle \(3x\) as a common factor and half-difference \(x\) as the surviving angle.
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\cos 4x-\cos 2x}{\sin 4x+\sin 2x} &=\frac{-2\sin 3x\sin x}{2\sin 3x\cos x}\\ &=-\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} =-\tan x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q5. \(\dfrac{\tan x-\sin x}{\tan x+\sin x}=\dfrac{1-\cos x}{1+\cos x}\)

Pattern chosen: \(\tan x\) is the only function outside sine and cosine. Replace it with \(\sin x/\cos x\); both numerator and denominator then share \(\sin x/\cos x\).
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\tan x-\sin x}{\tan x+\sin x} &=\frac{\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}-\sin x}{\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}+\sin x}\\ &=\frac{\frac{\sin x(1-\cos x)}{\cos x}} {\frac{\sin x(1+\cos x)}{\cos x}} =\frac{1-\cos x}{1+\cos x}. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q6. \(\dfrac{\sec x-\cos x}{\tan x}=\sin x\)

Pattern chosen: reciprocal and quotient functions can be converted to sine and cosine. The numerator then contains \(1-\cos^2x=\sin^2x\).
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\sec x-\cos x}{\tan x} &=\frac{\frac1{\cos x}-\cos x}{\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}} =\frac{\frac{1-\cos^2x}{\cos x}}{\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}}\\ &=\frac{\sin^2x}{\sin x} =\sin x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q7. \(\dfrac{\csc x-\sin x}{\cot x}=\cos x\)

Pattern chosen: convert reciprocal and quotient functions to sine and cosine. The numerator becomes \(1-\sin^2x=\cos^2x\).
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\csc x-\sin x}{\cot x} &=\frac{\frac1{\sin x}-\sin x}{\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}} =\frac{\frac{1-\sin^2x}{\sin x}}{\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}}\\ &=\frac{\cos^2x}{\cos x} =\cos x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q8. \(\dfrac{1+\sin 2x}{\sin x+\cos x}=\sin x+\cos x\)

Pattern chosen: the denominator is \(\sin x+\cos x\), and its square expands to \(1+\sin 2x\). Rewrite the numerator as that square.
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{1+\sin 2x}{\sin x+\cos x} &=\frac{\sin^2x+\cos^2x+2\sin x\cos x}{\sin x+\cos x}\\ &=\frac{(\sin x+\cos x)^2}{\sin x+\cos x} =\sin x+\cos x. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.

Q9. \(\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{1+\cos 2x}=\tan^2x\)

Pattern chosen: matched forms \(1-\cos 2x\) and \(1+\cos 2x\). Use the paired power-reduction identities \(2\sin^2x\) and \(2\cos^2x\).
\[ \frac{1-\cos 2x}{1+\cos 2x} =\frac{2\sin^2x}{2\cos^2x} =\left(\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\right)^2 =\tan^2x. \] RHS reached.

Q10. \(\left(\dfrac{1-\cos x}{\sin x}\right)^2=\dfrac{1-\cos x}{1+\cos x}\)

Pattern chosen: the LHS denominator is \(\sin^2x\), which factors as \((1-\cos x)(1+\cos x)\). That factorisation cancels one copy of \(1-\cos x\).
\[ \begin{aligned} \left(\frac{1-\cos x}{\sin x}\right)^2 &=\frac{(1-\cos x)^2}{\sin^2x} =\frac{(1-\cos x)^2}{1-\cos^2x}\\ &=\frac{(1-\cos x)^2}{(1-\cos x)(1+\cos x)} =\frac{1-\cos x}{1+\cos x}. \end{aligned} \] RHS reached.
