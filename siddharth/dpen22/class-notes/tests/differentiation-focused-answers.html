Group 1 — Differentiation Rules

Set 1 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Power rule: \(y'=5x^4-3(2x)+0=5x^4-6x.\)
Q2. Root recognition: \(y=x^{1/2}\), so \(y'=\tfrac12x^{-1/2}=\dfrac1{2\sqrt{x}}.\)
Q3. Negative power: \(f=4x^{-2}\), hence \(f'=-8x^{-3}=-\dfrac8{x^3}.\)
Q4. Power rule: \(y'=3(\tfrac23)x^{-1/3}-2(-1)x^{-2}=2x^{-1/3}+2x^{-2}.\)
Q5. Chain rule: \(y'=4(2x+1)^3(2)=8(2x+1)^3.\)
Q6. Power-chain: \(y=(3x-2)^{1/2}\), so \(y'=\tfrac12(3x-2)^{-1/2}(3)=\dfrac3{2\sqrt{3x-2}}.\)
Q7. Chain rule: \(f'=3(x^2+4)^2(2x)=6x(x^2+4)^2.\)
Q8. Negative-power chain: \(y=(5x+1)^{-2}\), so \(y'=-2(5x+1)^{-3}(5)=-\dfrac{10}{(5x+1)^3}.\)
Q9. Product rule: \(y'=2x(x+3)+x^2(1)=3x^2+6x.\)
Q10. Product rule: \(y'=2x(2x-5)+2(x^2+1)=6x^2-10x+2.\)
Q11. Product rule: \(y'=3x^2e^x+x^3e^x=e^x(x^3+3x^2).\)
Q12. Product rule: \(y'=1\sin x+x\cos x=\sin x+x\cos x.\)
Q13. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{(x-2)-(x+1)}{(x-2)^2}=-\dfrac3{(x-2)^2}.\)
Q14. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{2x(x+3)-x^2}{(x+3)^2}=\dfrac{x^2+6x}{(x+3)^2}.\)
Q15. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{x\cos x-\sin x}{x^2}.\)
Q16. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{e^x(x+1)-e^x}{(x+1)^2}=\dfrac{xe^x}{(x+1)^2}.\)
Q17. Exponential chain: \(y'=e^{3x}(3)=3e^{3x}.\)
Q18. Log chain: \(y'=\dfrac{(2x+5)'}{2x+5}=\dfrac2{2x+5}.\)
Q19. Trig chain: \(y'=\cos(4x)(4)=4\cos(4x).\)
Q20. Trig chain: \(y'=-\sin(x^2)(2x)=-2x\sin(x^2).\)
Q21. Product rule: \(y'=2xe^x+(x^2+1)e^x=e^x(x^2+2x+1).\)
Q22. Product rule: \(y'=\ln x+x(\tfrac1x)=\ln x+1.\)
Q23. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{(x^2+1)\cos x-2x\sin x}{(x^2+1)^2}.\)
Q24. Product and chain: \(y'=2e^{2x}\cos x-e^{2x}\sin x=e^{2x}(2\cos x-\sin x).\)

Set 2 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Power rule: \(y'=\tfrac72x^{5/2}+15x^{-4}.\)
Q2. Fractional powers: \(y'=\tfrac23x^{-1/3}-x^{-3/2}.\)
Q3. Simplify first: \(y=x^2+x^{-2}\), so \(y'=2x-2x^{-3}.\)
Q4. Power rule: \(y'=-\tfrac{10}{3}x^{-5/3}-\tfrac34x^{-3/4}.\)
Q5. Chain rule: \(y'=5(3x^2-1)^4(6x)=30x(3x^2-1)^4.\)
Q6. Negative-power chain: \(y'=-2(x^3+2)^{-3}(3x^2)=-6x^2(x^3+2)^{-3}.\)
Q7. Power-chain: \(y'=\tfrac12(1+x^2)^{-1/2}(2x)=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}.\)
Q8. Power-chain: \(y'=\tfrac72(4-3x)^{5/2}(-3)=-\tfrac{21}{2}(4-3x)^{5/2}.\)
Q9. Product plus chain: \(y'=6(2x-1)^2(x^2+1)+2x(2x-1)^3.\)
Q10. Product plus chain: \(y'=e^{2x}\!\left[2(x^3-1)+3x^2\right].\)
Q11. Product rule: \(y'=\dfrac{\cos x}{x}-(\ln x)\sin x.\)
Q12. Product plus chain: \(y'=2x\sin(3x)+3x^2\cos(3x).\)
Q13. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{2x(2x+3)-2(x^2-1)}{(2x+3)^2}=\dfrac{2x^2+6x+2}{(2x+3)^2}.\)
Q14. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{x^2/x-2x\ln x}{x^4}=\dfrac{1-2\ln x}{x^3}.\)
Q15. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{e^x(x^2+1)-2xe^x}{(x^2+1)^2}.\)
Q16. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{(x+1)\sec^2x-\tan x}{(x+1)^2}.\)
Q17. Exponential chain: \(y'=(2x-3)e^{x^2-3x}.\)
Q18. Log chain: \(y'=\dfrac{2x}{x^2+4}.\)
Q19. Nested trig chain: \(y'=\cos((2x-1)^2)\,2(2x-1)(2)=4(2x-1)\cos((2x-1)^2).\)
Q20. Trig chain: \(y'=\sec^2(3x^2)(6x)=6x\sec^2(3x^2).\)
Q21. Product and exponential chain: \(y'=e^{x^2}+2x^2e^{x^2}=e^{x^2}(1+2x^2).\)
Q22. Product and log chain: \(y'=2x\ln(3x)+(x^2+1)\dfrac{3}{3x}=2x\ln(3x)+\dfrac{x^2+1}{x}.\)
Q23. Product and two chains: \(y'=-e^{-x}\sin(2x)+2e^{-x}\cos(2x)=e^{-x}(2\cos2x-\sin2x).\)
Q24. Quotient plus log chain: \(y'=\dfrac{\frac{x^2+1}{x+1}-2x\ln(x+1)}{(x^2+1)^2}.\)

Set 3 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Power rule: \(y'=\tfrac53x^{2/3}-7x^{-9/2}.\)
Q2. Simplify first: \(y=2x^2-3x^{-1}+x^{-3}\), so \(y'=4x+3x^{-2}-3x^{-4}.\)
Q3. Root powers: \(y=x^{1/2}+x^{-2/3}\), so \(y'=\tfrac12x^{-1/2}-\tfrac23x^{-5/3}.\)
Q4. Power rule: \(y'=2x^{-1/2}+5x^{-8/3}.\)
Q5. Chain rule: \(y'=4(x^3-2x)^3(3x^2-2).\)
Q6. Negative-power chain: \(y'=-\tfrac32(x^2+1)^{-5/2}(2x)=-3x(x^2+1)^{-5/2}.\)
Q7. Nested power-chain: \(y'=4((x^2+1)^3-2)^3[3(x^2+1)^2(2x)]=24x(x^2+1)^2((x^2+1)^3-2)^3.\)
Q8. Negative-power chain: \(y'=-2(1-4x^3)^{-3}(-12x^2)=24x^2(1-4x^3)^{-3}.\)
Q9. Product plus chain: \(y'=6x(x^2+1)^2\ln x+\dfrac{(x^2+1)^3}{x}.\)
Q10. Product plus exponential chain: \(y'=e^{x^2}(2x\sin x+\cos x).\)
Q11. Product plus trig chain: \(y'=3x^2\cos(2x)-2(x^3-1)\sin(2x).\)
Q12. Product and chains: \(y'=\dfrac{2\tan x}{2x+1}+\ln(2x+1)\sec^2x.\)
Q13. Quotient plus chain: \(y'=\dfrac{4x(x^2+1)(x-1)-(x^2+1)^2}{(x-1)^2}.\)
Q14. Quotient and exponential chain: \(y'=\dfrac{e^{2x}[2(x^3+1)-3x^2]}{(x^3+1)^2}.\)
Q15. Quotient and log chain: \(y'=\dfrac{\frac{2x^2}{x^2+1}-\ln(x^2+1)}{x^2}.\)
Q16. Quotient and trig chain: \(y'=\dfrac{3(x^2+4)\cos(3x)-2x\sin(3x)}{(x^2+4)^2}.\)
Q17. Nested exponential chain: \(y'=e^{\sin(2x)}[2\cos(2x)].\)
Q18. Log and power chain: \(y'=\dfrac{3(x^2+1)^2(2x)}{(x^2+1)^3}=\dfrac{6x}{x^2+1}.\)
Q19. Nested trig chain: \(y'=-\sin(e^x)e^x=-e^x\sin(e^x).\)
Q20. Nested trig/power chain: \(y'=\sec^2(\sqrt{3x+1})\dfrac{3}{2\sqrt{3x+1}}.\)
Q21. Product and chains: \(y'=e^{x^2}\left(2x\ln x+\dfrac1x\right).\)
Q22. Product and trig chain: \(y'=2x\sin(x^3)+3x^2(x^2+1)\cos(x^3).\)
Q23. Product and chains: \(y'=e^{-x}\left[\dfrac2{2x+1}-\ln(2x+1)\right].\)
Q24. Quotient and exponential chain: \(y'=\dfrac{e^{\sin x}[(x^2+1)\cos x-2x]}{(x^2+1)^2}.\)

Group 2 — Higher Derivatives and Values at a Point

Set 1 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Differentiate twice: \(y'=4x^3-6x+2\), hence \(y''=12x^2-6.\)
Q2. Power recognition: \(y'= \tfrac12x^{-1/2}\), so \(y''=-\tfrac14x^{-3/2}.\)
Q3. Exponential chain twice: \(y'=2e^{2x}\), so \(y''=4e^{2x}.\)
Q4. Trig chain twice: \(y'=3\cos(3x)\), so \(y''=-9\sin(3x).\)
Q5. Differentiate, then substitute: \(f'=3x^2-2\), so \(f'(2)=12-2=10.\)
Q6. Exponential chain: \(f'=3e^{3x}\), so \(f'(0)=3.\)
Q7. Log chain: \(f'=\dfrac2{2x+1}\), so \(f'(1)=\dfrac23.\)
Q8. Trig chain: \(f'=-2\sin(2x)\), so \(f'(\pi/4)=-2\sin(\pi/2)=-2.\)

Set 2 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Differentiate three times: \(f'=10x^4-3x^2+4\), \(f''=40x^3-6x\), \(f'''=120x^2-6.\)
Q2. Chain twice: \(y'=8(2x+1)^3\), hence \(y''=48(2x+1)^2.\)
Q3. Product rule twice: \(y'=e^x(x+1)\), so \(y''=e^x(x+1)+e^x=e^x(x+2).\)
Q4. Log derivative then negative power: \(y'=x^{-1}\), so \(y''=-x^{-2}=-\dfrac1{x^2}.\)
Q5. Chain then substitute: \(f'=6x(x^2+1)^2\), so \(f'(1)=6(1)(2^2)=24.\)
Q6. Product rule: \(f'=e^x(x^2+2x)\), so \(f'(0)=0.\)
Q7. Quotient rule: \(f'=\dfrac{(x+2)-(x+1)}{(x+2)^2}=\dfrac1{(x+2)^2}\), so \(f'(1)=\dfrac19.\)
Q8. Trig chain: \(f'=3\cos(3x)\), so \(f'(\pi/6)=3\cos(\pi/2)=0.\)

Set 3 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Power rule three times: \(y'=\tfrac72x^{5/2}\), \(y''=\tfrac{35}{4}x^{3/2}\), \(y'''=\tfrac{105}{8}x^{1/2}.\)
Q2. Product after chain: \(y'=2xe^{x^2}\), so \(y''=2e^{x^2}+4x^2e^{x^2}=e^{x^2}(2+4x^2).\)
Q3. Product rule twice: \(y'=2x\ln x+x\), hence \(y''=2\ln x+3.\)
Q4. Negative-power chain twice: \(y'=-(x+1)^{-2}\), so \(y''=2(x+1)^{-3}.\)
Q5. Exponential chain: \(f'=2xe^{x^2-1}\), so \(f'(1)=2.\)
Q6. Log chain: \(f'=\dfrac{2x}{x^2+3}\), so \(f'(1)=\dfrac12.\)
Q7. Quotient rule: \(f'=\dfrac{x\cos x-\sin x}{x^2}\), so \(f'(\pi)=\dfrac{-\pi}{\pi^2}=-\dfrac1\pi.\)
Q8. Product and trig chain: \(f'=e^x[\cos(2x)-2\sin(2x)]\), so \(f'(0)=1.\)

Group 3 — Tangents and Applications

Set 1 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Tangent cue: \(y'=2x\), so \(m=4\) and the point is \((2,5)\). Thus \(y-5=4(x-2)\), or \(y=4x-3.\)
Q2. Tangent cue: \(y'=\dfrac1{2\sqrt{x}}\), so at \((4,2)\), \(m=\tfrac14\). Hence \(y-2=\tfrac14(x-4)\), or \(y=\tfrac14x+1.\)
Q3. Tangent cue: \(y'=e^x\). At \((0,1)\), \(m=1\), so \(y=x+1.\)
Q4. Tangent cue: \(y'=1/x\). At \((1,0)\), \(m=1\), so \(y=x-1.\)
Q5. Horizontal means \(y'=0\): \(3x^2-3=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1\). The points are \((-1,2)\) and \((1,-2)\).
Q6. Parallel means equal gradients: \(y'=2x-4=2\Rightarrow x=3\). Then \(y=-2\), so the point is \((3,-2)\).
Q7. Match the required gradient: \(y'=2x=6\Rightarrow x=3\). Since \(y=3^2=9\), the point is \((3,9)\).
Q8. Velocity is \(s'(t)\): \(v=3t^2-12t+9=3(t-1)(t-3)\). The particle rests at \(t=1,3.\)

Set 2 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Tangent cue: \(y'=3x^2-2\), so at \(x=-1\), \(m=1\) and \(y=1\). Thus \(y-1=x+1\), or \(y=x+2.\)
Q2. Tangent cue: \(y'=-x^{-2}\). At \((2,\tfrac12)\), \(m=-\tfrac14\), so \(y-\tfrac12=-\tfrac14(x-2)\), or \(y=-\tfrac14x+1.\)
Q3. Tangent cue: \(y'=2e^{2x}+1\). At \((0,1)\), \(m=3\), hence \(y=3x+1.\)
Q4. Tangent cue: \(y'=\cos x\). At \((\pi/3,\sqrt3/2)\), \(m=\tfrac12\), so \(y-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\tfrac12(x-\tfrac\pi3).\)
Q5. Horizontal means \(y'=0\): \(3x^2-6x=3x(x-2)=0\). The points are \((0,2)\) and \((2,-2)\).
Q6. Parallel gradients: \(3x^2-1=2\Rightarrow x=\pm1\). Both curve points have \(y=0\); the tangents are \(y=2x-2\) and \(y=2x+2.\)
Q7. Parallel means equal gradients: \(y'=1/x=1/e\Rightarrow x=e\). Since \(y=\ln e=1\), the point is \((e,1)\).
Q8. Acceleration is \(s''(t)\): \(v=4t^3-12t^2+2\), \(a=12t^2-24t\), so \(a(2)=0.\)

Set 3 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Tangent cue: \(y'=6x(x^2+1)^2\). At \((1,8)\), \(m=24\); \(y-8=24(x-1)\), so \(y=24x-16.\)
Q2. Product rule: \(y'=e^x(x+1)\). At \((1,e)\), \(m=2e\); hence \(y-e=2e(x-1).\)
Q3. Log chain: \(y'=\dfrac{2x}{x^2+1}\). At \((1,\ln2)\), \(m=1\); hence \(y-\ln2=x-1.\)
Q4. Quotient rule: \(y'=\dfrac{x\cos x-\sin x}{x^2}\). At \((\pi,0)\), \(m=-1/\pi\); \(y=-\dfrac1\pi(x-\pi)=1-\dfrac{x}{\pi}.\)
Q5. Horizontal means \(y'=0\): \(4x^3-8x=4x(x^2-2)=0\). Thus \(x=0,\pm\sqrt2\), giving \((0,0)\) and \((\pm\sqrt2,-4)\).
Q6. Parallel gradients: \(3x^2-2=10\Rightarrow x=\pm2\). At \(x=2\), \(y=4\): \(y=10x-16\). At \(x=-2\), \(y=-4\): \(y=10x+16\).
Q7. Parallel means equal gradients: \(y'=2e^{2x}=2\Rightarrow e^{2x}=1\Rightarrow x=0\). The curve point is \((0,1)\), so \(y-1=2(x-0)\), or \(y=2x+1\).
Q8. Differentiate motion functions: \(v=e^t(t^3+3t^2)\), \(a=e^t(t^3+6t^2+6t)\). Thus \(v(1)=4e\) and \(a(1)=13e.\)

Limits

Set 1 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Direct substitution: \(2^2+3(2)-1=9.\)
Q2. \(0/0\), factor: \(\dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3\) for \(x\ne3\), so the limit is \(6.\)
Q3. \(0/0\), rationalise: \(\dfrac{\sqrt{x}-2}{x-4}\dfrac{\sqrt{x}+2}{\sqrt{x}+2}=\dfrac1{\sqrt{x}+2}\to\dfrac14.\)
Q4. Infinity, equal degrees: divide by \(x^2\): \(\dfrac{3+1/x^2}{1-5/x^2}\to3.\)
Q5. Standard radian limit: \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1.\)
Q6. One-sided sign: for \(x>0\), \(|x|=x\), so \(|x|/x=1\). The limit is \(1.\)
Q7. Compare one-sided limits: LHL \(=-1\), RHL \(=1\). They differ, so the two-sided limit does not exist.
Q8. Piecewise one-sided limits: LHL \(=1+2=3\), RHL \(=3(1)=3\). Therefore the limit exists and equals \(3.\)

Set 2 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Sum of cubes: \(x^3+1=(x+1)(x^2-x+1)\). Cancel \(x+1\), then substitute: \(1+1+1=3.\)
Q2. Recognise conjugate factors: \(x-9=(\sqrt{x}-3)(\sqrt{x}+3)\), so the expression becomes \(\sqrt{x}+3\to6.\)
Q3. Infinity, equal degrees: divide by \(x\): \(\dfrac{5-2/x}{2+7/x}\to\dfrac52.\)
Q4. Denominator degree is larger: divide by \(x^2\): \(\dfrac{4/x+1/x^2}{1+3/x^2}\to0.\)
Q5. Standard sine limit: \(\dfrac{\sin(5x)}x=5\dfrac{\sin(5x)}{5x}\to5.\)
Q6. Left-hand sign: for \(x<2\), \(|x-2|=-(x-2)\), so the ratio is \(-1\). The limit is \(-1.\)
Q7. Piecewise one-sided limits: LHL \(=2(2)+1=5\); RHL \(=2^2-1=3\). Since they differ, the limit does not exist.
Q8. Factor: \(x^2+x-2=(x+2)(x-1)\). Cancel \(x-1\), then \(x+2\to3.\)

Set 3 — Worked Solutions

Q1. Factor both: \(\dfrac{(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)}{(x-2)(x+2)}\to\dfrac{12}{4}=3.\)
Q2. Rationalise: \(\dfrac{\sqrt{1+3x}-1}{x}\dfrac{\sqrt{1+3x}+1}{\sqrt{1+3x}+1}=\dfrac3{\sqrt{1+3x}+1}\to\dfrac32.\)
Q3. Infinity, equal highest degree: divide by \(x^3\); the limit is the leading-coefficient ratio \(\dfrac25.\)
Q4. Infinity and square root: \(\sqrt{x^4+1}=x^2\sqrt{1+1/x^4}\). Thus \(\dfrac{3+1/x}{\sqrt{1+1/x^4}}\to3.\)
Q5. Two standard sine limits: \(\dfrac{\sin3x}{\sin2x}=\dfrac{\sin3x}{3x}\dfrac{2x}{\sin2x}\dfrac32\to\dfrac32.\)
Q6. One-sided absolute value: for \(x>0\), \(x/|x|=1\); for \(x<0\), \(x/|x|=-1\). Therefore RHL \(=1\), LHL \(=-1.\)
Q7. Match one-sided limits: LHL \(=1+a\), RHL \(=3(1)-1=2\). Set \(1+a=2\), giving \(a=1.\)
Q8. Factor and cancel: \(\dfrac{(x+2)(x+3)}{(x+2)(x-2)}=\dfrac{x+3}{x-2}\to-\dfrac14.\)
