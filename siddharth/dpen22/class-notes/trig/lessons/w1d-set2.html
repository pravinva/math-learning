Amplitude, period & the general sine/cosine model

From Week 1D: transformations \(y=a\sin(bx)+d\) and \(y=a\cos(bx)+d\) — reading amplitude, period and midline.

1. Short lesson

For \(y=a\sin(bx)\) or \(y=a\cos(bx)\): amplitude is \(|a|\); period is \(\dfrac{2\pi}{|b|}\). The graph is stretched vertically by \(|a|\) and horizontally by \(\dfrac{1}{|b|}\).

A vertical shift \(+d\) moves the midline to \(y=d\). Range becomes \([d-|a|, d+|a|]\).

Always state amplitude, period, midline/range when describing a transformed wave.

Worked example. \(y=3\sin(2x)\): amp \(3\), period \(\pi\). \(y=2\cos\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)-1\): amp \(2\), period \(4\pi\), midline \(y=-1\), range \([-3,1]\).

2. Key points summary

  • Amplitude \(=|a|\).
  • Period \(=\dfrac{2\pi}{|b|}\) for sine/cosine.
  • Midline \(y=d\); range \([d-|a|,d+|a|]\).
  • Larger \(|b|\) → shorter period (more cycles).

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(y=a\sin(bx)+d\) or \(y=a\cos(bx)+d\)
  • Amplitude \(|a|\); period \(\dfrac{2\pi}{|b|}\); midline \(y=d\)
  • For tangent: \(y=a\tan(bx)\) has period \(\dfrac{\pi}{|b|}\)

  15. Describe the transformation from \(y=\cos x\) to \(y=\cos(3x)\).
