Radian conversion, arcs & exact values

From Week 1C: mixed degree–radian conversion, arc length and sector area, and exact trig values with radian arguments.

1. Short lesson

Arc length on a circle of radius \(r\) with central angle \(\theta\) in radians is \(s=r\theta\). Sector area is \(A=\dfrac12 r^2\theta\) (again with \(\theta\) in radians).

Mixed conversion questions may ask for both exact \(\pi\)-form and a decimal. Keep exact form until the last step when a decimal is required.

Exact trig values in radians use the same special triangles: \(\dfrac{\pi}{6},\dfrac{\pi}{4},\dfrac{\pi}{3}\) and their coterminal / reference-angle cousins in other quadrants.

When an arc length or sector is given in degrees, convert the angle to radians before using \(s=r\theta\) or \(A=\dfrac12 r^2\theta\).

Worked example. A sector of radius \(6\) and angle \(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\) has arc \(s=6\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{3}=2\pi\) and area \(A=\dfrac12\cdot 36\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{3}=6\pi\).

2. Key points summary

  • \(\pi\) rad \(=180^\circ\) is the conversion key.
  • Arc \(s=r\theta\) and sector \(A=\dfrac12 r^2\theta\) need \(\theta\) in radians.
  • Reduce angles into \([0,2\pi)\) before reading quadrants.
  • Leave exact answers in terms of \(\pi\) unless a decimal is asked.
  • Special values: \(\dfrac{\pi}{6},\dfrac{\pi}{4},\dfrac{\pi}{3}\) and axis angles.

3. Formulas & identities

  • deg \(\to\) rad: \(\times\dfrac{\pi}{180}\); rad \(\to\) deg: \(\times\dfrac{180}{\pi}\)
  • \(s=r\theta,\quad A=\dfrac12 r^2\theta\) (\(\theta\) in radians)
  • \(\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac12,\ \sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ \sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\)
  • Full circle: \(\theta=2\pi\), circumference \(2\pi r\), area \(\pi r^2\).
  • Q2 ref: \(\pi-\theta\); Q3: \(\theta-\pi\); Q4: \(2\pi-\theta\).

4. Practice problems (15)

  1. Convert \(210^\circ\) to radians (exact) and \(3.8\) rad to degrees (1 d.p.).
  2. Convert \(\dfrac{11\pi}{6}\) to degrees and \(75^\circ\) to radians (exact).
  3. A circle has radius \(5\) cm. Find the exact arc length subtended by a central angle of \(120^\circ\).
  4. For the same circle (radius \(5\) cm) and \(120^\circ\) central angle, find the exact sector area.
  5. An arc of length \(10\) cm on a circle of radius \(4\) cm subtends central angle \(\theta\). Find \(\theta\) in radians and in degrees (1 d.p.).
  6. A sector has area \(18\pi\) and radius \(6\). Find the exact central angle in radians and the exact arc length.
  7. Exact value of \(\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{3}+\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\).
  8. Exact value of \(\tan\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\cdot\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6}\).
  9. Exact value of \(\sec\dfrac{3\pi}{4}+\csc\dfrac{\pi}{6}\).
  10. Reduce \(\dfrac{19\pi}{4}\) into \([0,2\pi)\) and find exact \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) of the reduced angle.
  11. A wheel of radius \(0.35\) m rotates through \(250^\circ\). Find the distance a point on the rim travels, to 2 d.p.
  12. Which is larger: \(\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\) rad or \(140^\circ\)? Justify with a conversion.
  13. Exact \(\sin\!\left(-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right)\) and \(\cos\!\left(-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right)\).
  14. A sector of a circle of radius \(10\) has arc length \(4\pi\). Find the sector area exactly.
  15. Express \(1\) rad in degrees to the nearest minute.
