Radian equations on \([0,2\pi]\)

From Week 1C: solve sine, cosine and tangent equations in radians on a full turn, including rearranging and double-angle forms.

1. Short lesson

Isolate the trig function first, then find the reference angle in radians and place solutions using CAST. Answers should be exact multiples of \(\pi\) whenever possible.

When the equation involves \(2\theta\) or \(3\theta\), solve for the multiple angle on the expanded interval, then divide. Keep only solutions that land in the required interval for \(\theta\).

Rearrangements such as \(2\cos\theta+\sqrt3=0\) or \(\sqrt2\sin\theta-1=0\) are routine once the trig ratio is isolated. Reciprocal equations (\(\sec,\csc,\cot\)) convert to \(\sin,\cos,\tan\) first.

Always check the endpoints \(0\) and \(2\pi\) when they satisfy the equation, and discard solutions outside the stated interval.

Worked example. Solve \(2\cos\theta=-\sqrt3\) on \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\): \(\cos\theta=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\), ref \(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\), Q2/Q3 → \(\theta=\dfrac{5\pi}{6},\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\).

2. Key points summary

  • Isolate, reference angle, CAST, list all in the interval.
  • For \(k\theta\), expand the interval for \(k\theta\) first.
  • Write answers in terms of \(\pi\).
  • Convert sec/csc/cot to sin/cos/tan before solving.
  • Tan has period \(\pi\), so two solutions per \(2\pi\) interval when defined.

3. Formulas & identities

  • If \(\sin\theta=k\): Q1/Q2 (\(k\gt 0\)) or Q3/Q4 (\(k\lt 0\)).
  • If \(\cos\theta=k\): Q1/Q4 (\(k\gt 0\)) or Q2/Q3 (\(k\lt 0\)).
  • If \(\tan\theta=k\): period \(\pi\).
  • \(\sin 2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta\) (useful when a product appears).
  • Reciprocals: \(\csc\theta=\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}\), etc.

4. Practice problems (15)

  1. Solve \(\sin\theta=-\dfrac12\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  2. Solve \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  3. Solve \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  4. Solve \(2\sin\theta+\sqrt3=0\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  5. Solve \(\sqrt2\cos\theta-1=0\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  6. Solve \(3\tan\theta+\sqrt3=0\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  7. Solve \(\sin 2\theta=-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\) for \(0\le\theta\le\pi\).
  8. Solve \(2\cos 2\theta=1\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  9. Solve \(\csc\theta=-2\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  10. Solve \(\sec\theta=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  11. Solve \(\sin\theta=\cos\theta\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  12. Solve \(2\sin\theta\cos\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\) for \(0\le\theta\le\pi\).
  13. Solve \(\cos\theta(\cos\theta-1)=0\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  14. Solve \(2\sin^2\theta-1=0\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\) (use a double-angle idea or factor).
  15. Solve \(\tan\theta\sin\theta=\sin\theta\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\) (factor carefully).
