Answers (15)

Q1. Numerator \(1-\cos^2\theta=\sin^2\theta\), denominator \(1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta\). \[ \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}=\tan^2\theta. \] \[\boxed{\tan^2\theta}\]
Q2. Combine over a common denominator: \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\sec\theta}{\tan\theta}-\frac{\tan\theta}{\sec\theta} &=\frac{\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta}{\sec\theta\tan\theta} =\frac{1}{\sec\theta\tan\theta} =\frac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta} =\cos\theta\cot\theta. \end{aligned} \] \[\boxed{\cos\theta\cot\theta}\]
Q3. Factor \(\sin\theta\cos\theta(\tan\theta+\cot\theta)\). \[ \tan\theta+\cot\theta=\frac{\sin}{\cos}+\frac{\cos}{\sin}=\frac{\sin^2+\cos^2}{\sin\cos}=\frac{1}{\sin\cos}, \] so the product is \(\sin\cos\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sin\cos}=1\). \[\boxed{1}\]
Q4. Common denominator \((1-\sin\theta)(1+\sin\theta)=1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta\). \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\cos}{1-\sin}-\frac{\cos}{1+\sin} &=\cos\cdot\frac{(1+\sin)-(1-\sin)}{\cos^2} =\frac{\cos\cdot 2\sin}{\cos^2} =\frac{2\sin}{\cos}=2\tan\theta. \end{aligned} \] \[\boxed{2\tan\theta\text{ (proved)}}\]
Q5. Expand: \[ (\sin+\cos)^2+(\sin-\cos)^2=\sin^2+2\sin\cos+\cos^2+\sin^2-2\sin\cos+\cos^2=2\sin^2+2\cos^2=2. \] \[\boxed{2\text{ (proved)}}\]
Q6. \(\csc^2-1=\cot^2\), so \[ \frac{\cot^2\theta}{\csc^2\theta}=\frac{\cos^2/\sin^2}{1/\sin^2}=\cos^2\theta. \] \[\boxed{\cos^2\theta}\]
Q7. Left side: \(\dfrac{\sec^2\theta}{\csc^2\theta}\) because \(1+\tan^2=\sec^2\) and \(1+\cot^2=\csc^2\). \[ \frac{\sec^2}{\csc^2}=\frac{1/\cos^2}{1/\sin^2}=\frac{\sin^2}{\cos^2}=\tan^2\theta. \] \[\boxed{\tan^2\theta\text{ (proved)}}\]
Q8. Difference of squares: \(\sin^4-\cos^4=(\sin^2-\cos^2)(\sin^2+\cos^2)=\sin^2-\cos^2\). \[ \frac{\sin^4-\cos^4}{\sin^2-\cos^2}=1 \] (where \(\sin^2\neq\cos^2\)). \[\boxed{1}\]
Q9. Right side target from left: \[ \sec-\cos=\frac{1}{\cos}-\cos=\frac{1-\cos^2}{\cos}=\frac{\sin^2}{\cos}=\sin\cdot\frac{\sin}{\cos}=\sin\tan. \] \[\boxed{\text{proved}}\]
Q10. Common denominator \(\sec^2-1=\tan^2\): \[ \frac{1}{\sec-1}+\frac{1}{\sec+1}=\frac{(\sec+1)+(\sec-1)}{\sec^2-1}=\frac{2\sec}{\tan^2}. \] Also \(=\dfrac{2/\cos}{\sin^2/\cos^2}=\dfrac{2\cos}{\sin^2}=2\cot\theta\csc\theta\). In terms of cos: \(\dfrac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}\). \[\boxed{\dfrac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}\ \text{or}\ 2\cot\theta\csc\theta}\]
Q11. \(\dfrac{\sin}{\csc}=\sin^2\) and \(\dfrac{\cos}{\sec}=\cos^2\), so left side \(\sin^2-\cos^2\). \[\boxed{\text{proved}}\]
Q12. \[ \tan+\cot=\frac{\sin}{\cos}+\frac{\cos}{\sin}=\frac{\sin^2+\cos^2}{\sin\cos}=\frac{1}{\sin\cos}. \] \[\boxed{\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}}\]
Q13. Same calculation as Q12: \(\dfrac{\sin^2+\cos^2}{\sin\cos}=\dfrac{1}{\sin\cos}=\csc\theta\sec\theta\). \[\boxed{\csc\theta\sec\theta\text{ (proved)}}\]
Q14. Since \(\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta-1=(\sec\theta-1)(\sec\theta+1)\), \[ \frac{\tan^2\theta}{\sec\theta+1}=\sec\theta-1, \] and therefore \[ \frac{\tan^2\theta}{\sec\theta+1}+\sec\theta-1=(\sec\theta-1)+(\sec\theta-1)=2(\sec\theta-1). \] \[\boxed{2(\sec\theta-1)}\]
Q15. Cross-multiply check / start from left: \[ \frac{1-\sin}{\cos}\cdot\frac{1+\sin}{1+\sin}=\frac{1-\sin^2}{\cos(1+\sin)}=\frac{\cos^2}{\cos(1+\sin)}=\frac{\cos}{1+\sin}. \] \[\boxed{\text{proved}}\]
