1. Short lesson

Trigonometry studies the relationship between sides and angles in triangles. In a right-angled triangle the hypotenuse is opposite the \(90^\circ\) angle and is the longest side. Relative to a chosen acute angle \(\theta\), the other sides are the opposite and adjacent.

In similar right triangles the side ratios are constant for a fixed \(\theta\). Those constant ratios are \(\sin\theta\), \(\cos\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\). Their reciprocals are \(\csc\theta\), \(\sec\theta\) and \(\cot\theta\).

Complementary angles add to \(90^\circ\). This gives the co-function identities \(\sin\theta=\cos(90^\circ-\theta)\) and \(\cos\theta=\sin(90^\circ-\theta)\).

Worked example. In a \(3\)-\(4\)-\(5\) triangle, if the opposite to \(\theta\) is \(3\) and the hypotenuse is \(5\), then \(\sin\theta=\dfrac35\), \(\cos\theta=\dfrac45\), \(\tan\theta=\dfrac34\), \(\csc\theta=\dfrac53\), \(\sec\theta=\dfrac54\), \(\cot\theta=\dfrac43\).

2. Key points summary

  • Hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle.
  • Opposite/adjacent depend on which acute angle you choose.
  • Remember SOH-CAH-TOA.
  • \(\\sin\\theta\) and \(\\cos\\theta\) are never greater than \(1\) in a right triangle.
  • Reciprocals: \(\\csc=1/\\sin\), \(\\sec=1/\\cos\), \(\\cot=1/\\tan\).
  • Complementary: \(\\sin\\theta=\\cos(90^\\circ-\\theta)\).

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}},\quad \cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}},\quad \tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}\)
  • \(\csc\theta=\dfrac{\text{hyp}}{\text{opp}},\quad \sec\theta=\dfrac{\text{hyp}}{\text{adj}},\quad \cot\theta=\dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{opp}}\)
  • \(\csc\theta=\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta},\ \sec\theta=\dfrac{1}{\cos\theta},\ \cot\theta=\dfrac{1}{\tan\theta}=\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\)
  • \(\sin\theta=\cos(90^\circ-\theta),\quad \cos\theta=\sin(90^\circ-\theta),\quad \tan\theta=\cot(90^\circ-\theta)\)

4. Practice problems (15)

  1. In a right triangle, label opp/adj/hyp relative to an acute angle \(\theta\) when the sides touching \(\theta\) are \(5\) and \(12\), and the hypotenuse is \(13\).
  2. Using those sides, find \(\sin\theta\), \(\cos\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\).
  3. Find \(\sec\theta\) and \(\cot\theta\) for the same triangle.
  4. If \(\sin\alpha=\dfrac{5}{13}\) and \(\alpha\) is acute, find \(\cos\alpha\) and \(\tan\alpha\).
  5. If \(\cos\beta=\dfrac{8}{17}\) (acute), find \(\sin\beta\) and \(\sec\beta\).
  6. Write \(\cos 25^\circ\) as a sine of a complementary angle.
  7. Write \(\sin 72^\circ\) as a cosine of a complementary angle.
  8. In \(\triangle ABC\) right-angled at \(C\), \(AC=6\), \(BC=8\). Find \(\sin A\) and \(\cos A\).
  9. Find \(\tan A\) and \(\csc A\) for the triangle in Q8.
  10. If \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{7}{24}\) (acute), find \(\sin\theta\) and \(\cos\theta\).
  11. Simplify \(\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\).
  12. Simplify \(\sin\theta\cdot\csc\theta\).
  13. Explain why \(\sin\theta\) cannot equal \(1.2\) for a real acute angle in a right triangle.
  14. If \(\sec\theta=\dfrac{5}{3}\) (acute), find \(\cos\theta\) and \(\sin\theta\).
  15. Find \(\cot(90^\circ-\theta)\) in terms of \(\tan\theta\).
