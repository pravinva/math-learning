Pythagorean trigonometric identities

From Week 2A: deriving and using \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\) and the companion identities.

1. Short lesson

On the unit circle, \(x=\cos\theta\), \(y=\sin\theta\) and \(x^2+y^2=1\). Substituting gives the fundamental identity \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\).

Divide by \(\cos^2\theta\) (where defined) to get \(1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta\). Divide by \(\sin^2\theta\) to get \(1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta\).

These identities are true for all \(\theta\) where the functions exist. Use them to rewrite expressions and find exact ratios.

Worked example. If \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{29}}\) and \(\theta\) obtuse, then \(\cos\theta=-\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta}=-\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{29}}\) (negative in Q2).

2. Key points summary

  • Primary: \(\sin^2+\cos^2=1\).
  • Then: \(1+\tan^2=\sec^2\) and \(1+\cot^2=\csc^2\).
  • Choose the sign of the square root using the quadrant.
  • Identities hold for all valid \(\theta\), not just acute angles.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\)
  • \(1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta\)
  • \(1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta\)
  • \(\cos^2\theta=1-\sin^2\theta,\quad \sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta\)

4. Practice problems (15)

  1. Show that \(\theta=30^\circ\) satisfies \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\).
  2. If \(\sin\theta=\dfrac35\) (acute), find \(\cos\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\).
  3. If \(\cos\theta=-\dfrac{8}{17}\) and \(\theta\) is in Q2, find \(\sin\theta\).
  4. If \(\tan\theta=\dfrac43\) (acute), find \(\sec\theta\) using an identity.
  5. If \(\sec\theta=-\dfrac{17}{8}\) and \(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\lt \theta\lt \pi\), find \(\tan\theta\).
  6. Simplify \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta+\tan^2\theta\) in terms of \(\sec\theta\) where possible.
  7. Express \(\cos^2\theta\) in terms of \(\sin\theta\) only.
  8. Express \(\sin^2\theta\) in terms of \(\cos\theta\) only.
  9. Use \(1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta\) to find \(\sec\theta\) if \(\tan\theta=\sqrt3\) (Q1).
  10. If \(\cot\theta=2\) (acute), find \(\csc\theta\).
  11. True/false: \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\) for \(\theta=200^\circ\).
  12. Find \(\cos\theta\) if \(\sin\theta=-\dfrac{5}{13}\) and \(\theta\) in Q3.
  13. Find \(\sin\theta\) if \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{12}{13}\) and \(\theta\) in Q4.
  14. Simplify \(1-\sin^2\theta\).
  15. Simplify \(\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta\).
