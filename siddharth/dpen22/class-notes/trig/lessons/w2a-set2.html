Simplifying & proving identities

From Week 2A: rewrite trig expressions and prove identities by moving from one side to the other.

1. Short lesson

To simplify: expand products, factor, rewrite everything in \(\sin\) and \(\cos\), then apply Pythagorean identities.

To prove an identity: start from the more complicated side and transform it into the other side. Do not move terms across the equals sign as if solving an equation.

Common moves: \(1-\sin^2=\cos^2\), factor \(\sin^2\) or \(\cos^2\), and rewrite tan/sec in sin/cos.

Worked example. Simplify \(\sin^4\theta+\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta=\sin^2\theta(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta)=\sin^2\theta\).

2. Key points summary

  • Complicated side → simpler side.
  • Prefer \(\sin/\cos\) form for algebra.
  • Factor common terms early.
  • Quote the identity you use.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta},\ \sec\theta=\dfrac{1}{\cos\theta}\)
  • \((\csc\theta-1)(\csc\theta+1)=\cot^2\theta\)
  • Difference of squares and Pythagorean swaps are the main tools.

4. Practice problems (15)

  1. Simplify \((\csc\theta-1)(\csc\theta+1)\).
  2. Simplify \(\dfrac{\tan\theta}{\sec\theta}\).
  3. Simplify \(\cos\theta\tan\theta\).
  4. Simplify \(\sin\theta\cot\theta\).
  5. Simplify \(\dfrac{1-\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta}\).
  6. Simplify \(\sec^2\theta(1-\sin^2\theta)\).
  7. Prove \(\dfrac{1-\cos^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}=\tan^2\theta\).
  8. Prove \((\sec\theta-\tan\theta)(\sec\theta+\tan\theta)=1\).
  9. Prove \(\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\csc\theta}+\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sec\theta}=1\).
  10. Simplify \(\dfrac{\sec^2\theta-1}{\sec^2\theta}\).
  11. Simplify \(\sin^2\theta(\cot^2\theta+1)\).
  12. Prove \(1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta\) starting from \(\sin^2+\cos^2=1\).
  13. Simplify \(\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}+\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\).
  14. Prove \(\dfrac{\sin\theta}{1+\cos\theta}+\dfrac{1+\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}=2\csc\theta\) (optional challenge outline).
  15. Simplify \(\tan\theta\cos\theta\csc\theta\).
