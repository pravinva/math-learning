Exact ratios & quadrants in radians

From Week 1C: exact trig values using radian arguments, and identifying quadrants for angles in terms of \(\pi\).

1. Short lesson

The same special-triangle values apply with radian arguments: replace \(30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ\) by \(\dfrac{\pi}{6},\dfrac{\pi}{4},\dfrac{\pi}{3}\).

Quadrants for \(0< heta<2\pi\): Q1 \(0< heta<\dfrac{\pi}{2}\); Q2 \(\dfrac{\pi}{2}< heta<\pi\); Q3 \(\pi< heta<\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\); Q4 \(\dfrac{3\pi}{2}< heta<2\pi\).

Angles differing by \(2\pi k\) land on the same unit-circle point. Reduce first, then find the quadrant and reference angle.

Worked example. \(\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{6}=\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac12\) (Q2). \(\cos\dfrac{4\pi}{3}=-\dfrac12\) (Q3).

2. Key points summary

  • Memorise \(\sin,\cos,\tan\) of \(\dfrac{\pi}{6},\dfrac{\pi}{4},\dfrac{\pi}{3}\) and axis angles.
  • Reduce by \(\pm 2\pi\) to get into \([0,2\pi)\).
  • Reference angle in radians is still the acute angle to the \(x\)-axis.
  • CAST signs unchanged.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac12,\ \sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ \sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\)
  • \(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\ \cos\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ \cos\dfrac{\pi}{3}=\dfrac12\)
  • \(\tan\dfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3},\ \tan\dfrac{\pi}{4}=1,\ \tan\dfrac{\pi}{3}=\sqrt3\)
  • Q2 ref: \(\pi-\theta\); Q3: \(\theta-\pi\); Q4: \(2\pi-\theta\).
