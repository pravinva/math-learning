1. Short lesson

Exact trig values come from two special triangles: the isosceles right triangle (\(45^\circ\)-\(45^\circ\)-\(90^\circ\)) with sides \(1:1:\sqrt2\), and the half-equilateral triangle (\(30^\circ\)-\(60^\circ\)-\(90^\circ\)) with sides \(1:\sqrt3:2\).

Exam note. The exam expects you to know the exact ratios for \(30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ\) (that is \(\dfrac{\pi}{6},\dfrac{\pi}{4},\dfrac{\pi}{3}\)) straight from these two canonical right triangles — calculators are not accepted for exact-value questions. Learn to redraw both triangles from memory and read the ratios off them.

Isosceles right triangle — \(45^\circ\!-\!45^\circ\!-\!90^\circ\) \(\left(\tfrac{\pi}{4},\tfrac{\pi}{4},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\)

Sides \(1:1:\sqrt2\) — start from a unit square and cut along the diagonal.

Half-equilateral triangle — \(30^\circ\!-\!60^\circ\!-\!90^\circ\) \(\left(\tfrac{\pi}{6},\tfrac{\pi}{3},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\)

Sides \(1:\sqrt3:2\) — start from an equilateral triangle of side \(2\) and cut it in half.

Read every ratio straight off the triangles with SOH-CAH-TOA. For example \(\sin 30^\circ\) is the side opposite \(30^\circ\) over the hypotenuse, \(\dfrac12\); and \(\tan 60^\circ\) is opposite over adjacent, \(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{1}=\sqrt3\).

The same two triangles serve radian questions unchanged — \(30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ\) are just \(\dfrac{\pi}{6},\dfrac{\pi}{4},\dfrac{\pi}{3}\), so \(\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac12\) and \(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\).

These give exact \(\sin,\cos,\tan\) of \(30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ\) without a calculator. For other acute angles, use a calculator in degree mode (or radian mode when the question is in radians) and round as required.

Right-triangle applications: choose the ratio that links the known side/angle to the unknown. Elevation/depression problems are just right triangles standing upright.

Worked example. \(\sin 30^\circ=\dfrac12\), \(\cos 30^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\), \(\tan 30^\circ=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\); \(\sin 45^\circ=\cos 45^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\), \(\tan 45^\circ=1\); \(\sin 60^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\), \(\cos 60^\circ=\dfrac12\), \(\tan 60^\circ=\sqrt3\).

2. Key points summary

  • Exam requirement: know the exact ratios for \(30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ\) (\(\tfrac{\pi}{6},\tfrac{\pi}{4},\tfrac{\pi}{3}\)) from the two canonical triangles, in degrees and radians.
  • \(45^\circ\)-\(45^\circ\)-\(90^\circ\) (\(\tfrac{\pi}{4},\tfrac{\pi}{4},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\)) sides: \(1:1:\sqrt2\) — half a unit square.
  • \(30^\circ\)-\(60^\circ\)-\(90^\circ\) (\(\tfrac{\pi}{6},\tfrac{\pi}{3},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\)) sides: \(1:\sqrt3:2\) (short leg opposite \(30^\circ\)) — half an equilateral triangle of side \(2\).
  • If you can redraw the two triangles, you can rebuild every exact ratio without memorising the table.
  • Check the calculator mode matches the question: degrees for degree problems, radians for radian problems.
  • Elevation: angle up from horizontal; depression: angle down from horizontal.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\sin30^\circ=\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac12,\ \cos30^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\ \tan30^\circ=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\)
  • \(\sin45^\circ=\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ \cos45^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ \tan45^\circ=1\)
  • \(\sin60^\circ=\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\ \cos60^\circ=\dfrac12,\ \tan60^\circ=\sqrt3\)
  • Reciprocals: \(\csc30^\circ=2,\ \sec45^\circ=\sqrt2,\ \cot60^\circ=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\)
  • Degrees to radians: multiply by \(\dfrac{\pi}{180}\); radians to degrees: multiply by \(\dfrac{180}{\pi}\).
  • Unknown side: rearrange SOH-CAH-TOA, e.g. \(\text{opp}=\text{hyp}\sin\theta\).

4. Practice problems (15)

  1. Find the exact value of \(\sin 60^\circ+\cos 30^\circ\).
  2. Find the exact value of \(\tan 45^\circ\cdot\sin 30^\circ\).
  3. Find the exact value of \(\dfrac{\sin 60^\circ}{\cos 60^\circ}\).
  4. Simplify \(\sin^2 45^\circ+\cos^2 45^\circ\).
  5. A right triangle has an acute angle \(30^\circ\) and hypotenuse \(10\). Find the exact opposite and adjacent sides.
  6. A right triangle has an acute angle \(45^\circ\) and adjacent side \(6\). Find the exact hypotenuse and opposite.
  7. Find \(x\) to 2 d.p. if \(\sin 38^\circ=\dfrac{x}{12}\).
  8. Find \(\theta\) to the nearest degree if \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{7}{15}\) and \(\theta\) is acute.
  9. Find \(\theta\) to the nearest minute if \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{9}{20}\) and \(\theta\) is acute.
  10. From a point \(40\) m from a tower base, the angle of elevation to the top is \(28^\circ\). Find the tower height to 1 d.p.
  11. A \(15\) m ladder leans against a wall at \(65^\circ\) to the ground. How far is the base from the wall (2 d.p.)?
  12. Exact value of \(\sec 30^\circ\).
  13. Exact value of \(\csc 45^\circ\).
  14. Exact value of \(\cot 60^\circ\).
  15. A ship is \(1.8\) km from a lighthouse. Angle of elevation to the top is \(14^\circ\). Find the lighthouse height to 2 d.p. (in km).
