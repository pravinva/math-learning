Equations needing identities

From Week 2A: solve trig equations by rewriting with Pythagorean and double-angle-ready identities, then listing all solutions on a stated interval.

1. Short lesson

Replace \(\cos^2\theta\) by \(1-\sin^2\theta\) (or vice versa) to obtain a quadratic in one trig function. Factor or use the quadratic formula, discard impossible roots with absolute value greater than \(1\), then solve on the interval.

Equations such as \(\sin 2\theta=\cos\theta\) can be rewritten with \(\sin 2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta\), then factored. Always consider the cases carefully so you do not lose solutions.

Forms in \(\tan\) and \(\sec\) often use \(1+\tan^2=\sec^2\). Forms with a product \(\sin\theta\cos\theta\) suggest \(\sin 2\theta\).

Finish every question by listing all solutions in the given degree or radian interval, using CAST and reference angles.

Worked example. Solve \(2\cos^2\theta+\cos\theta-1=0\) on \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\): \((2\cos\theta-1)(\cos\theta+1)=0\Rightarrow\cos\theta=\dfrac12\) or \(-1\Rightarrow\theta=60^\circ,300^\circ,180^\circ\).

2. Key points summary

  • Rewrite to one trig function when possible.
  • Reject \(|u|\gt 1\) after substituting \(u=\sin\theta\) or \(\cos\theta\).
  • Factor products; do not cancel trig factors without checking zeros.
  • Use \(\sin 2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta\) when a double angle helps.
  • State every solution in the interval.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\cos^2\theta=1-\sin^2\theta,\ \sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta\)
  • \(1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta\)
  • \(\sin 2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta,\ \cos 2\theta=\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta=2\cos^2\theta-1=1-2\sin^2\theta\)
  • Quadratic in \(u=\sin\theta\) or \(\cos\theta\), then CAST.

4. Practice problems (15)

  1. Solve \(2\sin^2\theta+\sin\theta-1=0\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\).
  2. Solve \(2\cos^2\theta-3\cos\theta+1=0\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\).
  3. Solve \(\sin^2\theta+\sin\theta=0\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  4. Solve \(4\cos^2\theta-1=0\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\).
  5. Solve \(\tan^2\theta-\tan\theta=0\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\), \(\theta\neq 90^\circ,270^\circ\).
  6. Solve \(1+\tan^2\theta=2\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  7. Solve \(2\sin\theta\cos\theta=\cos\theta\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\).
  8. Solve \(\sin 2\theta=\sin\theta\) for \(0\le\theta\le 2\pi\).
  9. Solve \(\cos 2\theta=\cos\theta\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\) (use a double-angle expansion).
  10. Solve \(2\cos^2\theta+\sin\theta-1=0\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\) (rewrite \(\cos^2\)).
  11. Solve \(\tan^2\theta+\tan\theta-2=0\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\).
  12. Solve \(2\sin^2\theta=\sin 2\theta\) for \(0\le\theta\le\pi\).
  13. Solve \(\cos^2\theta=\dfrac34\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\).
  14. Solve \(\sin 2\theta=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le180^\circ\).
  15. Solve \(\cos 2\theta+3\sin\theta-2=0\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le360^\circ\) (expand \(\cos 2\theta\) in terms of \(\sin\theta\)).
