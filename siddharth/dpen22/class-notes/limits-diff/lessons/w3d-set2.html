Derivative of \(e^x\)

From Week 3D: the exponential function differentiates to itself: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x\).

1. Short lesson

The derivative of \(e^x\) is \(e^x\). This is the defining property of the natural exponential.

For \(y=ke^{ax}\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=ka e^{ax}\) (chain rule with constant multiple).

The graph of \(e^x\) is always increasing; slope at any point equals the \(y\)-value there.

Worked example. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(3e^{2x})=6e^{2x}\).

2. Key points summary

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{ax}=a e^{ax}\)
  • Exponential growth: derivative equals height.
  • Chain rule for inner linear \(ax+b\).
  • \(e^0=1\).

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{ax}=a e^{ax}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{ax+b}=a e^{ax+b}\)
