Product rule

From Week 3B: differentiate products \(f(x)g(x)\) using \((fg)^{\prime}=f^{\prime}g+fg^{\prime}\).

1. Short lesson

The product rule: if \(y=uv\) then \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=u\dfrac{dv}{dx}+v\dfrac{du}{dx}\), or \((fg)^{\prime}=f^{\prime}g+fg^{\prime}\).

Identify factors clearly. Differentiate each factor, then add the two product terms.

Do not differentiate factor-by-factor separately — the product rule has two terms.

Worked example. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[x^2\sin x]=2x\sin x+x^2\cos x\).

2. Key points summary

  • \((fg)^{\prime}=f^{\prime}g+fg^{\prime}\)
  • Label \(u\) and \(v\) (or \(f\) and \(g\)).
  • Differentiate each factor once.
  • Expand only if needed.
  • Check degree/terms make sense.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \((fg)^{\prime}=f^{\prime}g+fg^{\prime}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[u\cdot v]=u v^{\prime}+v u^{\prime}\)
  • Constants factor through product rule
