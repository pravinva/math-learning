Limits requiring rationalisation

From Week 2D: multi-step \(\frac00\) limits using rationalisation, cubic factorisation, and algebraic rewrite.

1. Short lesson

Substitution that yields \(\dfrac00\) needs algebra: multiply by a conjugate, factor a cubic, or rewrite radicals.

For expressions with square roots, multiply top and bottom by the conjugate so factors of \((x-a)\) appear.

For \(x^3-a^3=(x-a)(x^2+ax+a^2)\). Cancel the common factor, then substitute.

Worked example. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 4}\dfrac{\sqrt{x}-2}{x-4}\): multiply by \(\sqrt{x}+2\) to get \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}+2}\to\dfrac14\).

2. Key points summary

  • \(\frac00\): rewrite before substituting.
  • Conjugate for nested radicals.
  • Factor \(x^3-a^3\) when useful.
  • Cancel only common factors.
  • Finish with direct substitution.

3. Key limit results

  • \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a}\dfrac{\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{a}}{x-a}=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{a}}\)
  • \(x^3-a^3=(x-a)(x^2+ax+a^2)\)
  • Conjugate: \((\sqrt{u}-\sqrt{v})(\sqrt{u}+\sqrt{v})=u-v\)
