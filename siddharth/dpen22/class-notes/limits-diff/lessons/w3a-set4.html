Awkward powers, roots and negative exponents

From Week 3A: differentiate fractional and negative powers using the power rule after rewriting.

1. Short lesson

Rewrite roots and reciprocals as powers: \(\sqrt[n]{x}=x^{1/n}\), \(\dfrac{1}{x^k}=x^{-k}\).

Then apply \(\dfrac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}\) and constant/sum rules.

Simplify the answer to a neat radical or fraction form when requested.

Worked example. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}x^{-3}=-3x^{-4}=-\dfrac{3}{x^4}\); \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sqrt[3]{x}=\dfrac{1}{3}x^{-2/3}\).

2. Key points summary

  • Rewrite before differentiating.
  • Power rule for all real \(n\) used here.
  • Simplify negatives and fractions.
  • Constant multiples still apply.
  • Check by rewriting back.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sqrt{x}=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}x^{-1}=-x^{-2}\)
