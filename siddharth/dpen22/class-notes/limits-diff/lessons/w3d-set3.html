Derivative of \(e^{f(x)}\)

From Week 3D: chain rule for exponentials — \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{f(x)}=f^{\prime}(x)e^{f(x)}\).

1. Short lesson

For any differentiable \(f(x)\), \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{f(x)}=f^{\prime}(x)\,e^{f(x)}\).

Identify the exponent as \(f(x)\), differentiate it, multiply by \(e^{f(x)}\).

Combines chain rule with \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^u=e^u\dfrac{du}{dx}\).

Worked example. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{x^2}=2x e^{x^2}\).

2. Key points summary

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{f(x)}=f^{\prime}(x)e^{f(x)}\)
  • Differentiate the exponent.
  • Multiply by original exponential.
  • Works for any inner function.
  • Common in growth/decay models.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{f(x)}=f^{\prime}(x)e^{f(x)}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{x^2}=2x e^{x^2}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{\sin x}=\cos x\, e^{\sin x}\)
