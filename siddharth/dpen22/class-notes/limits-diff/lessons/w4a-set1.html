Derivatives of \(\ln x\) and \(\ln(f(x))\)

From Week 4A: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x=\dfrac1x\) and chain rule gives \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(f(x))=\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}\).

1. Short lesson

\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x=\dfrac{1}{x}\) for \(x>0\).

By the chain rule, \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(f(x))=\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}\) (logarithmic differentiation idea).

Domain: argument of \(\ln\) must be positive.

Worked example. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(3x^2+1)=\dfrac{6x}{3x^2+1}\).

2. Key points summary

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x=\dfrac1x\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(f)=\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{f}\)
  • Argument of log must be \(>0\).
  • Simplify logs using laws when helpful.
  • Chain rule on the inside.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x=\dfrac{1}{x}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(ax)=\dfrac{1}{x}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(f(x))=\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}\)
