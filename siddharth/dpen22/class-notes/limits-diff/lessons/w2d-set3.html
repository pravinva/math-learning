Infinite limits & limits at infinity

From Week 2D: vertical asymptotes (\(\pm\infty\)) and end behaviour as \(x\to\pm\infty\).

1. Short lesson

\(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a^+} f(x)=+\infty\) means \(f(x)\) grows without bound as \(x\) approaches \(a\) from the right. Similar definitions for \(a^-\) and \(-\infty\).

For rational functions at infinity, divide numerator and denominator by the highest power of \(x\). Dominant terms decide the limit.

A vertical asymptote at \(x=a\) occurs when the limit from one side is \(\pm\infty\). Horizontal asymptotes describe \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\pm\infty} f(x)\).

Worked example. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^2+1}{2x^2-x}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3+1/x^2}{2-1/x}=\dfrac32\).

2. Key points summary

  • Compare highest powers for limits at \(\infty\).
  • Divide top and bottom by \(x^n\) where \(n\) is the largest power.
  • \(+\infty\) and \(-\infty\) describe unbounded growth.
  • One-sided limits matter near vertical asymptotes.
  • Horizontal asymptote \(y=L\) if \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=L\).

3. Key limit results

  • If degree(top) \(<\) degree(bottom): \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}=0\)
  • If degrees equal: limit \(=\) ratio of leading coefficients
  • If degree(top) \(>\) degree(bottom): limit \(=\pm\infty\)
  • \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a^+}\frac{1}{x-a}=+\infty\) (when \(a\) finite)
