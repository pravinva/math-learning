Trig derivatives with chain and product

From Week 4A: exam-style trig composites using chain and product (no inverse trig).

1. Short lesson

Chain on angles: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin(u)=\cos(u)\,u^{\prime}\), similarly for \(\cos\) and \(\tan\).

Products like \(x\sin(2x)\) need both product and chain.

Powers of trig functions are compositions: \(\sin^n x=(\sin x)^n\).

Worked example. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin^3(2x)=3\sin^2(2x)\cos(2x)\cdot 2\).

2. Key points summary

  • Chain on the angle.
  • Product on separate factors.
  • Power rule for \(\sin^n\).
  • \(\tan^{\prime}=\sec^2\).
  • Radians.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin(u)=\cos(u)u^{\prime}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos(u)=-\sin(u)u^{\prime}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(\sin u)^n=n(\sin u)^{n-1}\cos(u)u^{\prime}\)
