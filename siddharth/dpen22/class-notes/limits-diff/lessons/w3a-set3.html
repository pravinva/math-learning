Gradient at a point & simple applications

1. Short lesson

After finding \(f^{\prime}(x)\), substitute \(x=a\) to get the tangent slope at that point.

If \(s(t)\) is displacement, then \(v(t)=s^{\prime}(t)\) is velocity and \(a(t)=v^{\prime}(t)=s^{\prime\prime}(t)\) is acceleration.

Positive derivative \(\Rightarrow\) function increasing; negative \(\Rightarrow\) decreasing (locally).

Worked example. For \(s(t)=t^2\), velocity at \(t=3\) is \(s^{\prime}(3)=6\) m/s.

2. Key points summary

  • Evaluate \(f^{\prime}(a)\) after differentiating.
  • Velocity = derivative of displacement.
  • Sign of \(f^{\prime}\) shows increase/decrease.
  • Tangent slope \(=f^{\prime}(a)\).
  • Units follow from context.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(v(t)=s^{\prime}(t)\)
  • \(a(t)=v^{\prime}(t)=s^{\prime\prime}(t)\)
  • Tangent slope at \(x=a\): \(m=f^{\prime}(a)\)
