Tangents and normals to exponential curves

From Week 3D: multi-step tangent and normal equations for \(e^{ax}\) and related exponentials.

1. Short lesson

Tangent: find point \((a,f(a))\) and slope \(m=f^{\prime}(a)\), then \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\).

Normal is perpendicular: slope \(-\dfrac{1}{m}\) when \(m\neq 0\).

For \(y=e^{kx}\), \(y^{\prime}=ke^{kx}\), so slope equals \(k\) times the \(y\)-value.

Worked example. For \(y=e^{2x}\) at \(x=0\): point \((0,1)\), \(m=2\), tangent \(y=2x+1\).

2. Key points summary

  • Slope from exponential derivative.
  • Point–gradient form.
  • Normal slope \(-1/m\).
  • \(e^0=1\).
  • Simplify carefully.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{ax}=a e^{ax}\)
  • \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\)
  • Normal: \(m_n=-1/m_t\)
