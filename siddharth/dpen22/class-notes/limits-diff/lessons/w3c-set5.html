Quotient and chain on trig & polynomials

From Week 3C: exam-style practice combining quotient and chain rules on trig and polynomial composites.

1. Short lesson

Trig compositions need chain on the angle: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin(u)=\cos(u)\,u^{\prime}\).

Quotients of trig functions use \(\dfrac{u^{\prime}v-uv^{\prime}}{v^2}\) with trig derivatives.

Polynomial composites mix power chain with quotient as needed.

Worked example. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\dfrac{\sin(2x)}{x}=\dfrac{2x\cos(2x)-\sin(2x)}{x^2}\).

2. Key points summary

  • Chain on trig arguments.
  • Quotient formula carefully.
  • Product as alternative when helpful.
  • Simplify with identities only if neat.
  • Radians.

3. Formulas & identities

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin(u)=\cos(u)u^{\prime}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos(u)=-\sin(u)u^{\prime}\)
  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\tan(u)=\sec^2(u)u^{\prime}\)
