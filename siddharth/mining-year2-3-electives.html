MAJ40194 · BE(Hons) Mining Engineering

Years 2–4 by term, then the electives

Every core from second year onwards, Autumn vs Spring. Year 2 is already full. The useful electives sit in Year 3 (typically two) and finish in Year 4 as List A / List B.

This is the published mining major structure (handbook years 2018–2020), which is still the shape of MAJ40194. Sessions for a few MINE subjects have moved between years. Confirm SOLS before enrolling. MINE234 has been discontinued.

Year 2 · 48 cp

Four subjects each session. Do not add a fifth in Autumn.

Autumn 24 cp

  1. ENGG251 Mechanics of Solids
  2. ENGG252 Engineering Fluid Mechanics
  3. MATH283 Advanced Engineering Mathematics and Statistics
  4. MINE211 Surface Mining Methods

Spring 24 cp

  1. MINE220 Underground Mining Methods
  2. CIVL272 Surveying
  3. EESC252 Geology for Engineers I
  4. MINE234 Computer Applications in Mining Engineering — discontinued. Ask EIS what replaces this 6 cp (often a List A elective such as CIVL201).

Year 3 · 48 cp

Six mining/civil cores plus two electives. The clean 24/24 split is the 2018 handbook. Later years sometimes park MINE324 or MINE333 in Autumn, which overloads that session — if that happens, move an elective to Spring.

Autumn 18 cp cores + 1 elective

  1. CIVL361 Geomechanics
  2. MINE312 Mine Ventilation
  3. MINE322 Foundations of Mine Planning
  4. Elective 1 (List A preferred — CIVL462 is Autumn)

Spring 18 cp cores + 1 elective

  1. MINE323 Mining Geomechanics
  2. MINE324 Mineral Processing
  3. MINE333 Resource Estimation
  4. Elective 2 (CIVL201 is Spring)

Session drift to check: 2019 listed MINE324 as Autumn and MINE333 as not available. 2020 listed MINE322 / MINE324 as not available and MINE333 as Autumn. Never take five 6 cp subjects in one session.

Year 4 · 48 cp + 0 cp placement

Four professional cores, a thesis, remaining electives, and 12 weeks of industry experience. Thesis A (ENGG452, 12 cp) is the usual path. Scholars take Thesis B (ENGG453, 18 cp) and one fewer elective.

Autumn

  1. ENGG461 Managing Engineering Projects
  2. MINE416 Mine Valuation
  3. MINE411 Health & Safety in Mines (Autumn in 2018; Spring in 2019)
  4. ENGG452 / ENGG453 thesis (annual — this session’s share)
  5. Remaining List A / B electives as the load allows

Spring

  1. MINE422 Mine Planning and Development
  2. MINE411 if it is running in Spring that year
  3. Thesis (remainder)
  4. Remaining electives

ENGG454 Professional Experience is 0 cp, Annual / Autumn / Spring. It is the 12-week placement. It does not replace a taught subject.

Elective count across Years 3–4: with ENGG452, four electives in total (two must be List A). With ENGG453, three electives (two must be List A). Year 3 already takes two of them.

MATH201 vs MATH283

MATH283 is the Year 2 Autumn core. It is half engineering calculus (partials, double integrals, Laplace, Fourier, special functions) and half statistics. MATH201 Multivariate and Vector Calculus is a List B maths-major subject: gradients, triple integrals, line and surface integrals, Green / Stokes / divergence. They are not equivalent. MATH201 is useful if you want that maths; it is not useful as a mining elective. STAT201 is closer to MINE333 if you want extra maths. Do not take MATH201 in Year 2 Autumn — that session is already four cores.

Which electives are worth taking

Take CIVL462 Principles of Foundation Engineering as the default technical elective. Pair it with CIVL201 Computer Modelling if you want numerical mechanics, or CHEM103 if chemistry is a gap. Do not take CSCI291 just to “learn programming”: at UOW it is C, and mining work does not use C or C++.

Do not add an elective in Year 2 Autumn. That session is already ENGG251, ENGG252, MATH283 and MINE211. CSCI291 and CHEM103 are also Autumn — they would make a five-subject load. Park them in Year 3 or Year 4.

Three packages

First pick · geotech

CIVL462 + CIVL201

Foundation engineering is bearing capacity, settlement and piles — the same stress/strain language as ENGG251 and CIVL361. Computer modelling is the numerical side of that mechanics. Best pair if you want underground or geotechnical work.

Process · environment

CHEM103 + ENVE220 or ENVE410

Chemistry feeds mineral processing. Water quality or site remediation feeds mine water and closure. Take CHEM103 if HSC Chemistry was weak or skipped. Light on maths; strong for processing and environmental paths.

Maps · data

EESC105 + Excel/Python on your own

GIS on top of surveying is useful. For actual scripting, learn Python or VBA, not C. CSCI291 is the wrong language for this degree. MINE234 (mine software) has been discontinued — that was the subject that used to cover computer applications in mining.

If you only pick two in Year 3

Default

CIVL462 and CIVL201. Mechanics plus numerical modelling — both reuse ENGG251.

If chemistry is a gap

Swap CIVL201 for CHEM103.

If MINE313 is actually running

Take it. Mine power and transport is rarer than another civil elective, and it uses fluid and solid mechanics on real plant and haulage. It has been marked “not available” in some past years, so check the timetable.

MATH201 and STAT201 are List B options, not mining cores. They stretch maths; they do not replace CIVL462 or CIVL201.

Skip unless you have a reason

  • Generic management (ENGG440) can wait until Year 4 next to ENGG461.
  • Language electives, astronomy and photography do not strengthen mining.
  • Ocean engineering (ENGG477) only if offered and you want that flavour.
  • Do not take MATH151, COMM121 or ECON222 — they are blocked for BE(Hons).
  • CSCI291 is a List A filler, not a mining skill. If the handbook forces a programming elective, check whether CIVL201 or a Python-based option is allowed instead.

Mining cores are calculation-heavy in places (ventilation networks, Mohr–Coulomb, geostats) but they are not new MATH codes. They reuse ENGG251, ENGG252 and MATH283.
