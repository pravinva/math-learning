Portfolio roadmap · Mining Engineering, UOW

Standing out beyond the transcript

A working plan for the next three sessions of study — the rest of Year 2 through to graduation — built around one project that deepens every year instead of a scattershot list of activities. Coursework and grades stay the baseline; this is what sits alongside them.

The spine of the plan: a single Databricks-based data pipeline, starting as a one-pump simulator this session and maturing into your Year 4 Honours thesis. Everything below — scholarships, memberships, competitions, placements — is scheduled around that thread so each year's work builds on the last instead of starting over.
Now · Year 2, Spring

Build the MVP

One simulated pump, a Bronze/Silver pipeline, and your first faculty conversation. Low effort, high proof-of-concept.

Year 3

Scale & certify

Multi-asset network, a formal Databricks credential, a vacation placement application, first competition.

Year 4

Convert & present

Fold the project into your Honours thesis, consolidate the portfolio, and present it to industry contacts.

Phase 01
Now

Rest of Year 2

Spring Session · MINE220, CIVL272, EESC252, CHEM103

Technical build

  • Simulate one mine-site dewatering pump (flow, discharge pressure, motor temperature) and land the output as JSON in a local folder. This is the whole project scaled down to one asset — the point is a working pipeline, not a big one.
  • Ingest with Databricks Auto Loader into a Bronze Delta table, then write one Silver-layer rule that flags anomalies using the Bernoulli relationship you're covering in ENGG252. This is the line that turns it from "a script" into "a mining engineer's pipeline" — the validation logic comes from the fluid mechanics, not from arbitrary thresholds.
  • Write a one-page note explaining the pressure-drop logic in plain terms, ready to show a lecturer.

Credentials & network

  • Join the AusIMM Illawarra Student Chapter — UOW's own local chapter, with events like their annual Student Meets Industry Night. This is the single easiest, lowest-effort membership to pick up this session, and it puts you in the same room as final-year students and local industry contacts.
  • Check eligibility and the current application window for the Yancoal Mining Engineering Scholarship and browse UOW's full scholarships and prizes list for the faculty. Criteria and cycles change year to year — verify directly before you invest time in an application.
  • Book one short, well-prepared conversation with a staff member connected to mine automation, ventilation, or asset-management research — UOW's Centre for Infrastructure and Mining Safety (CIPMS) is a reasonable place to start looking for the right fit. Bring the working pipeline, not just an idea. Ask for a reaction to the approach, not a commitment.

Visibility

  • Open a GitHub repo for the pipeline from day one, even before it's polished. Commit as you go.
  • Set up a LinkedIn profile if you don't have one, with the project listed as "in progress."
Phase 02
Year 3

Year 3

Autumn: CIVL361, MINE312, MINE322 · Spring: MINE323, MINE324, MINE411

Technical build

  • Scale the simulator from one pump to a small network — a dewatering system or a main ventilation fan array — timed to line up with MINE312 Mine Ventilation. The subject matter should track what you're actually studying that session; it keeps the domain logic credible and gives you a natural person to ask when you're unsure of a threshold.
  • Build out the full Bronze → Silver → Gold architecture and add one MLflow model that gives an early warning ahead of a simulated failure, not just a same-instant flag.

Credentials & network

  • Work toward the Databricks Certified Data Engineer Associate credential. Self-paced and free to study for — it turns "I built a pipeline" into a credential a recruiter or professor can verify without reading your code.
  • Return to the faculty contact from Year 2 with the v2 pipeline. Ask whether an independent-study or special-topic elective could formally credit this work. Treat this as a genuine question to the Program Director, not a guaranteed pathway — confirm what's actually on offer before you plan around it.
  • Start scoping your ENGG354 Professional Experience placement early. Vacation programs at majors like BHP typically open roughly a year ahead of the placement itself — track application windows now rather than in the term you need the placement.

Competitions & visibility

  • Enter the AusIMM Mining Games through your student chapter — a hands-on, traditional-skills competition against other university teams. Deliberately different from the data-pipeline work: it builds a team, a leadership line, and a very different resume story than "I code."
  • Write a short public case-study post (LinkedIn or a blog on your GitHub Pages) walking through the v2 architecture and what the fluid-mechanics-based anomaly detection actually caught.
Phase 03
Year 4

Year 4

Honours Thesis (ENGG452/453) · final electives

Technical build

  • Propose your own Honours thesis title built on the matured pipeline — e.g. a real-time asset-health framework for a specific class of underground equipment — anchored in one concrete mining-physics problem rather than "digital twin" in the abstract.
  • Where possible, replace or supplement your simulator with real or high-fidelity data from your placement industry partner, with their sign-off.

Consolidation & presentation

  • Clean up the GitHub repo into a single coherent history with a proper README and an architecture diagram — this is the artifact a hiring manager actually opens.
  • Offer to present the finished pipeline at an Illawarra Student Chapter event, in the same format as their industry nights — a short, concrete demo lands better than a slide deck of ambitions.
  • Write the final case study with numbers: what the early-warning model would have saved in downtime or maintenance cost, even on simulated data.

Applications

  • Go into graduate program applications with the completed placement, the Databricks credential, the thesis, and a public repo all pointing at the same body of work — not four disconnected lines on a CV.
