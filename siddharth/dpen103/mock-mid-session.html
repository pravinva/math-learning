Section A — Multiple choice

8 questions · 8 points · about 12 minutes
A1 · Bonding1 point

Which feature best explains why a metal usually conducts electricity and can deform without immediately shattering?

Show answer and reasoning
C

Metallic bonding leaves outer electrons free to carry charge. Metals also have non-directional bonding and available slip systems, so dislocations can move and the material can deform plastically.

A2 · Crystal packing1 point

Which statement about face-centred cubic (FCC) packing is correct?

Show answer and reasoning
B

FCC has 4 atoms per unit cell, 12 nearest neighbours and an atomic packing factor of 0.74. Its ductility comes from its many easy slip systems, not from loose packing.

A3 · Read the curve1 point

On the schematic stress–strain curve, what does the slope of segment O–Y represent?

O = origin, Y = yield, U = ultimate tensile strength, F = fracture.
Show answer and reasoning
A

In the linear elastic region, \(\sigma=E\varepsilon\), so the slope \(\sigma/\varepsilon\) is Young's modulus. Toughness is the entire area under the curve.

A4 · Design limit1 point

A bracket must return to its original shape after the service load is removed. Which value is the most direct design limit?

Show answer and reasoning
D

Yield marks the start of permanent plastic deformation. UTS is the peak stress, by which point the part has already yielded badly.

A5 · Hall–Petch1 point

The average grain diameter is reduced from \(40\,\mu\text{m}\) to \(10\,\mu\text{m}\). What happens to the Hall–Petch grain-size contribution \(k_y d^{-1/2}\)?

Show answer and reasoning
B

The grains are four times smaller. Since the contribution varies as \(1/\sqrt d\), it grows by \(\sqrt4=2\). The total yield strength does not necessarily double because \(\sigma_0\) is unchanged.

A6 · Fatigue1 point

Why can a shaft fail after many cycles even when its nominal stress remains below yield?

Show answer and reasoning
C

Fatigue is crack initiation and growth under repeated loading. Once the crack is large enough that \(K_I\) reaches \(K_{IC}\), final fracture is sudden even though nominal stress stayed below yield.

A7 · Creep1 point

Which part of a standard creep curve is normally used to estimate long-term service deformation?

Show answer and reasoning
B

Secondary creep is long and approximately linear, so its steady slope can be used to predict strain over service time. Tertiary creep means failure has already begun.

A8 · Thermal response1 point

A uniform steel bar is heated by \(100^\circ\text{C}\) and is completely free to slide at both ends. Which statement is best?

Show answer and reasoning
A

Temperature change produces thermal strain. Thermal stress appears only when that strain is restrained. Free bar: expansion, almost no stress.

Section B — True or false

4 questions · 4 points · about 8 minutes
B1 · Stiffness and strength1 point

Statement: A stronger grade of steel always has a higher Young's modulus than a weaker grade of steel.

Show answer and reasoning
False

Strengthening obstructs dislocations and raises yield strength. Young's modulus comes from bond stiffness and stays near 200–210 GPa across ordinary steel grades.

B2 · Vacancy equation1 point

Statement: Temperature in \(n_v=N\exp(-Q_v/kT)\) may be entered in degrees Celsius as long as \(Q_v\) and \(k\) use consistent energy units.

Show answer and reasoning
False

The exponential requires absolute temperature in kelvin. A Celsius value changes the ratio \(Q_v/kT\) completely.

B3 · Crack scaling1 point

Statement: If crack length becomes four times larger while \(K_{IC}\) and geometry remain fixed, allowable stress becomes half as large.

Show answer and reasoning
True

\(\sigma_{\text{allow}}\propto1/\sqrt a\), so multiplying \(a\) by 4 multiplies allowable stress by \(1/\sqrt4=1/2\).

B4 · Cold work1 point

Statement: Cold work raises yield strength and ductility at the same time because it creates more dislocations.

Show answer and reasoning
False

More dislocations tangle and block one another, raising strength but reducing ductility. Annealing restores ductility by recovery and recrystallisation.

Section C — Short calculation and explanation

4 questions · 12 points · about 40 minutes
C1 · Unit-cell density3 points

A body-centred cubic metal has atomic mass \(A=55.85\text{ g/mol}\) and lattice parameter \(a=0.286\text{ nm}\).

  1. Calculate its theoretical density in \(\text{g/cm}^3\).
  2. State one check that tells you whether the answer is physically believable.

Use \(N_A=6.022\times10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}\).

Show worked solution and marking guide
\(\rho\approx7.9\text{ g/cm}^3\)
  1. BCC has \(n=2\) atoms per cell.
  2. \(a=0.286\text{ nm}=0.286\times10^{-7}\text{ cm}\), so \(V_c=a^3=2.34\times10^{-23}\text{ cm}^3\).
  3. \(\rho=\dfrac{nA}{V_cN_A}=\dfrac{2(55.85)}{(2.34\times10^{-23})(6.022\times10^{23})}\approx7.9\text{ g/cm}^3\).
  4. This is in the expected iron/steel range. A result near 0.01 or \(10^{20}\) would signal a length conversion error.
Self-mark: 1 point for \(n=2\) and correct nm→cm conversion; 1 for correct density; 1 for a meaningful physical check.
C2 · Stress, strain and decision3 points

A tensile specimen has original area \(A_0=100\text{ mm}^2\) and gauge length \(L_0=50\text{ mm}\). Under a load of \(32\text{ kN}\), its length is \(50.10\text{ mm}\). The material has \(E=200\text{ GPa}\) and \(\sigma_y=280\text{ MPa}\).

  1. Calculate engineering stress and strain.
  2. Compare the measured strain with the elastic strain predicted by Hooke's law.
  3. State whether permanent deformation is likely.
Show worked solution and marking guide
\(\sigma=320\text{ MPa}\), \(\varepsilon=0.0020\); yielding is likely
  1. \(\sigma=F/A_0=32{,}000/100=320\text{ N/mm}^2=320\text{ MPa}\).
  2. \(\varepsilon=(50.10-50)/50=0.0020\).
  3. If response were elastic, \(\varepsilon_{\text{elastic}}=\sigma/E=320\text{ MPa}/200{,}000\text{ MPa}=0.0016\).
  4. The applied stress also exceeds yield: \(320>280\text{ MPa}\). Plastic strain is therefore likely, so the specimen will not fully return to its original length.
Self-mark: 1 point for stress and strain; 1 for the Hooke's-law comparison; 1 for the correct yielding conclusion with a reason.
C3 · Crack growth3 points

A plate contains a surface crack. Initially \(a=2\text{ mm}\), \(K_{IC}=40\text{ MPa}\sqrt{\text{m}}\), and \(Y=1.1\).

  1. Calculate the allowable stress using \(\sigma_{\text{allow}}=K_{IC}/(Y\sqrt{\pi a})\).
  2. The crack grows to \(8\text{ mm}\). Find the new allowable stress without repeating the full calculation.
  3. Name one practical design action and explain why it helps.
Show worked solution and marking guide
About \(459\text{ MPa}\), then \(230\text{ MPa}\)
  1. \(a=0.002\text{ m}\). \(\sigma_{\text{allow}}=40/[1.1\sqrt{\pi(0.002)}]\approx459\text{ MPa}\).
  2. The crack becomes four times longer, so allowable stress scales by \(1/\sqrt4=1/2\): about \(230\text{ MPa}\).
  3. Acceptable actions include reducing service load, inspecting often enough to catch the crack before 8 mm, removing the crack and blending the surface, or redesigning with lower stress concentration. Each acts on \(\sigma\), \(a\), or local geometry.
Self-mark: 1 point for the first result with metres; 1 for the square-root scaling; 1 for an action tied to the fracture mechanism.
C4 · Constrained heating3 points

A steel rail is fully restrained at both ends. It is heated by \(100^\circ\text{C}\). Take \(E=210\text{ GPa}\), \(\alpha=12\times10^{-6}/^\circ\text{C}\), and yield strength \(250\text{ MPa}\).

  1. Estimate the thermal stress.
  2. Compare it with yield.
  3. Explain how one geometry change could prevent this problem.
Show worked solution and marking guide
\(\sigma_{\text{thermal}}\approx252\text{ MPa}\), slightly above yield
  1. Fully constrained thermal stress: \(\sigma\approx E\alpha\Delta T\).
  2. \(\sigma=(210\times10^9)(12\times10^{-6})(100)=252\times10^6\text{ Pa}=252\text{ MPa}\).
  3. This slightly exceeds the stated \(250\text{ MPa}\) yield strength, so permanent deformation or buckling is plausible.
  4. Provide an expansion gap, sliding support or expansion joint. It allows the thermal strain to occur instead of converting it into stress.
Self-mark: 1 point for the stress; 1 for comparing with yield; 1 for a geometry change explained through released restraint.
