Section A — Multiple choice

8 questions · 8 points · about 12 minutes
A11 point

A horizontal tie-line is drawn through an \(\alpha+\beta\) region of a binary phase diagram. What do the two endpoints give directly?

Show answer and reasoning
B

The endpoints give \(C_\alpha\) and \(C_\beta\), the phase compositions. The relative segment lengths are then used in the lever rule to calculate phase fractions.

A21 point

A polymer operates at \(25^\circ\text{C}\), while its glass transition temperature is \(80^\circ\text{C}\). Its most likely behaviour is:

Show answer and reasoning
C

Service temperature is below \(T_g\), so segmental chain motion is frozen and the polymer is glassy. \(T_g\) is not melting; the material remains solid on both sides.

A31 point

Doping doubles carrier concentration \(n\), but the added impurity scattering halves carrier mobility \(\mu\). What happens to conductivity?

Show answer and reasoning
D

\(\sigma=nq\mu\). Doubling \(n\) and halving \(\mu\) leaves the product \(n\mu\) unchanged.

A41 point

Which magnetic material is best for a transformer core operating on alternating current?

Show answer and reasoning
A

The core reverses magnetisation each cycle. A thin loop has a small area and therefore low hysteresis energy loss per cycle; low coercivity makes reversal easy.

A51 point

A continuous unidirectional composite is loaded parallel to its fibres. Which relation predicts its elastic modulus?

Show answer and reasoning
B

Along the fibres, fibre and matrix experience the same strain, so their stiffness contributions add as a volume-weighted average. The reciprocal relation is the transverse case.

A61 point

Why do nominally identical ceramic specimens often show a wide spread in measured tensile strength?

Show answer and reasoning
C

Ceramic strength follows approximately \(\sigma_f=K_{IC}/(Y\sqrt{\pi a})\). The worst flaw \(a\) differs from specimen to specimen, so measured strength scatters even when chemistry is identical.

A71 point

A design must be both stiff and light. Which comparison is more useful than Young's modulus alone?

Show answer and reasoning
D

Specific stiffness measures elastic stiffness per unit density. Steel is roughly three times stiffer than aluminium but also roughly three times denser, so \(E/\rho\) reveals that they are similar by weight.

A81 point

Why can a fully cured thermoset not be reshaped by melting?

Show answer and reasoning
B

Cross-links prevent chains sliding independently. Further heating breaks chemical bonds and degrades the network rather than producing a flowable melt.

Section B — True or false

4 questions · 4 points · about 8 minutes
B11 point

Statement: If the overall composition \(C_0\) lies very close to \(C_\alpha\) on a tie-line, the alloy contains mostly \(\beta\).

Show answer and reasoning
False

It contains mostly \(\alpha\). The lever rule uses the opposite segment, so a point near \(C_\alpha\) gives a long opposite arm and therefore a large \(W_\alpha\).

B21 point

Statement: Heating a metal usually raises its resistivity because stronger atomic vibration lowers carrier mobility.

Show answer and reasoning
True

Carrier concentration in a metal is nearly fixed. Heating increases scattering and lowers \(\mu\), so conductivity falls and resistivity rises.

B31 point

Statement: A composite with a high longitudinal modulus will have approximately the same modulus when loaded across its fibres.

Show answer and reasoning
False

Composites are anisotropic. Along the fibres, stiff fibres dominate; across them, the reciprocal rule lets the soft matrix dominate, and modulus can be lower by a factor of twenty.

B41 point

Statement: A compressive surface layer can improve ceramic reliability because applied tension must first cancel that compression before a surface crack opens.

Show answer and reasoning
True

This is how toughened glass works. Cracks open under tension, so a compressed surface delays the tensile crack-driving state.

Section C — Short calculation and explanation

4 questions · 12 points · about 40 minutes
C1 · Read a phase diagram3 points

The point shown is an alloy with overall composition \(C_0=40\text{ wt\% B}\) at \(700^\circ\text{C}\). The tie-line intersects the \(\alpha\) boundary at \(C_\alpha=10\text{ wt\% B}\) and the \(\beta\) boundary at \(C_\beta=70\text{ wt\% B}\).

  1. Name the phases present.
  2. State the composition of each phase.
  3. Calculate the mass fraction of each phase and perform one sanity check.
Show worked solution and marking guide
50% \(\alpha\), 50% \(\beta\)
  1. The point lies in the \(\alpha+\beta\) region, so both phases are present.
  2. The tie-line endpoints give \(C_\alpha=10\text{ wt\% B}\) and \(C_\beta=70\text{ wt\% B}\).
  3. \(W_\alpha=(C_\beta-C_0)/(C_\beta-C_\alpha)=(70-40)/(70-10)=0.50\).
  4. \(W_\beta=(C_0-C_\alpha)/(C_\beta-C_\alpha)=(40-10)/60=0.50\).
  5. The fractions add to 1, and equal amounts are sensible because \(C_0\) is exactly midway between the endpoints.
Self-mark: 1 point for phases and their compositions; 1 for both fractions; 1 for a valid sanity check.
C2 · Polymer service decision3 points

A snap-fit clip must remain tough from \(-30^\circ\text{C}\) to \(40^\circ\text{C}\). Candidate P has \(T_g=80^\circ\text{C}\); candidate Q has \(T_g=-50^\circ\text{C}\). Both have adequate short-term yield strength at \(25^\circ\text{C}\).

  1. Choose the more suitable candidate and explain using chain mobility.
  2. Name one long-term property that still needs checking.
  3. Explain why room-temperature yield strength alone was not enough.
Show worked solution and marking guide
Choose Q, subject to creep verification
  1. Q remains above its \(T_g=-50^\circ\text{C}\) throughout the \(-30\) to \(40^\circ\text{C}\) range, so its chains retain segmental mobility and it remains relatively tough.
  2. P is below \(T_g=80^\circ\text{C}\) throughout service, so it is glassy and comparatively brittle. A snap-fit could crack during assembly or cold impact.
  3. Check creep or stress relaxation over the design life, because the clip must maintain force under sustained deflection.
  4. A room-temperature tensile value says nothing about the cold-end impact state or the loss of holding force over time.
Self-mark: 1 point for Q with the \(T_g\) comparison; 1 for creep/stress relaxation; 1 for explaining why the original data were insufficient.
C3 · Electrical property to component behaviour3 points

A sensor track has resistivity \(\rho=2.0\times10^{-6}\,\Omega\text{m}\), length \(L=0.40\text{ m}\), and cross-sectional area \(A=2.0\times10^{-6}\text{ m}^2\).

  1. Calculate its electrical resistance.
  2. If the same track is made twice as long and half as thick, find the new resistance.
  3. Explain the difference between resistivity and resistance.
Show worked solution and marking guide
\(R=0.40\,\Omega\), then \(R_{\text{new}}=1.60\,\Omega\)
  1. \(R=\rho L/A=(2.0\times10^{-6})(0.40)/(2.0\times10^{-6})=0.40\,\Omega\).
  2. Doubling length multiplies \(R\) by 2. Halving area multiplies it by another 2. Therefore \(R_{\text{new}}=4(0.40)=1.60\,\Omega\).
  3. Resistivity belongs to the material and is independent of specimen shape. Resistance belongs to this particular track and depends on \(\rho\), \(L\) and \(A\).
Self-mark: 1 point for the first resistance; 1 for the fourfold geometry scaling; 1 for distinguishing material property from component property.
C4 · Composite calculation and selection3 points

A continuous-fibre composite has \(E_f=230\text{ GPa}\), \(E_m=3\text{ GPa}\), and \(V_f=0.55\). It will be used as a lightweight tie loaded mainly along one axis.

  1. Calculate the longitudinal modulus \(E_L\).
  2. Explain why the modulus across the fibres will be much lower.
  3. State one manufacturing or service risk that must be considered before selecting it over aluminium.
Show worked solution and marking guide
\(E_L=127.85\text{ GPa}\)
  1. \(V_m=1-0.55=0.45\).
  2. \(E_L=E_fV_f+E_mV_m=230(0.55)+3(0.45)=127.85\text{ GPa}\).
  3. Across the fibres, load must pass through the soft matrix. The isostress relation \(1/E_T=V_f/E_f+V_m/E_m\) lets the low matrix modulus dominate.
  4. Acceptable risks include anisotropy under off-axis load, delamination after impact, difficult inspection, fibre damage at bolted joints, bonding quality, tooling cost or poor recyclability.
Self-mark: 1 point for \(E_L\); 1 for the matrix-dominated transverse explanation; 1 for a relevant practical risk tied to the design.
