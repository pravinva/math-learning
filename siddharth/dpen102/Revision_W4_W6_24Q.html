Before you start · what we studied in Weeks 4–6

Week 4 — Stress, strain & internal actions

Topics

Normal stress and strain; allowable stress and factor of safety; cutting a member to find \(N\), \(V\), \(M\).

Main ideas

  • Equilibrium of the whole body is not enough — the material can still fail under stress.
  • Stress is force per area; strain is relative change in length.
  • A cut exposes internal axial force \(N\), shear \(V\), and bending moment \(M\).

Remember

  • Design check: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{work}}\le\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}\) (not \(\sigma_y\)).
  • Keep units consistent (N with mm² → MPa).
  • Find reactions, then cut one side only to solve for \(N,V,M\).
Key formulas: \(\sigma=F/A\); \(\varepsilon=\Delta L/L_0\); \(\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}=\sigma_y/\mathrm{FoS}\); \(A_{\mathrm{req}}=F/\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}\).

Week 5 — Shear force & bending moment diagrams

Topics

Building \(V(x)\) and \(M(x)\) along a beam; point loads vs UDLs; locating maximum moment.

Main ideas

  • SFD/BMD show where shear and moment peak — the critical sections for design.
  • Point loads cause jumps in shear; UDLs cause sloping shear and parabolic moment.
  • Moment changes by the area under the shear diagram; \(M\) peaks where \(V=0\).

Remember

  • Always find support reactions first.
  • Shear must close to zero at the far end if reactions are correct.
  • For a full-span UDL on a simple beam: \(M_{\max}=wL^2/8\) at midspan.
Key formulas: \(dV/dx=-w\); \(dM/dx=V\); \(M_{\max}=wL^2/8\) (SS + full UDL); \(M_{\max}=PL/4\) (SS + central point load).

Week 6 — Truss analysis (method of sections)

Topics

Truss assumptions; zero-force members; global reactions; cutting through ≤3 members to find forces.

Main ideas

  • Truss members carry only axial force (tension or compression).
  • Method of sections gets one interior member without solving every joint.
  • Take moments about the intersection of two unknowns to isolate the third.

Remember

  • Assume tension; negative answer means compression.
  • Cut at most three unknown members (three planar equations).
  • Spot zero-force members by inspection before calculating.
Key formulas: \(\Sigma F_x=0\), \(\Sigma F_y=0\), \(\Sigma M=0\) on the cut piece; \(F_x=F\cos\theta\), \(F_y=F\sin\theta\).
Q1 W4 A rod of diameter 18 mm carries 42 kN tension. Sketch the loaded cross-section and find the axial stress.
Cross-section — circular rod in tension
Sign convention

Tension positive. Work in N and mm so that \(\text{N/mm}^2=\text{MPa}\).

Working

Radius \(r=d/2=9\text{ mm}\).

Area \(A=\pi r^2=\pi(9)^2=254.47\text{ mm}^2\).

Force \(F=42\text{ kN}=42000\text{ N}\).

\(\sigma=F/A=42000/254.47=165.0\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma=165.0\text{ MPa}\) (tension)
Q2 W4 Material has \(\sigma_y=300\) MPa, FoS \(=2.5\). Draw the stress scale and find the allowable stress.
Stress scale — yield vs allowable
Sign convention

Allowable stress is what design must stay under; yield is the material limit.

Working

Factor of safety definition: \(\text{FoS}=\sigma_y/\sigma_{allow}\).

Rearrange: \(\sigma_{allow}=\sigma_y/\text{FoS}=300/2.5\).

\(\sigma_{allow}=120\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma_{allow}=120\text{ MPa}\)
Q3 W4 For Q1–Q2, draw the comparison and state whether the design is safe.
Design check — working stress against allowable
Sign convention

Safe means the working stress bar finishes left of the dashed allowable line.

Working

Working stress from Q1: \(\sigma_{work}=165.0\text{ MPa}\).

Allowable from Q2: \(\sigma_{allow}=120\text{ MPa}\).

\(165.0 \gt 120\), so the bar overshoots the limit line by \(45\text{ MPa}\) (37.5%).

Fix by increasing area (larger diameter) or choosing a stronger material.

Answer: Not safe — working stress exceeds allowable
Q4 W4 Sketch the bar and find the required area if the tension is 75 kN and \(\sigma_{allow}=150\) MPa.
Axial member — sizing for allowable stress
Sign convention

Design inequality \(\sigma=F/A\le\sigma_{allow}\); the equality gives minimum area.

Working

Start from \(\sigma_{allow}=F/A_{req}\).

Rearrange: \(A_{req}=F/\sigma_{allow}\).

\(A_{req}=75000/150=500\text{ mm}^2\).

Answer: \(A_{req}=500\text{ mm}^2\)
Q5 W4 If \(L_0=1.8\) m and the extension is 1.35 mm, sketch the stretched bar and find the strain.
Elongation sketch — original length and extension
Sign convention

Convert everything to mm before dividing, otherwise strain is 1000× wrong.

Working

Convert: \(L_0=1.8\text{ m}=1800\text{ mm}\).

\(\varepsilon=\Delta L/L_0=1.35/1800\).

\(\varepsilon=7.5\times10^{-4}=750\ \mu\varepsilon\).

Answer: \(\varepsilon=7.5\times10^{-4}\)
Q6 W4 At a cut, the left segment has +12 kN rightward and 5 kN downward. Draw the cut free body and give \(N,V\) needed for equilibrium.
Cut free body — internal actions exposed
Sign convention

Isolate one side of the cut; whatever is missing must be supplied by \(N\), \(V\) and \(M\) on the exposed face.

Working

Horizontal: \(\Sigma F_x=0\Rightarrow N\) must balance the 12 kN, so \(N=12\text{ kN}\) acting leftward on the segment (axial, tension positive).

Vertical: \(\Sigma F_y=0\Rightarrow V\) must balance the 5 kN down, so \(V=5\text{ kN}\) upward on the segment.

A moment \(M\) also appears at the cut to balance rotation of the segment.

Answer: \(N=12\text{ kN}\), \(V=5\text{ kN}\) (signs follow your adopted convention)
Q7 W5 Simply supported beam of span 6 m has a central point load of 18 kN. Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions.
Loaded beam — supports and reactions
Sign convention

Up positive, anticlockwise moments positive, moments taken about A.

Working

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(6)=18(3)=54\Rightarrow R_B=9\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A=18-9=9\text{ kN}\).

The load sits at midspan, so symmetry confirms equal reactions.

Answer: \(R_A=R_B=9\text{ kN}\)
Q8 W5 Beam of span 8 m with UDL \(w=3\) kN/m over the full span. Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions.
Loaded beam — full-span UDL
Sign convention

Replace the UDL by its resultant at the centroid of the load block when writing equilibrium.

Working

Total load \(W=wL=3(8)=24\text{ kN}\) acting at \(x=4\text{ m}\).

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(8)=24(4)=96\Rightarrow R_B=12\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A=24-12=12\text{ kN}\).

Answer: \(R_A=R_B=12\text{ kN}\)
Q9 W5 For Q8, sketch the bending moment diagram and find the maximum bending moment.
BMD — parabola for a full-span UDL
Sign convention

Sagging moment plotted positive upward; the curve is zero at both simple supports.

Working

For a full-span UDL the moment is \(M(x)=\tfrac{wLx}{2}-\tfrac{wx^2}{2}\), a parabola.

Shear is zero at midspan, so the maximum moment sits there.

\(M_{max}=wL^2/8=3(8)^2/8=3(64)/8=24\text{ kN·m}\).

Answer: \(M_{max}=24\text{ kN·m}\) at midspan
Q10 W5 Beam of span 10 m carries 10 kN at 3 m and 16 kN at 7 m from A. Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions.
Loaded beam — two point loads
Sign convention

Moments about A, anticlockwise positive; unknown reactions assumed upward.

Working

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(10)=10(3)+16(7)=30+112=142\).

\(R_B=14.2\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A=(10+16)-14.2=26-14.2=11.8\text{ kN}\).

Check: \(11.8+14.2=26\text{ kN}=\) total load. Balanced.

Answer: \(R_A=11.8\text{ kN}\), \(R_B=14.2\text{ kN}\)
Q11 W5 For Q10, sketch the shear force diagram and read the shear just to the right of the first load.
SFD — step down at each point load
Sign convention

Shear taken as the resultant of everything to the left of the section, upward positive.

Working

Just right of A: \(V=R_A=11.8\text{ kN}\).

Crossing the 10 kN load drops the shear by 10 kN.

\(V=11.8-10=1.8\text{ kN}\).

Answer: \(V=1.8\text{ kN}\)
Q12 W5 For Q10, draw the left free body and find the moment at \(x=3\) m.
Cut free body — left of the section at x = 3 m
Sign convention

Moments about the cut, sagging positive. Only forces to the left of the cut appear.

Working

Only \(R_A\) lies strictly left of the section.

Its lever arm about the cut is 3 m.

The 10 kN load sits exactly at the cut, so its lever arm is zero.

\(M(3)=R_A(3)=11.8(3)=35.4\text{ kN·m}\).

Answer: \(M(3)=35.4\text{ kN·m}\) (sagging)
Q13 W6 Draw the joint and classify the member forces at an unloaded joint with only two non-collinear members.
Joint FBD — two non-collinear members, no load
Sign convention

Resolve along and perpendicular to one member; a pin carries no moment.

Working

Resolve perpendicular to member 2: only member 1 has a component there, so \(F_1=0\).

Resolve along member 2: with \(F_1=0\) nothing balances \(F_2\), so \(F_2=0\).

They still matter structurally — they brace the joint and carry load under other load cases.

Answer: Both members are zero-force members
Q14 W6 Draw the joint and state what happens to the third member at an unloaded joint with three members where two are collinear.
Joint FBD — two collinear plus one odd member
Sign convention

Choose axes along and perpendicular to the collinear pair.

Working

Resolve perpendicular to the collinear pair: the collinear members have no component in that direction.

Only \(F_3\) appears in that equation, so \(F_3=0\).

Along the pair: \(F_1=F_2\), so the collinear members simply pass force straight through.

Answer: The third (non-collinear) member is a zero-force member
Q15 W6 A member solves as \(-8.5\) kN under the tension assumption. Draw both arrow sets and interpret the sign.
Sign interpretation — assumed tension vs actual compression
Sign convention

Always assume tension (arrows pulling away from the joint). The algebra then reports the truth through the sign.

Working

A positive answer would confirm the tension assumption.

The negative sign means the true force acts opposite to the assumed arrows, i.e. pushing into the joint.

Magnitude is unchanged: 8.5 kN.

Answer: 8.5 kN compression
Q16 W6 A truss carries 30 kN total downward load. Draw the truss with its reactions and find \(R_B\) if \(R_A=12\) kN.
Truss FBD — whole-structure equilibrium
Sign convention

Up positive. The truss as a whole is a single free body.

Working

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A+R_B-30=0\).

\(R_B=30-R_A=30-12\).

\(R_B=18\text{ kN}\).

Answer: \(R_B=18\text{ kN}\)
Q17 W6 Draw a section cut and explain why it is usually limited to at most three unknown members.
Method of sections — three exposed members
Sign convention

The cut portion is a rigid free body in a plane.

Working

Planar statics gives exactly three independent equations: \(\Sigma F_x=0\), \(\Sigma F_y=0\), \(\Sigma M=0\).

Each cut member contributes one unknown force.

Four or more cut members leaves the system underdetermined by a single cut, so you would need a second cut or method of joints.

Answer: Only three independent planar equations exist, so a single cut can solve at most three unknowns
Q18 W4-6 Combined check: \(N=68\) kN in a tie, \(\sigma_y=280\) MPa, FoS = 2.8. Sketch the section and find the minimum diameter.
Cross-section — sizing a circular tie
Sign convention

Size for the allowable stress, then round up to the next available bar.

Working

\(\sigma_{allow}=\sigma_y/\text{FoS}=280/2.8=100\text{ MPa}\).

\(A_{req}=N/\sigma_{allow}=68000/100=680\text{ mm}^2\).

\(d=\sqrt{4A/\pi}=\sqrt{4(680)/\pi}=29.43\text{ mm}\).

Round up, never down, when sizing.

Answer: 29.43 mm minimum → specify a 30 mm rod
Q19 W5-6 A reaction check gives \(R_A=9\) kN and \(R_B=15\) kN for a total external load of 22 kN. Draw the FBD and diagnose.
Global FBD — equilibrium audit
Sign convention

Vertical equilibrium must close exactly before any internal work begins.

Working

Reactions sum: \(9+15=24\text{ kN}\).

External load: \(22\text{ kN}\).

Residual \(=+2\text{ kN}\), so \(\Sigma F_y\neq0\).

Recheck the moment equation, the load positions, and the transcription of load magnitudes before computing any shear, moment or stress.

Answer: Equilibrium is violated by 2 kN — a reaction or a load value is wrong; stop and fix it first
Q20 Challenge Beam of span 9 m with a 2 kN/m UDL over the full span plus 12 kN at 6 m. Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions.
Loaded beam — UDL plus point load
Sign convention

Superpose: handle the UDL by its resultant, then add the point load.

Working

UDL resultant \(=wL=2(9)=18\text{ kN}\) at \(x=4.5\text{ m}\).

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(9)=18(4.5)+12(6)=81+72=153\).

\(R_B=17\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A=(18+12)-17=30-17=13\text{ kN}\).

Answer: \(R_A=13\text{ kN}\), \(R_B=17\text{ kN}\)
Q21 Challenge For Q20, draw the left segment and find the moment at \(x=4\) m.
Cut free body — left segment at x = 4 m
Sign convention

Only what lies left of the cut contributes; UDL on that piece acts at its own midpoint.

Working

Reaction term: \(R_A(4)=13(4)=52\text{ kN·m}\).

UDL on the segment: \(w(4)=2(4)=8\text{ kN}\), acting at 2 m from the cut.

UDL term: \(8(2)=16\text{ kN·m}\) (hogging the section, so subtract).

\(M(4)=52-16=36\text{ kN·m}\).

The 12 kN load is at 6 m, right of the cut, so it does not appear.

Answer: \(M(4)=36\text{ kN·m}\)
Q22 Challenge The section at Q21 has \(I=24\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\) and \(c=90\) mm. Draw the stress distribution and find the bending stress.
Bending stress — section and linear distribution
Sign convention

Stress varies linearly from zero at the neutral axis to a maximum at the extreme fibre distance c.

Working

Convert: \(M=36\text{ kN·m}=36\times10^6\text{ N·mm}\).

\(\sigma=Mc/I=(36\times10^6)(90)/(24\times10^6)\).

\(\sigma=(3.24\times10^9)/(24\times10^6)=135\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma=135\text{ MPa}\)
Q23 Challenge The allowable bending stress is 120 MPa. Draw the comparison and state whether the Q22 section is acceptable.
Design check — bending stress against allowable
Sign convention

Compare like with like: maximum bending stress against the bending allowable.

Working

\(\sigma_{max}=135\text{ MPa}\) from Q22.

\(\sigma_{allow}=120\text{ MPa}\).

Overstress \(=(135-120)/120=12.5\%\).

Remedy: raise \(I\) (deeper section) or reduce the span/load.

Answer: No — the section is overstressed by 12.5% and fails the bending check
Q24 Capstone Sketch the error-check flow and list, in one line each, the checks to run after solving any W4–6 problem.
Method map — solve then audit
The five checks

Units: keep N and mm so stresses come out directly in MPa; convert kN and m first.

Signs: one convention declared at the top and used in every equation.

Reaction sum: reactions must equal the total external load.

Moment balance about a second point: an independent test that catches lever-arm errors.

Stress check: compare \(\sigma_{work}\) with \(\sigma_{allow}=\sigma_y/\text{FoS}\) and state pass/fail with a margin.

Answer: Units → signs → equilibrium → independent moment check → stress vs allowable
