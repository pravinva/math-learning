Before you start · what we studied in Weeks 4–6

Week 4 — Stress, strain & internal actions

Topics

Normal stress and strain; allowable stress and factor of safety; cutting a member to find \(N\), \(V\), \(M\).

Main ideas

  • Equilibrium of the whole body is not enough — the material can still fail under stress.
  • Stress is force per area; strain is relative change in length.
  • A cut exposes internal axial force \(N\), shear \(V\), and bending moment \(M\).

Remember

  • Design check: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{work}}\le\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}\) (not \(\sigma_y\)).
  • Keep units consistent (N with mm² → MPa).
  • Find reactions, then cut one side only to solve for \(N,V,M\).
Key formulas: \(\sigma=F/A\); \(\varepsilon=\Delta L/L_0\); \(\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}=\sigma_y/\mathrm{FoS}\); \(A_{\mathrm{req}}=F/\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}\).

Week 5 — Shear force & bending moment diagrams

Topics

Building \(V(x)\) and \(M(x)\) along a beam; point loads vs UDLs; locating maximum moment.

Main ideas

  • SFD/BMD show where shear and moment peak — the critical sections for design.
  • Point loads cause jumps in shear; UDLs cause sloping shear and parabolic moment.
  • Moment changes by the area under the shear diagram; \(M\) peaks where \(V=0\).

Remember

  • Always find support reactions first.
  • Shear must close to zero at the far end if reactions are correct.
  • For a full-span UDL on a simple beam: \(M_{\max}=wL^2/8\) at midspan.
Key formulas: \(dV/dx=-w\); \(dM/dx=V\); \(M_{\max}=wL^2/8\) (SS + full UDL); \(M_{\max}=PL/4\) (SS + central point load).

Week 6 — Truss analysis (method of sections)

Topics

Truss assumptions; zero-force members; global reactions; cutting through ≤3 members to find forces.

Main ideas

  • Truss members carry only axial force (tension or compression).
  • Method of sections gets one interior member without solving every joint.
  • Take moments about the intersection of two unknowns to isolate the third.

Remember

  • Assume tension; negative answer means compression.
  • Cut at most three unknown members (three planar equations).
  • Spot zero-force members by inspection before calculating.
Key formulas: \(\Sigma F_x=0\), \(\Sigma F_y=0\), \(\Sigma M=0\) on the cut piece; \(F_x=F\cos\theta\), \(F_y=F\sin\theta\).
Q1 W4 A rod of diameter 18 mm carries 42 kN tension. Find axial stress.
\(A=\pi(9)^2=254.47\text{ mm}^2\). \(\sigma=42000/254.47=165.0\text{ MPa}\).
Q2 W4 Material has \(\sigma_y=300\) MPa, FoS \(=2.5\). Find allowable stress.
\(\sigma_{allow}=300/2.5=120\text{ MPa}\).
Q3 W4 For Q1-Q2, is design safe?
\(\sigma_{work}=165\text{ MPa} > 120\text{ MPa}\). Not safe.
Q4 W4 Find required area if tension is 75 kN and \(\sigma_{allow}=150\) MPa.
\(A_{req}=F/\sigma_{allow}=75000/150=500\text{ mm}^2\).
Q5 W4 If \(L_0=1.8\) m and extension is 1.35 mm, find strain.
Convert \(L_0=1800\) mm. \(\varepsilon=\Delta L/L_0=1.35/1800=7.5\times10^{-4}\).
Q6 W4 At a cut, left segment has +12 kN rightward and 5 kN downward. Give \(N,V\) needed at cut for equilibrium.
Cut must balance: \(N=12\) kN left on segment, \(V=5\) kN upward on segment (sign per adopted convention).
Q7 W5 Simply supported beam span 6 m has central point load 18 kN. Find reactions.
Symmetry gives \(R_A=R_B=9\text{ kN}\).
Q8 W5 Beam span 8 m with UDL \(w=3\) kN/m over full span. Find reactions.
Total load \(=wL=24\) kN at midspan. So \(R_A=R_B=12\) kN.
Q9 W5 For Q8, find max bending moment.
For full-span UDL, \(M_{max}=wL^2/8=3(64)/8=24\text{ kN·m}\).
Q10 W5 Beam: span 10 m, 10 kN at 3 m and 16 kN at 7 m from A. Find reactions.
\(\Sigma M_A: R_B(10)=10(3)+16(7)=142\Rightarrow R_B=14.2\) kN. \(R_A=26-14.2=11.8\) kN.
Q11 W5 For Q10, shear just right of first load?
\(V=R_A-10=11.8-10=1.8\) kN.
Q12 W5 For Q10, moment at \(x=3\) m.
\(M(3)=R_A(3)=11.8(3)=35.4\text{ kN·m}\) (load at 3 m has zero arm at section).
Q13 W6 At unloaded joint with two non-collinear members only, what are member forces?
Both are zero-force members.
Q14 W6 At unloaded joint with three members where two are collinear, what about third?
Third non-collinear member is zero-force member.
Q15 W6 Member solved as \(-8.5\) kN assuming tension. Interpret.
Actual force is 8.5 kN compression.
Q16 W6 Truss total downward loads = 30 kN. If \(R_A=12\) kN, find \(R_B\).
\(\Sigma F_y=0\Rightarrow R_A+R_B=30\), so \(R_B=18\) kN.
Q17 W6 Why is a section cut often limited to at most three unknown members?
In planar statics you have three independent equilibrium equations; more than three unknowns makes single cut underdetermined.
Q18 W4-6 Combined check: \(N=68\) kN in tie, \(\sigma_y=280\) MPa, FoS=2.8. Find minimum diameter.
\(\sigma_{allow}=100\) MPa. \(A_{req}=68000/100=680\text{ mm}^2\). \(d=\sqrt{4A/\pi}=29.43\) mm \(\Rightarrow\) choose 30 mm or higher.
Q19 W5-6 Reaction check gives \(R_A=9\) kN, \(R_B=15\) kN for total external load 22 kN. Diagnose.
Reactions sum to 24 kN, violating vertical equilibrium by +2 kN. At least one reaction or load transcription is wrong.
Q20 Challenge Beam span 9 m, UDL 2 kN/m full span + 12 kN at 6 m. Find reactions.
UDL total \(=18\) kN at 4.5 m. \(\Sigma M_A: R_B(9)=18(4.5)+12(6)=153\Rightarrow R_B=17\) kN. \(R_A=30-17=13\) kN.
Q21 Challenge For Q20, find moment at \(x=4\) m (left segment).
\(M(4)=R_A(4)-w(4)(2)=13(4)-2(4)(2)=52-16=36\text{ kN·m}\).
Q22 Challenge Section at Q21 has \(I=24\times10^6\text{ mm}^4,\ c=90\) mm. Find bending stress.
\(M=36\text{ kN·m}=36\times10^6\text{ N·mm}\). \(\sigma=Mc/I=(36\times10^6)(90)/(24\times10^6)=135\text{ MPa}\).
Q23 Challenge Material allowable bending stress is 120 MPa. Is Q22 section acceptable?
No. \(135>120\) MPa, so section fails bending check.
Q24 Capstone In one line each, list the error checks after solving any W4-6 problem.
Check units (N/kN, mm/m), sign convention consistency, reaction sum vs external loads, moment balance about a second point, and stress against allowable with FoS logic.
