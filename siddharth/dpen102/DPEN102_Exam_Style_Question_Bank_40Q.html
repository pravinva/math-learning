Section A: Vectors, FBD, Equilibrium (Q1-Q5)

Q1
8 marks

Draw the force vectors on axes and find the resultant magnitude and direction. At a joint, two cables exert forces \(F_1=8\text{ kN}\) at \(35^\circ\) above +x and \(F_2=6\text{ kN}\) at \(120^\circ\) from +x.

Worked solution
Vector sum on axes
  1. \(F_{1x}=8\cos35^\circ=6.553,\quad F_{1y}=8\sin35^\circ=4.589\text{ kN}\).
  2. \(F_{2x}=6\cos120^\circ=-3,\quad F_{2y}=6\sin120^\circ=5.196\text{ kN}\).
  3. Resultant components: \(R_x=3.553,\ R_y=9.785\text{ kN}\).
  4. \(|R|=\sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}=\sqrt{3.553^2+9.785^2}=10.41\text{ kN}\).
  5. \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(R_y/R_x)=\tan^{-1}(9.785/3.553)=70.0^\circ\) above +x.
Q2
8 marks

Draw the FBD and find \(P\) at impending motion. A 450 N crate rests on a rough horizontal floor. A force \(P\) is applied at \(25^\circ\) above horizontal and \(\mu_s=0.35\).

Worked solution
FBD — impending motion
  1. Vertical equilibrium: \(N + P\sin25^\circ - 450=0 \Rightarrow N=450-P\sin25^\circ\).
  2. At impending motion: \(P\cos25^\circ=F_f=\mu_s N=0.35(450-P\sin25^\circ)\).
  3. \(P(0.9063+0.35\times0.4226)=157.5\).
  4. \(P(1.0542)=157.5\Rightarrow P=149.4\text{ N}\).
Q3
8 marks

Draw the joint diagram and resolve the 5 kN force along members AB and AC, where AB is at \(30^\circ\) and AC at \(110^\circ\) from +x.

Worked solution
Force resolved along two members

Use oblique resolution with simultaneous equations in x/y:

  1. Let components be \(F_{AB},F_{AC}\) along respective member directions.
  2. \(F_{AB}\cos30^\circ + F_{AC}\cos110^\circ = 5\).
  3. \(F_{AB}\sin30^\circ + F_{AC}\sin110^\circ = 0\).
  4. From second: \(0.5F_{AB}+0.9397F_{AC}=0 \Rightarrow F_{AB}=-1.879F_{AC}\).
  5. Substitute into first: \(-1.627F_{AC}-0.342F_{AC}=5 \Rightarrow F_{AC}=-2.54\text{ kN}\).
  6. \(F_{AB}=4.78\text{ kN}\). (Negative sign means AC component acts opposite assumed direction.)
Q4
8 marks

Draw the loaded beam with supports and find reactions \(A_x,A_y,B_y\). A pin at A and roller at B support the beam. Loads: 6 kN downward at 2 m from A and 9 kN downward at 5 m from A. Span AB = 8 m.

Worked solution
Loaded beam — reactions
  1. No horizontal load \(\Rightarrow A_x=0\).
  2. \(\Sigma M_A=0:\ 8B_y-6(2)-9(5)=0 \Rightarrow 8B_y=57 \Rightarrow B_y=7.125\text{ kN}\).
  3. \(\Sigma F_y=0:\ A_y+B_y-15=0 \Rightarrow A_y=7.875\text{ kN}\).
Q5
8 marks

Sketch a complete FBD that includes support reactions, then explain why those reactions must appear and list three common FBD errors that cause wrong equilibrium results.

Worked solution
FBD must show support reactions

Support reactions are external forces from constraints; without them, \(\Sigma F=0,\Sigma M=0\) is incomplete.

Common errors:

  1. Forgetting one reaction component at a pin support.
  2. Using wrong direction/sign for applied inclined forces.
  3. Taking moments with wrong lever arm (not perpendicular distance).

Section B: Moments, Beams, Reactions (Q6-Q10)

Q6
10 marks

Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions. Simply supported beam AB, span 10 m, UDL \(w=3\text{ kN/m}\) over full span, plus 12 kN point load at 3 m from A.

Worked solution
Loaded beam — UDL + point load
  1. UDL resultant \(=wL=30\text{ kN}\) at midspan (5 m from A).
  2. \(\Sigma M_A=0:\ 10B_y-30(5)-12(3)=0\Rightarrow B_y=18.6\text{ kN}\).
  3. \(\Sigma F_y=0:\ A_y+B_y-42=0\Rightarrow A_y=23.4\text{ kN}\).
Q7
10 marks

Sketch the SFD for Q6, find the shear immediately left and right of \(x=3\) m, and mark where shear crosses zero.

Worked solution
SFD — shear crosses zero at 3.8 m
  1. \(V(3^-)=A_y-w(3)=23.4-9=14.4\text{ kN}\).
  2. Jump by -12 kN at point load: \(V(3^+)=2.4\text{ kN}\).
  3. For \(x>3\): \(V=23.4-3x-12=11.4-3x\).
  4. Set \(V=0\Rightarrow x=3.8\text{ m}\) from A.
Q8
10 marks

Sketch the BMD for Q6 and determine the maximum bending moment (mark its location).

Worked solution
BMD — peak where V = 0
  1. Maximum \(M\) occurs where \(V=0\), at \(x=3.8\text{ m}\).
  2. \(M(x)=A_yx-\frac{wx^2}{2}-12(x-3)\) for \(x>3\).
  3. \(M_{max}=23.4(3.8)-\frac{3(3.8)^2}{2}-12(0.8)=57.54\text{ kN·m}\).
Q9
10 marks

Draw the cantilever with its loads and find the fixed-end shear and moment. Length 4 m, UDL 2 kN/m over the full span and a 5 kN tip load.

Worked solution
Cantilever — fixed-end actions
  1. UDL resultant \(=8\text{ kN}\) acting at 2 m from fixed end.
  2. Fixed shear \(V_f=8+5=13\text{ kN}\) (upward at support).
  3. Fixed moment \(M_f=8(2)+5(4)=16+20=36\text{ kN·m}\) (hogging sign convention).
Q10
8 marks

Sketch a completed SFD/BMD pair, then state and justify two checks that validate it before you finalise an exam answer.

Worked solution
Two SFD/BMD validation checks
  1. Area under SFD between two points equals BMD change between those points.
  2. Final BMD at simple support should return to zero; end shear sum must match total load.

Section C: Trusses (Q11-Q15)

Q11
10 marks

Draw the king-post truss and find the support reactions. Symmetric span 6 m, height 2 m, 18 kN downward at the apex.

Worked solution
King-post truss — symmetric reactions

By symmetry: \(A_y=B_y=9\text{ kN}\), \(A_x=0\).

Q12
12 marks

Draw the joint FBD at the left support for Q11 and find the force in the left rafter and left tie member.

Worked solution
Joint at the left support
  1. Rafter angle: \(\tan\theta=2/3\Rightarrow \sin\theta=0.5547,\cos\theta=0.8321\).
  2. \(\Sigma F_y=0:\ 9+F_r\sin\theta=0\Rightarrow F_r=-16.23\text{ kN}\) (compression).
  3. \(\Sigma F_x=0:\ F_t+F_r\cos\theta=0\Rightarrow F_t=13.50\text{ kN}\) (tension).
Q13
10 marks

Draw a section cut through three members including the central vertical, and determine the central vertical force.

Worked solution
Method of sections — central vertical

Take moments about intersection of the two other cut members so only vertical member remains. For this symmetric loading, central vertical carries \(18\text{ kN}\) tension.

Q14
8 marks

Sketch the joint condition that identifies a zero-force member, then explain how to spot zero-force members quickly in an exam.

Worked solution
Zero-force members at a joint

At a joint with no external load/reaction and only two non-collinear members, both member forces are zero. Also: with three members where two are collinear and no external load, the non-collinear member is zero-force.

Q15
8 marks

Draw both the assumed-tension and actual-force arrow sets. A member force is found negative under the tension assumption — what does this mean physically?

Worked solution
Negative result = compression

The member is actually in compression with magnitude equal to absolute value of the computed force.

Section D: SFD/BMD Interpretation (Q16-Q20)

Q16
8 marks

Sketch the load–shear–moment relationship chain and state the differential relationships between load \(w\), shear \(V\), and moment \(M\).

Worked solution
Load-shear-moment relationships

\(\dfrac{dV}{dx}=-w,\quad \dfrac{dM}{dx}=V\).

Q17
10 marks

Sketch the BMD from the given shear. Given \(V(x)=12-2x\) kN on \(0\le x\le6\) and \(M(0)=0\), derive \(M(x)\) and \(M(6)\).

Worked solution
BMD from V(x) = 12 − 2x
  1. \(M=\int(12-2x)\,dx=12x-x^2+C\).
  2. \(M(0)=0\Rightarrow C=0\).
  3. \(M(6)=72-36=36\text{ kN·m}\).
Q18
8 marks

Sketch an SFD that crosses zero and mark where bending moment becomes maximum in a beam segment.

Worked solution
Moment peaks where shear = 0

Where \(V=0\) (and changes sign in many practical cases).

Q19
8 marks

Sketch the SFD and BMD under a pure UDL and state the geometric shape of each.

Worked solution
UDL → linear SFD, parabolic BMD

Under constant UDL: SFD is linear, BMD is parabolic.

Q20
10 marks

Give a 4-step procedure (with a sketch outline) to build the SFD and BMD under mixed UDL + point loads.

Worked solution
4-step SFD/BMD procedure
  1. Find support reactions from equilibrium.
  2. Traverse beam left-to-right computing shear jumps/slopes.
  3. Integrate shear areas to get bending moments.
  4. Apply boundary checks at supports and load points.

Section E: Friction Systems (Q21-Q25)

Q21
10 marks

Draw the FBD and decide whether the block slips. Block \(W=600\text{ N}\) on a \(30^\circ\) incline, \(\mu_s=0.25\), no extra force applied.

Worked solution
Block on incline — will it slip?
  1. Down-slope component \(=W\sin30=300\text{ N}\).
  2. \(N=W\cos30=519.6\text{ N}\).
  3. \(F_{max}=\mu_sN=129.9\text{ N}\).
  4. Since \(300>129.9\), static friction insufficient; block slips down.
Q22
8 marks

Sketch the impending-motion FBD and define the angle of friction and the angle of repose, stating their relation.

Worked solution
Angle of repose = angle of friction

\(\tan\phi=\mu\). Angle of repose equals angle of friction at impending motion: \(\theta_r=\phi\).

Q23
10 marks

Draw the FBD and find the kinetic friction and acceleration. A 200 N block (mass 20 kg) is pushed on a horizontal floor with 80 N at \(20^\circ\) downward; \(\mu_k=0.3\).

Worked solution
FBD — push angled downward
  1. \(N=200+80\sin20=227.36\text{ N}\).
  2. \(F_k=\mu_kN=68.21\text{ N}\).
  3. Horizontal push \(=80\cos20=75.18\text{ N}\).
  4. Net \(=75.18-68.21=6.97\text{ N}\Rightarrow a=6.97/20=0.349\text{ m/s}^2\).
Q24
6 marks

Draw the FBD of a crate being pushed downward and explain why that usually increases friction.

Worked solution
Downward push raises N and friction

Downward component raises normal force \(N\), and friction capacity is proportional to \(N\): \(F=\mu N\).

Q25
10 marks

Draw the stacked-block FBD and find the maximum horizontal force on the top block without slipping. Top 50 N on lower 150 N; \(\mu=0.4\) between the blocks only.

Worked solution
Stacked blocks — interface friction
  1. Normal at interface \(=50\text{ N}\).
  2. Max static friction \(=0.4(50)=20\text{ N}\).
  3. Thus max applied force transferable without slip is \(20\text{ N}\).

Section F: Centroids and Second Moment (Q26-Q30)

Q26
10 marks

Sketch the section with the hole and find the centroid from the bottom-left corner. Rectangle \(200\times100\) mm with a centred circular hole of diameter 40 mm.

Worked solution
Rectangle with a centred hole

By symmetry, centroid remains at geometric center \((100,\ 50)\) mm.

Q27
12 marks

Sketch the T-section and find \(\bar{y}\). Flange \(120\times20\) mm on top of a centred web \(20\times100\) mm.

Worked solution
T-section — centroid from base
  1. \(A_f=2400,\ y_f=110\) mm from base.
  2. \(A_w=2000,\ y_w=50\) mm.
  3. \(\bar y=\dfrac{A_fy_f+A_wy_w}{A_f+A_w}=\dfrac{2400(110)+2000(50)}{4400}=82.73\text{ mm}\).
Q28
10 marks

Sketch the rectangle with its centroidal axis and find \(I_x\). Rectangle \(b=80\) mm, \(h=160\) mm.

Worked solution
Rectangle — centroidal I_x

\(I_x=\dfrac{bh^3}{12}=\dfrac{80(160)^3}{12}=27.31\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\).

Q29
8 marks

Sketch the parallel-axis transfer, state the theorem, and give one typical use in ENGG102 problems.

Worked solution
Parallel-axis transfer

\(I=I_c+Ad^2\). Used when transferring centroidal second moment of a component to a common reference axis for composite sections.

Q30
8 marks

Sketch a bent section with its neutral axis and explain why both centroid and second moment are needed in beam design checks.

Worked solution
Neutral axis and bending stress

Centroid locates neutral axis and section geometry; second moment quantifies bending stiffness and stress distribution via \(\sigma=\dfrac{My}{I}\).

Section G: Stress and Strain (Q31-Q35)

Q31
10 marks

Sketch the loaded rod and find the normal stress. Steel rod area \(400\text{ mm}^2\), tensile load 80 kN.

Worked solution
Rod in tension — normal stress

\(\sigma=P/A=80{,}000/(400\times10^{-6})=200\text{ MPa}\).

Q32
10 marks

Sketch the elongated bar for Q31 and, with \(E=200\text{ GPa}\), find the strain and the extension over a 1.2 m length.

Worked solution
Extension under load
  1. \(\epsilon=\sigma/E=200\times10^6/200\times10^9=0.001\).
  2. \(\delta=\epsilon L=0.001(1.2)=0.0012\text{ m}=1.2\text{ mm}\).
Q33
8 marks

Sketch a stress–strain curve marking the proportional limit and yield, then define proportional limit, yield stress, and factor of safety.

Worked solution
Stress-strain landmarks

Proportional limit: end of linear \(\sigma\)-\(\epsilon\). Yield: start of permanent deformation. FoS \(=\sigma_{allow}/\sigma_{working}\) or \(\sigma_y/\sigma_{working}\).

Q34
10 marks

Sketch the compressed bar (with sign convention) and find the stress. \(P=120\text{ kN}\), diameter 30 mm.

Worked solution
Bar in compression
  1. \(A=\pi d^2/4=\pi(30)^2/4=706.86\text{ mm}^2\).
  2. \(\sigma=120000/706.86=169.8\text{ MPa}\).
  3. Compression is often taken as negative stress by sign convention.
Q35
8 marks

Sketch a plate with a hole showing stress concentration, then state two reasons why that matters even when average stress looks safe.

Worked solution
Stress concentration at a hole
  1. Local peak stress near holes/notches can exceed yield while average stress is low.
  2. Fatigue cracks usually initiate at geometric stress raisers.

Section H: Work-Energy and Dynamics (Q36-Q40)

Q36
10 marks

Sketch the work–energy picture and find the final speed. A 10 kg block starts from rest and is pulled by a constant 80 N over 6 m on a frictionless floor.

Worked solution
Work-energy — frictionless pull
  1. Work done \(W=Fs=80(6)=480\text{ J}\).
  2. \(\Delta KE=W\Rightarrow \frac12mv^2=480\).
  3. \(v=\sqrt{2(480)/10}=9.80\text{ m/s}\).
Q37
10 marks

Sketch the energy diagram and find the impact speed. A 2 kg mass is dropped from 5 m (no air drag).

Worked solution
Free drop — energy
  1. \(mgh=\frac12mv^2\Rightarrow v=\sqrt{2gh}\).
  2. \(v=\sqrt{2(9.81)(5)}=9.90\text{ m/s}\).
Q38
8 marks

Sketch the distinction and differentiate conservative and non-conservative forces with one ENGG102 example of each.

Worked solution
Conservative vs non-conservative

Conservative: path-independent work (gravity, spring). Non-conservative: path-dependent dissipation (friction).

Q39
10 marks

Sketch the energy conversion and find \(h\). A 50 kg cart moves at 4 m/s and climbs a ramp, stopping after gaining height \(h\) with no losses.

Worked solution
Cart climbs a ramp — KE to PE
  1. \(\frac12mv^2=mgh\Rightarrow h=v^2/(2g)\).
  2. \(h=16/(19.62)=0.815\text{ m}\).
Q40
12 marks

Sketch the energy audit on the incline and find the final speed from rest. A 5 kg slider moves 3 m up a \(25^\circ\) incline under a 120 N pull parallel to the incline; \(\mu_k=0.2\).

Worked solution
Slider up an incline — energy audit
  1. Applied work: \(W_P=120(3)=360\text{ J}\).
  2. Weight component down slope: \(mg\sin25=20.73\text{ N}\Rightarrow W_g=-20.73(3)=-62.2\text{ J}\).
  3. Normal \(N=mg\cos25=44.45\text{ N}\Rightarrow F_k=\mu N=8.89\text{ N}\Rightarrow W_f=-26.7\text{ J}\).
  4. Net work \(=360-62.2-26.7=271.1\text{ J}=\Delta KE=\frac12mv^2\).
  5. \(v=\sqrt{2(271.1)/5}=10.41\text{ m/s}\).
