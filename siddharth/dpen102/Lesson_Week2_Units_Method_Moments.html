1 SI units & conversions

Every calculation in DPEN102 should run in one consistent unit system, preferably SI, before any arithmetic happens.

Key idea

Base SI units: length (m), mass (kg), time (s). Force is derived: \(\text{N}=\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2\). Common conversions: \(1\text{ kN}=1000\text{ N}\), \(1\text{ mm}=0.001\text{ m}\), \(1\text{ MPa}=10^6\text{ Pa}=1\text{ N/mm}^2\).

Visual conversion map

Exam rule: convert once at the top, box your unit system, and never mix N with kN or m with mm inside the same equation line.

Worked example 1

Convert 2.5 kN to N.

\(2.5\times1000=2500\text{ N}\).

Worked example 2

A force of 450 N acts on an area of 15 mm². Find the stress in MPa.

\(\sigma = 450/(15\times10^{-6}\text{ m}^2) = 30\times10^6\text{ Pa} = 30\text{ MPa}\).

Shortcut: since \(1\text{ N/mm}^2=1\text{ MPa}\), you can skip straight to \(450\text{ N}/15\text{ mm}^2=30\text{ N/mm}^2=30\text{ MPa}\).

Practice 1

Convert 3.2 kN to N.

Solution

\(3200\text{ N}\).

Practice 2

Convert 850 mm to m.

Solution

\(0.85\text{ m}\).

Practice 3

Convert 0.06 m² to mm².

Solution

\(0.06\times10^6=60{,}000\text{ mm}^2\) (since \(1\text{ m}^2=10^6\text{ mm}^2\)).

Practice 4

A stress of 85 MPa acts over an area of 200 mm². Find the force in N.

Solution

\(F=\sigma\times A=85(200)=17{,}000\text{ N}=17\text{ kN}\) (using the MPa \(=\) N/mm² shortcut directly).

Practice 5

Convert 45,000 Pa to kPa and to MPa.

Solution

\(45\text{ kPa}\), \(0.045\text{ MPa}\).

Practice 6

Show why 1 N/mm² is numerically equal to 1 MPa.

Solution

\(1\text{ N/mm}^2 = 1\text{ N}/(10^{-3}\text{ m})^2 = 1\text{ N}/10^{-6}\text{ m}^2 = 10^6\text{ N/m}^2 = 10^6\text{ Pa} = 1\text{ MPa}\). The conversion factor works out to exactly 1, making the two units interchangeable for stress calculations.

2 Significant figures

Report answers to a precision consistent with your least precise given value, no more, no less.

Key idea

The final answer's significant figures are limited by the least precise input value. Don't round mid-calculation, only round the final reported answer.

Worked example 1

Multiply 4.2 (2 sig figs) by 1.75 (3 sig figs). How many sig figs should the answer have?

The least precise input has 2 sig figs, so the answer is limited to 2: \(4.2\times1.75=7.35 \to 7.4\).

Worked example 2

A calculated stress comes out to 83.276491 MPa. The input data had 3 significant figures. How should this be reported?

83.3 MPa (3 sig figs).

Practice 7

How many significant figures does 0.00450 have?

Solution

3 (the trailing zero after the 5 is explicitly written, so it counts; the leading zeros do not).

Practice 8

Multiply \(2.5\times3.14159\) and report to appropriate significant figures (2.5 has 2 sig figs).

Solution

\(2.5\times3.14159=7.853975 \to \mathbf{7.9}\) (2 sig figs).

Practice 9

A length is measured as 12.400 m. How many significant figures does it have?

Solution

5 (all digits, including trailing zeros after a decimal point, are significant).

Practice 10

Explain why reporting a result as "83.276491 MPa" when your input data only had 3 sig figs is misleading.

Solution

It implies a level of certainty the original measurements never had. Carrying that many digits falsely suggests you know the answer to within millionths of a MPa, when the input data was only reliable to 3 significant figures.

Practice 11

During a multi-step calculation, should you round after every step, or only at the very end? Explain why.

Solution

Only at the very end. Rounding after every intermediate step introduces small errors that can compound and produce a noticeably wrong final answer. Keep full precision through the calculation and round only the final reported result.

3 The 6-step engineering method

Define → Data/Diagram → Theory → Estimate → Solve → Verify. Always loop back if a units, sign, or physics check fails.

Worked example

Apply the 6-step method to: "Find the tension in a rope holding a 20 kg mass at rest."

Define: the rope tension. Data/Diagram: \(m=20\text{ kg}\), \(g=9.8\), FBD showing \(W\) down, \(T\) up.

Theory: \(\Sigma F_y=0\) (static equilibrium). Estimate: roughly \(20\times10=200\text{ N}\).

Solve: \(T=W=20(9.8)=196\text{ N}\). Verify: close to the 200 N estimate, units are N (force), sign makes sense, checks out.

Practice 12

Why does "Estimate" come BEFORE "Solve" in the 6-step method?

Solution

A rough estimate gives something to sanity-check the precise answer against. Having it in advance prevents unconsciously adjusting the estimate to match whatever answer you calculate, it keeps the check honest.

Practice 13

A calculated answer comes out in the wrong units (expected N, got N·m). Which step catches this, and what should you do?

Solution

Caught at Verify. Loop back through the calculation (likely to Theory or Solve) to find where an extra length term was accidentally introduced.

Practice 14

Why is drawing the FBD placed under "Data/Diagram" rather than "Theory"?

Solution

The FBD is a visual organisation of the given information (what forces exist and where), part of correctly capturing the data, not yet applying the physics laws (equilibrium equations) that come next under Theory.

Practice 15

Apply the 6-step method (briefly, one line per step) to: "Find the acceleration of a 4 kg block pushed by a net force of 20 N."

Solution

Define: find acceleration. Data: \(m=4\text{ kg}, F=20\text{ N}\). Theory: \(\Sigma F=ma\). Estimate: roughly \(5\text{ m/s}^2\). Solve: \(a=20/4=5\text{ m/s}^2\). Verify: matches the estimate, units correct ✓.

4 Moments: ΣM=0 basics

A moment is force's rotational cousin, how much a force tends to twist something around a pivot.

Key idea

$$M=F\times d$$ where \(d\) is the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the force's line of action. Fix a sign convention (commonly counter-clockwise positive) before calculating. For rigid-body equilibrium: \(\Sigma M_O=0\) about any chosen point \(O\).

Worked example 1

A 50 N force is applied 3 m from a pivot, perpendicular to the lever arm. Find the moment.

\(M=50(3)=150\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

Worked example 2

A seesaw is balanced with a 40 kg child sitting 1.5 m from the pivot on one side. Where must a 30 kg child sit on the other side to balance it?

\(M_1=M_2\): \(40(9.8)(1.5)=30(9.8)(d)\), mass and \(g\) both cancel: \(40(1.5)=30d \Rightarrow d=2\text{ m}\).

Practice 16

A 100 N force acts 2 m from a pivot, perpendicular. Find the moment.

Solution

\(M=100(2)=200\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

Practice 17

A wrench applies 80 N at the end of a 0.3 m handle, perpendicular. Find the moment on the bolt.

Solution

\(M=80(0.3)=24\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

Practice 18

A 25 kg child sits 2 m from a seesaw's pivot. Find the distance for a 20 kg child on the other side to balance it.

Solution

\(25(2)=20d \Rightarrow d=2.5\text{ m}\).

Practice 19

Two moments act about a pivot: 60 N·m clockwise and 60 N·m counter-clockwise. Is the system in rotational equilibrium?

Solution

Yes. The two moments are equal and opposite, so \(\Sigma M=0\), satisfying rotational equilibrium.

Practice 20

Explain why the sign convention (clockwise vs counter-clockwise) must be fixed BEFORE calculating moments, not decided afterward.

Solution

Without a fixed convention, it's easy to accidentally add two moments that should actually be subtracted (or vice versa), since a moment's sign depends entirely on its rotational direction relative to the chosen convention. Deciding it first ensures every term in \(\Sigma M=0\) is combined correctly.

Practice 21

A tie bar carries 72 kN over an area of 450 mm². Compute stress in (i) N/mm² and (ii) MPa, then report to 3 significant figures.

Solution

Convert force: \(72\text{ kN}=72{,}000\text{ N}\).

\(\sigma=F/A=72{,}000/450=160\text{ N/mm}^2\).

Since \(1\text{ N/mm}^2=1\text{ MPa}\), stress is \(160\text{ MPa}\).

To 3 s.f.: \(\boxed{160\text{ N/mm}^2=160\text{ MPa}}\).

Practice 22

A plate area is given as \(0.00325\text{ m}^2\). Convert to mm², then find stress if load is 95 kN. Report final stress to 3 significant figures.

Solution

\(A=0.00325\times10^6=3250\text{ mm}^2\).

\(F=95\text{ kN}=95{,}000\text{ N}\).

\(\sigma=95{,}000/3250=29.2308\text{ N/mm}^2=29.2308\text{ MPa}\).

3 s.f. result: \(\boxed{29.2\text{ MPa}}\).

Practice 23

A pressure gauge reads 0.875 MPa. Convert this to Pa and kPa, then state which form is most practical for hand stress calculations with mm² areas.

Solution

\(0.875\text{ MPa}=0.875\times10^6\text{ Pa}=875{,}000\text{ Pa}\).

In kPa: \(875{,}000/1000=875\text{ kPa}\).

For hand calculations using N and mm², MPa is most practical because \(1\text{ MPa}=1\text{ N/mm}^2\).

\(\boxed{0.875\text{ MPa}=875{,}000\text{ Pa}=875\text{ kPa}}\).

Practice 24

A rod diameter is measured as 16.4 mm (3 s.f.). Load is 21.8 kN (3 s.f.). Find stress and report using correct significant figures.

Solution

\(A=\pi d^2/4=\pi(16.4)^2/4=211.241\text{ mm}^2\).

\(F=21.8\text{ kN}=21{,}800\text{ N}\).

\(\sigma=F/A=21{,}800/211.241=103.199\text{ N/mm}^2\).

Inputs are 3 s.f., so report 3 s.f.: \(\boxed{103\text{ MPa}}\).

Practice 25

Compute \((4.86\times10^3)(2.1\times10^{-2})\) and report to proper significant figures. Explain the sig-fig limit.

Solution

Raw value: \(4.86\times10^3 \times 2.1\times10^{-2}=102.06\).

First factor has 3 s.f., second has 2 s.f., so product must have 2 s.f.

\(102.06\to 1.0\times10^2\) (2 s.f.).

\(\boxed{1.0\times10^2}\).

Practice 26

An answer from your calculator is \(0.0034067\text{ m}\), but all measured inputs had 2 significant figures. Report the value correctly in mm.

Solution

Convert first: \(0.0034067\text{ m}=3.4067\text{ mm}\).

With 2 s.f. allowed, \(3.4067\to3.4\text{ mm}\).

Reporting 3.4067 mm would overstate measurement certainty.

\(\boxed{3.4\text{ mm}}\).

Practice 27

Apply the 6-step method to this case: "Find the force needed to produce \(55\text{ MPa}\) stress in a bar of area \(640\text{ mm}^2\)." Provide one concise line for each step and a final numerical answer.

Solution

Define: unknown force \(F\). Data/Diagram: \(\sigma=55\text{ MPa}\), \(A=640\text{ mm}^2\).

Theory: \(\sigma=F/A\Rightarrow F=\sigma A\). Estimate: about \(50\times600\approx30{,}000\text{ N}\).

Solve: \(F=55(640)=35{,}200\text{ N}=35.2\text{ kN}\).

Verify: units N/mm²×mm² = N; answer magnitude close to estimate.

\(\boxed{F=35.2\text{ kN}}\).

Practice 28

A student solves a stress problem and gets \(F=92\text{ N}\) for a member known to carry about 100 kN in service. Which 6-step check should fail immediately, and why?

Solution

The Estimate and Verify steps should fail immediately.

An estimate based on known scale (order of \(10^5\text{ N}\)) would expose 92 N as physically implausible.

Verify then forces a loop back to data/units, likely missed kN-to-N conversion or mm² to m² conversion.

\(\boxed{\text{Estimate/Verify catch the error before final reporting.}}\)

Practice 29

A simply-supported beam spans 5 m. A 300 N downward point load acts 2 m from the left support A. Find reactions at A and B.

Solution

Take moments about A (CCW positive): \(R_B(5)-300(2)=0\Rightarrow R_B=120\text{ N}\).

Vertical equilibrium: \(R_A+R_B-300=0\Rightarrow R_A=180\text{ N}\).

Check: \(180+120=300\), balances load.

\(\boxed{R_A=180\text{ N},\ R_B=120\text{ N}}\).

Practice 30

A 4.2 m beam has two downward point loads: 180 N at 1.0 m from A and 260 N at 3.4 m from A. Supports at A and B. Find reactions.

Solution

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(4.2)-180(1.0)-260(3.4)=0\).

\(R_B=(180+884)/4.2=253.33\text{ N}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A+R_B-180-260=0\Rightarrow R_A=440-253.33=186.67\text{ N}\).

\(\boxed{R_A\approx187\text{ N},\ R_B\approx253\text{ N}}\).

Practice 31

A bracket is held by two bolts 140 mm apart vertically. A horizontal force of 2.8 kN acts, creating a couple resisted by the bolt pair. Find the resisting force in each bolt (equal/opposite pair assumption).

Solution

Applied moment about bolt-group center: \(M=F d=2.8\text{ kN}\times140\text{ mm}=392\text{ kN}\cdot\text{mm}\).

For equal force pair \(T\) separated by 140 mm, resisting couple \(=T(140)\).

\(T=392/140=2.8\text{ kN}\).

\(\boxed{2.8\text{ kN tension/compression in each bolt line}}\).

Practice 32

A wrench applies 95 N at 25° to a 0.30 m handle. Find the effective perpendicular force component and resulting moment at the nut.

Solution

Only perpendicular component causes twist: \(F_\perp=F\sin25^\circ=95(0.4226)=40.15\text{ N}\).

Moment: \(M=F_\perp L=40.15(0.30)=12.05\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

Equivalent direct form: \(M=FL\sin25^\circ\), same result.

\(\boxed{F_\perp=40.2\text{ N},\ M=12.1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}\).

Practice 33

A 2.0 m signboard is hinged at the wall on the left and supported by a vertical cable at the right end. The board weight is 180 N acting at its center, plus a 120 N lamp at the far end. Find cable tension.

Solution

Take moments about hinge (CCW positive): \(T(2.0)-180(1.0)-120(2.0)=0\).

\(2T-180-240=0\Rightarrow2T=420\Rightarrow T=210\text{ N}\).

Then vertical force check gives hinge reaction if needed, but tension already found.

\(\boxed{T=210\text{ N}}\).

Practice 34

A lever balances when a 350 N load sits 0.18 m from pivot. Where must a 220 N effort be applied on the opposite side for equilibrium?

Solution

Set clockwise = counter-clockwise moments: \(350(0.18)=220(d)\).

\(d=63/220=0.28636\text{ m}\).

To 3 s.f., \(d=0.286\text{ m}=286\text{ mm}\).

\(\boxed{d=0.286\text{ m}}\).

Practice 35

Using clockwise positive, a point has moments: \(+45\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\), \(-18\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\), and \(-x\). Find \(x\) for rotational equilibrium and interpret its direction.

Solution

\(\Sigma M=0:\ 45-18-x=0\Rightarrow x=27\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

The term appears as \(-x\), so its physical direction is counter-clockwise in this sign convention.

\(\boxed{x=27\text{ N}\cdot\text{m (counter-clockwise)}}\).

Practice 36

A beam reaction was computed as \(R_B=3.8421\text{ kN}\) using measured loads with 2 significant figures. Report \(R_B\) correctly and justify the rounding.

Solution

Least precise inputs (2 s.f.) limit the reported answer to 2 s.f.

\(3.8421\text{ kN}\to3.8\text{ kN}\).

Reporting extra digits would imply unsupported precision in load data.

\(\boxed{R_B=3.8\text{ kN}}\).

Practice 37

For a cantilever of length 1.8 m with end load 420 N downward, find fixed-end moment and shear. Include sign using a clockwise-positive moment convention.

Solution

Vertical equilibrium at support: shear reaction \(V_A=420\text{ N}\) upward.

Moment at fixed end to balance load: \(M_A=420(1.8)=756\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

Load creates clockwise tendency about support, so support moment is counter-clockwise; with clockwise positive, \(M_A=-756\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

\(\boxed{V_A=420\text{ N},\ M_A=-756\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}\).

Practice 38

An engineer calculates a required area \(A=5.67\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^2\). Convert to mm², choose a practical rounded design value to 2 s.f., and state why you round up not down.

Solution

\(A=5.67\times10^{-4}\times10^6=567\text{ mm}^2\).

2 s.f. gives \(570\text{ mm}^2\).

As a required minimum area, rounding down could violate allowable stress. Conservatively choose equal or larger.

\(\boxed{A_{design}\approx570\text{ mm}^2\ \text{(minimum, round up in selection)}}\).

Practice 39

A 6-step check finds a final answer with correct magnitude but wrong units (\(\text{N}\cdot\text{m}\) instead of N). Which equation form was likely misapplied, and where in the method do you repair it?

Solution

The theory step likely used a moment equation (\(\Sigma M\)) where a force equation (\(\Sigma F\)) was required, or included an extra distance factor.

Repair loop starts at Verify, then back to Theory and Solve to rebuild equations with dimensional consistency.

\(\boxed{\text{Root cause: wrong governing equation type; fix via Verify}\rightarrow\text{Theory loop}}\).

Practice 40

A beam (span 7 m) carries UDL \(w=1.8\text{ kN/m}\) over full span plus a 6.0 kN point load at 2 m from A. Find reactions at A and B.

Solution

Total UDL load: \(W=wL=1.8(7)=12.6\text{ kN}\), acting at midspan (3.5 m from A).

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(7)-12.6(3.5)-6.0(2)=0\).

\(R_B=(44.1+12.0)/7=8.014\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A+R_B-12.6-6.0=0\Rightarrow R_A=18.6-8.014=10.586\text{ kN}\).

\(\boxed{R_A\approx10.6\text{ kN},\ R_B\approx8.01\text{ kN}}\).
