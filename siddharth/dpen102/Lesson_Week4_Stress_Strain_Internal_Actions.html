Stress, Strain & Internal Actions

External equilibrium tells you a structure isn't moving. It says nothing about whether the material inside can actually survive the forces running through it. Intro What this lesson is about & why it matters

Statics so far has asked: does the whole body balance? (\(\Sigma F=0\), \(\Sigma M=0\)). That is necessary, but not enough. A cable can be in perfect equilibrium and still snap if the force is concentrated on too little material. This week adds the missing layer: how hard the material itself is being asked to work.

Stress \(\sigma\) is force per unit area, how concentrated the load is on the cross-section. Strain \(\varepsilon\) is how much the material stretches or shortens as a fraction of its length. And when a beam or rod is loaded, three internal actions run through every cut: axial force \(N\), shear force \(V\), and bending moment \(M\). Find those, and you can size the member so it survives.

Equilibrium of the whole body is not enough: cut inside, find \(N,V,M\), then check stress in the material.

Why should I care?

Every cable, bolt, column, and beam is sized with these ideas. Too little area and stress exceeds what the material can take (yield or fracture). Too much area and you waste material and cost. The factor of safety sits between those extremes: it is the deliberate margin engineers leave for uncertain loads, imperfect analysis, and material variation. This lesson is the bridge from "the structure doesn't tip" to "the structure doesn't break."

0 Why external equilibrium isn't enough

A cable holding a 5-tonne load can be in perfect static equilibrium, \(\Sigma F=0\), right up until the instant it snaps. Equilibrium only checks whether a body as a whole is balanced; it says nothing about whether the material itself can withstand the forces passing through it. This week adds that missing layer: stress (how concentrated a force is over the material's cross-section) and internal actions (what forces and moments are actually running through any given point inside a loaded member).

1 Normal stress & strain

Stress is force spread over area, how concentrated the load is. Halve the area and you double the stress for the same force. Strain is how much the material actually deforms in response, as a fraction of its original length. Stress is what the load does to the material; strain is how the material answers.

Key idea

$$\sigma = \frac{F}{A} \quad(\text{normal stress, units Pa} = \text{N/m}^2), \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L_0} \quad(\text{strain, dimensionless}).$$

Tension (stretching) is conventionally positive; compression is negative. Strain has no units since it's a ratio of two lengths.

Worked example

A steel rod of diameter 10 mm carries an axial tensile load of 15,000 N. Its original length is 2 m, and it stretches by 1.2 mm. Find the stress and the strain.

\(A=\pi r^2 = \pi(0.005)^2 = 7.854\times10^{-5}\text{ m}^2\).

\(\sigma = \dfrac{15000}{7.854\times10^{-5}} = 190.9\times10^6\text{ Pa} = 190.9\text{ MPa}\).

\(\varepsilon = \dfrac{0.0012}{2} = 6\times10^{-4}\) (0.06%).

Axial bar in tension: equal and opposite forces \(F\) produce normal stress \(\sigma=F/A\) and stretch \(\Delta L\).

Practice 1

A bar of cross-sectional area 500 mm² carries an axial load of 40 kN. Find the stress.

Solution

\(\sigma = \dfrac{40000}{500\times10^{-6}} = 80\times10^6\text{ Pa} = 80\text{ MPa}\).

A cable of diameter 8 mm carries 12 kN tension. Find the stress.

Solution

\(A=\pi(0.004)^2=5.027\times10^{-5}\text{ m}^2\). \(\sigma=\dfrac{12000}{5.027\times10^{-5}}=238.7\text{ MPa}\).

Practice 3

A 3 m long rod stretches 2.4 mm under load. Find the strain.

Solution

\(\varepsilon = \dfrac{0.0024}{3}=8\times10^{-4}\).

Practice 4

A column of cross-sectional area 0.02 m² carries a compressive load of 500 kN. Find the stress.

Solution

\(\sigma = \dfrac{500000}{0.02}=25\times10^6\text{ Pa}=25\text{ MPa}\) (compressive).

Practice 5

A wire of original length 1.5 m is stretched to 1.5036 m. Find the strain.

Solution

\(\Delta L=0.0036\text{ m}\). \(\varepsilon = \dfrac{0.0036}{1.5}=0.0024\).

Practice 6

Two rods carry the same force \(F\). Rod A has twice the cross-sectional area of rod B. Which has higher stress, and by what factor?

Solution

Since \(\sigma=F/A\) and \(F\) is the same for both, the smaller area carries higher stress. Rod B has twice the stress of rod A.

2 Allowable stress design & factor of safety

Materials don't get loaded right up to their yield point in practice, a margin is always built in. The factor of safety (FoS) is exactly that margin: how many times stronger the material is than it strictly needs to be. Divide yield by FoS to get the allowable stress, then size the area so working stress stays under that ceiling.

Factor of safety turns yield strength into a safe design limit.

Key idea

$$\sigma_{allow} = \frac{\sigma_y}{FoS}, \qquad \text{design requires } \sigma_{work} \le \sigma_{allow}, \qquad A_{req} = \frac{F}{\sigma_{allow}}.$$

Worked example

A steel bracket must carry 80 kN. \(\sigma_y=250\text{ MPa}\), required \(FoS=2.5\). Find the required cross-sectional area, and the minimum diameter if circular.

\(\sigma_{allow}=250/2.5=100\text{ MPa}\).

\(A_{req}=80000/(100\times10^6)=8\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^2 = 800\text{ mm}^2\).

\(d=\sqrt{4A/\pi}=\sqrt{4(8\times10^{-4})/\pi}=0.0319\text{ m}=31.9\text{ mm}\).

Practice 7

A tie rod must carry 60 kN. \(\sigma_y=300\text{ MPa}\), \(FoS=3\). Find \(\sigma_{allow}\) and \(A_{req}\).

Solution

\(\sigma_{allow}=100\text{ MPa}\). \(A_{req}=60000/(100\times10^6)=6\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^2=600\text{ mm}^2\).

Practice 8

For Practice 7, find the minimum diameter if a circular rod is used.

Solution

\(d=\sqrt{4(6\times10^{-4})/\pi}=\sqrt{7.639\times10^{-4}}=0.02764\text{ m}=27.64\text{ mm}\).

Practice 9

A column, \(\sigma_y=350\text{ MPa}\), needs \(FoS=2\) while carrying 700 kN. Find \(A_{req}\).

Solution

\(\sigma_{allow}=175\text{ MPa}\). \(A_{req}=700000/(175\times10^6)=4\times10^{-3}\text{ m}^2=4000\text{ mm}^2\).

Practice 10

A cable has \(\sigma_y=400\text{ MPa}\) and is currently working at 150 MPa. Find the actual factor of safety in use.

Solution

\(FoS=\sigma_y/\sigma_{work}=400/150=2.67\).

Practice 11

A bolt with \(A=113\text{ mm}^2\) carries 20 kN. \(\sigma_y=240\text{ MPa}\). Find the actual factor of safety.

Solution

\(\sigma_{work}=20000/(113\times10^{-6})=176.99\text{ MPa}\). \(FoS=240/176.99=1.36\).

Practice 12

Design A uses \(FoS=2\), Design B uses \(FoS=4\), same material and load. Which requires more material, and why?

Solution

Design B. A higher \(FoS\) means a lower \(\sigma_{allow}\), which forces a larger required area (\(A_{req}=F/\sigma_{allow}\)) to carry the same load.

3 Internal actions at a cut: N, V, M

Stress needs a force to act on, but what force is actually running through the middle of a loaded beam? To find out, imagine cutting the member at that point and looking at just one side. Whatever the removed half was doing to hold the cut piece in equilibrium becomes three internal actions: axial force \(N\) (along the member), shear force \(V\) (perpendicular to it), and bending moment \(M\). Week 5 will plot \(V\) and \(M\) along the whole beam; here you learn to find them at one chosen cut.

The three internal actions exposed at a cut: axial force \(N\) pulls along the member, shear \(V\) slices across it, and bending moment \(M\) curves it.

Key idea

At any cut, isolate one side only, replace the removed portion with \(N\), \(V\), and \(M\), then solve using ordinary equilibrium: \(\Sigma F_x=0\) gives \(N\), \(\Sigma F_y=0\) gives \(V\), \(\Sigma M_{cut}=0\) gives \(M\).

Worked example

A simply-supported beam, 6 m long, carries a single 120 N downward load at its centre. Find \(N\), \(V\), and \(M\) just to the left of the load.

Reactions (symmetric load): \(R_A=R_B=60\text{ N}\).

Cut at \(x=3\text{ m}\) (just left of the load), keep the left segment: only \(R_A=60\text{ N}\) acts on it besides the cut itself.

\(\Sigma F_x=0\): \(N=0\) (no horizontal loads). \(\ \Sigma F_y=0\): \(V=R_A=60\text{ N}\).

\(\Sigma M_{cut}=0\): \(M=R_A\times3=60\times3=180\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

Method of sections: keep the left piece; \(V\) and \(M\) at the cut restore equilibrium.

Practice 13

A beam, span 4 m, symmetric central 200 N load (\(R_A=R_B=100\text{ N}\)). Find \(V\) and \(M\) just left of the load.

Solution

\(V=R_A=100\text{ N}\). \(M=R_A\times2=200\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

Practice 14

A cantilever, fixed at A, 3 m long, with a 90 N load at the free end. Find \(N\), \(V\), \(M\) at a cut 1 m from the fixed end (using the free-end side, 2 m long).

Solution

\(N=0\). \(\Sigma F_y\): \(V=90\text{ N}\). \(\Sigma M_{cut}\): \(M=90\times2=180\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

Practice 15

At a cut 2.5 m from the left support, the left segment's only external force is the 80 N reaction at the far end (nothing between). Find \(V\) and \(M\) at the cut.

Solution

\(V=80\text{ N}\). \(M=80\times2.5=200\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

Practice 16

Explain why the internal axial force \(N=0\) everywhere along a beam that carries only vertical loads and has only vertical support reactions.

Solution

With no horizontal external forces anywhere (all loads and reactions are vertical), \(\Sigma F_x=0\) forces \(N=0\) at every section, there's simply nothing horizontal to balance.

Practice 17

Explain why cutting a member and analysing only one side's FBD (not both) is enough to find the internal actions at that section.

Solution

By Newton's third law, whatever internal force and moment one side exerts on the other at the cut, the other side exerts back equally and oppositely. Since both sides individually must be in equilibrium, solving either one alone gives exactly the same \(N,V,M\) at that section, one side is sufficient.

Practice 18

A simply-supported beam, span 8 m, has a single point load 6 m from A. Find the reactions, then \(V\) and \(M\) just left of the load.

Solution

Let the load be \(P\). \(\Sigma M_A\): \(R_B(8)=P(6) \Rightarrow R_B=0.75P\), \(R_A=0.25P\). Just left of the load: \(V=R_A=0.25P\), \(M=R_A(6)=1.5P\). (Numerically, for \(P=200\text{ N}\): \(V=50\text{ N}\), \(M=300\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).)

4 Advanced: sizing from internal actions

Once you know the internal axial force \(N\) at a critical section, from method-of-sections or a beam cut, you can size that section exactly like a simple axial member: apply \(\sigma=N/A\) and the allowable-stress method from Section 2 directly. The only change is that \(F\) in the formulas becomes the internal \(N\) you just found. That is the full chain: cut → find \(N\) → check or size with \(\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}\).

Worked example

A tie rod carries an internal axial tension of \(N=45\text{ kN}\) at its critical section. \(\sigma_y=280\text{ MPa}\), required \(FoS=2.8\). Find the minimum diameter.

\(\sigma_{allow}=280/2.8=100\text{ MPa}\).

\(A_{req}=45000/(100\times10^6)=4.5\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^2=450\text{ mm}^2\).

\(d=\sqrt{4(450)/\pi}=\sqrt{572.96}=23.9\text{ mm}\).

Practice 19

A truss member carries an internal axial tension \(N=32\text{ kN}\). \(\sigma_y=250\text{ MPa}\), \(FoS=2.5\). Find \(A_{req}\) and the minimum diameter.

Solution

\(\sigma_{allow}=100\text{ MPa}\). \(A_{req}=32000/(100\times10^6)=3.2\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^2=320\text{ mm}^2\). \(d=\sqrt{4(320)/\pi}=20.19\text{ mm}\).

Practice 20

A column's internal axial compressive force is \(N=180\text{ kN}\). \(\sigma_y=300\text{ MPa}\), \(FoS=3\). Find \(A_{req}\).

Solution

\(\sigma_{allow}=100\text{ MPa}\). \(A_{req}=180000/(100\times10^6)=1.8\times10^{-3}\text{ m}^2=1800\text{ mm}^2\).

Practice 21

A hanger rod carries \(N=25\text{ kN}\). The cross-section is rectangular, 20 mm wide, \(\sigma_{allow}=125\text{ MPa}\). Find the required height.

Solution

\(A_{req}=25000/(125\times10^6)=2\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^2=200\text{ mm}^2\). Height \(=200/20=10\text{ mm}\).

Practice 22

A beam's maximum internal bending moment \(M\) occurs at midspan, while its maximum shear \(V\) occurs at the supports. Explain why a designer checking bending-related failure should evaluate stress at midspan, not at the supports.

Solution

Bending-induced normal stress is directly proportional to the local bending moment \(M\) (via the flexure formula), so the section with the largest \(M\), here, midspan, is where bending stress and bending failure risk peak. Shear stress is a separate failure mode that peaks where \(V\) is largest, at the supports, so each mode must be checked at its own critical section, not at the same location.

Practice 23

A 14 mm diameter steel rod carries 42 kN tension and extends by 1.05 mm over a 1.8 m gauge length. Find stress and strain.

Solution

\(A=\pi d^2/4=\pi(14^2)/4=153.94\text{ mm}^2\).

\(\sigma=F/A=42{,}000/153.94=272.8\text{ N/mm}^2=272.8\text{ MPa}\).

\(\varepsilon=\Delta L/L_0=1.05/1800=5.83\times10^{-4}\).

\(\boxed{\sigma\approx273\text{ MPa},\ \varepsilon=5.83\times10^{-4}}\).

Practice 24

A rectangular bar \(30\text{ mm}\times12\text{ mm}\) is in compression under 96 kN. Determine stress (with sign convention) and whether this exceeds an allowable compressive stress of 240 MPa.

Solution

Area \(A=30\times12=360\text{ mm}^2\).

\(\sigma=F/A=96{,}000/360=266.7\text{ MPa}\), compressive so \(\sigma=-266.7\text{ MPa}\).

Compare magnitude: \(266.7>240\text{ MPa}\), so allowable stress is exceeded.

\(\boxed{\sigma=-266.7\text{ MPa},\ \text{not acceptable}}\).

Practice 25

A member has area 650 mm² and allowable stress 125 MPa. Find maximum permissible axial load and report in kN.

Solution

\(F_{max}=\sigma_{allow}A=125\times650=81{,}250\text{ N}\).

In kN: \(81{,}250/1000=81.25\text{ kN}\).

\(\boxed{F_{max}=81.3\text{ kN} \text{ (3 s.f.)}}\).

Practice 26

A 2.4 m tie extends by 1.8 mm under service load. Find strain, then decide if it satisfies a strain limit of \(9.0\times10^{-4}\).

Solution

\(\varepsilon=\Delta L/L_0=1.8/2400=7.50\times10^{-4}\).

Compare to limit: \(7.50\times10^{-4} < 9.0\times10^{-4}\).

So strain criterion is satisfied.

\(\boxed{\varepsilon=7.50\times10^{-4},\ \text{acceptable}}\).

Practice 27

A bracket load is 125 kN. Material yield stress is 300 MPa and required FoS is 2.4. Find allowable stress and required area in mm².

Solution

\(\sigma_{allow}=\sigma_y/FoS=300/2.4=125\text{ MPa}\).

\(A_{req}=F/\sigma_{allow}=125{,}000/125=1000\text{ mm}^2\).

\(\boxed{\sigma_{allow}=125\text{ MPa},\ A_{req}=1000\text{ mm}^2}\).

Practice 28

For Practice 27, compute minimum circular diameter and select the next whole-millimeter size that is safe.

Solution

\(d=\sqrt{4A/\pi}=\sqrt{4(1000)/\pi}=35.68\text{ mm}\).

Nearest whole size not less than required is 36 mm.

Check area at 36 mm: \(A=\pi(36^2)/4=1017.9\text{ mm}^2\ge1000\), safe.

\(\boxed{d_{min}=35.7\text{ mm},\ \text{select }36\text{ mm}}\).

Practice 29

A rod of area 420 mm² carries 58 kN. Material yield stress is 260 MPa. Find working stress and actual FoS.

Solution

\(\sigma_{work}=F/A=58{,}000/420=138.1\text{ MPa}\).

\(FoS_{actual}=\sigma_y/\sigma_{work}=260/138.1=1.88\).

\(\boxed{\sigma_{work}=138\text{ MPa},\ FoS_{actual}=1.88}\).

Practice 30

A design currently uses FoS = 1.6 with \(\sigma_y=320\text{ MPa}\). Standard requires FoS = 2.5. By what factor must area increase for same load?

Solution

\(\sigma_{allow}=\sigma_y/FoS\), and \(A_{req}=F/\sigma_{allow}\propto FoS\).

So area scale factor \(=FoS_{new}/FoS_{old}=2.5/1.6=1.5625\).

Area must increase by \(56.25\%\).

\(\boxed{A_{new}=1.5625A_{old}\ (\text{56.3\% increase})}\).

Practice 31

A simply-supported beam of span 6 m carries a 180 N point load at 2 m from A. Find reactions, then determine \(V\) and \(M\) at a cut 1.5 m from A (left segment method).

Solution

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(6)-180(2)=0\Rightarrow R_B=60\text{ N}\), so \(R_A=120\text{ N}\).

Cut at \(x=1.5\text{ m}\) is left of the point load, so only \(R_A\) acts on left segment.

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ V=R_A=120\text{ N}\).

\(\Sigma M_{cut}=0:\ M=R_A(1.5)=180\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

\(\boxed{V=120\text{ N},\ M=180\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}\).

Practice 32

For the same beam as Practice 31, find \(V\) and \(M\) at a cut 4.5 m from A using the left segment.

Solution

At \(x=4.5\text{ m}\), left segment includes \(R_A=120\text{ N}\) and the 180 N downward load at \(x=2\text{ m}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ V=120-180=-60\text{ N}\) (downward on left-face convention).

\(\Sigma M_{cut}=0:\ M=120(4.5)-180(4.5-2)=540-450=90\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

\(\boxed{V=-60\text{ N},\ M=90\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}\).

Practice 33

A cantilever of length 2.8 m carries an end load of 240 N downward. At a cut 1.0 m from the fixed end, find internal \(N\), \(V\), and \(M\) using the free-end segment.

Solution

Free-end segment length beyond cut is \(2.8-1.0=1.8\text{ m}\).

No horizontal loads, so \(N=0\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0\Rightarrow V=240\text{ N}\).

\(\Sigma M_{cut}=0\Rightarrow M=240(1.8)=432\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

\(\boxed{N=0,\ V=240\text{ N},\ M=432\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}\).

Practice 34

A section cut in a beam gives internal actions \(N=18\text{ kN}\) tension and \(V=24\text{ kN}\). Find the resultant internal force magnitude at that section and its angle to the beam axis.

Solution

Resultant internal force \(R=\sqrt{N^2+V^2}=\sqrt{18^2+24^2}=30\text{ kN}\).

Angle to beam axis: \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(V/N)=\tan^{-1}(24/18)=53.13^\circ\).

\(\boxed{R=30\text{ kN at }53.1^\circ\text{ to beam axis}}\).

Practice 35

A beam section has internal bending moment \(M=14\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}\). Explain with a brief equilibrium statement why replacing the removed half by a pure force only (without a moment) cannot satisfy section equilibrium in general.

Solution

For equilibrium of the kept segment, both \(\Sigma F=0\) and \(\Sigma M=0\) must hold at the cut.

A pure force at the cut can balance translational effects, but it cannot represent an independent net couple from external loads.

When external loads create nonzero section moment demand, an internal couple \(M\) is required as a separate unknown.

\(\boxed{\text{Internal }M\text{ is essential whenever }\Sigma M_{cut}\neq0\text{ from external forces}}\).

Practice 36

A simply-supported beam (span 8 m) carries a UDL of \(50\text{ N/m}\) over the full span. Find reactions, then \(V\) and \(M\) at midspan \(x=4\text{ m}\). Include a compact section sketch to justify the cut loading.

Solution

Total load \(W=wL=50(8)=400\text{ N}\), symmetric, so \(R_A=R_B=200\text{ N}\).

Left segment at midspan carries UDL over 4 m: equivalent \(w(4)=200\text{ N}\) acting 2 m from A.

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ V=R_A-200=0\text{ N}\).

\(\Sigma M_{cut}=0:\ M=R_A(4)-200(2)=800-400=400\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

\(\boxed{R_A=R_B=200\text{ N},\ V(4)=0,\ M(4)=400\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}\).

Practice 37

A tie member at a critical section carries internal axial force \(N=84\text{ kN}\). Steel yield is 350 MPa and FoS required is 2.8. Find minimum required area and diameter.

Solution

\(\sigma_{allow}=350/2.8=125\text{ MPa}\).

\(A_{req}=N/\sigma_{allow}=84{,}000/125=672\text{ mm}^2\).

\(d=\sqrt{4A/\pi}=\sqrt{4(672)/\pi}=29.25\text{ mm}\).

\(\boxed{A_{req}=672\text{ mm}^2,\ d_{min}=29.3\text{ mm}}\).

Practice 38

A compression member currently has area 1800 mm² and carries \(N=210\text{ kN}\). With \(\sigma_y=300\text{ MPa}\), find actual FoS and required extra area to meet FoS = 3.

Solution

\(\sigma_{work}=210{,}000/1800=116.67\text{ MPa}\).

Actual \(FoS=300/116.67=2.57\).

Required allowable at FoS 3 is \(100\text{ MPa}\), so \(A_{req}=210{,}000/100=2100\text{ mm}^2\).

Extra area \(=2100-1800=300\text{ mm}^2\).

\(\boxed{FoS_{actual}=2.57,\ \Delta A_{needed}=300\text{ mm}^2}\).

Practice 39

A section cut in a member under combined actions gives \(N=40\text{ kN}\) tension and bending moment \(M=9\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}\). Explain why checking only axial stress \(\sigma=N/A\) could be unconservative.

Solution

\(\sigma=N/A\) captures only uniform direct stress from axial force.

Nonzero \(M\) creates additional bending stress distribution (tension on one face, compression on the other) that can significantly raise peak stress.

Ignoring \(M\) may underpredict the maximum fiber stress and overestimate safety.

\(\boxed{\text{Combined }N+M\text{ checks are required for reliable design.}}\)

Practice 40

A simply-supported beam, span 10 m, carries a single 500 N load at 4 m from A. Find \(R_A, R_B\), then internal actions \(N,V,M\) at a cut 3 m from A.

Solution

\(\Sigma M_A: R_B(10)-500(4)=0\Rightarrow R_B=200\text{ N}\), so \(R_A=300\text{ N}\).

Cut at \(x=3\text{ m}\) is left of load, so left segment has only \(R_A\).

\(N=0\) (no horizontal loads), \(V=R_A=300\text{ N}\).

\(M=R_A(3)=900\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

\(\boxed{R_A=300\text{ N},\ R_B=200\text{ N},\ N=0,\ V=300\text{ N},\ M=900\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}\).

Practice 41

For the same beam in Practice 40, find \(N,V,M\) at a cut 7 m from A (left segment method).

Solution

Left segment includes \(R_A=300\text{ N}\) and the 500 N load at \(x=4\text{ m}\).

\(N=0\).

\(\Sigma F_y:\ V=300-500=-200\text{ N}\).

\(\Sigma M_{cut}:\ M=300(7)-500(7-4)=2100-1500=600\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

\(\boxed{N=0,\ V=-200\text{ N},\ M=600\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}\).

Practice 42

A steel tie has chosen diameter 24 mm and must safely carry 50 kN. Yield stress is 280 MPa. Find achieved FoS and judge if it meets a minimum FoS requirement of 2.0.

Solution

Area \(A=\pi(24^2)/4=452.39\text{ mm}^2\).

\(\sigma_{work}=50{,}000/452.39=110.52\text{ MPa}\).

\(FoS=280/110.52=2.53\).

Since \(2.53>2.0\), requirement is satisfied.

\(\boxed{FoS=2.53,\ \text{design passes}}\).

Practice 43

A rod initially 1.2 m long experiences strain \(1.1\times10^{-3}\) in tension. Find extension in mm, then find final length.

Solution

\(\Delta L=\varepsilon L_0=(1.1\times10^{-3})(1.2)=0.00132\text{ m}=1.32\text{ mm}\).

Final length \(L=L_0+\Delta L=1200+1.32=1201.32\text{ mm}\).

\(\boxed{\Delta L=1.32\text{ mm},\ L=1201.32\text{ mm}}\).

Practice 44

A 6 m beam carries two equal point loads of 160 N located 1.5 m from each support. Find reactions, then compute the maximum internal bending moment (value and location).

Solution

Loads are symmetric, so \(R_A=R_B=(160+160)/2=160\text{ N}\).

Between the two loads (from \(x=1.5\) to \(x=4.5\) m), shear is zero because left load exactly cancels \(R_A\).

Thus bending moment is constant and maximal in that region: \(M=R_A(1.5)=160(1.5)=240\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).

\(\boxed{M_{max}=240\text{ N}\cdot\text{m},\ \text{anywhere between the two loads (including midspan)}}\).

Where to go from here. Section 3's internal-actions method, cutting a beam to find \(V\) and \(M\) at one point, is exactly what Week 5 extends into full shear force and bending moment diagrams, finding \(V(x)\) and \(M(x)\) at every point along the beam, not just one cut.
