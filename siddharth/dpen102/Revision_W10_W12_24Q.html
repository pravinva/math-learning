Before you start · what we studied in Weeks 10–12

Week 10 — Integrated statics practice

Topics

Multi-step problems that chain reactions → internal \(V,M\) → stress checks in one workflow.

Main ideas

  • Exam questions rarely stay in one week — they combine equilibrium with stress.
  • Order matters: diagram and reactions first, then internals, then design check.
  • Global checks (reaction sum = total load) catch most arithmetic errors early.

Remember

  • If \(R_A+R_B\neq\) total downward load, stop and debug before going further.
  • State the critical section before computing \(M\) or \(\sigma\).
  • Keep one sign convention for the whole solution.
Key formulas: \(\Sigma M_A=0\Rightarrow R_B\); \(M(x)\) from left segment; \(\sigma=Mc/I\) or \(F/A\).

Week 11 — Design blocks

Topics

Pass/fail stress checks; max safe load; required area/diameter; composite sections; shape vs stiffness.

Main ideas

  • A design block is: load + material + FoS → section → \(\sigma_{\mathrm{work}}\) vs \(\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}\) → pass/fail → redesign.
  • Pure axial needs only \(A\); bending needs centroid, \(I\), and \(c\).
  • Same area, different shape → same axial capacity, different bending capacity.

Remember

  • Report margin as a percentage, not only a raw MPa difference.
  • "Design fails" can happen well below yield — that is what FoS is for.
  • Choose the next standard diameter up from \(d_{\min}\).
Key formulas: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}=\sigma_y/\mathrm{FoS}\); \(F_{\max}=\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}A\); \(A_{\mathrm{req}}=F/\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}\); \(d=\sqrt{4A/\pi}\); \(\sigma=Mc/I\).

Week 12 — Exam synthesis & triage

Topics

Classifying problem type; solution templates; verification checklists; writing a clear pass/fail verdict.

Main ideas

  • First ask: what is demanded, and which model applies (equilibrium / stress / energy)?
  • Box the sign convention before computing moments.
  • Algebraically consistent answers can still be wrong — check magnitude and units.

Remember

  • Pre-submit: diagram, units, signs, equilibrium back-check, boxed answer.
  • Tiny margin (e.g. 2%) formally passes but is practically fragile — say so.
  • Template: computed \(\sigma_{\mathrm{work}}\) vs \(\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}\) → pass/fail with margin %.
Key habit: classify → diagram → equations → solve → verify (units, \(\Sigma F\), \(\Sigma M\), realism).
Q1 W10 Simply supported beam of span 12 m with a 4 kN/m UDL over the full span. Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions.
Loaded beam — full-span UDL
Sign convention

Up positive, moments about A anticlockwise positive.

Working

Total load \(W=wL=4(12)=48\text{ kN}\) at \(x=6\text{ m}\).

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(12)=48(6)=288\Rightarrow R_B=24\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A=48-24=24\text{ kN}\).

Symmetry confirms equal reactions.

Answer: \(R_A=R_B=24\text{ kN}\)
Q2 W10 For Q1, sketch the bending moment diagram and find the maximum bending moment.
BMD — parabola peaking at midspan
Sign convention

Sagging positive; moment is zero at both simple supports.

Working

Shear crosses zero at midspan, so the moment peaks there.

\(M_{max}=wL^2/8\).

\(=4(12)^2/8=4(144)/8=576/8\).

\(M_{max}=72\text{ kN·m}\).

Answer: \(M_{max}=72\text{ kN·m}\)
Q3 W11 The section at Q2 has \(I=45\times10^6\) mm⁴ and \(c=120\) mm. Draw the stress distribution and find the bending stress.
Bending stress — section and linear distribution
Sign convention

Work in N and mm so the answer lands directly in MPa.

Working

\(M=72\text{ kN·m}=72\times10^6\text{ N·mm}\).

\(\sigma=Mc/I=(72\times10^6)(120)/(45\times10^6)\).

\(=(8.64\times10^9)/(45\times10^6)\).

\(\sigma=192\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma=192\text{ MPa}\)
Q4 W11 If the allowable stress is 160 MPa, draw the comparison and give the verdict for Q3.
Design check — working stress against allowable
Sign convention

The bar must finish left of the dashed allowable line to pass.

Working

\(\sigma_{work}=192\text{ MPa}\), \(\sigma_{allow}=160\text{ MPa}\).

Overstress \(=(192-160)/160=20\%\).

Remedy: increase \(I\), reduce \(c\) relative to \(I\), or reduce load/span.

Answer: Fail — overstressed by 20%
Q5 W11 Under 58 kN tension with \(\sigma_{allow}=145\) MPa, draw the section and find the required area and equivalent diameter.
Cross-section — sizing a circular tie
Sign convention

Size from the equality, then round the diameter up to a stock size.

Working

\(A_{req}=F/\sigma_{allow}=58000/145=400\text{ mm}^2\).

\(d=\sqrt{4A/\pi}=\sqrt{4(400)/\pi}=\sqrt{509.3}\).

\(d=22.57\text{ mm}\).

Answer: \(A_{req}=400\text{ mm}^2\), \(d=22.57\text{ mm}\)
Q6 W11 Material has \(\sigma_y=360\) MPa and FoS 3.2. Draw the stress scale and find the allowable stress.
Stress scale — yield reduced by the factor of safety
Sign convention

Factor of safety divides yield down to a working limit.

Working

\(\sigma_{allow}=\sigma_y/\text{FoS}\).

\(=360/3.2\).

\(\sigma_{allow}=112.5\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma_{allow}=112.5\text{ MPa}\)
Q7 W10 Beam of span 10 m with 20 kN at 3 m and 15 kN at 8 m. Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions.
Loaded beam — two point loads
Sign convention

Moments about A, anticlockwise positive, reactions assumed upward.

Working

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(10)=20(3)+15(8)=60+120=180\).

\(R_B=18\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A=35-18=17\text{ kN}\).

Check: \(17+18=35\text{ kN}\) matches the total load.

Answer: \(R_A=17\text{ kN}\), \(R_B=18\text{ kN}\)
Q8 W10 For Q7, draw the left segment and find the moment at \(x=5\) m.
Cut free body — left segment at x = 5 m
Sign convention

Only loads left of the cut enter; sagging moment positive.

Working

Reaction term: \(R_A(5)=17(5)=85\text{ kN·m}\).

The 20 kN load lies 2 m left of the cut: \(20(5-3)=20(2)=40\text{ kN·m}\).

\(M(5)=85-40\).

\(M(5)=45\text{ kN·m}\). The 15 kN load at 8 m is right of the cut.

Answer: \(M(5)=45\text{ kN·m}\)
Q9 W11 The Q8 location has \(I=30\times10^6\) mm⁴ and \(c=90\) mm. Draw the stress distribution and find the stress.
Bending stress — section at the critical cut
Sign convention

Extreme fibre at distance c carries the maximum bending stress.

Working

\(M=45\times10^6\text{ N·mm}\).

\(\sigma=(45\times10^6)(90)/(30\times10^6)\).

\(=(4.05\times10^9)/(30\times10^6)\).

\(\sigma=135\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma=135\text{ MPa}\)
Q10 W12 Sketch the triage map and give the first two questions to ask when reading a new exam problem.
Method map — triage before algebra
The two triage questions

1. What is the demanded output? A reaction, an internal force, a stress, a safety verdict, or a speed?

2. Which governing model comes first? Equilibrium, a constitutive stress check, or an energy balance?

Answering these two picks the diagram you must draw, and the diagram then drives the equations.

Answer: (1) What output is demanded? (2) Which governing model applies first?
Q11 W12 Draw the sign-convention box and explain why you should fix it before computing moments.
Sign convention — fix it once, use it everywhere
Sign convention

Up positive, anticlockwise positive, sagging positive — declared before any algebra.

Working

Moments change sign depending on the direction you call positive.

If the convention drifts between equations, terms silently cancel that should add (and vice versa).

A declared convention also makes a negative answer meaningful: it says "opposite to my assumed direction", not "I made a mistake".

Answer: It keeps every rotational term consistent, so the equations cannot contradict one another
Q12 W12 Draw the FBD and say what it implies if reactions do not sum to the external load.
Global FBD — the equilibrium audit
Sign convention

\(\Sigma F_y=0\) is a hard constraint, not a guideline.

Working

Global vertical equilibrium demands \(R_A+R_B=\) total downward load.

A mismatch means at least one of: a wrong moment equation, a sign slip, a mis-read load magnitude, or a mis-placed lever arm.

Every downstream shear, moment and stress inherits the error, so there is no value in continuing.

Answer: At least one equation, sign or load is wrong — stop and fix global equilibrium before going any further
Q13 Challenge A section of area 5200 mm² carries 420 kN axial with \(\sigma_y=300\) MPa and FoS 2.5. Draw the check and give pass/fail with the margin.
Design check — working stress, allowable and margin
Sign convention

Margin measured against the allowable, not against yield.

Working

\(\sigma_{work}=F/A=420000/5200=80.77\text{ MPa}\).

\(\sigma_{allow}=\sigma_y/\text{FoS}=300/2.5=120\text{ MPa}\).

\(80.77 \lt 120\), so the section passes.

Margin \(=(120-80.77)/120=32.7\%\).

Answer: Pass, with a 32.7% margin on the allowable stress
Q14 Challenge Beam of span 9 m with a 2.4 kN/m UDL over the full span plus 12 kN at 6 m. Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions.
Loaded beam — UDL plus point load
Sign convention

Superpose the UDL resultant and the point load, moments about A.

Working

UDL resultant \(=2.4(9)=21.6\text{ kN}\) at \(x=4.5\text{ m}\).

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(9)=21.6(4.5)+12(6)=97.2+72=169.2\).

\(R_B=18.8\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A=(21.6+12)-18.8=33.6-18.8=14.8\text{ kN}\).

Answer: \(R_A=14.8\text{ kN}\), \(R_B=18.8\text{ kN}\)
Q15 Challenge For Q14, draw the left segment and find the moment at 4 m.
Cut free body — left segment at x = 4 m
Sign convention

UDL on the isolated piece acts at the midpoint of that piece.

Working

Reaction term: \(R_A(4)=14.8(4)=59.2\text{ kN·m}\).

UDL on the segment: \(2.4(4)=9.6\text{ kN}\) at 2 m from the cut.

UDL term: \(9.6(2)=19.2\text{ kN·m}\).

\(M(4)=59.2-19.2=40.0\text{ kN·m}\); the 12 kN load at 6 m is right of the cut.

Answer: \(M(4)=40.0\text{ kN·m}\)
Q16 Challenge The Q15 section has \(I=22\times10^6\) mm⁴ and \(c=100\) mm. Draw the distribution and find \(\sigma_b\).
Bending stress — section at x = 4 m
Sign convention

Convert to N and mm first.

Working

\(M=40\times10^6\text{ N·mm}\).

\(\sigma=(40\times10^6)(100)/(22\times10^6)\).

\(=(4\times10^9)/(22\times10^6)\).

\(\sigma=181.8\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma_b=181.8\text{ MPa}\)
Q17 Challenge The allowable for the Q16 material is 170 MPa. Draw the comparison and find the percentage overstress.
Design check — overstress measured against the allowable
Sign convention

Percentage overstress is measured relative to the allowable value.

Working

Excess \(=181.8-170=11.8\text{ MPa}\).

Overstress \(=11.8/170\).

\(=6.94\%\).

Small but real: the section still fails and must be revised.

Answer: Overstressed by 6.94%
Q18 Challenge Design target: carry 95 kN with \(\sigma_{allow}=125\) MPa. Draw the candidate areas and choose the minimum standard diameter from [28, 30, 32] mm.
Sizing chart — candidate areas against the requirement
Sign convention

Bars must reach past the dashed requirement line, so here bigger is better.

Working

\(A_{req}=F/\sigma_{allow}=95000/125=760\text{ mm}^2\).

\(d_{req}=\sqrt{4A/\pi}=\sqrt{4(760)/\pi}=31.11\text{ mm}\).

\(d=28\) gives 615.8 mm² and \(d=30\) gives 706.9 mm² — both short of 760.

\(d=32\) gives 804.2 mm², which clears the requirement.

Answer: Choose the 32 mm diameter
Q19 Challenge A solution reports \(R_A=-4\) kN upward for an all-downward-loaded simply supported beam. Draw it and say what to check first.
FBD — a negative reaction means a downward arrow
Sign convention

A negative result means the true arrow points opposite to the assumed direction.

Working

A negative reaction is physically legitimate only when the support has to hold the beam down (uplift), for example with overhangs or upward loads.

With all loads downward between two simple supports, both reactions must be upward.

So check the \(\Sigma M\) equation signs and the load positions first, then confirm the total load and lever arms.

Answer: Check the moment equation signs and load positions — on a plain simply supported beam with downward loads a negative reaction signals an error
Q20 Challenge Draw the magnitude ruler and explain why a "reasonable magnitude" check is essential even after algebraically correct work.
Reasonableness ruler — does the number belong?
Sign convention

Compare the answer with the range you know real materials occupy.

Working

Algebra can be internally perfect and still be fed wrong inputs or mixed units.

A kN/m mix-up shifts a result by \(10^3\); an m/mm mix-up can shift it by \(10^3\) or \(10^6\).

Those errors leave the equations looking correct, so only a magnitude check catches them.

Answer: Reasonableness catches the 10×–1000× scaling errors that pure symbol manipulation cannot
Q21 Challenge Draw the one-line triage for the prompt "Find if the beam is safe under the given loading."
Method map — safety check in four moves
Sign convention

Never skip forward: each box needs the previous one.

Working

Reactions come first because internal actions depend on them.

Then locate the critical section — usually where shear crosses zero.

Compute the bending stress there with \(\sigma=Mc/I\).

Finish with a comparison against the allowable, stating the margin.

Answer: Reactions → critical internal moment → \(\sigma=Mc/I\) → compare with allowable and state the pass/fail margin
Q22 Challenge A member has \(\sigma_{work}=98\) MPa and \(\sigma_{allow}=100\) MPa. Draw the check and say whether this is automatically good design.
Design check — a pass with almost no headroom
Sign convention

Passing the inequality is necessary, not sufficient.

Working

\(98 \lt 100\), so formally the design passes.

Headroom \(=(100-98)/100=2\%\).

Real loads vary, material properties scatter, and fabrication tolerances shift the section properties.

A 2% margin can vanish with a modest load increase or a slightly undersized member.

Answer: It passes formally, but 2% headroom is thin — discuss load variability and uncertainty before calling it good design
Q23 W12 Draw the pre-submit checklist and list five items on it.
Pre-submit checklist
The five items

A complete, labelled diagram — markers award it and it prevents errors.

Unit consistency, checked by dimensional back-substitution.

Sign convention consistency across every equation.

An equilibrium back-check on the reactions.

A boxed final answer with units and a one-line interpretation.

Answer: Diagram → units → signs → equilibrium back-check → boxed answer with interpretation
Q24 Capstone Sketch the answer structure and write a compact template sentence for any pass/fail result.
Answer template — model, numbers, verdict
The template

Name the model so the marker knows which theory you applied.

Quote the computed working value with units.

Quote the limit you compared against, and where it came from.

State the verdict together with the margin as a percentage.

Answer: "Using the \(X\) model, \(\sigma_{work}=\ldots\) MPa against \(\sigma_{allow}=\ldots\) MPa, so the section passes/fails with a margin of \(\ldots\)%."
