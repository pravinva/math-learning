Before you start · what we studied in Weeks 7–9

Week 7 — Friction systems

Topics

Static vs kinetic friction; inclines and angle of repose; wedges; belt/capstan friction.

Main ideas

  • Friction is whatever is needed up to \(F_s^{\max}=\mu_s N\); once sliding, use \(F_k=\mu_k N\).
  • On an incline, \(N=W\cos\theta\) and the downslope pull is \(W\sin\theta\).
  • Belt friction grows exponentially with wrap angle: \(T_2/T_1=e^{\mu\beta}\).

Remember

  • Decide the friction state first (stick / impending / slide).
  • Angle of repose: \(\tan\theta_r=\mu_s\) (mass cancels).
  • \(\beta\) must be in radians in the belt formula.
Key formulas: \(F_s^{\max}=\mu_s N\); \(F_k=\mu_k N\); \(\tan\theta_r=\mu_s\); \(T_2/T_1=e^{\mu\beta}\).

Week 8 — Centroids & second moment of area

Topics

Composite centroids (add/subtract); \(I\) for standard shapes; parallel-axis theorem.

Main ideas

  • Centroid is the area-weighted balance point of a shape.
  • Holes are negative area in both centroid and \(I\) calculations.
  • \(I\) measures bending stiffness; material far from the axis helps most (\(Ad^2\)).

Remember

  • Find the composite centroid before transferring \(I\) to a shared axis.
  • Same area, different shape → same axial capacity, very different \(I\).
  • I-beams put material in the flanges for a reason.
Key formulas: \(\bar x=\sum A_ix_i/\sum A_i\); \(I=bh^3/12\) (rectangle); \(I=I_c+Ad^2\).

Week 9 — Work, energy & conservation

Topics

Work–energy theorem; gravitational and spring potential; conservation with or without friction losses.

Main ideas

  • Net work equals change in kinetic energy: \(W_{\mathrm{net}}=\Delta K\).
  • Conservative forces store energy as \(U_g=mgh\) or \(U_s=\tfrac12 kx^2\); path does not matter.
  • Friction removes mechanical energy: include \(W_{\mathrm{nc}}\) (usually negative).

Remember

  • Pick two clear states; list every energy form at each end.
  • KE can never be negative — a negative result means the object stopped earlier.
  • From rest falling height \(h\): \(v=\sqrt{2gh}\) (no losses).
Key formulas: \(K=\tfrac12 mv^2\); \(U_g=mgh\); \(U_s=\tfrac12 kx^2\); \(K_1+U_1+W_{\mathrm{nc}}=K_2+U_2\).
Q1 W7 A 20 kg block sits on an 18° incline with \(\mu_s=0.30\). Draw the FBD and decide whether it moves.
FBD — block on an incline
Sign convention

Axes along and perpendicular to the slope. Friction can only supply up to \(\mu_s N\).

Working

Weight \(W=mg=20(9.8)=196\text{ N}\).

Down-slope demand: \(W\sin18^\circ=196(0.309)=60.58\text{ N}\).

Normal: \(N=W\cos18^\circ=196(0.951)=186.43\text{ N}\).

Friction capacity: \(F_{s,max}=\mu_s N=0.30(186.43)=55.93\text{ N}\).

\(60.58 \gt 55.93\), so friction cannot hold it.

Answer: Yes — the block slides (demand exceeds friction capacity by 4.65 N)
Q2 W7 For Q1 with \(\mu_k=0.22\), draw the FBD with the net force and find the acceleration.
FBD — sliding block, kinetic friction
Sign convention

Once sliding, friction is fixed at \(f_k=\mu_k N\) and acts up the slope (opposing motion).

Working

\(f_k=\mu_k N=0.22(186.43)=41.01\text{ N}\).

Net down-slope force \(=60.58-41.01=19.57\text{ N}\).

\(a=F_{net}/m=19.57/20\).

\(a=0.978\text{ m/s}^2\) down the slope.

Answer: \(a=0.978\text{ m/s}^2\)
Q3 W7 Belt friction with \(\mu=0.28\), wrap \(=210^\circ\), \(T_1=160\) N. Draw the wrap diagram and find \(T_2\).
Belt over a drum — wrap angle and tensions
Sign convention

Tight side is the larger tension; the wrap angle must be in radians.

Working

Convert: \(\beta=210^\circ=210\times\pi/180=3.665\text{ rad}\).

\(\mu\beta=0.28(3.665)=1.026\).

\(T_2=T_1e^{\mu\beta}=160e^{1.026}=160(2.791)\).

\(T_2=446.6\text{ N}\).

Answer: \(T_2=446.6\text{ N}\)
Q4 W7 The angle of repose is observed as 27°. Draw the impending-motion FBD and estimate \(\mu_s\).
FBD — block on the verge of sliding
Sign convention

Angle of repose is the steepest slope at which the block is still just in equilibrium.

Working

Along the slope: \(mg\sin\theta=\mu_s N\).

Perpendicular: \(N=mg\cos\theta\).

Substitute: \(mg\sin\theta=\mu_s mg\cos\theta\Rightarrow\mu_s=\tan\theta\).

\(\mu_s=\tan27^\circ=0.510\).

Answer: \(\mu_s=0.510\)
Q5 W7 Draw the incline FBD and explain why \(N\neq mg\) on a slope.
FBD — weight resolved along and across the slope
Sign convention

Resolve the weight into components along and perpendicular to the surface, not into horizontal and vertical.

Working

The normal force acts perpendicular to the contact surface, not vertically.

Perpendicular equilibrium only involves the perpendicular slice of the weight: \(N=mg\cos\theta\).

The remaining slice \(mg\sin\theta\) is left for friction (or acceleration) to deal with.

Only when \(\theta=0\) does \(\cos\theta=1\) and \(N=mg\).

Answer: \(N\) balances only the perpendicular component, so \(N=mg\cos\theta\), which is less than \(mg\) on any slope
Q6 W7 A 120 N pull at 30° above horizontal acts on a 25 kg block with \(\mu_s=0.4\). Draw the FBD and find the maximum static friction.
FBD — block with an inclined pull
Sign convention

Up positive. The vertical component of the pull unloads the surface.

Working

Weight \(=mg=25(9.8)=245\text{ N}\).

Vertical pull component \(=P\sin30^\circ=120(0.5)=60\text{ N}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ N=245-60=185\text{ N}\).

\(F_{s,max}=\mu_s N=0.4(185)=74\text{ N}\).

Answer: \(F_{s,max}=74\text{ N}\)
Q7 W8 Composite centroid: \(A_1=1200\) at \(x=20\), \(A_2=800\) at \(x=70\). Draw the composite and find \(\bar x\).
Composite area — parts and their centroids
Sign convention

First moment of area about the datum: \(\bar x=\Sigma A_ix_i/\Sigma A_i\).

Working

Numerator: \(1200(20)+800(70)=24000+56000=80000\).

Denominator: \(1200+800=2000\text{ mm}^2\).

\(\bar x=80000/2000=40\text{ mm}\).

Sanity check: 40 mm lies between 20 and 70, closer to the larger area.

Answer: \(\bar x=40\text{ mm}\)
Q8 W8 Add a hole \(A_3=-300\) at \(x=50\). Redraw the composite and recompute \(\bar x\).
Composite area — hole shown dashed (negative area)
Sign convention

A hole enters with a negative area in both the numerator and the denominator.

Working

Numerator: \(1200(20)+800(70)-300(50)=24000+56000-15000=65000\).

Denominator: \(1200+800-300=1700\text{ mm}^2\).

\(\bar x=65000/1700=38.24\text{ mm}\).

The centroid shifts left because material was removed from the right of it.

Answer: \(\bar x=38.24\text{ mm}\)
Q9 W8 Rectangle \(b=80\) mm, \(h=200\) mm. Draw the section with its centroidal axis and find \(I_x\).
Rectangular section — centroidal axis and dimensions
Sign convention

The centroidal axis of a rectangle is at mid-depth; depth is cubed, so it dominates.

Working

\(I_x=bh^3/12\).

\(h^3=200^3=8\times10^6\text{ mm}^3\).

\(I_x=80(8\times10^6)/12=640\times10^6/12\).

\(I_x=53.33\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\).

Answer: \(I_x=53.33\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\)
Q10 W8 Using Q9, shift the axis by 40 mm with area 16000 mm². Draw both axes and find the new \(I\).
Parallel-axis theorem — centroidal and shifted axes
Sign convention

Parallel-axis transfer only works from the centroidal axis outward.

Working

Transfer term: \(Ad^2=16000(40)^2=16000(1600)=25.6\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\).

\(I_{new}=I_c+Ad^2=53.33\times10^6+25.6\times10^6\).

\(I_{new}=78.93\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\).

Moving away from the centroid can only increase \(I\).

Answer: \(I_{new}=78.93\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\)
Q11 W8 Draw a shape with a hole and explain why the hole area is negative in centroid and \(I\) calculations.
Negative area — the hole subtracts
Sign convention

Model the real shape as (solid outline) minus (the removed piece).

Working

The physical shape is the full outline with the hole material removed.

Superposition says: property of shape = property of solid − property of hole.

So the hole contributes \(-A_h\) to area, \(-A_hx_h\) to the first moment, and \(-(I_h+A_hd_h^2)\) to \(I\).

Using a positive hole area would count material that is not there.

Answer: A hole removes material, so its area, first moment and second moment all subtract
Q12 W8 Draw both sections and say which has higher \(I\): a same-area solid rectangle or an I-section with material farther from the neutral axis.
Section comparison — where the area sits
Sign convention

\(I=\int y^2\,dA\): each element counts by the square of its distance from the neutral axis.

Working

\(I\) weights area by distance squared, so far-away material is far more effective.

The I-section concentrates area in flanges at large \(y\), producing a big \(Ad^2\) contribution.

The rectangle spreads the same area evenly, much of it close to the neutral axis where it does little work.

Answer: The I-section, because \(I\) grows with the square of distance from the neutral axis
Q13 W9 A 5 kg object starts from rest with 90 J of net work done on it. Draw the work-energy picture and find the final speed.
Work–energy — net work becomes kinetic energy
Sign convention

Work–energy theorem: \(W_{net}=\Delta K=K_2-K_1\).

Working

Starting from rest, \(K_1=0\), so \(W_{net}=\tfrac12mv^2\).

\(90=\tfrac12(5)v^2=2.5v^2\).

\(v^2=90/2.5=36\).

\(v=6\text{ m/s}\).

Answer: \(v=6\text{ m/s}\)
Q14 W9 A 2 kg mass drops 3.5 m with no losses. Draw the energy diagram and find the impact speed.
Energy diagram — free drop, no losses
Sign convention

Take the landing level as the datum for gravitational potential energy.

Working

Energy conservation: \(mgh=\tfrac12mv^2\); mass cancels.

\(v=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{2(9.8)(3.5)}\).

\(v=\sqrt{68.6}\).

\(v=8.28\text{ m/s}\).

Answer: \(v=8.28\text{ m/s}\)
Q15 W9 A spring with \(k=600\) N/m compressed 0.18 m launches a 1.5 kg block on a frictionless level surface. Draw the energy diagram and find the speed.
Energy diagram — spring launch
Sign convention

Frictionless, so stored elastic energy converts fully to kinetic energy.

Working

\(\tfrac12kx^2=\tfrac12mv^2\Rightarrow v^2=kx^2/m\).

\(x^2=0.18^2=0.0324\text{ m}^2\).

\(v^2=600(0.0324)/1.5=19.44/1.5=12.96\).

\(v=3.60\text{ m/s}\).

Answer: \(v=3.60\text{ m/s}\)
Q16 W9 Draw a sliding block with friction and displacement, and explain why the work done by kinetic friction is negative.
Work of friction — force opposite to displacement
Sign convention

Work is \(\vec F\cdot\vec s=Fs\cos\theta\), with \(\theta\) the angle between force and displacement.

Working

Kinetic friction always points opposite to the direction of sliding.

So \(\theta=180^\circ\) and \(\cos180^\circ=-1\).

\(W_f=f_ks\cos180^\circ=-f_ks\).

The negative sign is the mechanical energy leaving the system as heat.

Answer: Friction opposes displacement, so \(W_f=-f_ks\) and mechanical energy always decreases
Q17 W7-9 An 8 kg block slides 5 m down a 20° incline with \(\mu_k=0.18\) from rest. Draw the energy diagram and find the final speed.
Energy on an incline — gravity in, friction out
Sign convention

Down-slope motion positive; friction acts up the slope and does negative work.

Working

Height drop: \(s\sin20^\circ=5(0.342)=1.710\text{ m}\).

\(W_g=mg(s\sin20^\circ)=8(9.8)(1.710)=134.06\text{ J}\).

\(N=mg\cos20^\circ=8(9.8)(0.940)=73.66\text{ N}\), so \(f_k=0.18(73.66)=13.26\text{ N}\).

\(W_f=-f_ks=-13.26(5)=-66.30\text{ J}\).

\(\Delta K=134.06-66.30=67.76\text{ J}\Rightarrow v=\sqrt{2(67.76)/8}=4.12\text{ m/s}\).

Answer: \(v=4.12\text{ m/s}\)
Q18 W8-9 A beam section has \(I=26\times10^6\) mm⁴, \(c=75\) mm and carries 28 kN·m. Draw the stress distribution and find the stress.
Bending stress — section and linear distribution
Sign convention

Maximum stress occurs at the extreme fibre, distance c from the neutral axis.

Working

Convert: \(M=28\text{ kN·m}=28\times10^6\text{ N·mm}\).

\(\sigma=Mc/I=(28\times10^6)(75)/(26\times10^6)\).

\(\sigma=(2.1\times10^9)/(26\times10^6)\).

\(\sigma=80.77\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma=80.77\text{ MPa}\)
Q19 Challenge A pulley has \(T_1=240\) N, \(\mu=0.25\), \(\beta=\pi\). Draw the wrap and find \(T_2\) and the net tangential force \(T_2-T_1\).
Belt over a pulley — half wrap
Sign convention

Tight-side tension is the one being resisted by friction over the wrap.

Working

\(\mu\beta=0.25(\pi)=0.785\).

\(T_2=T_1e^{\mu\beta}=240e^{0.785}=240(2.195)\).

\(T_2=526.8\text{ N}\).

Net tangential (driving) force \(=T_2-T_1=526.8-240=286.8\text{ N}\).

Answer: \(T_2=526.8\text{ N}\), net tangential force \(=286.8\text{ N}\)
Q20 Challenge Composite: \(A_1=1800,y_1=20\); \(A_2=1200,y_2=75\); hole \(A_3=-400,y_3=50\). Draw the composite and find \(\bar y\).
Composite area — vertical centroid with a hole
Sign convention

\(\bar y=\Sigma A_iy_i/\Sigma A_i\), with the hole entering negatively.

Working

Numerator: \(1800(20)+1200(75)-400(50)=36000+90000-20000=106000\).

Denominator: \(1800+1200-400=2600\text{ mm}^2\).

\(\bar y=106000/2600\).

\(\bar y=40.77\text{ mm}\) above the datum.

Q21 Challenge A 3 kg block at 5 m/s compresses a spring \(k=900\) N/m on a frictionless path. Draw the energy diagram and find the maximum compression.
Energy diagram — kinetic energy stored in the spring
Sign convention

Maximum compression is where all kinetic energy has become spring energy.

Working

\(\tfrac12mv^2=\tfrac12kx^2\Rightarrow x=v\sqrt{m/k}\).

\(m/k=3/900=0.003333\), so \(\sqrt{m/k}=0.05774\).

\(x=5(0.05774)\).

\(x=0.2887\text{ m}\).

Answer: \(x=0.2887\text{ m}\) (288.7 mm)
Q22 Challenge Same as Q21 but 18 J is lost to friction before the spring. Draw the energy split and find the new compression.
Energy audit — where the 37.5 J goes
Sign convention

Energy accounting: initial kinetic energy = losses + stored energy.

Working

Initial KE \(=\tfrac12(3)(5)^2=37.5\text{ J}\).

Energy reaching the spring \(=37.5-18=19.5\text{ J}\).

\(\tfrac12(900)x^2=19.5\Rightarrow450x^2=19.5\).

\(x^2=0.04333\Rightarrow x=0.208\text{ m}\).

Answer: \(x=0.208\text{ m}\) (208 mm, noticeably less than the 289 mm frictionless case)
Q23 Challenge Draw the link and state in one sentence how friction analysis feeds work-energy analysis.
Method map — friction feeds the energy equation
Sign convention

\(N\) comes from the free body; only then can \(f_k\) and \(W_f\) be evaluated.

Working

The FBD gives \(N\), which is not always \(mg\).

Kinetic friction follows as \(f_k=\mu_kN\).

That force enters the energy equation as the dissipation term \(W_f=-f_ks\).

Answer: Friction analysis supplies \(f_k=\mu_kN\), which becomes the dissipation term \(W_f=-f_ks\) in the work–energy equation
Q24 Capstone Sketch the solution flow and give a 5-line template for mixed W7–9 problems.
Method map — five-line template
The five lines

1. State the regime: is it held by static friction, or already sliding?

2. Compute the geometric components: \(N\), \(mg\sin\theta\), \(mg\cos\theta\).

3. Write the governing equation with signs declared — force balance for accelerations, energy balance for speeds over distances.

4. Solve for the single unknown.

5. Verify units and ask whether the magnitude is physically plausible.

Answer: Regime → components → governing equation → solve → verify
