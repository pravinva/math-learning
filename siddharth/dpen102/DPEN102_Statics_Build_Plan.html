Concept Explainer: The "Samurai Cut" (Truss Analysis)

Use this before Week 4, Block 12 (Method of Sections). When a question asks for the internal force of one bar buried in a large truss, don't calculate every joint from the outside in.

1The Slice
  • Slice completely through the truss with an imaginary blade, cutting it into two separate pieces.
2The Golden Rule
  • The cut can pass through a maximum of 3 bars. One of those must be the mystery bar.
3Pick the Easy Side
  • Discard one half entirely. Keep the side with fewer arrows, wall mounts, or forces acting on it.
4Draw the FBD
  • Treat the remaining piece as a normal floating object. The cut bars become external force arrows.
5The Ultimate Pivot
  • Take moments (ΣM = 0) at the joint where two unknown cut bars meet — this zeroes both out, leaving one simple equation for the mystery bar.

Build: The Team D I-Beam~5 hrs incl. drying
1The Main Web Cut15 min
  1. Take one flat 900 mm × 75 mm balsa sheet.
  2. Measure a line exactly 30 mm wide down the entire length using the steel ruler.
  3. Cut cleanly along the line with the utility knife — this is the Web, the vertical spine of the beam.
  4. Cut the strip down to exactly 650 mm long so it clears the exam span safely.
2Preparing the Flanges10 min
  1. Take two 5 mm × 5 mm square balsa strips.
  2. Cut both to exactly 650 mm long to match the Web.
3Assembly Alignment20 min + 2 hr drying
  1. Lay the 30 mm Web flat on the cutting board.
  2. Run a thin bead of glue along the top edge.
  3. Press one 5 mm × 5 mm strip against the edge, flush and centred.
  4. Repeat on the bottom edge to form an "I" cross-section.
Critical step: weigh the drying assembly down flat with a textbook and soup cans. This prevents the warping that caused Team D's original accuracy problems. Let it dry 2 hours before continuing.
4Double Flange Reinforcement15 min + 2 hr drying
  1. Flip the beam over once dry.
  2. Glue the remaining two 650 mm strips onto the opposite top and bottom edges.
  3. Clamp or weigh down flat again until fully dried — this completes a symmetrical university-spec I-beam.
Home Testing Lab~20 min
  1. Place two heavy matching textbooks exactly 630 mm apart on a flat table.
  2. Rest the dry balsa beam across the gap, overlapping each book by 10 mm on each side.
  3. Hang a light bag or bucket from the exact middle point (315 mm mark).
  4. Fill the bag with tins, sugar bags, or hand weights until the kitchen scale reads exactly 3.5 kg.
  5. Hold a ruler vertically behind the bag and measure the centre deflection. A dip between 2.0 mm and 5.5 mm without snapping is a pass.
