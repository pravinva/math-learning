Integrated Newton/equilibrium, units/moments, vectors/FBD, stress/internal actions, SFD/BMD, and truss logic in one exam-style checkpoint.

Diagrams required. Every question asks you to draw or sketch something first (FBD, loaded beam, cut section, SFD/BMD, truss joint, cross-section, or energy sketch). Formula-only answers are not enough.
Use as a mock: 75-90 minutes total. Do Q1-12 first pass, then Q13-24 challenge pass. Show complete setup for every multi-step question.

Before you start · Weeks 1–6 at a glance

Week 1 — Newton's laws & equilibrium

Topics

Force, weight, normal reaction; equilibrium vs acceleration.

Main ideas / remember

  • Net force causes acceleration; zero net force ⇒ no acceleration.
  • Draw an FBD and fix +x/+y before writing \(\Sigma F\).
Key: \(\Sigma F=ma\); \(W=mg\); equilibrium \(\Rightarrow\Sigma F_x=\Sigma F_y=0\).

Week 2 — Units, method & moments

Topics

SI conversions; solving method; moments of forces.

Main ideas / remember

  • Convert units once at the start (kN↔N, MPa↔Pa, mm↔m).
  • Moment \(M=Fd_\perp\); pick a moment centre that kills an unknown.
Key: \(M=Fd_\perp\); \(\Sigma M=0\).

Week 3 — Vectors, components & FBDs

Topics

Resolving forces; resultant; 2D FBD equilibrium.

Main ideas / remember

  • Resolve first: \(F_x=F\cos\theta\), \(F_y=F\sin\theta\).
  • Check quadrant when reporting the resultant angle.
Key: \(F_x=F\cos\theta\), \(F_y=F\sin\theta\); \(|R|=\sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}\).

Week 4 — Stress, strain & internal actions

Topics

Stress/strain; FoS and allowable stress; cut for \(N,V,M\).

Main ideas / remember

  • Material check: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{work}}\le\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}=\sigma_y/\mathrm{FoS}\).
  • Find reactions, then cut one side for internals.
Key: \(\sigma=F/A\); \(\varepsilon=\Delta L/L_0\); \(A_{\mathrm{req}}=F/\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}\).

Week 5 — SFD & BMD

Topics

Shear and moment diagrams; UDL vs point loads; max moment.

Main ideas / remember

  • Reactions first; shear must close to zero at the far end.
  • \(M\) peaks where \(V=0\); full-span UDL \(\Rightarrow M_{\max}=wL^2/8\).
Key: \(dM/dx=V\); \(M_{\max}=wL^2/8\) (SS + full UDL).

Week 6 — Truss method of sections

Topics

Zero-force members; reactions; cut ≤3 members; tension/compression signs.

Main ideas / remember

  • Assume tension; negative ⇒ compression.
  • Moment about the intersection of two unknowns isolates the third.
Key: \(\Sigma F_x=\Sigma F_y=\Sigma M=0\) on the cut piece.
Q1 W1 An 18 kg body has a net horizontal force of 63 N. Draw the FBD and find the acceleration.
FBD — body under a net horizontal force
Sign convention

+x in the direction of the applied force, +y upward.

Working

Vertically: \(N=mg=18(9.8)=176.4\text{ N}\), so no vertical acceleration.

Horizontally: \(\Sigma F_x=ma\).

\(a=F/m=63/18\).

\(a=3.50\text{ m/s}^2\) in the direction of the force.

Answer: \(a=3.50\text{ m/s}^2\)
Q2 W1 A 10 kg block is at rest on level ground while pushed right by 45 N and opposed by friction. Draw the FBD and find the friction magnitude.
FBD — block held at rest by static friction
Sign convention

+x to the right. At rest means zero acceleration, so all sums vanish.

Working

At rest: \(\Sigma F_x=0\).

\(P-f=0\Rightarrow f=P\).

\(f=45\text{ N}\) acting leftward.

Note this is the required friction, not \(\mu_sN\); the maximum available is only an upper bound.

Answer: 45 N, directed left (opposing the push)
Q3 W2 Draw the conversion ladder and convert 2.75 kN to N and 350 MPa to Pa.
Unit ladder — the two conversions that matter most
Sign convention

Prefixes are pure powers of ten; convert before substituting.

Working

\(2.75\text{ kN}=2.75\times10^3=2750\text{ N}\).

\(350\text{ MPa}=350\times10^6\text{ Pa}=3.5\times10^8\text{ Pa}\).

Useful shortcut: \(1\text{ MPa}=1\text{ N/mm}^2\).

Answer: \(2750\text{ N}\) and \(350\times10^6\text{ Pa}\)
Q4 W2 Draw the moment diagram and find the moment about O from a 420 N force at 0.65 m perpendicular distance.
Moment about a point — force times perpendicular distance
Sign convention

Anticlockwise positive; d must be measured perpendicular to the force.

Working

\(M=Fd\).

\(=420(0.65)\).

\(M=273\text{ N·m}\).

Check the units: N × m gives N·m, as expected.

Answer: \(M=273\text{ N·m}\)
Q5 W3 Draw the component triangle and resolve a 160 N force at 28° above horizontal into components.
Resolving a force — component triangle
Sign convention

Angle measured from the +x axis; cosine goes with the adjacent (x) side.

Working

\(F_x=F\cos\theta=160\cos28^\circ=160(0.8829)\).

\(F_x=141.23\text{ N}\).

\(F_y=F\sin\theta=160\sin28^\circ=160(0.4695)\).

\(F_y=75.12\text{ N}\). Check: \(\sqrt{141.23^2+75.12^2}=160\) ✓

Answer: \(F_x=141.23\text{ N}\), \(F_y=75.12\text{ N}\)
Q6 W3 A vector has components \((-9,12)\) N. Draw it on axes and find its magnitude and direction from +x.
Vector on axes — quadrant II
Sign convention

Direction quoted anticlockwise from the +x axis.

Working

Magnitude \(=\sqrt{(-9)^2+12^2}=\sqrt{81+144}=\sqrt{225}=15\text{ N}\).

Reference angle \(=\tan^{-1}(12/9)=53.13^\circ\).

Components are \((-,+)\), so the vector lies in quadrant II.

\(\theta=180^\circ-53.13^\circ=126.87^\circ\).

Answer: 15 N at \(126.87^\circ\) from the +x axis
Q7 W4 A bar of area 320 mm² carries 38 kN tension. Draw the loaded bar and find the stress in MPa.
Axial member — force spread over the cut area
Sign convention

Tension positive; stress is uniform over the cross-section.

Working

\(F=38\text{ kN}=38000\text{ N}\).

\(\sigma=F/A=38000/320\).

\(\sigma=118.75\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma=118.75\text{ MPa}\)
Q8 W4 If \(\sigma_y=300\) MPa and FoS \(=2.5\), draw the check and say whether Q7 passes.
Design check — a pass with a very thin margin
Sign convention

Compare working stress with the allowable, not with yield.

Working

\(\sigma_{allow}=\sigma_y/\text{FoS}=300/2.5=120\text{ MPa}\).

\(\sigma_{work}=118.75\text{ MPa}\) from Q7.

\(118.75 \lt 120\), so it passes.

Margin \(=(120-118.75)/120=1.04\%\) — almost no headroom, worth flagging in a design report.

Answer: Pass, but with only about 1% margin
Q9 W5 Simply supported beam of span 8 m carries a central point load of 24 kN. Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions.
Loaded beam — central point load
Sign convention

Up positive; moments about A anticlockwise positive.

Working

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(8)=24(4)=96\Rightarrow R_B=12\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A=24-12=12\text{ kN}\).

The load is central, so symmetry confirms the split.

Answer: \(R_A=R_B=12\text{ kN}\)
Q10 W5 Beam of span 6 m with a full UDL \(w=5\) kN/m. Sketch the BMD and find the maximum moment.
BMD — parabola for a full-span UDL
Sign convention

Sagging positive; zero moment at both simple supports.

Working

\(M_{max}=wL^2/8\) for a full-span UDL on a simply supported beam.

\(=5(6)^2/8=5(36)/8\).

\(=180/8\).

\(M_{max}=22.5\text{ kN·m}\) at midspan.

Answer: \(M_{max}=22.5\text{ kN·m}\)
Q11 W6 Draw the joint and classify the forces at an unloaded joint with only two non-collinear members.
Joint FBD — two non-collinear members, no load
Sign convention

Resolve perpendicular to one member first; a pin transmits no moment.

Working

Resolve perpendicular to member 2: only member 1 has a component, so \(F_1=0\).

Resolve along member 2: with \(F_1=0\), \(F_2\) has nothing to balance, so \(F_2=0\).

They are still needed for stability and for other load cases.

Answer: Both are zero-force members
Q12 W6 A member force from analysis is \(-11\) kN under the tension assumption. Draw both arrow sets and interpret.
Sign interpretation — assumed tension vs actual compression
Sign convention

Assume tension everywhere; let the sign report the truth.

Working

Positive would mean the tension assumption was right.

Negative means the real force points opposite to the assumed arrows, pushing into the joints.

The magnitude is unchanged at 11 kN.

Answer: 11 kN compression
Q13 Mixed A 30 kg crate is pulled by 150 N at 25° on a frictionless floor. Draw the FBD and find the acceleration and normal force.
FBD — crate with an inclined pull
Sign convention

+x along the pull direction of travel, +y upward. Frictionless floor.

Working

\(F_x=150\cos25^\circ=150(0.9063)=135.95\text{ N}\).

\(F_y=150\sin25^\circ=150(0.4226)=63.39\text{ N}\).

\(a=F_x/m=135.95/30=4.53\text{ m/s}^2\).

\(N=mg-F_y=294-63.39=230.61\text{ N}\).

Answer: \(a=4.53\text{ m/s}^2\), \(N=230.61\text{ N}\)
Q14 Mixed Beam of span 10 m with 8 kN at 2 m and 14 kN at 7 m from A. Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions.
Loaded beam — two point loads
Sign convention

Moments about A, anticlockwise positive, reactions assumed upward.

Working

\(\Sigma M_A=0:\ R_B(10)=8(2)+14(7)=16+98=114\).

\(R_B=11.4\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ R_A=22-11.4=10.6\text{ kN}\).

Check: \(10.6+11.4=22\text{ kN}\) ✓

Answer: \(R_A=10.6\text{ kN}\), \(R_B=11.4\text{ kN}\)
Q15 Mixed For Q14, draw the left segment and find the moment at \(x=5\) m.
Cut free body — left segment at x = 5 m
Sign convention

Only loads left of the cut contribute; sagging positive.

Working

Reaction term: \(R_A(5)=10.6(5)=53\text{ kN·m}\).

The 8 kN load is 3 m left of the cut: \(8(5-2)=24\text{ kN·m}\).

\(M(5)=53-24\).

\(M(5)=29\text{ kN·m}\). The 14 kN load at 7 m lies right of the cut.

Answer: \(M(5)=29\text{ kN·m}\)
Q16 Mixed The Q15 section has \(I=20\times10^6\) mm⁴ and \(c=85\) mm. Draw the stress distribution and find the bending stress.
Bending stress — section at the critical cut
Sign convention

Convert to N and mm; maximum stress at the extreme fibre.

Working

\(M=29\text{ kN·m}=29\times10^6\text{ N·mm}\).

\(\sigma=Mc/I=(29\times10^6)(85)/(20\times10^6)\).

\(=(2.465\times10^9)/(20\times10^6)\).

\(\sigma=123.25\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma=123.25\text{ MPa}\)
Q17 Mixed The allowable stress is 110 MPa. Draw the comparison and say whether Q16 passes.
Design check — bending stress against allowable
Sign convention

The bar must stop short of the dashed line to pass.

Working

\(\sigma_{work}=123.25\text{ MPa}\), \(\sigma_{allow}=110\text{ MPa}\).

Overstress \(=(123.25-110)/110=12.05\%\).

Fix by increasing \(I\) (deeper section) or reducing the applied moment.

Answer: No — it fails, overstressed by about 12%
Q18 Mixed Draw the member and find the required area for 72 kN axial tension with an allowable of 125 MPa.
Axial member — sizing for the allowable stress
Sign convention

Design inequality \(F/A\le\sigma_{allow}\); equality gives the minimum area.

Working

\(A_{req}=F/\sigma_{allow}\).

\(=72000/125\).

\(A_{req}=576\text{ mm}^2\).

Answer: \(A_{req}=576\text{ mm}^2\)
Q19 Mixed Draw the circular section and convert the Q18 area to a required diameter.
Cross-section — area converted to a diameter
Sign convention

Round the final diameter up to the next available stock size.

Working

\(A=\pi d^2/4\Rightarrow d=\sqrt{4A/\pi}\).

\(4A/\pi=4(576)/\pi=733.4\).

\(d=\sqrt{733.4}=27.08\text{ mm}\).

In practice specify 28 mm or the next stock size up.

Answer: \(d=27.08\text{ mm}\)
Q20 Challenge Vector sum: \(F_1=(40,15)\), \(F_2=(-18,26)\), \(F_3=(-12,-19)\) N. Draw them on axes and find the resultant magnitude and direction.
Vector sum — three forces and their resultant
Sign convention

Add x-components and y-components separately, then recombine.

Working

\(R_x=40-18-12=10\text{ N}\).

\(R_y=15+26-19=22\text{ N}\).

\(|R|=\sqrt{10^2+22^2}=\sqrt{100+484}=\sqrt{584}=24.17\text{ N}\).

\(\theta=\tan^{-1}(22/10)=65.56^\circ\); both components positive, so quadrant I.

Answer: \(|R|=24.17\text{ N}\) at \(65.56^\circ\) from the +x axis
Q21 Challenge Draw the dependency chain and explain why solving reactions before internal forces is non-negotiable in W5/W6.
Method map — everything downstream depends on the reactions
Sign convention

Boundary conditions first, interior second.

Working

Shear, moment and member forces are all computed from the forces acting on an isolated piece — and reactions are part of that set.

A reaction that is wrong by even a small amount shifts every shear and moment value along the beam.

The stress check then inherits the error, so a "correct" looking pass/fail verdict can be entirely wrong.

Answer: Internal actions are computed from the reactions, so a wrong reaction shifts every downstream value
Q22 Challenge A reaction solution gives \(R_A+R_B=26\) kN but the external downward load is 24 kN. Draw the FBD and say what to do now.
Global FBD — equilibrium audit fails
Sign convention

\(\Sigma F_y=0\) must hold exactly before any internal work begins.

Working

Reactions sum to 26 kN but the load is 24 kN, leaving a 2 kN residual.

Equilibrium is violated, so at least one equation, sign or load value is wrong.

Recheck the moment equation, then the load positions and magnitudes.

Do not compute any shear, moment or stress until the sums close.

Answer: Stop and debug — a 2 kN residual means an equation, sign or load value is wrong
Q23 Challenge Draw the dimensional check and explain in one line how to detect a hidden unit bug in a stress answer.
Dimensional check — back-substitute the units
Sign convention

Every formula must balance dimensionally, not just numerically.

Working

Strip the numbers and substitute units into \(\sigma=F/A\).

N/mm² gives MPa directly; N/m² gives Pa, which is \(10^6\) times smaller.

If your force is in kN but your area in mm², the result is in GPa, not MPa.

A result that does not reduce cleanly to your intended unit signals mixed inputs.

Answer: Back-substitute the units — if \(F/A\) does not reduce to N/mm\(^2\) (or Pa) consistently, the inputs are mixed
Q24 Capstone Draw the answer skeleton and write the minimum structure for a cumulative W1–6 problem.
Answer skeleton — five compulsory parts
The skeleton

Start with the diagram — it earns marks and prevents most errors.

Declare the sign convention so negative answers stay meaningful.

Write the governing equations before substituting any numbers.

Solve cleanly, keeping one unknown per equation where possible.

Close with a verification statement, not just a boxed number.

Answer: Diagram/FBD → sign convention → governing equations → clean solve → verification statement
