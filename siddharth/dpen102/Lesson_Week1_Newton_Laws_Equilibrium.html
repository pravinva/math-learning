1 Newton's three laws

Statics is the branch of mechanics dealing with bodies that don't accelerate, everything from here on is built directly on Newton's laws applied to that special case.

Key idea

1st law (inertia): an object at rest stays at rest, and an object moving at constant velocity keeps moving at that same velocity, unless acted on by a net force.

2nd law: \(\Sigma F=ma\).

3rd law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

Worked example 1

A 1000 kg car is parked on level ground. Using Newton's 1st law, explain why it doesn't move even though gravity acts on it.

Gravity (the car's weight) is exactly balanced by an equal and opposite normal force from the ground. Net force \(=0\), so by the 1st law the car's state (at rest) doesn't change.

Worked example 2

A book presses down on a table with 20 N. By Newton's 3rd law, what force does the table exert on the book?

\(20\text{ N}\) upward, the equal and opposite reaction.

Practice 1

A 5 kg object rests on a table. State the two forces acting on it and their relationship, per Newton's 1st law.

Solution

Weight \(=49\text{ N}\) down, Normal \(=49\text{ N}\) up, equal and opposite, net force \(=0\), so it stays at rest.

Practice 2

A hockey puck slides across frictionless ice at constant velocity. Which law explains why it doesn't slow down?

Solution

The 1st law: no net horizontal force acts on it, so there's no change in velocity.

Practice 3

A rocket expels gas downward to launch upward. Which law explains this, and how?

Solution

The 3rd law: the rocket pushes gas down (action), the gas pushes the rocket up (equal and opposite reaction), propelling it upward.

Practice 4

A 2 kg object has a net force of 10 N applied. Find its acceleration.

Solution

\(a=F/m=10/2=5\text{ m/s}^2\).

Practice 5

Is a car moving at a constant 100 km/h on a straight highway in equilibrium? Explain.

Solution

Yes. Constant velocity means zero acceleration, so by \(\Sigma F=ma=0\), the car is in equilibrium even though it's moving, equilibrium only requires zero net force, not being at rest.

Practice 6

Explain why "balanced forces" and "no acceleration" mean the same thing, using Newton's 2nd law.

Solution

Since \(\Sigma F=ma\), if \(\Sigma F=0\) (balanced forces) then \(a\) must also be \(0\) (for any nonzero mass), the two statements are mathematically the same thing, viewed through the 2nd law.

2 Weight, mass & normal force

Mass and weight get used interchangeably in casual speech, but they're not the same thing, and mixing them up is one of the most common early mistakes in mechanics.

Key idea

Mass \(m\) (kg): amount of matter, doesn't change with location. Weight \(W=mg\) (N): a force, depends on local gravity. Normal force \(N\): the contact reaction perpendicular to a surface, adjusts to whatever's needed for equilibrium, it is not always equal to weight.

Worked example 1

A 60 kg person stands on a scale on Earth (\(g=9.8\)). Find their weight.

\(W=60(9.8)=588\text{ N}\).

Worked example 2

The same person stands on a scale inside an elevator accelerating upward at 2 m/s². Find the scale reading.

\(N-W=ma \Rightarrow N=W+ma=588+60(2)=708\text{ N}\), the scale reads more than the person's actual weight while accelerating upward.

Practice 7

Find the weight of a 75 kg person on Earth.

Solution

\(W=75(9.8)=735\text{ N}\).

Practice 8

Find the weight of an 80 kg astronaut on the Moon (\(g_{moon}=1.62\text{ m/s}^2\)).

Solution

\(W=80(1.62)=129.6\text{ N}\).

Practice 9

Explain why mass stays the same on the Moon but weight changes.

Solution

Mass is the amount of matter (unchanged anywhere). Weight depends on local gravity via \(W=mg\), and \(g\) on the Moon (1.62) differs from Earth's (9.8), so \(W\) changes even though \(m\) doesn't.

Practice 10

A 50 kg person stands on a scale in an elevator accelerating DOWNWARD at 1.5 m/s². Find the scale reading.

Solution

Taking up as positive: \(N-W=m(-1.5)\). \(W=490\text{ N}\). \(N=490-50(1.5)=490-75=415\text{ N}\).

Practice 11

For Practice 10, what would the scale read if the elevator were in free fall (\(a=g\) downward)?

Solution

\(N=W-mg=W-W=0\), weightlessness, the scale reads zero.

Practice 12

A block rests on a table with weight 50 N, plus an additional 30 N pressed down on it. Find the normal force.

Solution

\(N=50+30=80\text{ N}\), the normal force adjusts to balance whatever is actually pressing down, not just the object's own weight.

3 Static equilibrium: ΣF=0

An object in static equilibrium has zero net force in every direction, not just one.

Key idea

$$\Sigma F_x=0, \qquad \Sigma F_y=0.$$

Worked example 1

A 40 N sign hangs from a single vertical rope. Find the rope tension.

\(\Sigma F_y=0\): \(T-W=0 \Rightarrow T=40\text{ N}\).

Worked example 2

A 25 kg crate on a frictionless surface is held by two horizontal ropes pulling in opposite directions, \(T_1=80\text{ N}\) left. Find \(T_2\) (right) for equilibrium.

\(\Sigma F_x=0\): \(T_2-T_1=0 \Rightarrow T_2=80\text{ N}\).

Practice 13

A 60 N lamp hangs from a single vertical cable. Find the tension.

Solution

\(T=60\text{ N}\).

Practice 14

A 15 kg object hangs from a vertical rope. Find the tension.

Solution

\(T=15(9.8)=147\text{ N}\).

Practice 15

Two horizontal forces act on a stationary block: 45 N left and \(F\) right. Find \(F\) for equilibrium.

Solution

\(F=45\text{ N}\).

Practice 16

A block in equilibrium has three horizontal forces: 20 N right, 15 N right, and \(F\) left. Find \(F\).

Solution

\(F=20+15=35\text{ N}\).

Practice 17

Explain why equilibrium requires BOTH \(\Sigma F_x=0\) AND \(\Sigma F_y=0\), not just one.

Solution

An object could have zero net force in one direction but a nonzero net force in the other, still causing acceleration in that other direction. True equilibrium (zero net force overall) needs both components to independently sum to zero.

Practice 18

A 90 N sign is held by two vertical ropes sharing the load equally. Find the tension in each.

Solution

Each rope \(=45\text{ N}\) (split equally by symmetry).

4 Newton's second law for dynamics

Statics (\(\Sigma F=0\)) is only the special case where nothing accelerates. When forces don't balance, the imbalance itself, the net force, tells you exactly how fast the object speeds up.

Key idea

$$\Sigma F=ma.$$

Worked example 1

A 10 kg cart is pushed with a net horizontal force of 25 N. Find its acceleration.

\(a=25/10=2.5\text{ m/s}^2\).

Worked example 2

A 5 kg block experiences 30 N forward and 12 N of friction backward. Find the net force and acceleration.

Net force \(=30-12=18\text{ N}\). \(a=18/5=3.6\text{ m/s}^2\).

Practice 19

An 8 kg object has a net force of 40 N. Find its acceleration.

Solution

\(a=40/8=5\text{ m/s}^2\).

Practice 20

A 12 kg cart has 50 N forward and 20 N of resistance backward. Find the net force and acceleration.

Solution

Net \(=50-20=30\text{ N}\). \(a=30/12=2.5\text{ m/s}^2\).

Practice 21

A 3 kg object accelerates at 4 m/s². Find the net force required.

Solution

\(F=3(4)=12\text{ N}\).

Practice 22

Explain the key difference between solving a "statics" problem (\(\Sigma F=0\)) and a "dynamics" problem (\(\Sigma F=ma\neq0\)).

Solution

In statics, unknown forces are solved so the equations balance to zero (nothing moves). In dynamics, the imbalance itself, the net force, is used directly to compute how fast the object accelerates. Statics finds unknown forces assuming no motion; dynamics finds the resulting motion from known force imbalances.

Practice 23

A 500 kg rocket has thrust 8000 N upward and weight 4900 N. Find its acceleration.

Solution

Net force \(=8000-4900=3100\text{ N}\). \(a=3100/500=6.2\text{ m/s}^2\) upward.

Practice 24

A 12 kg toolbox rests on a rough floor (\(\mu_s=0.40\), \(\mu_k=0.30\)). A worker pulls horizontally with 38 N. Decide whether it moves, then find acceleration if it does.

Solution

Normal force \(N=mg=12(9.8)=117.6\text{ N}\).

Maximum static friction \(f_{s,max}=\mu_sN=0.40(117.6)=47.04\text{ N}\).

Since applied force \(38\text{ N} < 47.04\text{ N}\), static friction can fully oppose it, so \(\Sigma F_x=0\).

The toolbox does not move and \(a=0\).

Practice 25

A 180 N sign is supported by two identical cables, each at 35° above horizontal. Find the tension in each cable.

Solution

By symmetry, both cable tensions are equal (\(T\)). Horizontal components cancel.

Vertical equilibrium: \(2T\sin35^\circ-180=0\).

\(T=\dfrac{180}{2\sin35^\circ}=156.9\text{ N}\).

Each cable carries \(\boxed{157\text{ N}}\) (3 s.f.).

Practice 26

A 220 N light is held by two cables: left cable at 60° to horizontal, right cable at 30°. Find both tensions.

Solution

Let \(T_L\) be left-cable tension and \(T_R\) right-cable tension.

\(\Sigma F_x=0:\ T_R\cos30^\circ=T_L\cos60^\circ \Rightarrow T_R=0.5774T_L\).

\(\Sigma F_y=0:\ T_L\sin60^\circ+T_R\sin30^\circ=220\).

\(0.8660T_L+0.5(0.5774T_L)=220 \Rightarrow 1.1547T_L=220\Rightarrow T_L=190.5\text{ N}\).

\(T_R=0.5774(190.5)=110.0\text{ N}\). Final: \(\boxed{T_L\approx191\text{ N},\ T_R\approx110\text{ N}}\).

Practice 27

A 68 kg person stands on a scale in an elevator. Find scale readings for (a) \(a=1.8\text{ m/s}^2\) upward and (b) \(a=1.8\text{ m/s}^2\) downward.

Solution

Weight \(W=mg=68(9.8)=666.4\text{ N}\).

(a) Upward acceleration: \(N-W=ma\Rightarrow N=W+ma=666.4+68(1.8)=788.8\text{ N}\).

(b) Downward acceleration: \(N-W=-ma\Rightarrow N=W-ma=666.4-122.4=544.0\text{ N}\).

Readings: \(\boxed{789\text{ N upward case},\ 544\text{ N downward case}}\).

Practice 28

A 950 kg service lift accelerates upward at \(0.85\text{ m/s}^2\). Find the supporting cable tension.

Solution

Take upward positive. \(\Sigma F_y=T-W=ma\).

\(W=mg=950(9.8)=9310\text{ N}\).

\(T=ma+W=950(0.85)+9310=10117.5\text{ N}\).

\(\boxed{T\approx10.1\text{ kN}}\).

Practice 29

A 40 kg crate is pulled to the right by 190 N on a level floor. Kinetic friction is 62 N. Find acceleration and distance traveled in 4 s from rest.

Solution

Net horizontal force: \(F_{net}=190-62=128\text{ N}\).

\(a=F_{net}/m=128/40=3.20\text{ m/s}^2\).

From rest, \(s=\tfrac12at^2=\tfrac12(3.20)(4^2)=25.6\text{ m}\).

\(\boxed{a=3.20\text{ m/s}^2,\ s=25.6\text{ m}}\).

Practice 30

A 72 kg passenger in a car accelerates from rest to 18 m/s in 6 s. Find (i) the acceleration and (ii) the horizontal force from the seat on the passenger.

Solution

\(a=\Delta v/\Delta t=(18-0)/6=3.0\text{ m/s}^2\).

By Newton's 2nd law, seat force on passenger: \(F=ma=72(3.0)=216\text{ N}\) forward.

By Newton's 3rd law, passenger pushes back on seat with 216 N.

\(\boxed{a=3.0\text{ m/s}^2,\ F=216\text{ N}}\).

Practice 31

A 1500 kg car moves at constant speed up a straight road. Engine traction is 2100 N forward. Air + rolling resistance totals 1680 N backward. Find the additional backward force needed to keep speed constant if the traction remains unchanged.

Solution

Constant speed means \(a=0\), so \(\Sigma F_x=0\).

Current net force \(=2100-1680=420\text{ N}\) forward, so not yet equilibrium.

An extra \(420\text{ N}\) backward force is required to bring net to zero.

\(\boxed{F_{extra}=420\text{ N backward}}\).

Practice 32

A 420 kg test rocket is launched vertically. If thrust is 6100 N, find its upward acceleration at liftoff.

Solution

Weight \(W=mg=420(9.8)=4116\text{ N}\) downward.

Net upward force \(=6100-4116=1984\text{ N}\).

\(a=F_{net}/m=1984/420=4.72\text{ m/s}^2\) upward.

\(\boxed{a\approx4.72\text{ m/s}^2\ \text{upward}}\).

Practice 33

A 14 kg block slides down a frictionless 25° incline. Find (i) acceleration along the slope and (ii) normal force.

Solution

Resolve weight: along slope \(W_{\parallel}=mg\sin25^\circ\), normal \(W_{\perp}=mg\cos25^\circ\).

\(\Sigma F_{\parallel}=ma \Rightarrow a=g\sin25^\circ=9.8(0.4226)=4.14\text{ m/s}^2\).

Normal equilibrium: \(N=mg\cos25^\circ=14(9.8)(0.9063)=124.4\text{ N}\).

\(\boxed{a=4.14\text{ m/s}^2,\ N=124\text{ N}}\).

Practice 34

Two blocks are connected over a frictionless pulley: \(m_1=6\text{ kg}\) hangs vertically, \(m_2=4\text{ kg}\) on a frictionless table. Find system acceleration and rope tension.

Solution

For \(m_1\): \(m_1g-T=m_1a\). For \(m_2\): \(T=m_2a\).

Substitute: \(m_1g-m_2a=m_1a \Rightarrow a=\dfrac{m_1g}{m_1+m_2}=\dfrac{6(9.8)}{10}=5.88\text{ m/s}^2\).

\(T=m_2a=4(5.88)=23.52\text{ N}\).

\(\boxed{a=5.88\text{ m/s}^2,\ T=23.5\text{ N}}\).

Practice 35

A 3.5 kg textbook is pushed horizontally against a rough vertical wall. Coefficient of static friction is 0.55. Find the minimum push force to keep the book from sliding down.

Solution

Horizontal equilibrium gives \(N=P\) (push equals normal).

To prevent downward motion, maximum upward friction must at least equal weight: \(f_{s,max}=\mu_sN=\mu_sP\ge mg\).

\(P_{min}=mg/\mu_s=3.5(9.8)/0.55=62.36\text{ N}\).

\(\boxed{P_{min}\approx62.4\text{ N}}\).

Practice 36

A crate of mass 28 kg is pulled on level ground by a rope at 20° above horizontal with tension 160 N. Kinetic friction is 70 N. Find normal force and horizontal acceleration.

Solution

Vertical equilibrium: \(N+T\sin20^\circ-mg=0\Rightarrow N=mg-T\sin20^\circ\).

\(N=28(9.8)-160(0.342)=274.4-54.7=219.7\text{ N}\).

Horizontal net force: \(F_x=T\cos20^\circ-f_k=160(0.940)-70=80.4\text{ N}\).

\(a=F_x/m=80.4/28=2.87\text{ m/s}^2\).

\(\boxed{N\approx220\text{ N},\ a\approx2.87\text{ m/s}^2}\).

Practice 37

A 90 N hanging lamp is supported by a ceiling hook. Using Newton's 3rd law, identify the action-reaction pair involving the hook and the lamp, including magnitudes and directions.

Solution

The hook exerts an upward force of 90 N on the lamp (for lamp equilibrium).

By Newton's 3rd law, the lamp exerts a downward force of 90 N on the hook.

These two are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and act on different bodies.

\(\boxed{F_{\text{hook on lamp}}=90\text{ N up},\ F_{\text{lamp on hook}}=90\text{ N down}}\).

Practice 38

A 25 kg crate rests on a 30° incline with static friction coefficient \(\mu_s=0.35\). Determine whether static equilibrium is possible.

Solution

Down-slope component: \(W_{\parallel}=mg\sin30^\circ=25(9.8)(0.5)=122.5\text{ N}\).

Normal force: \(N=mg\cos30^\circ=25(9.8)(0.866)=212.2\text{ N}\).

Maximum static friction: \(f_{s,max}=\mu_sN=0.35(212.2)=74.3\text{ N}\).

Since \(W_{\parallel}(122.5\text{ N}) > f_{s,max}(74.3\text{ N})\), friction cannot hold it.

\(\boxed{\text{No static equilibrium; the crate will slide down.}}\)

Practice 39

A 1200 kg car is parked on a 12° slope. Find the minimum static friction force and minimum \(\mu_s\) required to prevent rolling.

Solution

Required up-slope friction equals down-slope weight component: \(f_{req}=mg\sin12^\circ\).

\(f_{req}=1200(9.8)(0.2079)=2444.9\text{ N}\).

\(N=mg\cos12^\circ=1200(9.8)(0.9781)=11501.9\text{ N}\).

\(\mu_{s,min}=f_{req}/N=\tan12^\circ=0.213\).

\(\boxed{f_{req}\approx2.45\text{ kN},\ \mu_{s,min}\approx0.213}\).

Practice 40

A 5 kg package is pushed across a floor with 26 N. It accelerates at \(3.0\text{ m/s}^2\). Find kinetic friction, then predict acceleration if push increases to 34 N (same friction model).

Solution

From first case: \(F_{net}=ma=5(3.0)=15\text{ N}\).

\(F_{push}-f_k=15\Rightarrow f_k=26-15=11\text{ N}\).

Second case: \(F_{net}=34-11=23\text{ N}\).

\(a=F_{net}/m=23/5=4.6\text{ m/s}^2\).

\(\boxed{f_k=11\text{ N},\ a_{new}=4.6\text{ m/s}^2}\).

Practice 41

An 80 kg person stands in a lift whose cable suddenly snaps. Using Newton's laws, find the normal force during free fall and explain the scale reading.

Solution

In free fall, person and lift accelerate downward at \(a=g\).

Take up positive: \(N-W=m(-g)\Rightarrow N-mg=-mg\Rightarrow N=0\).

The scale reads normal force, so it reads zero even though gravity still acts.

\(\boxed{N=0\text{ N (apparent weightlessness)}}\).

Practice 42

A drone of mass 2.4 kg can produce maximum upward thrust of 31 N. Determine whether it can (i) hover and (ii) accelerate upward at \(3\text{ m/s}^2\).

Solution

Weight \(W=mg=2.4(9.8)=23.52\text{ N}\).

(i) Hover requires \(T=W\). Since \(31>23.52\), hover is possible.

(ii) For upward \(a=3\): \(T-W=ma \Rightarrow T=W+ma=23.52+2.4(3)=30.72\text{ N}\).

Since \(T_{req}=30.72\text{ N} \le T_{max}=31\text{ N}\), this acceleration is also possible (very near limit).

\(\boxed{\text{Both are possible; }T_{req,up}=30.72\text{ N}}\).

Practice 43

A 10 kg block on a horizontal floor is connected to a rope over a pulley to a hanging 3 kg mass. Kinetic friction coefficient under the 10 kg block is 0.20. Find acceleration of the system.

Solution

Friction on 10 kg block: \(f_k=\mu_kN=0.20(10)(9.8)=19.6\text{ N}\).

Driving weight from hanging mass: \(m_hg=3(9.8)=29.4\text{ N}\).

Net driving force for system: \(29.4-19.6=9.8\text{ N}\).

Total mass \(=10+3=13\text{ kg}\), so \(a=9.8/13=0.754\text{ m/s}^2\).

\(\boxed{a\approx0.75\text{ m/s}^2}\) (3 kg mass downward).

Practice 44

A test sled is pulled so that its speed rises from 6 to 18 m/s over 5 s on level ground. Pull force is 520 N and resistance is 160 N. Find the sled mass.

Solution

Acceleration: \(a=(18-6)/5=2.4\text{ m/s}^2\).

Net force: \(F_{net}=520-160=360\text{ N}\).

From \(F_{net}=ma\): \(m=F_{net}/a=360/2.4=150\text{ kg}\).

\(\boxed{m=150\text{ kg}}\).

Practice 45

An astronaut's weight is 784 N on Earth and 129.4 N on the Moon. Use these data to find (i) mass and (ii) lunar gravitational field implied by the readings.

Solution

From Earth reading, \(m=W_E/g_E=784/9.8=80.0\text{ kg}\).

From Moon reading, \(g_M=W_M/m=129.4/80.0=1.6175\text{ m/s}^2\).

So the data are consistent with lunar gravity \(\approx1.62\text{ m/s}^2\).

\(\boxed{m=80.0\text{ kg},\ g_M\approx1.62\text{ m/s}^2}\).

Practice 46

A 22 kg crate is pushed on level ground. \(\mu_s=0.28,\ \mu_k=0.20\). Determine (a) minimum force to start motion and (b) acceleration if a constant 90 N push is then maintained.

Solution

Normal force \(N=mg=22(9.8)=215.6\text{ N}\).

(a) Breakaway force \(F_{start}=f_{s,max}=\mu_sN=0.28(215.6)=60.37\text{ N}\).

(b) Once moving, \(f_k=\mu_kN=0.20(215.6)=43.12\text{ N}\).

Net force under 90 N push: \(F_{net}=90-43.12=46.88\text{ N}\).

\(a=F_{net}/m=46.88/22=2.13\text{ m/s}^2\). Final: \(\boxed{F_{start}=60.4\text{ N},\ a=2.13\text{ m/s}^2}\).

Where to go from here. Every equilibrium equation used from here on, \(\Sigma F_x=0\), \(\Sigma F_y=0\), and later \(\Sigma M=0\), is just Newton's laws applied systematically. Week 2 adds the bookkeeping discipline (units, significant figures, the 6-step method) and the FBD workflow that makes all of this reliable at scale.
