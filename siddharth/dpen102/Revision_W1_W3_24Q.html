Q1 W1 A 9 kg block rests on a horizontal floor. Find normal force.
Static vertical equilibrium: \(N-W=0\). \(W=mg=9(9.8)=88.2\text{ N}\). So \(N=88.2\text{ N}\).
Q2 W1 A 15 kg trolley is pushed by 72 N on frictionless floor. Find acceleration.
\(\Sigma F_x=ma\Rightarrow a=72/15=4.8\text{ m/s}^2\).
Q3 W1 A 1200 kg car has engine force 2600 N and resistive force 800 N. Find net force and acceleration.
\(F_{net}=2600-800=1800\text{ N}\). \(a=F_{net}/m=1800/1200=1.5\text{ m/s}^2\).
Q4 W1 Explain why a nonzero force can act on a body while acceleration is still zero.
Acceleration depends on net force, not individual forces. If all forces balance, \(\Sigma F=0\), then \(a=0\) despite each force being nonzero.
Q5 W2 Convert 3.6 kN to N and 0.42 m to mm.
\(3.6\text{ kN}=3600\text{ N}\). \(0.42\text{ m}=420\text{ mm}\).
Q6 W2 A load of 56 kN acts on area 700 mm². Find stress in MPa.
\(\sigma=F/A=56000/700=80\text{ N/mm}^2=80\text{ MPa}\).
Q7 W2 Find clockwise moment about A for 280 N at perpendicular distance 1.8 m.
\(M=Fd=280(1.8)=504\text{ N·m}\) clockwise.
Q8 W2 Beam has 9 kN downward at 2 m and 5 kN downward at 4 m from A. Find total moment about A.
\(\Sigma M_A=9(2)+5(4)=18+20=38\text{ kN·m}\) clockwise.
Q9 W3 Resolve 180 N at 32° above +x into components.
\(F_x=180\cos32^\circ=152.65\text{ N}\), \(F_y=180\sin32^\circ=95.39\text{ N}\).
Q10 W3 Given \(F_x=-14\) N, \(F_y=48\) N, find magnitude and direction from +x.
\(F=\sqrt{(-14)^2+48^2}=50\text{ N}\). Reference angle \(=\tan^{-1}(48/14)=73.74^\circ\). Quadrant II \(\Rightarrow \theta=180-73.74=106.26^\circ\).
Q11 W3 Sum vectors: \(A=(12,-3)\) N and \(B=(-5,9)\) N.
\(R=(7,6)\) N. Magnitude \(=\sqrt{85}=9.22\) N. Direction \(=\tan^{-1}(6/7)=40.6^\circ\).
Q12 W1-3 A 25 kg crate pulled by 110 N at 20° on frictionless floor. Find horizontal acceleration and normal.
\(F_x=110\cos20=103.37\text{ N}\), \(F_y=110\sin20=37.62\text{ N}\). \(a=F_x/m=4.13\text{ m/s}^2\). Vertical equilibrium: \(N+F_y-mg=0\Rightarrow N=245-37.62=207.38\text{ N}\).
Q13 Challenge A 40 kg box on rough floor (\(\mu_s=0.35,\mu_k=0.25\)) is pushed horizontally by 110 N. Determine motion state and acceleration if moving.
\(N=mg=392\text{ N}\). \(F_{s,max}=\mu_sN=137.2\text{ N}\). Since 110 N < 137.2 N, static friction can balance it. Box does not move, \(a=0\).
Q14 Challenge Same as Q13 but push is 170 N. Find acceleration.
170 N exceeds static limit, so motion occurs. \(f_k=\mu_kN=98\text{ N}\). Net \(=170-98=72\text{ N}\). \(a=72/40=1.8\text{ m/s}^2\).
Q15 W2 A couple consists of two equal opposite 420 N forces separated by 0.65 m. Find couple moment.
\(M_c=Fd=420(0.65)=273\text{ N·m}\).
Q16 W2 About point O, moments are +14, -9, -4, +3 kN·m (CCW positive). Is system in rotational equilibrium?
\(\Sigma M_O=14-9-4+3=+4\text{ kN·m}\neq0\). Not in rotational equilibrium.
Q17 W3 Force of 250 N makes 60° to vertical. Find \(F_x,F_y\) using horizontal x and vertical y axes.
Angle to horizontal is \(30^\circ\). \(F_x=250\cos30=216.51\text{ N}\), \(F_y=250\sin30=125\text{ N}\) (signs depend on quadrant).
Q18 W3 Two forces act at a point: 90 N east and 130 N north. Find resultant magnitude and bearing from east.
\(R=\sqrt{90^2+130^2}=158.11\text{ N}\). Angle from east \(=\tan^{-1}(130/90)=55.3^\circ\) north of east.
Q19 W1-3 Hanging sign (300 N) is supported by two identical ropes each at 35° above horizontal. Find rope tension.
Vertical balance: \(2T\sin35=300\Rightarrow T=300/(2\sin35)=261.5\text{ N}\).
Q20 W2-3 A bar has reactions \(R_A,R_B\). Downward loads are 6 kN at 2 m and 10 kN at 5 m from A; span 8 m. Find reactions.
\(\Sigma M_A: R_B(8)=6(2)+10(5)=62\Rightarrow R_B=7.75\text{ kN}\). \(\Sigma F_y=0\Rightarrow R_A=16-7.75=8.25\text{ kN}\).
Q21 Challenge A force vector is \((-24,-7)\) N. Give magnitude and direction in [0°,360°) from +x.
Magnitude \(=\sqrt{24^2+7^2}=25\text{ N}\). Reference \(=\tan^{-1}(7/24)=16.26^\circ\). Quadrant III \(\Rightarrow 180+16.26=196.26^\circ\).
Q22 Challenge A 2 m beam is hinged at A. A 500 N force at 40° above beam acts at free end. Find moment about A.
Only perpendicular component contributes: \(F_\perp=500\sin40=321.39\text{ N}\). \(M_A=F_\perp L=321.39(2)=642.8\text{ N·m}\).
Q23 Challenge Why is unit consistency a mechanics skill, not just arithmetic housekeeping?
Wrong units change physics interpretation (e.g., \(N\) vs \(kN\) makes 1000x load error). Equilibrium, stress, and energy equations are dimensionally meaningful only in a consistent system.
Q24 Capstone Write a 6-step mini-solution plan for any force-equilibrium question.
Define unknowns → draw complete FBD → choose axes/sign convention → write \(\Sigma F_x,\Sigma F_y,\Sigma M\) as needed → solve with consistent units → verify magnitude/sign and physical sense.
