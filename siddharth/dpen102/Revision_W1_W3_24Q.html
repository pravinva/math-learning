Before you start · what we studied in Weeks 1–3

Week 1 — Newton's laws & equilibrium

Topics

Force, mass, acceleration; free-body thinking; when a body is in equilibrium vs accelerating.

Main ideas

  • A net force causes acceleration; zero net force means constant velocity (often rest).
  • Weight \(W=mg\) always acts downward; the normal force is the contact push perpendicular to the surface.
  • Individual forces can be nonzero even when acceleration is zero — they just cancel.

Remember

  • Draw an FBD before writing equations.
  • Choose a clear +x / +y direction and stick to it.
  • At rest on a level floor with no other vertical loads: \(N=W\).
Key formulas: \(\Sigma F=ma\); \(W=mg\); for equilibrium \(\Sigma F_x=0\), \(\Sigma F_y=0\).

Week 2 — Units, method & moments

Topics

SI unit conversions; a repeatable problem-solving method; moments (turning effects) of forces.

Main ideas

  • Wrong units destroy otherwise correct algebra — convert once at the start.
  • A moment is \(M=F\times d_\perp\): force times perpendicular lever arm about the chosen point.
  • Clockwise vs anticlockwise must follow one signed convention for the whole problem.

Remember

  • kN → N (×1000); MPa → Pa (×10⁶); mm → m (÷1000) when mixing systems.
  • Take moments about a point that eliminates an unknown when possible.
  • Method: diagram → knowns/unknowns → equations → solve → check.
Key formulas: \(M=Fd_\perp\); \(\Sigma M=0\) for rotational equilibrium.

Week 3 — Vectors, components & FBDs

Topics

Resolving forces into components; adding vectors; building FBDs for 2D equilibrium problems.

Main ideas

  • Any force at angle \(\theta\) splits into \(F_x=F\cos\theta\), \(F_y=F\sin\theta\).
  • Resultant: add all \(x\)-components and all \(y\)-components separately, then find magnitude and direction.
  • The FBD shows only forces on the isolated body — never include internal forces of a rigid whole.

Remember

  • Check which quadrant the resultant sits in before reporting \(\theta\).
  • An angled pull reduces the normal force (\(N=mg-P\sin\theta\) if pulling upward).
  • Equilibrium still means \(\Sigma F_x=0\) and \(\Sigma F_y=0\) after resolving.
Key formulas: \(F_x=F\cos\theta\), \(F_y=F\sin\theta\); \(|R|=\sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}\); \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(R_y/R_x)\).
Q1 W1 A 9 kg block rests on a horizontal floor. Draw the FBD and find the normal force.
FBD — isolated block
Sign convention

+y upward, +x to the right. Block is at rest, so \(\Sigma F_x=0\) and \(\Sigma F_y=0\).

Working

Weight: \(W=mg=9(9.8)=88.2\text{ N}\) (downward).

Vertical equilibrium: \(\Sigma F_y=0 \Rightarrow N-W=0\).

So \(N=W=88.2\text{ N}\) upward.

Answer: \(N=88.2\text{ N}\)
Q2 W1 A 15 kg trolley is pushed by 72 N on a frictionless floor. Draw the FBD and find the acceleration.
FBD — trolley on frictionless floor
Working

Frictionless floor \(\Rightarrow f=0\). Only horizontal force is push \(P=72\text{ N}\) to the right.

\(\Sigma F_x=ma \Rightarrow 72=15a\).

\(a=72/15=4.8\text{ m/s}^2\) to the right.

Answer: \(a=4.8\text{ m/s}^2\)
Q3 W1 A 1200 kg car has engine force 2600 N and resistive force 800 N. Draw the FBD and find the net force and acceleration.
FBD — car (horizontal line of action)
Working

Choose +x in direction of motion (engine force direction).

\(F_{net}=\Sigma F_x=2600-800=1800\text{ N}\).

\(a=F_{net}/m=1800/1200=1.5\text{ m/s}^2\).

Answer: \(F_{net}=1800\text{ N}\), \(a=1.5\text{ m/s}^2\)
Q4 W1 Draw a balanced FBD and explain why a nonzero force can act on a body while acceleration is still zero.
Balanced-forces diagram

Acceleration is set by the net force, not by any single force.

Each force can be nonzero, but if they balance then \(\Sigma F=0\).

From Newton II, \(a=\Sigma F/m\), so balanced forces give \(a=0\) even though individual forces are acting.

Answer: individual forces may be nonzero, but zero net force gives zero acceleration.
Q5 W2 Draw the unit-conversion ladder and convert 3.6 kN to N and 0.42 m to mm.
Unit ladder — both conversions
Working

Force: \(1\text{ kN}=1000\text{ N}\), so \(3.6\text{ kN}=3.6\times1000=3600\text{ N}\).

Length: \(1\text{ m}=1000\text{ mm}\), so \(0.42\text{ m}=0.42\times1000=420\text{ mm}\).

Answer: \(3600\text{ N}\) and \(420\text{ mm}\)
Q6 W2 Sketch the loaded cross-section. A load of 56 kN acts on area 700 mm². Find the stress in MPa.
Diagram — normal force on area A
Working

Convert force: \(F=56\text{ kN}=56{,}000\text{ N}\).

Use consistent area units: \(A=700\text{ mm}^2\).

\(\sigma=F/A=56{,}000/700=80\text{ N/mm}^2\).

Since \(1\text{ N/mm}^2=1\text{ MPa}\), \(\sigma=80\text{ MPa}\).

Answer: \(\sigma=80\text{ MPa}\)
Q7 W2 Draw the moment diagram and find the clockwise moment about A for 280 N at perpendicular distance 1.8 m.
Moment diagram about pivot A
Working

Moment about A: \(M_A=F\,d\) with \(d\) measured perpendicular to force line of action.

\(M_A=280(1.8)=504\text{ N·m}\).

Direction: clockwise (CW).

Answer: \(M_A=504\text{ N·m}\) clockwise
Q8 W2 Draw the loaded beam and find the total moment about A. Beam has 9 kN downward at 2 m and 5 kN downward at 4 m from A.
Beam loading diagram
Working (CW positive)

\(\Sigma M_A = (9\text{ kN})(2\text{ m}) + (5\text{ kN})(4\text{ m})\).

\(=18+20=38\text{ kN·m}\) clockwise.

Answer: \(\Sigma M_A=38\text{ kN·m}\) CW
Q9 W3 Draw the component triangle and resolve 180 N at 32° above +x into components.
Component diagram
Working

\(F_x=F\cos\theta=180\cos32^\circ=152.65\text{ N}\).

\(F_y=F\sin\theta=180\sin32^\circ=95.39\text{ N}\).

Answer: \(F_x=152.65\text{ N}\), \(F_y=95.39\text{ N}\)
Q10 W3 Draw the vector on axes. Given \(F_x=-14\) N, \(F_y=48\) N, find magnitude and direction from +x.
Vector in Quadrant II
Working

Magnitude: \(F=\sqrt{(-14)^2+48^2}=\sqrt{196+2304}=50\text{ N}\).

Reference angle: \(\alpha=\tan^{-1}(48/14)=73.74^\circ\).

Quadrant II \(\Rightarrow \theta=180^\circ-\alpha=180-73.74=106.26^\circ\) from +x.

Answer: \(F=50\text{ N}\) at \(106.26^\circ\) from +x
Q11 W3 Draw the vectors on axes and sum \(A=(12,-3)\) N and \(B=(-5,9)\) N.
Component addition diagram
Working

Add components: \(R_x=12+(-5)=7\text{ N}\), \(R_y=-3+9=6\text{ N}\).

So \(\mathbf{R}=(7,6)\text{ N}\).

Magnitude: \(|R|=\sqrt{7^2+6^2}=\sqrt{85}=9.22\text{ N}\).

Direction from +x: \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(6/7)=40.6^\circ\).

Answer: \(\mathbf{R}=(7,6)\text{ N}\), \(|R|=9.22\text{ N}\), \(\theta=40.6^\circ\)
Q12 W1-3 A 25 kg crate is pulled by 110 N at 20° on a frictionless floor. Draw the FBD and find the horizontal acceleration and normal force.
FBD — inclined pull on crate
Working

Resolve pull: \(F_x=110\cos20^\circ=103.37\text{ N}\), \(F_y=110\sin20^\circ=37.62\text{ N}\) (upward).

Horizontal: \(\Sigma F_x=ma \Rightarrow a=F_x/m=103.37/25=4.13\text{ m/s}^2\).

Vertical equilibrium (+y up): \(N+F_y-mg=0\).

\(mg=25(9.8)=245\text{ N}\), so \(N=245-37.62=207.38\text{ N}\).

Answer: \(a=4.13\text{ m/s}^2\), \(N=207.38\text{ N}\)
Q13 Challenge A 40 kg box on a rough floor (\(\mu_s=0.35,\mu_k=0.25\)) is pushed horizontally by 110 N. Draw the FBD, determine the motion state, and find the acceleration if moving.
FBD — rough floor, static case
Working

Normal: \(N=mg=40(9.8)=392\text{ N}\).

Max static friction: \(f_{s,\max}=\mu_sN=0.35(392)=137.2\text{ N}\).

Applied push \(P=110\text{ N} < f_{s,\max}\), so static friction can balance it.

Box remains at rest: \(a=0\). (If it were moving, use \(f_k=\mu_kN=98\text{ N}\).)

Answer: no motion, \(a=0\)
Q14 Challenge Same as Q13 but the push is 170 N. Draw the FBD and find the acceleration.
FBD — kinetic friction while moving
Working

\(N=392\text{ N}\) as before. \(f_{s,\max}=137.2\text{ N}\).

Since \(170\text{ N} > 137.2\text{ N}\), motion occurs.

Kinetic friction: \(f_k=\mu_kN=0.25(392)=98\text{ N}\) (opposite motion).

Net horizontal force: \(F_{net}=170-98=72\text{ N}\).

\(a=F_{net}/m=72/40=1.8\text{ m/s}^2\).

Answer: \(a=1.8\text{ m/s}^2\)
Q15 W2 Draw the couple and find its moment. Two equal opposite 420 N forces separated by 0.65 m.

Couple moment magnitude: \(M_c=F\,d\), where \(d\) is perpendicular distance between force lines.

\(M_c=420(0.65)=273\text{ N·m}\).

Q16 W2 Sketch the moment balance about O. Moments are +14, −9, −4, +3 kN·m (CCW positive). Is the system in rotational equilibrium?
Moment sign convention at O

\(\Sigma M_O=14-9-4+3=+4\text{ kN·m}\).

Q18 W3 Draw the two forces on axes and find the resultant magnitude and bearing from east. 90 N east and 130 N north.
Perpendicular force addition
Working

\(R=\sqrt{90^2+130^2}=\sqrt{8100+16900}=158.11\text{ N}\).

Bearing from east: \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(130/90)=55.3^\circ\) north of east.

Answer: \(R=158.11\text{ N}\) at \(55.3^\circ\) N of E
Q19 W1-3 Draw the FBD of the hanging sign. A 300 N sign is supported by two identical ropes each at 35° above horizontal. Find the rope tension.
Sign FBD with symmetric rope tensions
Working

Each rope tension \(T\) has vertical component \(T\sin35^\circ\) (upward).

Vertical equilibrium: \(2T\sin35^\circ=300\).

\(T=300/(2\sin35^\circ)=300/(2\times0.5736)=261.5\text{ N}\).

Answer: \(T=261.5\text{ N}\) in each rope
Q20 W2-3 Draw the loaded beam and find the reactions. A bar has reactions \(R_A,R_B\). Downward loads are 6 kN at 2 m and 10 kN at 5 m from A; span 8 m.
Simply supported beam (pin A, roller B)
Working

Total downward load \(=6+10=16\text{ kN}\).

\(\Sigma M_A=0\) (CW +): \(R_B(8)=6(2)+10(5)=12+50=62\).

\(R_B=62/8=7.75\text{ kN}\) upward.

\(\Sigma F_y=0\): \(R_A+R_B=16 \Rightarrow R_A=16-7.75=8.25\text{ kN}\) upward.

Answer: \(R_A=8.25\text{ kN}\), \(R_B=7.75\text{ kN}\)

Q22 Challenge Draw the hinged beam and find the moment about A. A 2 m beam is hinged at A. A 500 N force at 40° above the beam acts at the free end.
Beam with angled end force
Working

Only the component perpendicular to the beam creates moment about A.

\(F_\perp=500\sin40^\circ=321.39\text{ N}\).

\(M_A=F_\perp L=321.39(2)=642.8\text{ N·m}\).

Q23 Challenge Sketch the unit-consistency check and explain why unit consistency is a mechanics skill, not just arithmetic housekeeping.
Reasonableness ruler — what a unit slip does to the number
Reasoning

Mechanics equations are dimensionally meaningful: \(\Sigma F\) must be in N, moments in N·m, stress in Pa/MPa.

Mixing kN with N or m with mm can create 1000× errors that still look numerically plausible.

A wrong unit changes the physical interpretation (safe vs unsafe stress, static vs moving body).

Answer: consistent units protect both arithmetic and physical correctness.
Q24 Capstone Sketch the 6-step solution map and write a mini-solution plan for any force-equilibrium question.
6-step exam method

1. Define unknowns clearly (forces, reactions, accelerations).

2. Draw a complete FBD with all external forces labeled.

3. Choose axes and sign convention (+ directions marked).

4. Write governing equations: \(\Sigma F_x\), \(\Sigma F_y\), and \(\Sigma M\) as needed.

5. Solve with consistent units and show substitution.

6. Verify magnitude/sign and check physical sense (equilibrium/motion direction).

Answer: follow this 6-step sequence on every equilibrium problem.
