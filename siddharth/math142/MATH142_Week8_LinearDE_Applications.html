First-Order Linear DE Applications: Changing-Volume Mixing & RL Circuits

The separable mixing problems from last week assumed constant tank volume. Once inflow and outflow rates differ, the volume — and the outflow concentration's denominator — changes with time, turning the equation genuinely linear rather than separable. RL circuits share exactly the same linear structure. Exam-level worked examples and practice throughout. Solutions stay hidden until you click.

Start here The big picture — what this week is about

Week 7 gave you the machinery for first-order linear differential equations; this week is where that machinery meets the physical world. The pattern is always the same three steps: translate a physical situation into a DE (this is modelling), solve the DE, then interpret the answer — the steady state, the transient, the time constant. Learning to set up the equation correctly is often harder, and more valuable, than solving it.

The recurring structure is “rate of change = input − output.” A tank’s salt content changes at (inflow concentration × inflow rate) − (outflow concentration × outflow rate). A capacitor’s charge changes as the driving voltage fights the resistor. Newton’s cooling balances heat gained against heat lost. Recognising this common template lets you write the governing equation for a system you have never seen before.

Why this matters

This is engineering mathematics doing its actual job. RC / RL circuit response, thermal cooling and heating, chemical mixing and concentration control, drug dosing in pharmacokinetics, and terminal velocity all reduce to the first-order linear model. The concepts of time constant and steady state you meet here are spoken daily by electrical, chemical, and mechanical engineers.

The roadmap — how the pieces connect

We work through mixing-tank problems, Newton’s law of cooling and heating, and electrical circuits, each time modelling → solving → interpreting. Watch for the shared skeleton (rate in minus rate out) and for the physical meaning of the constants. This consolidates Week 7 and sets up the second-order systems of Week 9.

Lay of the land — this week's toolkit
  • Changing-volume mixing: if inflow rate $r_{in}$ and outflow rate $r_{out}$ differ, $V(t)=V_0+(r_{in}-r_{out})t$; the DE becomes $\dfrac{dA}{dt}+\dfrac{r_{out}}{V(t)}A = r_{in}\cdot c_{in}$ — linear, not separable, since the coefficient of $A$ now depends on $t$.
  • RL circuits: $L\dfrac{dI}{dt}+RI=V(t)$ — the exact same linear structure as a mixing problem, with $I$ playing the role of $A$.
  • Steady-state behaviour: as $t\to\infty$, the transient term (multiplying $e^{-kt}$ for some $k>0$) decays to zero, leaving whatever constant/steady term remains — this is the long-run circuit current or mixing concentration.
  • Solution method: always the same integrating-factor machinery from Week 7 — the challenge here is correctly setting up $V(t)$ and the outflow term from the word problem, not the algebra of solving once it's set up.

If inflow rate $r_{in}$ and outflow rate $r_{out}$ differ, the volume at time $t$ is $V(t)=V_0+(r_{in}-r_{out})t$ — no longer constant. The outflow concentration is $\dfrac{A(t)}{V(t)}$, so the DE becomes linear in $A$ (with a $t$-dependent coefficient), exactly the shape the integrating factor method was built for.

RL circuits. By Kirchhoff's voltage law, $L\dfrac{dI}{dt}+RI=V(t)$ — an equation with the exact same linear structure as mixing, just with different physical labels.

Key idea

$$\frac{dA}{dt}+\frac{r_{out}}{V(t)}A = r_{in}\cdot c_{in}, \qquad L\frac{dI}{dt}+RI=V(t)$$

Worked example

Example 1 — constant volume (reviewed as linear). A 200L tank starts with 50g salt. Pure water flows in at 5L/min, mixture flows out at 5L/min. Find $A(t)$.

$\dfrac{dA}{dt}=-\dfrac{A}{40}$; $\mu=e^{t/40}$; $A=Ce^{-t/40}$; $A(0)=50\implies A(t)=50e^{-t/40}$.

Example 2 — changing volume, setup only. A 100L tank starts with pure water. Brine at 3g/L flows in at 4L/min; mixture flows out at 2L/min. Set up the DE for $A(t)$.

$V(t)=100+2t$. $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=12-\dfrac{2A}{100+2t}$, i.e. $\dfrac{dA}{dt}+\dfrac{2}{100+2t}A=12$ — linear, ready for the integrating factor.

Practice 1

A 100L tank starts with pure water. Brine at 3g/L flows in at 4L/min; mixture flows out at 2L/min (as in the worked example). Solve the full DE for $A(t)$.

Solution

$V(t)=100+2t$. DE: $\dfrac{dA}{dt}+\dfrac{2}{100+2t}A=12$, i.e. $\dfrac{dA}{dt}+\dfrac{1}{50+t}A=12$.

$\mu=e^{\int\frac{1}{50+t}\,dt}=e^{\ln(50+t)}=50+t$.

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\big[(50+t)A\big]=12(50+t) \implies (50+t)A=6(50+t)^2+C$.

At $t=0,A=0$: $50(0)=6(2500)+C\cdot1 \implies 0=15000+C \implies C=-15000$.

Answer: $A(t)=6(50+t)-\dfrac{15000}{50+t}$.

Practice 2

An RL circuit has $L=2$ H, $R=10\ \Omega$, connected to a constant voltage source $V=20$ V, with $I(0)=0$. Solve for $I(t)$ and find the steady-state current.

Solution

$2\dfrac{dI}{dt}+10I=20 \implies \dfrac{dI}{dt}+5I=10$.

$\mu=e^{5t}$; $\dfrac{d}{dt}[e^{5t}I]=10e^{5t} \implies e^{5t}I=2e^{5t}+C \implies I=2+Ce^{-5t}$.

At $t=0,I=0$: $0=2+C \implies C=-2$: $I(t)=2(1-e^{-5t})$.

As $t\to\infty$, $e^{-5t}\to0$, so $I\to2$ A.

Answer: $I(t)=2(1-e^{-5t})$; steady-state current is 2 A.

Practice 3

A 500L tank starts with 100 kg of salt. Brine at 2 kg/L flows in at 5L/min; the mixture flows out at 8L/min. Set up the DE for $A(t)$, noting the tank will empty at some finite time — find that time.

Solution

$V(t)=500+(5-8)t=500-3t$. The tank empties when $V=0$: $500-3t=0 \implies t=\dfrac{500}{3}\approx166.7$ min.

DE: $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=5(2)-8\cdot\dfrac{A}{500-3t}=10-\dfrac{8A}{500-3t}$, valid for $0\le t<\dfrac{500}{3}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{dA}{dt}+\dfrac{8}{500-3t}A=10$; the model is only valid up to $t=\dfrac{500}{3}$ min, when the tank empties.

Practice 4

An RL circuit has $L=4$ H, $R=8\ \Omega$, with a time-varying voltage source $V(t)=16e^{-2t}$, and $I(0)=1$ A. Solve for $I(t)$.

Solution

$4\dfrac{dI}{dt}+8I=16e^{-2t} \implies \dfrac{dI}{dt}+2I=4e^{-2t}$.

$\mu=e^{2t}$; $\dfrac{d}{dt}[e^{2t}I]=e^{2t}\cdot4e^{-2t}=4 \implies e^{2t}I=4t+C$.

At $t=0,I=1$: $1=0+C \implies C=1$.

Answer: $I(t)=(4t+1)e^{-2t}$.

Practice 5

A 250L tank starts with 40 kg salt. Brine at 0.5 kg/L flows in at 3L/min; mixture flows out at 5L/min. Find $A(t)$ (the DE will be linear with a shrinking volume).

Solution

$V(t)=250-2t$. DE: $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=3(0.5)-5\cdot\dfrac{A}{250-2t}=1.5-\dfrac{5A}{250-2t}$.

Standard form: $\dfrac{dA}{dt}+\dfrac{5}{250-2t}A=1.5$.

$\mu=e^{\int\frac{5}{250-2t}\,dt}$. Let $w=250-2t,dw=-2\,dt$: $\int\dfrac{5}{w}\cdot\left(-\dfrac{dw}{2}\right)=-\dfrac52\ln|w|$, so $\int\dfrac{5}{250-2t}\,dt=-\dfrac52\ln(250-2t)$ (taking $250-2t>0$).

$\mu=e^{-\frac52\ln(250-2t)}=(250-2t)^{-5/2}$.

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\big[(250-2t)^{-5/2}A\big]=1.5(250-2t)^{-5/2}$.

Integrating the right side (using the same $w$-substitution): $\displaystyle\int1.5(250-2t)^{-5/2}\,dt = 1.5\cdot\dfrac{(250-2t)^{-3/2}}{3}=0.5(250-2t)^{-3/2}+C$ (signs work out from the chain rule on $w=250-2t$).

$(250-2t)^{-5/2}A = 0.5(250-2t)^{-3/2}+C \implies A=0.5(250-2t)+C(250-2t)^{5/2}$.

At $t=0,A=40$: $40=0.5(250)+C(250)^{5/2} \implies 40=125+C(250)^{5/2} \implies C=\dfrac{40-125}{250^{5/2}}=\dfrac{-85}{250^{5/2}}$.

Answer: $A(t)=0.5(250-2t)-\dfrac{85}{250^{5/2}}(250-2t)^{5/2}$ (a genuinely involved exam-level setup — the key skill tested is correct DE setup and integrating-factor mechanics, even when the resulting algebra is heavy).

Practice 6

An RL circuit has $L=1$ H, $R=4\ \Omega$, $V(t)=e^{-t}$, $I(0)=2$. Solve for $I(t)$.

Solution

$\dfrac{dI}{dt}+4I=e^{-t}$.

$\mu=e^{4t}$; $\dfrac{d}{dt}[e^{4t}I]=e^{4t}\cdot e^{-t}=e^{3t} \implies e^{4t}I=\dfrac13e^{3t}+C$.

$I=\dfrac13e^{-t}+Ce^{-4t}$. At $t=0,I=2$: $2=\dfrac13+C \implies C=\dfrac53$.

Answer: $I(t)=\dfrac13e^{-t}+\dfrac53e^{-4t}$.

Practice 7

Explain why the mixing-with-changing-volume DE is linear but generally NOT separable, contrasting it with the constant-volume case, using the specific form of the coefficient of $A$.

Solution

In the constant-volume case, $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=(\text{const})-(\text{const})\cdot A$, which can be separated since both sides only involve $A$ and $t$ separately once rearranged — $\dfrac{dA}{\text{const}-\text{const}\cdot A}=dt$.

With changing volume, the coefficient of $A$ is $\dfrac{r_{out}}{V(t)}$, a genuine function of $t$ (not a constant) — the DE is $\dfrac{dA}{dt}+f(t)A=\text{const}$, which is linear (degree 1 in $A$) but the presence of both $A$ and an independently-varying function of $t$ multiplying it means $A$ and $t$ cannot be algebraically separated onto opposite sides of the equation.

Practice 8

A capacitor-free RL circuit reaches 90% of its steady-state current after 2 seconds, with $L=5$ H. If $R=10\ \Omega$, verify this is consistent with the circuit's time constant $\tau=L/R$.

Solution

Time constant: $\tau=L/R=5/10=0.5$ s.

For $I(t)=I_{ss}(1-e^{-t/\tau})$, reaching 90% means $1-e^{-t/\tau}=0.9 \implies e^{-t/\tau}=0.1 \implies t=\tau\ln10=0.5(2.3026)\approx1.151$ s.

Answer: the circuit reaches 90% of steady-state after approximately 1.15 s, not 2 s — so the stated 2-second observation is not consistent with $\tau=0.5$ s; either the component values or the observation must be re-checked (a good example of using the model to sanity-check reported data).

Practice 9

A tank has $V(t)=100+t$ (inflow exceeds outflow by 1 L/min), with outflow rate 3 L/min and inflow concentration 2 kg/L. Write the full standard-form linear DE for $A(t)$ (do not solve).

Solution

Rate in: $(1+3)\times2=8$ kg/min (total inflow rate is outflow $+$ net volume increase $=3+1=4$ L/min... let's use given inflow rate directly: since $V(t)=100+t$ implies $r_{in}-r_{out}=1$ and $r_{out}=3$, so $r_{in}=4$ L/min). Rate in $=4\times2=8$ kg/min. Rate out $=3\cdot\dfrac{A}{100+t}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{dA}{dt}+\dfrac{3}{100+t}A=8$.

Practice 10

Explain the physical meaning of the steady-state current $I=V/R$ in an RL circuit driven by a constant voltage $V$, in terms of what happens to the inductor's behaviour once the transient has died away.

Solution

An inductor opposes changes in current (via the term $L\,dI/dt$), but once the current has settled into a constant value ($dI/dt=0$), the inductor contributes no voltage drop at all — the circuit then behaves exactly like a plain resistor connected directly to the voltage source, obeying simple Ohm's law: $V=IR \implies I=V/R$. The inductor's influence is entirely a transient effect, shaping how the current approaches this steady value, not what the steady value itself is.

Exam Challenge Block

In this block, always identify the physical meaning of each coefficient before solving (rate in/out, resistance/inductance, or long-run level).

Practice 11

(a) Solve $I'+4I=12$, $I(0)=1$. (b) Find the steady-state current and time constant.

Solution

(a) $I=3+Ce^{-4t}$, $I(0)=1\Rightarrow C=-2$, so $I(t)=3-2e^{-4t}$.

(b) Steady state $I_\infty=3$ A; time constant $\tau=1/4$.

Practice 12

(a) Set up the changing-volume DE for $V_0=80$, $r_{in}=5$, $c_{in}=2$, $r_{out}=3$. (b) Write integrating factor only.

Solution

(a) $V(t)=80+2t$, so $A'+\frac{3}{80+2t}A=10$.

(b) $\mu(t)=\exp\!\int\frac{3}{80+2t}\,dt=(80+2t)^{3/2}$.

Practice 13

(a) Solve $y'+\frac{2}{t}y=t^3$ for $t>0$. (b) Apply $y(1)=0$.

Solution

$\mu=t^2$, so $(t^2y)'=t^5$.

$t^2y=t^6/6+C\Rightarrow y=t^4/6+C/t^2$. Using $y(1)=0$: $C=-1/6$.

Answer: $y=\frac{t^4}{6}-\frac{1}{6t^2}$.

Practice 14

(a) Solve $y'-3y=e^{2x}$. (b) State transient vs forcing response.

Solution

$\mu=e^{-3x}$, so $(e^{-3x}y)'=e^{-x}$.

$e^{-3x}y=-e^{-x}+C\Rightarrow y=-e^{2x}+Ce^{3x}$.

Transient/homogeneous: $Ce^{3x}$, forcing particular: $-e^{2x}$.

Practice 15

(a) RL model $0.5I'+10I=50$. Solve with $I(0)=0$. (b) Time to reach 95% steady-state?

Solution

$I'+20I=100\Rightarrow I=5+Ce^{-20t}$, so $C=-5$: $I=5(1-e^{-20t})$.

$0.95\cdot5=5(1-e^{-20t})\Rightarrow e^{-20t}=0.05\Rightarrow t=\frac{-\ln0.05}{20}\approx0.150$ s.

Practice 16

(a) For $A'+p(t)A=q(t)$ with $p=\frac{4}{100+t}$, find $\mu$. (b) If $q=20$, write exact-solution template.

Solution

(a) $\mu=(100+t)^4$.

(b) $(\mu A)'=20\mu\Rightarrow A=\mu^{-1}\!\left(\int 20\mu\,dt+C\right)$.

Practice 17

(a) Decide if $A'=\frac{A}{50+t}$ is separable, linear, or both. (b) Solve generally.

Solution

Both separable and linear.

$\frac{dA}{A}=\frac{dt}{50+t}\Rightarrow \ln|A|=\ln|50+t|+C\Rightarrow A=C(50+t)$.

Practice 18

(a) Solve $y'+2y=4+e^{-2x}$. (b) Apply $y(0)=1$.

Solution

$\mu=e^{2x}$, so $(e^{2x}y)'=4e^{2x}+1$.

$e^{2x}y=2e^{2x}+x+C\Rightarrow y=2+xe^{-2x}+Ce^{-2x}$.

$1=2+C\Rightarrow C=-1$, so $y=2+(x-1)e^{-2x}$.

Practice 19

(a) Mixing: $V_0=60$ L, in $=6$ L/min at 5 g/L, out $=4$ L/min, initial salt 30 g. Write solved $A(t)$ form. (b) Find long-time concentration trend.

Solution

$V=60+2t$, DE: $A'+\frac{4}{60+2t}A=30$ i.e. $A'+\frac{2}{30+t}A=30$.

$\mu=(30+t)^2$, so $(\mu A)'=30(30+t)^2$.

$A=10(30+t)+\frac{C}{(30+t)^2}$; from $A(0)=30$, $C=-243000$.

$c(t)=A/V\to5$ g/L (inflow concentration).

Practice 20

(a) Explain in one sentence why unequal flow rates usually break separability in mixing models. (b) Name the resulting DE class.

Solution

(a) Outflow term uses $A/V(t)$ and $V(t)$ depends on $t$, so coefficient of $A$ is time-dependent and cannot be isolated as pure $h(A)$ product.

(b) First-order linear DE.

Practice 21

(a) A tank of volume $V(t)=40+t$ has brine in at $2$ g/L and $3$ L/min, out at $2$ L/min. Write the linear DE for salt $A(t)$. (b) Find $\mu(t)$.

Solution

(a) $A'+\dfrac{2}{40+t}A=6$. (b) $\mu=(40+t)^2$.

Practice 22

(a) Solve $I'+\dfrac{R}{L}I=\dfrac{E_0}{L}\sin\omega t$ conceptually: what form does the particular solution take? (b) For $R/L=2$, $\omega=2$, why is resonance-like forcing special?

Solution

(a) Particular solution of form $A\cos\omega t+B\sin\omega t$. (b) When forcing frequency matches a natural scale of the damping, amplitudes are large but for first-order RL there is no true resonance pole — still, undetermined coefficients need careful algebra when $\omega=R/L$.

Practice 23

Tank empties: $V=100-4t$, in $0$ (pure water stopped), out $4$ L/min, $A(0)=20$. Solve $A(t)$ while the model is valid, and state the emptying time.

Solution

DE: $A'+\dfrac{4}{100-4t}A=0\Rightarrow A'+\dfrac{1}{25-t}A=0$. $A=C(25-t)$. $A(0)=20\Rightarrow C=20/25=4/5$, so $A=\tfrac45(25-t)$. Empties at $t=25$.

Practice 24

(a) Solve $y'+(\tan x)y=\cos x$ on $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. (b) Apply $y(0)=1$.

Solution

$\mu=\sec x$. $(y\sec x)'=1\Rightarrow y\sec x=x+C\Rightarrow y=(x+C)\cos x$. $y(0)=1\Rightarrow C=1$, so $y=(x+1)\cos x$.

Practice 25

RL: $L=1$, $R=2$, $E(t)=e^{-t}$, $I(0)=0$. Solve and identify transient vs forced response.

Solution

$I'+2I=e^{-t}$. $\mu=e^{2t}$, $(e^{2t}I)'=e^{t}\Rightarrow e^{2t}I=e^{t}+C\Rightarrow I=e^{-t}+Ce^{-2t}$. $I(0)=0\Rightarrow C=-1$: $I=e^{-t}-e^{-2t}$.

Forced/particular $e^{-t}$; transient $-e^{-2t}$.

Practice 26

(a) Mixing with equal rates is separable — show why. (b) Give one physical reason unequal rates force a linear non-autonomous model.

Solution

(a) $V$ constant $\Rightarrow A'=r c_{\mathrm{in}}-r A/V$ is $A'=a-bA$, autonomous separable. (b) $V(t)$ varies, so coefficient of $A$ depends on $t$ explicitly.

Practice 27

Find time for the current in $I'+5I=10$, $I(0)=0$ to reach $90\%$ of steady state.

Solution

$I=2(1-e^{-5t})$. $0.9\cdot2=2(1-e^{-5t})\Rightarrow e^{-5t}=0.1\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{\ln10}{5}$.

Practice 28

Changing volume: $V=50+3t$, $r_{\mathrm{out}}=5$, $c_{\mathrm{in}}=1$, $r_{\mathrm{in}}=8$, $A(0)=10$. Write $A(t)$ in closed form.

Solution

$A'+\dfrac{5}{50+3t}A=8$. $\mu=(50+3t)^{5/3}$. Then $A=\mu^{-1}\big(\int 8\mu\,dt+C\big)$. Integrating: $\int(50+3t)^{5/3}\,dt=\tfrac14(50+3t)^{8/3}$ times $8/3$ adjustments: $A=\tfrac{24}{8}(50+3t)+C(50+3t)^{-5/3}=3(50+3t)+C(50+3t)^{-5/3}$. $A(0)=10\Rightarrow150+C\cdot50^{-5/3}=10\Rightarrow C=-140\cdot50^{5/3}$.

Practice 29

(a) Convert $t y'+2y=t^3$ into standard form. (b) Solve for $t>0$ with $y(1)=1$.

Solution

(a) $y'+\dfrac{2}{t}y=t^2$. (b) $\mu=t^2$, $(t^2y)'=t^4$, $y=t^2/5+C/t^2$. $y(1)=1\Rightarrow C=4/5$: $y=\dfrac{t^2}{5}+\dfrac{4}{5t^2}$.

Practice 30

Interpretation: in an RL circuit with constant $E$, why does larger $R$ make the time constant smaller? Link to $\tau=L/R$.

Solution

$\tau=L/R$. Larger $R$ increases damping in $I'+(R/L)I=E/L$, so exponential $e^{-(R/L)t}$ decays faster — steady state reached sooner, at the cost of smaller steady current $E/R$.

Practice 31

(a) Solve $A'-\dfrac{1}{t}A=t$ for $t>0$. (b) What goes wrong if you try $t=0$ as initial time?

Solution

$\mu=1/t$, $(A/t)'=1\Rightarrow A/t=t+C\Rightarrow A=t^2+Ct$. (b) Coefficient $1/t$ blows up at $0$; existence theorem fails at $t=0$.

Practice 32

Two-tank idea (setup only): Tank 1 feeds Tank 2. Explain in one sentence why the DE for Tank 2 is still first-order linear if Tank 1's outflow concentration is a known function of $t$.

Solution

Tank 2 sees an inflow rate times a known $c_1(t)$, so its salt equation is $A_2'+p(t)A_2=q(t)$ with known $q$ — linear, non-homogeneous.

Practice 33

Solve $I'+I=\begin{cases}1,&0\le t<1\\0,&t\ge1\end{cases}$ with $I(0)=0$, matching $I$ continuous at $t=1$.

Solution

On $[0,1)$: $I=1+Ce^{-t}$, $I(0)=0\Rightarrow C=-1$, $I=1-e^{-t}$. At $t=1$, $I=1-e^{-1}$. For $t>1$: $I'+I=0\Rightarrow I=Ke^{-t}$, match $K e^{-1}=1-e^{-1}\Rightarrow K=e-1$, so $I=(e-1)e^{-t}$.

Practice 34

(a) Mixing: equal in/out rates $r$, volume $V$, $c_{\mathrm{in}}=0$ (flushing). Solve $A(t)$. (b) Time to remove $95\%$ of initial salt.

Solution

$A=A_0 e^{-(r/V)t}$. Need $e^{-(r/V)t}=0.05\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{V}{r}\ln20$.

Practice 35

Find integrating factor for $y'+\dfrac{2t}{1+t^2}y=\dfrac{1}{1+t^2}$ and solve with $y(0)=0$.

Solution

$\mu=1+t^2$. $((1+t^2)y)'=1\Rightarrow(1+t^2)y=t+C$. $y(0)=0\Rightarrow C=0$: $y=\dfrac{t}{1+t^2}$.

Practice 36

RL with switch: after long time at steady $E/R$, voltage is set to $0$ at $t=0$. Write $I(t)$ for $t>0$.

Solution

For $t>0$, $I'+(R/L)I=0$, $I(0)=E/R$, so $I(t)=\dfrac{E}{R}e^{-(R/L)t}$.

Practice 37

(a) Show $A'=\dfrac{2A}{30-t}$ is both separable and linear. (b) Solve with $A(0)=5$, domain of validity.

Solution

$\dfrac{dA}{A}=\dfrac{2\,dt}{30-t}\Rightarrow\ln|A|=-2\ln|30-t|+C\Rightarrow A=\dfrac{K}{(30-t)^2}$. $A(0)=5\Rightarrow K=5\cdot900=4500$, $A=\dfrac{4500}{(30-t)^2}$, valid for $t<30$.

Practice 38

Dimension check: verify that in $L I'+R I=E$, each term has units of voltage, and explain why the integrating factor $e^{(R/L)t}$ is dimensionless in the exponent.

Solution

$L\cdot$(A/s) $=\mathrm{H}\cdot\mathrm{A/s}=\mathrm{V}$; $R I$ in ohms$\cdot$amps $=$ V. Exponent $(R/L)t$ has units $\mathrm{s}^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{s}=1$ (dimensionless), as required.

Practice 39

Challenge modelling: brine enters at rate $4$ L/min with concentration $2+\sin t$ g/L into a $100$ L constant-volume tank, out at $4$ L/min, $A(0)=0$. Write the DE and the form of $A(t)$ after using $\mu=e^{t/25}$.

Solution

$A'+\dfrac{4}{100}A=4(2+\sin t)$, i.e. $A'+\dfrac{1}{25}A=8+4\sin t$. With $\mu=e^{t/25}$, $(e^{t/25}A)'=(8+4\sin t)e^{t/25}$; $A=e^{-t/25}\int(8+4\sin t)e^{t/25}\,dt$ (integration by parts on the sine term).

Practice 40

Capstone: compare time constants for mixing $A'+\dfrac{r}{V}A=rc_{\mathrm{in}}$ vs RL $I'+\dfrac{R}{L}I=E/L$. In what sense are $\tau=V/r$ and $\tau=L/R$ analogous?

Solution

Both measure time for the homogeneous transient $e^{-t/\tau}$ to decay by factor $1/e$. Mixing: throughput rate over capacity; RL: resistance over inductance. Larger capacity or inductance slows the approach to equilibrium.
