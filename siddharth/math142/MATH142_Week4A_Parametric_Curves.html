Start here The big picture — what this week is about

Not every curve is the graph of a function $y=f(x)$. Circles, loops, projectile paths, and cam profiles often need two equations driven by a parameter $t$: $x=x(t)$, $y=y(t)$. That is a parametrized curve. Once you have it, calculus becomes derivatives and integrals with respect to $t$ — slope, arc length, and (with polar or Cartesian conversion) area.

This sub-week sits beside Week 4 (polar). Polar is one special parametrization ($x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$). Here we treat general parametric form, which the MATH142 outline lists together with polar under “calculus with parametrized and polar curves.”

Why this matters

Engineering paths are almost always parametric in time: a robot arm tip, a CNC toolpath, a satellite orbit. Arc length of a parametric path is the distance travelled; $dy/dx=(dy/dt)/(dx/dt)$ is the slope of the track.

The roadmap

Section 0: slope and tangents for $x(t),y(t)$. Section 1: parametric arc length and area under a parametric curve. Then practice.

Lay of the land — this week's toolkit
  • Slope: $dy/dx=y\'(t)/x\'(t)$ when $x\'\\ne0$.
  • Arc length: $L=\\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\\sqrt{(x\')^2+(y\')^2}\\,dt$.
  • Area: $A=\\int_{t_1}^{t_2} y(t)x\'(t)\\,dt$ (with orientation care).
  • Link to polar: $x=r\\cos\\theta$, $y=r\\sin\\theta$ is a special parametric case.

0 Parametric slopes and tangents

If $x=x(t)$ and $y=y(t)$ are differentiable and $x'(t)\ne0$, the chain rule gives $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}=\frac{y'(t)}{x'(t)}.$$ Horizontal tangents where $y'=0$ and $x'\ne0$; vertical where $x'=0$ and $y'\ne0$. A cusp can occur when both vanish.

Key idea

$$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{y'(t)}{x'(t)},\qquad \dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{\dfrac{d}{dt}\big(y'/x'\big)}{x'(t)}$$

Worked example

For $x=t^2$, $y=t^3-3t$, find $dy/dx$ and the points with horizontal tangents.

$y'/x'=(3t^2-3)/(2t)$. Horizontal when $3t^2-3=0$ and $t\ne0$: $t=\pm1$, giving points $(1,-2)$ and $(1,2)$.

Practice 1

For $x=\cos t$, $y=\sin t$, find $dy/dx$ and simplify. What curve is this?

Solution

$dy/dx=(\\cos t)/(-\\sin t)=-\\cot t$. Eliminating $t$: $x^2+y^2=1$, the unit circle.

Practice 2

For $x=t^3-3t$, $y=t^2$, find where the tangent is vertical.

Solution

$x\'=3t^2-3=0\\Rightarrow t=\\pm1$. At those $t$, $y\'=2t\\ne0$, so vertical tangents at $(-2,1)$ and $(2,1)$.

Practice 3

Find the equation of the tangent line to $x=e^t$, $y=te^{2t}$ at $t=0$.

Solution

At $t=0$: $(x,y)=(1,0)$. $dy/dx=(e^{2t}+2te^{2t})/e^t=e^t(1+2t)$. At $0$: slope $1$. Tangent: $y-0=1(x-1)\\Rightarrow y=x-1$.

1 Parametric arc length and area

Arc length follows Pythagoras in $t$: $$L=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\sqrt{\big(x'(t)\big)^2+\big(y'(t)\big)^2}\,dt.$$ Area under a parametric curve (assuming $y\ge0$ and $x$ increasing) is $$A=\int_{t_1}^{t_2} y(t)\,x'(t)\,dt.$$

Key idea

$$L=\int\sqrt{(x')^2+(y')^2}\,dt,\qquad A=\int y\,x'\,dt$$

Worked example

Arc length of $x=\cos^3 t$, $y=\sin^3 t$ from $t=0$ to $\pi/2$ (astroid quarter).

$x'=-3\cos^2 t\sin t$, $y'=3\sin^2 t\cos t$. Then $(x')^2+(y')^2=9\sin^2 t\cos^2 t$, so $L=\int_0^{\pi/2}3|\sin t\cos t|\,dt=\tfrac32$.

Practice 4

Find the arc length of $x=3t^2$, $y=2t^3$ from $t=0$ to $t=1$.

Solution

$L=\\int_0^1\\sqrt{(6t)^2+(6t^2)^2}\\,dt=\\int_0^1 6t\\sqrt{1+t^2}\\,dt$. Let $u=1+t^2$: $3\\int_1^2 u^{1/2}du=2(2\\sqrt2-1)$.

Practice 5

Find the area under one arch of the cycloid $x=a(t-\\sin t)$, $y=a(1-\\cos t)$, $0\\le t\\le 2\\pi$.

Solution

$A=\\int_0^{2\\pi} a(1-\\cos t)\\cdot a(1-\\cos t)\\,dt=a^2\\int_0^{2\\pi}(1-\\cos t)^2\\,dt=a^2\\int_0^{2\\pi}(1-2\\cos t+\\tfrac{1+\\cos2t}{2})\\,dt=3\\pi a^2$.

Practice 6

For $x=t-\\sin t$, $y=1-\\cos t$ ($a=1$), set up $L$ on $[0,2\\pi]$ and evaluate.

Solution

$L=\\int_0^{2\\pi}\\sqrt{(1-\\cos t)^2+\\sin^2 t}\\,dt=\\int_0^{2\\pi}\\sqrt{2-2\\cos t}\\,dt=\\int_0^{2\\pi}2\\big|\\sin(t/2)\\big|\\,dt=8$.

Practice 7

Explain why polar $r=f(\\theta)$ is parametric with $x=f(\\theta)\\cos\\theta$, $y=f(\\theta)\\sin\\theta$, and write the polar arc-length formula from the parametric one.

Solution

Parameter is $\\theta$. Differentiating and simplifying gives $L=\\int\\sqrt{r^2+(dr/d\\theta)^2}\\,d\\theta$, the standard polar arc-length formula.

Practice 8

Find $dy/dx$ for the parametric ellipse $x=2\\cos t$, $y=3\\sin t$, and the slope at $t=\\pi/4$.

Solution

$dy/dx=(3\\cos t)/(-2\\sin t)=-\\tfrac32\\cot t$. At $\\pi/4$: $-\\tfrac32$.
