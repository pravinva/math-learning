Start here The big picture — what this week is about

Weeks 7–8 covered separable and standard linear first-order DEs. The MATH142 outline also requires four more first-order types: exact equations, special integrating factors, homogeneous equations, and the Bernoulli equation. This sub-week fills that gap.

Each method is a recognition skill: spot the structure, apply one rewrite, then integrate. Together with separable/linear they cover essentially every first-order exam DE.

Why this matters

Many engineering models are first-order but not obviously separable or already linear. Bernoulli appears in nonlinear drag and some population models; homogeneous equations appear in dilution and geometric similarity problems; exact equations are the differential form of conservation laws $dF=0$.

The roadmap

Exact & special IF → homogeneous (degree match) → Bernoulli (power of $y$) → mixed practice choosing the method.

Lay of the land — this week's toolkit
  • Exact: $M\,dx+N\,dy=0$ with $M_y=N_x$; solution $F(x,y)=C$.
  • Special IF: $\mu(x)$ if $(M_y-N_x)/N$ depends on $x$ only.
  • Homogeneous: $y'=f(y/x)$; substitute $v=y/x$.
  • Bernoulli: $y'+Py=Qy^n$; set $w=y^{1-n}$ to linearise.

0 Exact equations & special integrating factors

Write the DE as $M(x,y)\,dx+N(x,y)\,dy=0$. It is exact if $\partial M/\partial y=\partial N/\partial x$. Then there is $F(x,y)$ with $dF=0$, so $F(x,y)=C$ is the implicit solution. Find $F$ by integrating $M$ in $x$ and matching $\partial F/\partial y=N$.

If not exact, a special integrating factor depending on $x$ alone exists when $\dfrac{M_y-N_x}{N}$ is a function of $x$ only; then $\mu(x)=\exp\int\dfrac{M_y-N_x}{N}\,dx$. A factor depending on $y$ alone exists when $\dfrac{N_x-M_y}{M}$ is a function of $y$ only.

Key idea

Exact: $M_y=N_x$. Special IF ($x$ only): $\mu=e^{\int(M_y-N_x)/N\,dx}$.

Worked example

$(2x+y)\,dx+(x-2y)\,dy=0$. Check $M_y=1$, $N_x=1$ — exact. $F=x^2+xy-y^2=C$.

Practice 1

Test exactness and solve $(2xy+1)\,dx+(x^2-2y)\,dy=0$.

Solution

$M_y=2x=N_x$ — exact. $F=x^2y+x-y^2=C$.

Practice 2

Show $(y^2+2x)\,dx+2xy\,dy=0$ is exact and solve.

Solution

$M_y=2y=N_x$. $F=xy^2+x^2=C$.

Practice 3

Find an IF depending on $x$ only for $(3xy+y^2)\,dx+(x^2+xy)\,dy=0$, then solve.

Solution

$M_y-N_x=3x+2y-(2x+y)=x+y$. $(M_y-N_x)/N=(x+y)/(x^2+xy)=1/x$. So $\mu=x$. Multiply: $(3x^2y+xy^2)\,dx+(x^3+x^2y)\,dy=0$ (exact). Solution $x^3y+\tfrac12 x^2y^2=C$.

Practice 4

Is $(2x+\tan y)\,dx+(x\sec^2 y)\,dy=0$ exact? Solve it.

Solution

$M_y=\sec^2 y=N_x$ — exact. $F=x^2+x\tan y=C$.

Practice 5

Reconstruct the potential for $(3x^2y^2+2ye^x)\,dx+(2x^3y+2e^x+4y^3)\,dy=0$, and verify the result by differentiation.

Solution

Here $M_y=6x^2y+2e^x=N_x$, so a potential exists. Integrating $M$ with respect to $x$ gives $F=x^3y^2+2ye^x+g(y)$. Matching $F_y=2x^3y+2e^x+g'(y)$ with $N$ gives $g'(y)=4y^3$, hence $g=y^4$.

Answer: $x^3y^2+2ye^x+y^4=C$. Indeed, differentiating this expression gives exactly $M\,dx+N\,dy$.

Practice 6

Determine the parameter $a$ that makes $(2axy+3y^2)\,dx+(x^2+6xy+4y^3)\,dy=0$ exact, then solve the resulting equation.

Solution

$M_y=2ax+6y$ and $N_x=2x+6y$. Equality for all $(x,y)$ requires $a=1$. With this value, integrate $M=2xy+3y^2$ in $x$:

$$F=x^2y+3xy^2+g(y).$$

Then $F_y=x^2+6xy+g'(y)=N$, so $g'(y)=4y^3$ and $g=y^4$. Thus $x^2y+3xy^2+y^4=C$.

Practice 7

Solve the IVP $(2xy+e^y)\,dx+(x^2+xe^y+2y)\,dy=0$, $y(0)=1$, and identify the local condition needed to regard the answer as $y(x)$.

Solution

$M_y=2x+e^y=N_x$, so the equation is exact. Integrating $M$ in $x$ gives $F=x^2y+xe^y+g(y)$; comparison with $N$ yields $g'(y)=2y$, hence $g=y^2$.

The initial point gives $C=1$, so $x^2y+xe^y+y^2=1$. Since $F_y=x^2+xe^y+2y$ equals $2$ at $(0,1)$, the implicit-function theorem guarantees a unique local function $y(x)$ there.

Practice 8

Find an integrating factor depending only on $x$ for $(3y+2y^2/x)\,dx+(x+2y)\,dy=0$ on an interval not containing $x=0$, then solve.

Solution

With $M=3y+2y^2/x$ and $N=x+2y$, $$\frac{M_y-N_x}{N}=\frac{2+4y/x}{x+2y}=\frac{2}{x}.$$

Thus $\mu(x)=e^{\int 2/x\,dx}=x^2$ (any nonzero constant multiple is equivalent). Multiplication gives $(3x^2y+2xy^2)\,dx+(x^3+2x^2y)\,dy=0$. Its potential is $F=x^3y+x^2y^2$, so $x^3y+x^2y^2=C$ on each interval $x>0$ or $x<0$.

Practice 9

Find an integrating factor depending only on $y$ for $(2xy+y^2)\,dx+(3x^2+4xy)\,dy=0$, and state the domain on which the derivation is valid.

Solution

For $M=2xy+y^2$ and $N=3x^2+4xy$, $$\frac{N_x-M_y}{M}=\frac{(6x+4y)-(2x+2y)}{2xy+y^2}=\frac{2}{y}.$$

Hence $\mu(y)=y^2$. The multiplied form has coefficients $2xy^3+y^4$ and $3x^2y^2+4xy^3$, and its potential is $F=x^2y^3+xy^4$. Therefore $x^2y^3+xy^4=C$. The ratio test assumes $y\ne0$; the original equation also admits the integral curve $y=0$, which corresponds to $C=0$ after extension.

Practice 10

A lossless device has flow coordinate $q$ and head $h$ satisfying $(q/A+h)\,dq+(q+2gh)\,dh=0$, where $A,g>0$. Find the conserved quantity and the trajectory through $(q,h)=(0,H)$.

Solution

$M_h=1=N_q$, so the balance is the differential of a state function. Integrating $M$ in $q$ gives $F=q^2/(2A)+qh+\phi(h)$. Since $F_h=q+\phi'(h)=q+2gh$, we get $\phi=gh^2$.

The conserved quantity is $F=q^2/(2A)+qh+gh^2$. At $(0,H)$ it equals $gH^2$, so the required trajectory is $$\frac{q^2}{2A}+qh+gh^2=gH^2.$$

Practice 11

A thermodynamic state function has differential $dS=(2Tz+\ln z)\,dT+(T^2+T/z+\psi(z))\,dz$. If $\psi(z)=3z^2-2/z^2$, reconstruct $S(T,z)$ and verify path independence for $z>0$.

Solution

The cross derivatives are $\partial_z(2Tz+\ln z)=2T+1/z$ and $\partial_T(T^2+T/z+\psi)=2T+1/z$, so the form is exact. Integrating the $dT$ coefficient, $$S=T^2z+T\ln z+g(z).$$

Then $S_z=T^2+T/z+g'(z)$, so $g'=3z^2-2z^{-2}$ and $g=z^3+2/z$. Thus $S=T^2z+T\ln z+z^3+2/z+C$. Its differential reproduces both coefficients, proving path independence on the simply connected half-plane $z>0$.

Practice 12

Show that $(2xy+2y^2+y)\,dx+(x+2y)\,dy=0$ has an integrating factor $\mu(x)$, solve it, and verify the potential directly.

Solution

$M_y=2x+4y+1$ and $N_x=1$, hence $(M_y-N_x)/N=(2x+4y)/(x+2y)=2$. Therefore $\mu=e^{2x}$.

After multiplication, a potential is $F=e^{2x}(xy+y^2)$ because $$F_x=e^{2x}(2xy+2y^2+y),\qquad F_y=e^{2x}(x+2y).$$

Thus $e^{2x}(xy+y^2)=C$, and the displayed derivatives provide the requested algebraic verification.

1 Homogeneous first-order equations

A first-order DE is homogeneous (of degree 0) if it can be written $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=f\big(\dfrac{y}{x}\big)$. Substitute $v=y/x$ so $y=vx$, $y'=v+xv'$. The equation becomes separable in $v$ and $x$.

Key idea

$y=vx$ turns $y'=f(y/x)$ into $x v'=f(v)-v$, then separate.

Worked example

$y'=(x+y)/x$. Then $y'=1+y/x$. With $v=y/x$: $v+xv'=1+v\Rightarrow xv'=1\Rightarrow v=\ln|x|+C\Rightarrow y=x\ln|x|+Cx$.

Practice 13

Solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{y}{x}+\dfrac{x}{y}$.

Solution

$y'=v+1/v$ with $v=y/x$. Then $xv'=1/v\Rightarrow v\,dv=dx/x\Rightarrow v^2/2=\ln|x|+C\Rightarrow y^2=x^2(2\ln|x|+C')$.

Practice 14

Solve $(x^2+y^2)\,dx-2xy\,dy=0$.

Solution

$dy/dx=(x^2+y^2)/(2xy)=\tfrac12(1/v+v)$ with $v=y/x$. Since $y'=v+xv'$,

$$x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{1-v^2}{2v},\qquad \frac{2v}{1-v^2}\,dv=\frac{dx}{x}.$$

Integrating gives $-\ln|1-v^2|=\ln|x|+C$, hence $1-v^2=C/x$. Substituting $v=y/x$:

Answer: $y^2=x^2-Cx$ (equivalently $y^2=x^2+Kx$ after renaming the arbitrary constant).

Practice 15

Solve $y'=\dfrac{y-x}{y+x}$.

Solution

$v=(v-1)/(v+1)$. Then $v+xv'=(v-1)/(v+1)\Rightarrow xv'=\dfrac{v-1}{v+1}-v=-\dfrac{v^2+1}{v+1}$. Separate: integrate to $\tfrac12\ln(v^2+1)+\arctan v=-\ln|x|+C$.

Practice 16

Solve $y'=y/x+(y/x)^2$, retaining any solution lost during separation and stating the natural $x$-domains.

Solution

Set $v=y/x$. Then $v+xv'=v+v^2$, so $xv'=v^2$. For $v\ne0$, $dv/v^2=dx/x$, giving $-1/v=\ln|x|+C$. Hence $$y=-\frac{x}{\ln|x|+C}.$$

Division by $v^2$ loses the equilibrium $v=0$, so $y=0$ is also a solution. Solutions live on intervals contained in $x>0$ or $x<0$; the nonzero family must also avoid points where $\ln|x|+C=0$.

Practice 17

Solve the IVP $y'=\dfrac{x+2y}{2x-y}$, $y(1)=0$, implicitly, and identify where the differential equation itself is undefined.

Solution

With $v=y/x$, $v+xv'=(1+2v)/(2-v)$, hence $$\frac{2-v}{1+v^2}\,dv=\frac{dx}{x}.$$

Integration gives $2\arctan v-\tfrac12\ln(1+v^2)=\ln|x|+C$. Since $(x,y)=(1,0)$ gives $v=0$, $C=0$. Therefore $$2\arctan\!\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)-\frac12\ln\!\left(1+\frac{y^2}{x^2}\right)=\ln x.$$

The IVP lies in $x>0$. The original slope is undefined on $2x-y=0$, so its maximal continuation must stop before meeting that line.

Practice 18

Find the general non-straight solutions of $y'=(y/x)^2-2(y/x)+2$, and determine all straight-line solutions through the origin.

Solution

For $v=y/x$, $v+xv'=v^2-2v+2$, so $xv'=(v-1)(v-2)$. Separating away from $v=1,2$, $$\int\frac{dv}{(v-1)(v-2)}=\int\frac{dx}{x},$$ and partial fractions give $\ln|(v-2)/(v-1)|=\ln|x|+C$. Thus $$\frac{y-2x}{y-x}=Cx.$$

The divided factors yield the additional equilibrium ratios $v=1$ and $v=2$, hence the straight solutions $y=x$ and $y=2x$. The formula is used only on intervals with $x\ne0$ and $y\ne x$.

Practice 19

A similarity trajectory for a spreading jet obeys $dy/dx=2+y/x-(y/x)^2$. Solve it and interpret the constant-ratio trajectories.

Solution

Putting $v=y/x$ gives $xv'=2-v^2$. For $|v|\ne\sqrt2$, $$\int\frac{dv}{2-v^2}=\int\frac{dx}{x},\qquad \frac{1}{2\sqrt2}\ln\left|\frac{\sqrt2+v}{\sqrt2-v}\right|=\ln|x|+C.$$

Equivalently, $$\frac{\sqrt2+y/x}{\sqrt2-y/x}=C|x|^{2\sqrt2}$$ on any fixed-sign interval for $x$. The equilibrium ratios $v=\pm\sqrt2$ give the self-similar straight trajectories $y=\pm\sqrt2\,x$; they must be added because separation divided by $2-v^2$.

Practice 20

Solve $y'=1+y/x+(y/x)^2$ and use the transformed equation to describe the finite-domain obstruction of every real solution.

Solution

With $v=y/x$, $v+xv'=1+v+v^2$, so $dv/(1+v^2)=dx/x$. Therefore $$\arctan v=\ln|x|+C,\qquad y=x\tan(\ln|x|+C).$$

Direct substitution gives $y'=\tan\theta+\sec^2\theta$ with $\theta=\ln|x|+C$, while $1+y/x+(y/x)^2=1+\tan\theta+\tan^2\theta=\tan\theta+\sec^2\theta$. Each real branch is confined between consecutive zeros of $\cos(\ln|x|+C)$, where the tangent and hence $y$ blow up.

Practice 21

For $x>0$, solve $y'=\dfrac{y}{x}+\sin(y/x)$ implicitly. Classify the constant-ratio solutions and determine their stability as $x$ increases.

Solution

Set $v=y/x$ and $t=\ln x$. Then $dv/dt=\sin v$. Away from $v=k\pi$, $$\int\csc v\,dv=t+C,\qquad \ln\left|\tan\frac{v}{2}\right|=\ln x+C.$$

Thus $\tan\!\big(y/(2x)\big)=Cx$ on branches where the substitution is valid. The excluded equilibria give $v=k\pi$, hence $y=k\pi x$. Since the linearisation is $d(v-k\pi)/dt\approx(-1)^k(v-k\pi)$, even $k$ are unstable and odd $k$ are asymptotically stable as $x$ increases.

2 Bernoulli equations

A Bernoulli equation has the form $$y'+P(x)y=Q(x)y^n,\qquad n\ne0,1.$$ Divide by $y^n$ and set $w=y^{1-n}$. Then $w'=(1-n)y^{-n}y'$, and the equation becomes linear in $w$. Solve for $w$, then return to $y$.

Key idea

$w=y^{1-n}$ linearises Bernoulli to $w'+(1-n)P(x)w=(1-n)Q(x)$.

Worked example

$y'+y=xy^3$. Here $n=3$, $w=y^{-2}$. Then $-\tfrac12 w'+w=x\Rightarrow w'-2w=-2x$. IF $e^{-2x}$: solve, $w=x+\tfrac12+Ce^{2x}$, so $y^{-2}=x+\tfrac12+Ce^{2x}$.

Practice 22

A population $P(t)$ satisfies $P'=2P-\tfrac15P^2$, $P(0)=1$. Solve by the Bernoulli substitution and describe the long-term behavior.

Solution

Write $P'-2P=-P^2/5$. With $w=P^{-1}$, multiplication by $-P^{-2}$ gives $w'+2w=1/5$. Hence $w=1/10+Ce^{-2t}$. Since $w(0)=1$, $C=9/10$.

Therefore $$P(t)=\frac{10}{1+9e^{-2t}}.$$

For $t\ge0$, $P$ increases monotonically from $1$ and approaches the carrying capacity $10$. Substitution verifies $P'=2P(1-P/10)$.

Practice 23

A stored quantity $A(t)>0$ obeys $A'+\dfrac{2}{t+1}A=(t+1)\sqrt A$, $A(0)=4$. Solve and state the interval on which the positive model applies.

Solution

This is Bernoulli with $n=1/2$. Let $w=\sqrt A$; then $w'=A'/(2\sqrt A)$. Dividing by $\sqrt A$ and halving gives $$w'+\frac{1}{t+1}w=\frac{t+1}{2}.$$

The integrating factor is $t+1$, so $((t+1)w)'=(t+1)^2/2$. Thus $w=(t+1)^2/6+C/(t+1)$. From $w(0)=2$, $C=11/6$, and $$A(t)=\left(\frac{(t+1)^3+11}{6(t+1)}\right)^2.$$

The branch $w=\sqrt A>0$ containing $t=0$ extends over $t>-1$.

Practice 24

Solve $y'+\dfrac{2}{x}y=x^2y^{-1}$, $y(1)=1$, and explain how the initial sign selects a branch.

Solution

Here $n=-1$ and $w=y^2$. Multiplying the DE by $2y$ gives $$w'+\frac{4}{x}w=2x^2.$$

For $x>0$ the integrating factor is $x^4$, so $(x^4w)'=2x^6$. Hence $w=2x^3/7+Cx^{-4}$. The initial condition gives $C=5/7$, and positivity of $y(1)$ chooses $$y(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2x^3}{7}+\frac{5}{7x^4}},\qquad x>0.$$

The negative square root solves the transformed equation but not the stated IVP.

Practice 25

Choose the most direct method for each equation, then fully solve the Bernoulli one: (a) $y'=xy$; (b) $y'+2y=e^{-x}y^3$; (c) $y'=x/y$.

Solution

(a) is linear (also separable); (b) is Bernoulli with $n=3$; (c) is separable. For (b), put $w=y^{-2}$. Multiplication by $-2y^{-3}$ yields $$w'-4w=-2e^{-x}.$$

Using integrating factor $e^{-4x}$ gives $(e^{-4x}w)'=-2e^{-5x}$, so $w=(2/5)e^{-x}+Ce^{4x}$. Therefore $$y=\pm\left(\frac25e^{-x}+Ce^{4x}\right)^{-1/2},$$ wherever the bracket is positive. The equilibrium solution $y=0$, lost in forming $y^{-2}$, must also be included.

Practice 26

Verify algebraically that $y=[x^2(C-2\ln x)]^{-1/2}$ solves $xy'+y=x^2y^3$ for $x>0$, wherever the bracket is positive.

Solution

Let $B=C-2\ln x$, so $y=x^{-1}B^{-1/2}$. Since $B'=-2/x$, $$y'=-x^{-2}B^{-1/2}+x^{-2}B^{-3/2}.$$

Therefore $xy'+y=x^{-1}B^{-3/2}$. Also $x^2y^3=x^2(x^{-3}B^{-3/2})=x^{-1}B^{-3/2}$, so both sides agree. The conditions $x>0$ and $B>0$ ensure the displayed real branch is defined.

Practice 27

Solve $y'=y+y^2$, $y(0)=y_0$, and classify decay, convergence, or finite-time blow-up for all initial values.

Solution

For $y\ne0$, $w=y^{-1}$ gives $w'+w=-1$, hence $w=Ce^{-t}-1$. If $y_0\ne0$, $C=1+1/y_0$, so $$y(t)=\frac{y_0e^t}{1+y_0-y_0e^t}.$$

Also $y=0$ and $y=-1$ are equilibria. If $y_0>0$, the denominator vanishes at $t_*=\ln(1+1/y_0)$, giving finite-time blow-up. If $-1<y_0<0$, then $y(t)\to-1$ from above; if $y_0<-1$, it approaches $-1$ from below. Thus $-1$ is stable while $0$ is unstable forward in time.

Practice 28

Solve the IVP $y'+\dfrac{1}{x}y=x^2y^3$, $y(1)=1$, and determine the maximal positive-$x$ interval containing $1$.

Solution

Set $w=y^{-2}$. Then $w'-(2/x)w=-2x^2$. The integrating factor is $x^{-2}$, so $$(x^{-2}w)'=-2.$$

Thus $x^{-2}w=-2x+C$. Since $w(1)=1$, $C=3$, giving $w=x^2(3-2x)$. The positive initial value selects $$y=\frac{1}{x\sqrt{3-2x}}.$$

Reality and regularity require $x>0$ and $3-2x>0$, so the maximal interval containing $1$ is $(0,3/2)$.

Practice 29

Turbulent energy $E(s)>0$ along a duct is modelled by $E'+aE=bE^{3/2}$, where $a,b>0$ and $E(0)=E_0$. Solve and identify the threshold separating decay from growth.

Solution

Let $w=E^{-1/2}$. Since $w'=-(1/2)E^{-3/2}E'$, multiplication by $-\tfrac12E^{-3/2}$ gives $$w'-\frac{a}{2}w=-\frac{b}{2}.$$

Hence $w=b/a+(E_0^{-1/2}-b/a)e^{as/2}$ and $$E(s)=\left[\frac{b}{a}+\left(E_0^{-1/2}-\frac{b}{a}\right)e^{as/2}\right]^{-2}.$$

The nonzero equilibrium is $E_*=(a/b)^2$. If $0<E_0<E_*$, then $E\to0$; if $E_0=E_*$ it is constant; if $E_0>E_*$, the bracket reaches zero at finite $s$, signalling model blow-up.

Practice 30

Solve $y'+(\tan x)y=(\sin x)y^2$ on an interval with $\cos x\ne0$. Include the solution excluded by the substitution and verify the family in the original equation.

Solution

For $y\ne0$, let $w=1/y$. Then $w'-(\tan x)w=-\sin x$. An integrating factor is $\cos x$, giving $$(w\cos x)'=-\sin x\cos x.$$

Thus $w\cos x=\tfrac12\cos^2x+C$, and $$y=\frac{\cos x}{C+\tfrac12\cos^2x}.$$

Also $y=0$ solves the original equation. For verification, $w=(C+\cos^2x/2)/\cos x$ satisfies $w'-\tan x\,w=-\sin x$; reversing the identity $w'=-y'/y^2$ yields the original DE wherever the denominator is nonzero.

Practice 31

Solve $y'-\dfrac{2}{x}y=x^2\sqrt y$ for nonnegative $y$ on $x>0$. Explain why the transformed family does not capture every solution.

Solution

On an interval where $y>0$, let $w=\sqrt y$. Since $y'=2ww'$, division by $w$ gives $$w'-\frac{1}{x}w=\frac{x^2}{2}.$$

With integrating factor $x^{-1}$, $(w/x)'=x/2$, so $w=x^3/4+Cx$. Therefore $$y=\left(\frac{x^3}{4}+Cx\right)^2$$ on intervals where $x^3/4+Cx>0$. The equilibrium $y=0$ was lost by division by $\sqrt y$. Because $\sqrt y$ is not Lipschitz at $y=0$, solutions may also remain at zero up to a joining point and then follow a positive branch with matching $w=0$.

Practice 32

Solve $y'+\dfrac{1}{x}y=x y^2$ ($x>0$).

Solution

$n=2$, $w=y^{-1}$. Then $-w'+(1/x)w=x\Rightarrow w'-(1/x)w=-x$. $\mu=1/x$: $(w/x)'=-1\Rightarrow w/x=-x+C\Rightarrow w=-x^2+Cx\Rightarrow y=1/(Cx-x^2)$.

Practice 33

Solve $y'-y=e^x y^2$.

Solution

$n=2$, $w=y^{-1}$. $-w'-w=e^x\Rightarrow w'+w=-e^x$. $\mu=e^x$: $(e^x w)'=-e^{2x}\Rightarrow e^x w=-\tfrac12 e^{2x}+C\Rightarrow w=-\tfrac12 e^x+Ce^{-x}$. Hence $y=1/(-\tfrac12 e^x+Ce^{-x})$.

Practice 34

Classify and solve $xy'+y=x^2 y^3$.

Solution

Write $y'+(1/x)y=x y^3$ — Bernoulli with $n=3$. Set $w=y^{-2}$:

$$-\tfrac12w'+\frac{1}{x}w=x\Rightarrow w'-\frac{2}{x}w=-2x.$$

The integrating factor is $x^{-2}$, so $(x^{-2}w)'=-2/x$. Thus $x^{-2}w=-2\ln|x|+C$.

Answer: $w=x^2(C-2\ln|x|)$ and $y=\pm\big[x^2(C-2\ln|x|)\big]^{-1/2}$ on intervals where the expression is positive.

Practice 35

Which method? (a) $y'=(x+y)/x$ (b) $(2x+y)\,dx+(x+2y)\,dy=0$ (c) $y'+y=y^3\sin x$. State the method only.

Solution

(a) homogeneous ($v=y/x$). (b) exact ($M_y=1=N_x$). (c) Bernoulli ($n=3$).

Practice 36

Solve the exact equation $(e^y+2x)\,dx+(xe^y-1)\,dy=0$.

Solution

$M_y=e^y=N_x$. $F=xe^y+x^2-y=C$.
