01 Inequality proof toolkit

Most algebraic inequality proofs transform the difference between two sides into an expression known to be non-negative. The engine is usually a square:

$$(u-v)^2\ge0\quad\Rightarrow\quad u^2+v^2\ge2uv.$$

  • Adding the same quantity preserves direction.
  • Multiplying by a positive quantity preserves direction.
  • Multiplying by a negative quantity reverses direction.
  • Never divide by an expression until its sign is known.

Example — a rational inequality

For $x>0$, prove $x+\dfrac1x\ge2$. Since $(x-1)^2\ge0$, $x^2+1\ge2x$. Dividing by $x>0$ gives the result.

02 Arithmetic mean–geometric mean

For positive $a,b$:

$$\frac{a+b}{2}\ge\sqrt{ab},$$

with equality exactly when $a=b$. It converts fixed-product problems into minimum-sum results and fixed-sum problems into maximum-product results.

Proof from a square

$(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\ge0$ gives $a+b-2\sqrt{ab}\ge0$. Rearranging and dividing by $2$ proves AM–GM.

Two common failures

Do not assume $P(k+1)$; that is what you must prove. Do not merely replace every $k$ by $k+1$ in the hypothesis. Start from one side of $P(k+1)$ and deliberately make the $P(k)$ expression appear.

04 Induction for sums and divisibility

Series move

For a sum, write the $k+1$ case as “the sum to $k$ + the next term”, then substitute the inductive hypothesis.

Divisibility move

If the hypothesis says $A_k=dm$, manipulate $A_{k+1}$ until it contains a multiple of $A_k$ plus another obvious multiple of $d$.

05 Induction for inequalities

An equality hypothesis can be substituted exactly. An inequality hypothesis only supplies a bound. The inductive step therefore usually has two links:

$$L_{k+1}\ \ge\ \underbrace{\text{bound from }P(k)}_{\text{use the hypothesis}}\ \ge\ R_{k+1}.$$

You must justify both inequalities and check the range where the second one is true.

Example — prove $2^n\ge n+1$ for $n\ge0$

Base: $2^0=1$. Assume $2^k\ge k+1$. Then $2^{k+1}=2\cdot2^k\ge2(k+1)\ge k+2$ because $k\ge0$. Therefore the claim follows by induction.

06 Practice — from foundations to Extension 2
P01
Prove that $x^2+4x+7>0$ for every real $x$.
Worked solution

Complete the square: $x^2+4x+7=(x+2)^2+3$. Since $(x+2)^2\ge0$, the expression is at least $3$, hence strictly positive.

P02
For positive $a,b$, prove $\dfrac ab+\dfrac ba\ge2$.
Worked solution

Because $(a-b)^2\ge0$, $a^2+b^2\ge2ab$. Divide by $ab>0$ (so the direction is unchanged): $\dfrac ab+\dfrac ba\ge2$. Equality occurs at $a=b$.

P03
Use AM–GM to find the minimum of $x+\dfrac9x$ for $x>0$.
Worked solution

AM–GM gives $x+\dfrac9x\ge2\sqrt{x(9/x)}=6$. Equality requires $x=9/x$, so $x=3$. The minimum is $\boxed6$.

P04
Prove by induction that $1+3+\cdots+(2n-1)=n^2$ for $n\ge1$.
Worked solution

Base: $n=1$: $1=1^2$.

Hypothesis: assume $1+3+\cdots+(2k-1)=k^2$.

Step: add the next odd number: $k^2+[2(k+1)-1]=k^2+2k+1=(k+1)^2$.

Therefore the identity holds for all $n\ge1$ by induction.

P05
Prove by induction that $6\mid(7^n-1)$ for every integer $n\ge1$.
Worked solution

Base: $7^1-1=6$.

Assume $7^k-1=6m$. Then

$$7^{k+1}-1=7(7^k-1)+6=7(6m)+6=6(7m+1).$$

Thus it is divisible by $6$, completing the induction.

P06
Prove by induction that $2^n\ge n+1$ for all $n\ge0$.
Worked solution

Base: $2^0=1=0+1$.

Assume $2^k\ge k+1$. Then $2^{k+1}=2\cdot2^k\ge2(k+1)\ge k+2$ because $k\ge0$. Hence the result follows by induction.

P07
Prove by induction that $3^n>n^2$ for every integer $n\ge3$.
Worked solution

Base: $3^3=27>9$.

Assume $3^k>k^2$ for $k\ge3$. Then $3^{k+1}>3k^2$. Also

$$3k^2-(k+1)^2=2k^2-2k-1>0\quad(k\ge3).$$

Therefore $3^{k+1}>(k+1)^2$, and induction completes the proof.

P08
Prove $1+\dfrac12+\cdots+\dfrac1{2^n}=2-\dfrac1{2^n}$ for $n\ge0$.
Worked solution

Base: at $n=0$, both sides equal $1$.

Assume the result for $k$. Then

$$\left(2-\frac1{2^k}\right)+\frac1{2^{k+1}} =2-\frac2{2^{k+1}}+\frac1{2^{k+1}} =2-\frac1{2^{k+1}}.$$

The result follows by induction.

P09
What is wrong with: “Assume $2^k>k^2$. Then $2^{k+1}>2k^2>(k+1)^2$”? Repair it for $k\ge5$.
Worked solution

The second inequality is not automatic and must be proved. For $k\ge5$:

$$2k^2-(k+1)^2=k^2-2k-1=(k-1)^2-2>0.$$

With this extra justification, the inductive step is valid (after checking the base case $n=5$).

P10
A rectangle has positive side lengths $x,y$ and fixed area $xy=36$. Prove its perimeter is at least $24$.
Worked solution

By AM–GM, $x+y\ge2\sqrt{xy}=12$. Thus the perimeter $2x+2y=2(x+y)\ge24$. Equality occurs when $x=y=6$.
