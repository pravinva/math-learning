GELFAND & FOMIN

Calculus of Variations

Guided lessons through I. M. Gelfand and S. V. Fomin, Calculus of Variations: functionals, Euler’s equation, canonical form, second variation, fields, multiple integrals, direct methods, and optimal control.

Chapter 1
Elements of the Theory
Functionals, function spaces, the variation, and Euler’s equation for the simplest problem.
Chapter 2
Further Generalizations
Functionals depending on several functions, higher derivatives, and variable endpoints.
Chapter 3
The General Variation of a Functional
The general variation, transversality, and broken extremals.
Chapter 4
The Canonical Form of the Euler Equations
Hamiltonian form, the Hamilton–Jacobi equation, and conservation laws.
Chapter 5
The Second Variation
Jacobi’s condition and sufficient conditions for a weak extremum.
Chapter 6
Fields and the Strong Extremum
Fields of extremals, Hilbert’s invariant integral, and Weierstrass’s condition.
Chapter 7
Variational Problems Involving Multiple Integrals
Functionals of several independent variables and the Euler–Ostrogradsky equation.
Chapter 8
Direct Methods in the Calculus of Variations
Minimizing sequences, Ritz’s method, and related approximation ideas.
Appendix I
Propagation of Disturbances and the Canonical Equations
Wave fronts, characteristics, and the link with Hamilton’s equations.
Appendix II
Variational Methods in Problems of Optimal Control
Pontryagin’s principle as a variational statement for control problems.
